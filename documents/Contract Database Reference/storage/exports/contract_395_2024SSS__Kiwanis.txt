PRINCIPAL SERVICES (describe) 
 
The Sixth Street Shelter assists families experiencing homelessness to secure safe housing through family-centered 
services, in-house programming, and referrals to community resources while advocating that affordable housing is a 
human right. Located in Center City Allentown, Sixth Street Shelter provides safe, short-term housing for families with 
at least one child under the age of 18 experiencing homelessness. The property has 25 private apartments, a Family 
Resource Center, and a playground for residents. The Sixth Street Shelter is unique because it is the only shelter in the 
city where entire families of any configuration (including those with fathers and teenaged boys, multi-generational 
families, and those from the LGBTQ+ community) stay together in their own apartments.  
 
For many families, a 60-90 day stay at the Sixth Street Shelter enables them to begin to stabilize their income and address 
the problems that led to their homelessness. The families who come to the Shelter do so for a variety of reasons: low-
paying jobs, lack of affordable housing, mental and/or physical health crises, domestic violence, and lack of access to 
reliable transportation, healthcare, and/or childcare. The Shelter provides each family with a fully furnished apartment 
and supportive services, including individualized case management. Staff work to connect them with the necessary 
resources to address their individual needs, such as financial literacy and parenting classes, access to childcare and 
healthcare, and mental health support. During their residency, the family develops a Family Service Plan with their 
caseworker, setting household, budgeting, educational, and vocational goals and seeking suitable housing and 
employment. Our Benchmark Family Care Initiative aims to ensure that all residents have or obtain medical insurance 
and are connected to appropriate providers so that they can achieve a list of benchmarks related to their relevant 
medical, dental, and mental health care, as well as track financial goals. We have added a Housing Specialist to our staff 
to work with our residents and local landlords in order to identify affordable housing options and facilitate faster 
placements for families. We have also expanded our educational and vocational programming at the Sixth Street Shelter 
to increase residents’ earning capacity, which will help them to remain stably housed when they move out of the Shelter 
and into permanent housing. Staff maintain contact with the family following their move into permanent housing to 
offer additional assistance and determine how well they are continuing to move toward self-sufficiency.  
 
In our last fiscal year, the Sixth Street Shelter provided shelter to 91 families consisting of 341 people, including 213 
children (85 of whom were 5 and under). 91% of those families had a female head of household, and 61% were headed 
by a single mother. Of last year’s families, 43% identified as Black or African American, 21% as White, 3% as Native 
Hawaiian or Pacific Islander, 3% as American Indian, and 30% did not report their race; and 44% identified as 
Hispanic/Latino. The Shelter endeavors to engage staff that reflect the community we serve and have lived expertise 
with homelessness. Despite the stark affordable housing crisis in the Lehigh Valley, 36% of families moved into stable 
affordable housing upon leaving the Sixth Street Shelter last fiscal year.

SPECIFIC PURPOSE: 
 
Recent local events have served as a reminder of the importance of fire safety education. Fires can spread quickly, 
impacting multiple residences and displacing families in their aftermath. The Sixth Street Shelter wants to ensure that 
families, and children in particular, are prepared for a fire emergency. We plan to purchase fire blankets as an additional 
safety resource. Fire extinguishers need to be properly maintained and recharged after each use. Individuals must know 
how to use the extinguisher correctly, and they can be unwieldy for children. Also, in the event of an emergency, someone 
may panic and forget the instructions. Alternatively, fire blankets are lightweight and easier to use, especially for children. 
You simply pull the blanket out of the storage pouch by the tabs, open it to expand, and toss it over the flames. Fire 
blankets are effective in quickly extinguishing different types of small fires, including grease fires and electric fires, and can 
also serve as a thermal or heat shield. They do not expire or require additional maintenance. If they are not damaged, they 
can be folded up and reused.  
 
We plan to purchase fire blankets to keep in each apartment and in common spaces of the Shelter, including the office 
and Family Resource Center. As long as we have a sufficient supply of blankets, we want to allow families to take a fire 
blanket with them when they move out of the Shelter and into their own housing. We recognize that this is an important 
safety tool that families may not have funds to invest in for themselves. We will teach each family how to use the fire 
blanket properly when they enter the Shelter. We also intend to connect with local fire departments to arrange additional 
educational sessions with our families related to overall fire safety. The Sixth Street Shelter already provides a variety of 
services and programming to support our families in their path to self-sufficiency, and adding fire safety as another 
educational component will help to protect them beyond their stay at the Shelter. Importantly, this will enable children to 
protect themselves, and possibly others, in the event of a fire emergency.
