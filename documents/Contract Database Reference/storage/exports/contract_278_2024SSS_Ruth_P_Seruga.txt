Project Details
 
*Project/Program ObjectivesThe Sixth Street Shelter assists families
experiencing homelessness secure safe
housing through family-centered services,

in-house programming, and referrals to
community resources while advocating to
end homelessness and housing insecurity
in the Lehigh Valley. Located in Center City
Allentown, the property has 25 private
apartments, a Family Resource Center,
playground, and full-choice food pantry for
residents. A stay at the Shelter enables
families to begin to stabilize their income
and address the problems that led to their
homelessness. The Shelter provides each
family with a fully furnished apartment and
supportive services, including individualized
case management. Staff work to connect
them with the necessary resources to
address their individual needs, such as
financial literacy and parenting classes,
access to childcare and healthcare, and
mental health support. During their
residency, the family develops a Family
Service Plan with their caseworker, setting
household, budgeting, educational, and
vocational goals and seeking suitable
housing and employment. Staff maintain
contact with the family following their move
into permanent housing to offer additional
assistance and determine how well they are
continuing to move toward self-sufficiency.
In addition to the comprehensive services
provided to Shelter residents, the Sixth
Street Shelter has become a resource
center for former residents and people
from the surrounding neighborhood.
For this project specifically, the objective is
to provide a clean and healthy environment
for our residents as they work towards their
goals. This entails having the proper
appliances in each apartment to support
the residents' health and well-being. Each
family residing at the Sixth Street Shelter
has access to an on-site, full-choice food
pantry and their own kitchen in their
apartment so that they can store fresh

foods and prepare their own meals. We
collaborate with local partners and health
professionals to offer educational sessions
on healthy cooking and eating and on
general wellness. Parents and children
learn to cook alongside each other. These
programs and services are essential
because the negative health impacts of
homelessness are well documented,
including short- and long-term adverse
health outcomes. According to the Housing
Alliance of Pennsylvania, "Experiencing
homelessness leads to new and
exacerbates existing health problems,"
through the lack of access to "clean water,
refrigeration, shelter from the elements,
and safety from harm." Long-term impacts
include chronic illnesses, over-reliance on
costly and acute health care services, and
significantly higher morbidity. The National
Low Income Housing Coalition cites that
"People experiencing homelessness die on
average 12 years sooner than people in the
general population in the U.S." Housing is a
key social determinant of health, as it
influences one's mental and physical health,
well-being and quality of life. By providing
residents with shelter, a kitchen, food, and
educational resources, we support them in
developing healthier habits to improve their
health and well-being, which ultimately
supports their housing stability.

whether the appliances are purchased,

More broadly, this project will contribute to
reaching our overall performance targets
for the Sixth Street Shelter in our current
fiscal year: 1) At least 85 families will reside
at the Shelter and develop Family Service
Plans based on their housing, educational
and financial goals; 2) 50% of families will
save money toward rent or a security
deposit upon leaving the Shelter; 3) 75% of
families will achieve their Family Service
Plan goals; 4) 100% of families will create a
written budget to understand credit and
money management as a part of their goal
plan; 5) 20% of families will move into
affordable housing upon leaving.
Each year, a work plan is developed with
measurable goals and performance targets
that guide program activities. In order to
measure effectiveness of services and
outcomes, the work plan is evaluated twice
yearly by the Program Development &
Evaluation Committee of CALV's Board of
Directors and is then approved by CALV's
full Board. In addition, Sixth Street Shelter
utilizes the Homeless Management
Information System (HMIS) and ClientTrack
to track and analyze participant data. The
Shelter also tracks qualitative data to
document the positive long-term impact of
the Shelter in the community. Staff conduct
follow-up evaluations with former residents
that can be located at 3-, 6-, and 12-month
intervals to ascertain their continued
progress. 
*CollaborationThe Sixth Street Shelter partners with many
government entities and non-profit

agencies to ensure that residents receive
the services and gain the skills they will
need as part of a comprehensive plan to
help them to become self-sufficient. The
Shelter has a nearly 30-year partnership
with Lehigh County Office of Children and
Youth Services, and many other local
organizations contribute to programming
for our residents. For instance, ESSA Bank
leads financial literacy classes, CareerLink
assists with job searches and resume
writing, and Highmark Wholecare facilitates
"Wellness Friday" sessions for both Shelter
residents and community members. The
Shelter is also a member of the Lehigh
Valley Regional Homeless Advisory Board.
The LVRHAB is a consortium of
representatives from City and County
governments, social service agencies,
veterans support groups, faith-based and
other community organizations, homeless
advocates, homeless or formerly homeless
people, representatives from legislators,
and other interested parties with the
mission to prevent and end homelessness
in the Lehigh Valley. 
*Project/Program BackgroundSince 1984, the Sixth Street Shelter, a
program of Community Action Lehigh
Valley, has been offering services to help
prepare families for the challenges of a
difficult job and rental market that too often
result in homelessness. It is the only family
shelter in the region that admits families of
any configuration (with at least one child
under 18) and houses them in their own
private apartments. The Sixth Street Shelter
has proven to be a vital resource in the
community, and while it has been operating
for almost 40 years, the program has
adapted to the changing needs of the
community. For instance, there is currently
a severe shortage of available, affordable
housing units locally, and landlords have

been very selective in whom they rent to.
Our caseworkers report that they struggle
to find housing for their clients despite
having rental assistance and supportive
services available. This means that
residents are experiencing longer stays in
the Shelter before moving into permanent
housing. We recently added a Housing
Innovation Specialist to our staff to work
with our residents and local landlords in
order to identify affordable housing options
and facilitate faster placements for families.
We have also been expanding our
educational and vocational programming at
the Sixth Street Shelter to increase
residents' earning capacity, which will help
them to remain stably housed when they
move out of the Shelter and into
permanent housing.
