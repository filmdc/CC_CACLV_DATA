Brief description for which funding is being requested. 
Second Harvest Food Bank requests funding to support the purchase, storage, and distribution of 
nutritious foods to our network of food pantries within Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne Counties.  
 
Briefly summarize the mission of your organization. 
The mission of Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast Pennsylvania is to obtain 
food and distribute it to people in need through area non-profits and to provide resources for education 
and advocacy to end hunger. We provide nutritious food, offer nutrition education, advocate to end 
food insecurity, dismantle barriers to create access, and work to attain food justice. We respect and 
preserve the dignity of each of our neighbors.  
Second Harvest Food Bank serves Lehigh, Northampton, Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne counties. 
Last year, Second Harvest distributed 14.7 million pounds of food to our network of 200+ nonprofit 
agencies (food pantries, schools, meal centers, shelters, childcare agencies, senior centers), a 44% 
increase from the previous year. Included in this total is 6.7 million pounds of fresh produce, perishable 
protein, and fresh dairy products (46% of total distribution). Annually, we rescue more than 8 million 
pounds of food from ending up in a landfill.

3 
 
On average, the Second Harvest network serves more than 87,000 people each month - a 52% increase 
from last year. The pantries within the Second Harvest network are operated almost solely through the 
work of community volunteers. Our food distribution is a model of neighbors helping neighbors. 
Second Harvest is a program of Community Action Lehigh Valley and a member of Feeding America. 
 
Narrative of the Project/Program (Max 2,000 words) 
Second Harvest Food Bank will increase access to nutritious foods for the people of Carbon, Monroe, Pike, 
and Wayne counties.  
Many people in Northeast Pennsylvania are struggling. According to Feeding America hunger statistics, 
there are 31,860 people facing food insecurity across the counties of Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne – 
representing 9.2% of the population. Inflation continues to have an impact and is exacerbated by the 
affordable housing crisis affecting our region. As neighbors look to stretch every dollar of their food 
budget, nutritious foods are often priced out. It is our continued mission, through partnerships with 
organizations like the AllOne Foundation, to not only increase the amount of food available to our 
communities but to improve the quality of that food.  
Last year, we distributed a total of 5.7 million pounds of food to agencies in Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and 
Wayne counties, including 3.4 million pounds (59%) of nutritious “Foods to Encourage” (F2E). Feeding 
America developed the F2E framework to align with recommendations from USDA's MyPlate and Dietary 
Guidelines for Americans. The framework promotes the consumption of fruits, vegetables, lean protein, 
whole grains, and low-fat dairy products. 
To increase our distribution of fresh and nutritious foods, and as part of our larger nutrition strategy, we 
developed and implemented a Food and Nutrition Policy based on Healthy Eating Research (HER) 
guidelines (which were created in partnership between the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation, Duke 
University, and the charitable food network). Our Nutrition Policy prioritizes and deprioritizes food by 
categorizing them as “choose often”, “choose sometimes”, and “choose rarely”. “Choose often” foods 
coincide with Feeding America’s Foods to Encourage. Our Nutrition Policy informs all aspects of our food 
sourcing and distribution strategies.  
Because research shows that nutrition education is a critical component in changing food purchasing 
and eating behaviors in people with lower-incomes, we implement our Healthy Pantry Initiative (HPI) in 
tandem with our Nutrition Policy. HPI is a program of Feeding Pennsylvania that works to encourage 
pantry customers to choose healthier food items. Second Harvest’s Nutrition Educator / Healthy Pantry 
Coordinator works intimately with pantry directors and volunteers to help them create pantry spaces 
that are client-centered and promote the consumption of healthy foods. Our Nutrition Educator also 
works closely with pantry participants to ensure that the foods available to take home are prepared and 
consumed in healthy ways. Our Nutrition Educator coordinates taste-testing events, cooking 
demonstrations, and provides educational materials and recipes cards. Currently, our cohort of 16 HPI 
sites includes 6 sites within Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne counties. After about one and a half to 
two years in the program, pantry sites move into a maintenance phase, remaining in the program to 
receive support and access to nutrition education. As pantries move through into the maintenance

4 
 
phase, staff capacity frees up, and new pantries are introduced. Second Harvest has plans to onboard 
additional agencies within our northern tier.  
It is our mission to provide access to food to the increasing number of individuals and families facing 
food insecurity, with a distinct focus on fresh and healthy options. We are strengthening our community 
through equitable food access. We are currently working to shore up our pantry network to ensure 
sustainability and expand capacity. One particular project includes providing wide support and 
attention, including identifying funding opportunities, grant-writing, infrastructure needs, staff training 
and succession planning assistance, to a Second Harvest member agency in Carbon County, as they 
purchase a new site and complete extensive renovations, to create a pantry, congregate meal site, and 
community center with wrap-around services all under one roof. This project will provide much needed 
resources to the residents of Lansford Borough and the surrounding area.  
As part of this deeper work in the community, to identify additional areas that need more focused 
attention, we are eagerly collaborating with the Commission on Economic Opportunity’s (CEO) 
Weinberg Northeast Regional Food Bank and Central Pennsylvania Food Bank. The collective impact 
work will leverage the strengths of each food bank while enabling learning and sharing of information, 
best proactive models, and resources to close the meal gap in this region of the Commonwealth. 
CEO/Weinberg is contracting with The Institute, a data analysis, research, and consulting organization 
with offices in Scranton and Wilkes-Barre, to conduct an environmental scan of the charitable food 
assistance programs across the region.  This full-scale scan will identify food pantries, youth and senior 
feeding programs, congregant meal sites, and will result in a zip code-level mapping of the entire 
charitable food system.   
This work will provide collaborative data that identifies gaps in service, duplication of services, and needs 
within the food-insecure population.  This assessment and planning will include focus groups with people 
facing food insecurity, visits with school officials and pantries, understanding of state and federal feeding 
requirements, engagement with the broader community and identification of potential partners. As a 
result, recommendations will be made, and a strategy developed to implement best practices to close the 
meal gap.   
The food resource and infrastructure investments made to our pantry network will ultimately increase 
access to fresh and nutritious foods for the residents of Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne counties.  
 
Describe the need, issue, or opportunity that your proposal addresses. 
Access to nutritious food for under-resourced populations is an immediate need. 
According to Feeding America’s Map the Meal Gap 2021, the food insecurity rate for Carbon, Monroe, 
Pike, and Wayne counties is 9.6% (31,860 people). The rate is higher for children, at 13.4%. Populations 
facing food insecurity are strongly correlated with those that have been impacted by systemic racism and 
economic exploitation. The food insecurity rate for Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne counties is higher 
for Black (12%) and Hispanic (17%) individuals, as compared to their white counterparts (8%).

5 
 
Per U.S. Census data, there are 31,254 people living below the poverty level in Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and 
Wayne counties, representing 11% of the total population of the area.  
The current economic climate continues to impact our neighbors. Monthly reports submitted by our 
partner agencies show that the number of people receiving food in Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne 
counties increased by 32% from this same time last year.  
The Food Research and Action Center’s (FRAC) report titled, Hunger & Health: Impact of Poverty, Food 
Insecurity, and Poor Nutrition, cites that childhood poverty and socioeconomic inequalities have health 
implications that carry through into adulthood. For example, lower childhood socioeconomic status is 
associated with chronic disease, poor mental health, and unfavorable health behaviors in adulthood. 
Poverty in childhood has been linked to serious, long-term economic consequences, including higher 
health care expenditures, lower educational achievement, and increased risk of poverty later in life. 
 
Poverty, food insecurity, and poor nutrition have serious consequences for the health and well-being of 
children and adults, including a greater risk for chronic disease and poor mental health. Beyond the 
consequences for individuals and families, these consequences also have costly implications for the 
economy and healthcare system.  
 
To broaden access and opportunity, it is essential that Second Harvest continues to provide nutritious 
foods to the people facing food insecurity.  
 
 
Describe the consumers who will be served. Estimate number served by your proposal, age range, 
gender, income status, etc. 
This project will serve under-resourced residents of Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne counties with 
incomes equal to or less than 185% of the federal poverty guidelines. The poverty rate for this area is 
11%, and 17,355 households are receiving SNAP benefits.  
People who visit food pantries and participate in Second Harvest’s programs are diverse in terms of age, 
race, and ethnicity. Within Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne counties, on average, Second Harvest 
currently serves 25,260 individuals per month, including 7,750 children and 6,200 seniors, through our 
network of 54 member agencies within the four-county region. This represents a 32% increase in 
individuals seeking food assistance in just one year.

Describe all collaborative relationships (financial and programmatic) with other organizations for this

collaborate with 200+ nonprofit agencies, including faith-based organizations, schools, community 
programs, health organizations, and residential facilities. To procure food, we partner with myriad 
manufacturers, wholesalers, retail outlets, and local farms.

distribution programs.
