Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast Pennsylvania  	1 
 
 
Fresh Food Distribution Program Description 
2024 ShopRite LPGA Classic Grant Opportunity 
 
Access to nutritious food for under-resourced populations is an immediate need. Nationally, the 
emergency food network is serving historic volumes of people. Current inflation, coupled with the close 
of federally-funded COVID-related supplemental programs, is causing many families to visit food pantries 
for the first time. Often, the only fresh, healthy food a family facing food insecurity has access to comes 
from the emergency food network.   
 
The mission of the Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast Pennsylvania is “to 
obtain food and distribute it to people in need through area nonprofits, and to provide resources for 
education and advocacy to end hunger.” We distribute nutritious food, break barriers to create access, 
provide nutrition education, advocate to end food insecurity, and work to attain food justice. Second 
Harvest respects and preserves the dignity of every person served.  
 
Last year, Second Harvest distributed 14.7 million pounds of food to our network of 200+ nonprofit 
agencies (food pantries, schools, meal centers, shelters, childcare agencies, senior centers, veterans 
organizations), a 44% increase from the previous year. Included in this total is 6.7 million pounds of fresh 
produce, perishable protein, and fresh dairy products (46% of total distribution). Annually, we rescue 
more than 8 million pounds of food from ending up in a landfill.  
 
Currently, the Second Harvest network serves more than 90,000 people each month. Second Harvest will 
continue to provide the fresh, nutritious, and culturally-relevant foods families need to thrive.  
 
The Need(s) To Be Addressed 
People are utilizing food pantries in historic volumes. According to Feeding America’s Map the Meal Gap 
2022, the food insecurity rate for Second Harvest’s service area is 11.7% (120,400 people). The rate is 
higher for children, at 16.4% (34,420 children). Populations facing food insecurity are strongly correlated 
with those that have been impacted by systemic racism and economic exploitation. The food insecurity 
rate across the Second Harvest service area is higher for Black (21%) and Hispanic (24%) individuals, as 
compared to their white counterparts (9%). 
A pre-pandemic study found that following the USDA’s MyPlate Dietary Guidelines would cost a family of 
four between $1,000-$1,200 a month ($12,000-$14,400 annually) depending on the age of the family 
members. For a comparison, the average middle-income family in the United States spends roughly

Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast Pennsylvania  	2 
$6,224 on food each year with the average lower-income family spending even less at roughly $3,862 per 
year. Current inflation is having a devastating impact. Inflation continues to have a devastating impact. 
According to USDA’s Economic Research Service, U.S. food prices rose by 25% from 2019 to 2023. Eating 
healthy food consistently is simply out of reach for too many families. More people need access to 
affordable fresh, nutritious foods. 
The Second Harvest network of member agencies continues to serve historic volumes of people – 
currently 90,000+ each month. Second Harvest plays an integral role in ensuring all people have access to 
the nutritious foods needed to thrive.  
 
Activities/Services and Program Guidelines 
Annually, Second Harvest distributes more than 14 million pounds of food to our network of 200+ 
nonprofit agencies. Last year, 46% of this food was fresh produce, perishable protein, and fresh dairy. 
 
It has always been our focus to provide fresh, nutritious, and culturally-relevant foods. Toward that end, 
in 2021, Second Harvest developed a Food and Nutrition Policy and began implementing Healthy Eating 
Research (HER) Guidelines to inform food sourcing priorities. HER is a project of the Robert Wood Johnson 
Foundation and has been adopted by Feeding America as guiding principle. Since the onset of 
implementation, SHFB has made significant strides in improving the nutritional content of our inventory 
of food, including food that is distributed to our network and food that Second Harvest provides directly 
through community programs.   
In addition to food distribution to our network, Second Harvest operates several programs aimed at 
improving the health of our community and working toward a more just and equitable local food system. 
We administer the Pennsylvania Senior Food Box Program (PASB). PASB works to improve the health of 
low-income seniors by supplementing their diets with shelf-stable nutritious foods. We work to 
supplement the boxes with healthy, fresh items when possible. Currently, Second Harvest provides senior 
food boxes to 2,350 seniors across 49 sites each month. 
 
Our MilitaryShare program works “to serve those who served us” by providing access to nutritious food, 
with a goal of improving health outcomes and easing financial strain among low-income active duty, 
reserve, and retired military members and their families. Our MilitaryShare program provides 500+ 
military families with a share of fresh and shelf-stable food each month.  
 
Our SNAP (Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program) outreach coordinators work with local county 
offices of Health and Human Services to offer SNAP application assistance. Our SNAP coordinators offer 
dedicated personal application support to ensure that all eligible participants receive needed benefits. 
 
We offer dynamic, community-based nutrition education. Our Cooking Matters® program, provides free 
cooking classes and demonstrations for parents, kids and childcare providers where participants learn 
about cooking, meal prep, grocery shopping, food budgeting, and nutrition. Through our Healthy Pantry 
Initiative (HPI), a program of Feeding Pennsylvania, our HPI Coordinator works with pantry coordinators 
and volunteers to encourage consumption of nutritious and fresh foods, and also provides direct nutrition 
education to pantry clients.

Population To Be Served 
Our target population is low-income individuals and families in need of food who reside within Second 
Harvest’s service area: Carbon, Lehigh, Monroe, Northampton, Pike, Wayne counties. 
 
 
Project/Program Timeline  
Second Harvest operates on a July 1-June 30 fiscal year. We distribute fresh foods to our network of 
member agencies year-round.  
 
 
Evaluation Criteria 
Evaluation is a crucial part of our programming - measurable outcomes make it possible to gauge what is 
working and what needs to be modified to successfully serve our community. 
 
Evaluation of all Community Action programs, including Second Harvest, begins with recordkeeping and 
data collection. Annually, Second Harvest develops a work plan with measurable performance targets that 
guide the activities of our programs. Targets are monitored and evaluated by the Community Action Board 
of Directors. Progress is monitored at the six-month mark, and all performance targets are fully evaluated 
at the end of the program year.  
 
Additionally, Second Harvest solicits feedback from its partner agencies and food pantry participants, in 
the form of formal surveys, conversational feedback, and committee work. The results and feedback guide 
Second Harvest’s strategic planning, program development, and service provision. We are working toward 
a more participant-centric model of operation. We are taking the necessary, often tough, steps to shift 
power to the people we serve.   
 
Qualifications of Staff Involved  
This grant will be administered by Second Harvest’s Agency Relations Team, Leslie Avecillas and Brandon 
Campos. The Agency Relations Coordinators serve as liaisons between Second Harvest and our 200+ 
nonprofit member agencies. Leslie Avecillas recently joined the Second Harvest team. She has a bachelor’s 
degree in public health and psychology and has experience in the areas of customer service, food access, 
and public health. Brandon Campos moved to the Agency Relations Team after working as Second 
Harvest’s Food Sourcing Coordinator, where he increased the amount of food collected through our Retail 
Pickup program by 19% within one year.

Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast Pennsylvania  	4 
Nicole Folino, Community Services Manager, oversees the Agency Relations Team. Prior to her current 
role, Nicole served as an Agency Relations Coordinator, where she played a key part in increasing Second 
Harvest’s distribution of fresh foods. She has many years of experience managing volunteers and a strong 
background in small business development. 
 
Administration of the grant will be overseen by Second Harvest’s Associate Executive Director, Allison 
Czapp, who joined the Second Harvest team in March 2022. She brings to the food bank extensive 
knowledge of local food and farming systems and has administered several large federal grants aimed at 
reducing food insecurity by increasing consumption of locally grown fresh fruits and vegetables.
