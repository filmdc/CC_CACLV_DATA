ARTICLE III.  Program Overview 
 
A. Choice Neighborhoods is designed to address struggling neighborhoods with distressed 
public housing or HUD-assisted housing through a comprehensive approach to neighborhood 
transformation.  Local leaders, residents, and other stakeholders, such as public housing 
authorities, cities, schools, police, business owners, nonprofits, and private developers, come 
together to create a plan that transforms distressed HUD housing and addresses the 
challenges in the surrounding neighborhood.  The program helps communities transform 
neighborhoods by revitalizing severely distressed public and/or assisted housing and 
investing and leveraging investments in well-functioning services, high quality public 
schools and education programs, high quality early learning programs and services, crime 
prevention strategies, public assets, public transportation, and improved access to jobs.  
Choice Neighborhoods is focused on three core goals: 
 
1. Housing:  Replace severely distressed public and assisted housing with high-quality 
mixed-income housing that is well-managed and responsive to the needs of the 
surrounding neighborhood; 
2. People:  Improve outcomes of households living in the target housing related to 
employment and income, health, and children’s education; and 
3. Neighborhood:  Create the conditions necessary for public and private reinvestment  
in distressed neighborhoods to offer the kinds of amenities and assets, including 
safety, good schools, and commercial activity, that are important to families’ choices 
about their community. 
 
 
ARTICLE IV.  Choice Neighborhoods Transformation Plan and Requirements

a Transformation Plan.  The Transformation Plan should identify specific, integrated, and 
effective strategies to implement public and/or assisted housing revitalization, the 
coordination and design of supportive services, including educational opportunities for 
children, and neighborhood-level planning to improve a range of neighborhood assets.  The 
Transformation Plan should be created as part of a collaborative planning process that 
involves neighborhood stakeholders and local governmental entities. 
 
The Transformation Plan should translate the three core goals of Choice Neighborhoods – 
Housing, People and Neighborhood – into a strategy that will direct investments, demonstrate 
the commitment among a range of public and private partners to address interdependent 
neighborhood challenges, utilize data to set and monitor progress toward implementation 
goals, and engage community stakeholders and residents in meaningful decision-making

A. Prohibition.  The Grantee shall comply with the conflict of interest requirements in 2 CFR

FY 2023 Choice Neighborhoods Planning Grant Agreement – page 14 
interest in any contract, subcontract, or agreement with respect thereto, or the proceeds

B. HUD-Approved Exception.   
 
1. Standard.  HUD may grant an exception to the exclusion in paragraph (A) of this Article 
on a case-by-case basis when it determines that such an exception will serve to further the 
purposes of Choice Neighborhoods and its effective and efficient administration.  
 
2. Procedure.  HUD will consider granting an exception only after the Grantee has provided 
a disclosure of the nature of the conflict, accompanied by:  
a. an assurance that there has been public disclosure of the conflict; 
b. a description of how the public disclosure was made; and  
c. an opinion of the Grantee’s attorney that the interest for which the exception is sought 
does not violate State or local laws.  
 
3. Consideration of Relevant Factors.  In determining whether to grant a requested 
exception under paragraph (B) of this Article, HUD will consider the cumulative effect of 
the following factors, where applicable: 
a. whether the exception would provide a significant cost benefit or an essential degree 
of expertise to the Transformation Plan that would otherwise not be available; 
b. whether an opportunity was provided for open competitive bidding or negotiation; 
c. whether the person affected is a member of a group or class intended to be the 
beneficiaries of the Transformation Plan and the exception will permit such person to 
receive generally the same interests or benefits as are being made available or 
provided to the group or class; 
d. whether the affected person has withdrawn from his or her functions or 
responsibilities, or the decision-making process, with respect to the specific activity 
in question; 
e. whether the interest or benefit was present before the affected person was in a 
position as described in paragraph (A) of this Article; 
f. whether undue hardship will result either to the Grantee or the person affected when 
weighed against the public interest served by avoiding the prohibited conflict; and 
g. any other relevant considerations.

Pembroke Choice Neighborhood
Community Leadership Development Program
Goal
Prepare neighborhood residents to serve in leadership roles throughout the Choice Neighborhoods planning process through a comprehensive set of training workshops that build capacity to engage in all facets of community planning and community development work. 
Details
Sessions will be held at a location within the Pembroke neighborhood (Northeast Community Center, Boys and Girls Club, or Marvine Elementary School depending on scheduling availability). 
Each session will last approximately 2 hours and will be scheduled based on the overall availability of participants. Evenings and weekends are a possibility. 
Participants
Participation will be required for all community ambassadors in the Pembroke Choice project. Trainings will also be open to other community residents and stakeholders, including but not limited to representatives of partner organizations and BHA and City staff. 
Timeline
Target date ranges for training sessions are as follows, depending on the availability of participants and hiring of community ambassadors:
Session 1 – 3/18 – 3/24
Session 2 – 4/8 – 4/14
Session 3 – 4/29 – 5/5
Session 4 – 5/13 – 5/19
Session 5 – 6/3 – 6/9
Session 6 – 6/17 – 6/23
Curriculum
Session 1: Intro to Choice Neighborhoods and Community Leadership Program
Understand the core components of the Choice Neighborhoods planning process and role of community members in this process.
Get to know other community members and begin to develop a collective identity as a team of Pembroke community leaders.
Practice initial engagement approaches for the Choice Neighborhoods project. 
Understand the components of the community leadership development program and requirements for completion.
Session 2: Community Planning and Facilitation
Continue developing a collective identity as a team of Pembroke community leaders.
Develop a working understanding of planning terminology.
Learn about different approaches to gathering community input. 
Practice small group facilitation.
Session 3: Building Power and Creating Change
Continue developing a collective identity as a team of Pembroke community leaders.
Learn different approaches to social change (community organizing, community development). 
Learn methods for organizing residents to make change.
Envision a permanent leadership structure for the Pembroke neighborhood. 
Session 4: Effective communication and conflict management
Continue developing a collective identity as a team of Pembroke community leaders.
Identify personal communication style and those of other team members.
Practice techniques for managing conflict in individual and group settings.
Session 5: Data collection 
Continue developing a collective identity as a team of Pembroke community leaders.
Learn about the importance of primary and secondary data in community planning and decision-making.
Introduce and practice responsible data collection techniques.
Understand how data is aggregated and presented following collection.
Session 6: Community Leadership
Continue developing a collective identity as a team of Pembroke community leaders. 
Identify personal leadership style and understand other approaches to leadership.
Understand the role of a neighborhood leader in a community process.
Address any remaining questions or concerns, and outline future opportunities for program participants.
Additional sessions may be offered based on the interest of participants. CADB plans to offer ongoing workshops on relevant topics throughout 2024-2025 that will be open to Choice stakeholders as well as community members in other areas of the city.

INTRODUCTION
Our approach is focused on making a 
difference and creating a lasting impact 
through authentic collaboration, early 
action and community empowerment. 
LISTENING, LEARNING AND REPRESENTING
Our first step in any planning project is to listen and 
learn from local residents and stakeholders about the 
history, culture and character of the neighborhood before 
analyzing data, making our own observations or crafting 
recommendations. Often this is done through a combination 
of resident-led walking tours along with key person interviews 
that are designed as informal chats rather than question and 
answer sessions. 
When possible, we incorporate art-making into this stage 
in order to encourage residents to tell stories through 
their own creativity. This element can help to generate the 
“graphic soul” and logo that will be used throughout the 
planning process. Through listening, we can have a greater 
understanding of the values that neighbors share, the 
important events that bring people together, and the places 
that are utilized as true local assets. 
 
EMBRACING DIVERSITY
We recognize the importance of inclusion and diversity in all 
that we do. It is highly important for our team and partners 
to reflect the unique viewpoints and background of the 
communities in which we serve. COLLABO maintains a strong 
commitment to diversity and actively seeks out partnerships 
with individuals and firms that can help to further this goal. 
When possible, we will hire local Community Ambassadors to 
help lead various elements of the project. 
COLLABO continuously strives to exceed all MBE/WBE/
DBE goals in its contracting process for public agencies 
by partnering or subcontracting with minority and women 
owned firms. We are sensitive to the cultural and historical 
elements of this effort, and hope to foster a seamless and 
inclusive collaboration with local leaders and those currently 
working in the neighborhood to develop strategies and 
solutions that are driven through community ownership. In 
addition, if selected, we are more than willing to discuss 
potential partnerships with local firms or agencies that 
can further enrich both the diversity and outcomes of the 
planning effort.

BUILDING TRUST AND CAPACITY
When residents are genuinely at the center of a planning 
process, a unique outcome unfolds. Relationships become 
the focal point of the work. This is because for residents, 
planning is personal – it’s about their children, their families, 
their homes, their neighbors and their streets. It is about 
making their day-to-day lives better in real time. 
As we envision, design, and seek to implement the 
investment and physical changes necessary to revitalize a 
community, it is critical that residents are engaged from day 
one, so that they can help to assume a leadership role in the 
process. An indicator of the level of genuine engagement 
achieved is the establishment of trust. Relationships are 
the currency of collective impact, and the ability to achieve 
lasting results. A denser network of relationships produces 
a wider array of possibilities, a more robust set of potential 
opportunities, and a larger implementation ripple effect. 
 
FUN, ENTERTAINING AND UNIQUE 
ENGAGEMENT
No one wants to spend their evenings listening to consultants 
lecture. We structure our community meetings as hands-on 
events so that engagement doesn’t become a chore. This is 
done through a variety of methods including utilizing “instant 
feedback” survey tools, game-show style activities, crafting 
plans and models in real time in small teams, and creative 
storyboarding. 
Whenever possible, we connect our planning activities to 
existing neighborhood events in order to connect with 
stakeholders who may not typically attend a formal meeting. 
This can be a block party, farmers market, holiday event, job 
fair or church gathering... The important thing is to go where 
people go... and find out what they know. 
Making engagement fun encourages long-term buy-in of the 
Plan, helps to involve all members of the community, young 
and old, and facilitates the building of genuine connections 
and relationships within the neighborhood.

CREATING A GUIDE FOR COMMUNITY ACTION 
AND EMPOWERMENT
Often times, a typical plan can be a static document, a 
snapshot in time and a purely technical report. Our plans 
are organized and designed to act as guides for community 
action and empowerment. They are living documents, with 
compelling visuals, trackable metrics and clearly defined 
priority projects. 
Through the local capacity building that is incorporated in the 
process, community members will become empowered to act 
as the “champions” of the Plan, ensuring that elected officials 
put recommendations to the forefront in their decision making 
processes. The Plan becomes a tool for action and a roadmap 
for success.  
 
BUILDING FROM LOCAL ASSETS AND VALUES
In many neighborhoods, it is easy to identify challenges, 
whether they are physical conditions, lack of opportunities or 
institutional racism and mistrust. People are generally open 
and direct in discussing what affects community residents. 
If we only focus on these challenges, the dialog can shift 
towards a sense of hopelessness in what must be overcome.  
No matter the current conditions, every community has 
unique assets and values that should form the building 
blocks of the vision, strategies and projects in the Plan. By 
utilizing an assets-based approach, residents can begin to 
gain new perspectives on the community, which can help to 
take the sole focus off of challenges and towards elevating 
what already makes the neighborhood great. These assets 
often include history, culture, natural features, buildings, 
organizations and most importantly people! Leveraging 
and connecting these assets into a bold vision can directly 
contribute to revitalization efforts.

CONNECTING THE DOTS
Once visioning is complete, COLLABO will utilize a focused 
engagement approach through the incorporation of Working 
Groups to help to define and prioritize projects. These groups 
will be made up of key partners who will be asked to help 
direct implementation efforts. 
Often times through these discussions, partners will reveal 
similar goals, objectives and planned initiatives. The 
planning process can serve as “connective tissue” between 
stakeholders to begin to stitch together ideas and actions 
through collaboration. By connecting the dots, the Plan 
can begin to leverage the power of multiple agencies and 
institutions and direct this energy towards achieving the 
community’s goals. 
IDENTIFYING AND ACQUIRING RESOURCES 
FOR SUCCESS
Once the Plan is in motion, COLLABO will work to detail out 
specific projects, including estimated timing, general cost 
estimates, action steps and implementation leaders. In order 
to ensure success of key priority projects, we will then work 
to identify and potentially acquire resources and funding. 
Depending on the project, this could include leveraging 
local dollars for additional benefit, developing applications 
for federal or state grants, or helping to guide partnership 
resources.

BRIDGING BEYOND THE PLAN
We are both planners and do’ers. Early action helps to create 
momentum and larger projects and initiatives keep the 
community moving forward. Whenever possible, we seek to 
bridge beyond the Plan to directly assist with implementation 
efforts. 
Our Choice Neighborhoods planning efforts have been 
awarded significant action and implementation funding 
through HUD. Other recently completed neighborhood plans 
in Easton, PA and Kansas City, KS have formed the foundation 
for successful state and national grant applications leading to 
approximately $2 million for community based projects and 
programs. Along with local partnerships, our grant writing 
experience can directly help to move the needle on critical 
neighborhood improvements.

Planning coordinator scope
INTRODUCTION
COLLABO will undertake the following activities to support 
the City of Bethlehem (Client) in the development of a 
Choice Neighborhoods (CN) Transformation Plan. 
COLLABO will work with the Client to ensure that activities 
are completed in accordance with the Planning Schedule 
approved by HUD. 
PHASE 1 - LISTENING AND LEARNING
COLLABO will listen and learn from local residents and 
stakeholders about the history, culture and character of 
the neighborhood before analyzing data, making our own 
observations or crafting recommendations. COLLABO will 
provide advice, counsel and technical assistance to the Client 
regarding the approach, structure and design of the CN 
project management plan, reporting and communication 
protocols consistent with CN principles and HUD 
requirements. COLLABO will provide and confirm a common 
understanding of the conditions and issues affecting the 
neighborhood through (i) primary/secondary data research, 
(ii) field data collection, and (iii) resident and stakeholder 
meetings. Activities and deliverables under this Phase will 
include, but not be limited to, the following Tasks:
TASK 1.1 - PROJECT COORDINATION
COLLABO will conduct ongoing project coordination with the 
Client and HUD project teams to ensure timely completion 
of the planning process and required deliverables. COLLABO 
will also assist the Client in preparation of the narrative 
sections of required quarterly reports to HUD.
TASK 1.2 – COMMUNITY TOUR
COLLABO will conduct a community tour, including initial 
kickoff meetings with Client, key stakeholders and residents. 
TASK 1.3 – STEERING COMMITTEE FORMATION
COLLABO will work with Client to identify partners, key 
stakeholders and residents to be part of the project Steering 
Committee. 
TASK 1.4 – PROJECT BRANDING
COLLABO will work with Client to create a project brand, 
including logo, colors and fonts that will be utilized to create a 
cohesive design style for the project.
TASK 1.5 – PROJECT WEBSITE
COLLABO will work with Client to create, maintain and update 
a project website to document planning events, activities 
and products, and facilitate communication among all 
stakeholders. 
TASK 1.6 - SOCIAL MEDIA UPDATES
COLLABO will work with Client to maintain and update 
social media pages in order to share information among all 
stakeholders. 
TASK 1.7 – PHYSICAL MODEL
COLLABO will work with Client to create an interactive 
physical model of the Choice Neighborhood planning area 
that can be utilized as an engagement tool throughout the 
process.

TASK 1.8 - INFORMATIONAL HANDOUTS AND 
NEIGHBORHOOD SNAPSHOT
COLLABO will develop a “Frequently Asked Questions” 
handout for use during the planning process. COLLABO will 
also produce a Neighborhood Snapshot document which will 
include a summary of existing conditions and demographic 
information about the community. 
TASK 1.9 CN HEADQUARTERS 
COLLABO will assist in developing a CN Headquarters space 
in order to provide a flexible resource and visual presence 
for the process. The CN Headquarters will allow COLLABO 
to hold smaller in-person meetings throughout the Plan, and 
serve as a location for materials and documents.
TASK 1.10 COMMUNITY AMBASSADORS 
COLLABO will work with client to identify 3 Community 
Ambassadors who will help to support the planning process 
through targeted engagement and project leadership. 
Community Ambassadors identified through this process will 
become part of the COLLABO, assisting with engagement, 
Early Action Projects, and decision making while building their 
leadership skills and making important connections within the 
neighborhood. COLLABO will hold bi-weekly check in calls 
with Ambassadors and Client.
TASK 1.11 – STEERING COMMITTEE MEETING 1
COLLABO will conduct Steering Committee Meeting 1 to 
set goals and desired outcomes for the Plan. Meetings will 
be held in person, or virtually, inline with current COVID-19 
protocols.
TASK 1.12 – HUD SITE VISIT
COLLABO will lead the HUD Site Visit, either in person, or 
virtually, along with members of the HUD Team, Client and 
key partners. 
TASK 1.14 – QUARTERLY REPORT 1
COLLABO will assist Client in creating the Quarterly Report, 
for submission to HUD.
 

PHASE 2 - VISIONING
COLLABO will organize, prepare and facilitate Community 
Meetings 1 and 2, which will identify an inclusive, 
neighborhood-wide working vision for the future that will 
guide the planning process, and the identification of potential 
strategic initiatives to support attainment of that vision. 
Youth engagement workshops will be convened as needed 
to ensure that the voices of younger residents are fully 
incorporated into the development of the vision. Visioning 
will have a strong focus on potential Early Action ideas as 
well as other neighborhood improvements. Activities and 
deliverables under this Phase will include, but not be limited 
to, the following tasks:
TASK 2.1 – COMMUNITY MEETING 1
COLLABO will conduct Community Meeting 1, which will 
focus on the identification of assets, challenges and early 
action project ideas. Meeting will be held in person, or 
virtually, inline with current COVID-19 protocols.
TASK 2.2 – STEERING COMMITTEE MEETING 2
COLLABO will conduct Steering Committee Meeting 2 to 
review community input and data related to assets, challenges 
and early action project ideas. Meeting will be held in person, 
or virtually, inline with current COVID-19 protocols.
TASK 2.3 – CN FIELD TRIP
COLLABO will assist in organizing a field trip to a 
CN community that has been successful in achieving 
implementation efforts. The field trip should include members 
of the Client team, local decision makers, and Community 
Ambassadors, and will serve as an opportunity for learning 
and capacity building. 
TASK 2.4 - MARKET ASSESSMENT
COLLABO will work with Client to help secure the target 
neighborhood Market Assessment, which is a required HUD 
deliverable. The Market Assessment will build directly off of

ARTICLE III.  Program Overview 
A.Choice Neighborhoods is designed to address struggling neighborhoods with distressed 
public housing or HUD-assisted housing through a comprehensive approach to neighborhood 
transformation.  Local leaders, residents, and other stakeholders, such as public housing 
authorities, cities, schools, police, business owners, nonprofits, and private developers, come 
together to create a plan that transforms distressed HUD housing and addresses the 
challenges in the surrounding neighborhood.  The program helps communities transform 
neighborhoods by revitalizing severely distressed public and/or assisted housing and 
investing and leveraging investments in well-functioning services, high quality public 
schools and education programs, high quality early learning programs and services, crime 
prevention strategies, public assets, public transportation, and improved access to jobs.  
Choice Neighborhoods is focused on three core goals: 
1.Housing:  Replace severely distressed public and assisted housing with high-quality 
mixed-income housing that is well-managed and responsive to the needs of the 
surrounding neighborhood; 
2.People:  Improve outcomes of households living in the target housing related to 
employment and income, health, and children’s education; and 
3.Neighborhood:  Create the conditions necessary for public and private reinvestment  
in distressed neighborhoods to offer the kinds of amenities and assets, including 
safety, good schools, and commercial activity, that are important to families’ choices 
about their community. 
ARTICLE IV.  Choice Neighborhoods Transformation Plan and Requirements 
A.General.  The end product of this grant award is the submission to and acceptance by HUD of 
a Transformation Plan.  The Transformation Plan should identify specific, integrated, and 
effective strategies to implement public and/or assisted housing revitalization, the 
coordination and design of supportive services, including educational opportunities for 
children, and neighborhood-level planning to improve a range of neighborhood assets.  The 
Transformation Plan should be created as part of a collaborative planning process that 
involves neighborhood stakeholders and local governmental entities. 
The Transformation Plan should translate the three core goals of Choice Neighborhoods – 
Housing, People and Neighborhood – into a strategy that will direct investments, demonstrate 
the commitment among a range of public and private partners to address interdependent 
neighborhood challenges, utilize data to set and monitor progress toward implementation 
goals, and engage community stakeholders and residents in meaningful decision-making
