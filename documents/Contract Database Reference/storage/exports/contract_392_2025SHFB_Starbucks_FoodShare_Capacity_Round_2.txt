FY25 Starbucks FoodShare Capacity Building Grant 
 
Grant Funding and Reporting Requirements 
 
The funding and reporting requirements for the Starbucks FoodShare Capacity Building Grant are 
outlined below. We ask once you have reviewed and agreed to these details, please scroll to the 
button and select “Agree” or “Do Not Agree” and submit. 
 
If you agree, you will be prompted to provide details about your food bank on the next page. 
 
Purpose of Funding 
The purpose of the Starbucks FoodShare Capacity Building Grant is to support food banks and agency 
partners in receiving and distributing FoodShare product donations. Partners of FoodShare are eligible 
to receive various funding levels; the intention is to support a robust, localized program that 
incorporates stewardship of all key stakeholders of the retail store and distribution center teams. It is 
expected that funds are first used to improve agency infrastructure and operations directly associated 
with FoodShare product distribution. (see detailed “Use of Funds” section). In addition, in FY25 funds 
are available for supplemental food procurement expenses once approved by Starbucks, as outlined in 
Whole Plate Grant and detailed “Use of Funds” section.  
 
*NEW* Whole Plate Grant 
For fiscal year 2025, we are adding a new spending component to our FoodShare grants called “Whole 
Plate.” This additional grant unlocks a new eligible expense to provide support for food access. As part 
of the FoodShare Capacity Building Grant, the “Whole Plate” grant funds can be used to purchase 
additional food. The spirit of “Whole Plate” is to supplement and broaden diversity of product provided 
by Starbucks. 
Grant Period 
The grant period will run from October 1, 2024 – June 30, 2025 (9 months). Grantees and award 
amounts are determined by Starbucks FoodShare team. If you are unable to spend the full grant amount 
prior to the close of this grant period, you may request an extension to the grant terms by contacting 
Starbucks via FoodShareUS@Starbucks.com, using the subject line: “FY25 Capacity Building Grant 
Extension Request: <Insert Food Bank Name>” prior to June 20, 2025.  
 
Use of Funds 
Funds shall be used to support direct FoodShare-related expenses and core processes to the program, 
including purchasing assets, building infrastructure, and paying for staff time needed for FoodShare-
related food distribution. In instances where your food bank’s agency partner is directly receiving 
FoodShare deliveries, it is expected grant funds be used towards their eligible expenses or re-granted 
from your food bank to the agency partner to help cover direct or indirect costs arising from operating

FoodShare. Regardless of funds being spent by your organization or re-granted, your organization is 
responsible for consolidating and communicating all impact reporting laid out in the Reporting 
Requirements section. 
 
Eligible expenses (primary) may include, but are not limited to the following: 
• Building infrastructure such as walk-in or reach-in coolers, freezers, or refurbishing a space for 
enhanced distribution 
• Purchasing of receiving/distribution equipment and assets such as pallet jacks (electric or 
manual), scales, forklifts, push carts, shelving, associated sanitizing equipment, logistics 
equipment, etc.  
• Purchasing of Tote cleaning supplies (i.e. power washers) 
• Staff time related to FoodShare operation 
• Establishment of sustainable food waste practices associated with downstream impact of 
increased product donation (i.e. composting) 
• Fuel stipends 
• FoodShare communication amplification and 3PL and store-level stewardship 
 
Whole Plate (secondary) grant component: 
In addition to capacity building expenses, food banks can use up to 50% of funds towards produce 
purchasing efforts. These supplemental expenses may include the following: 
• Procurement of fresh produce and agricultural product 
• Direct or indirect related expenses to produce procurement such as transportation costs, freight 
subsidy, paying for farmer labor fees, pick & pack out fees, bulk produce & materials. 
 
Ineligible expenses include the following: 
• Expenses unrelated to the FoodShare Program 
• Non-refrigerated and open vehicles 
• General operating or unrelated staff time 
• Non-produce procurement 
• Capital Campaigns 
• Lobbying and advocacy efforts 
 
Reporting Requirements 
Depending on grant amounts, there will be up to three (3) reports required for this grant opportunity.  
 
For Food Banks that receive $10,000 or more: 
• Midterm Report due February 16, 2025, you will be provided with a brief template on learnings 
and challenges, along with opportunities to pivot funds, if needed 
• Budget Report due June 30, 2025, you will be provided with a budget template to report on 
spend. Please note, grant funds are expected to be fully utilized by this date. If you need an 
extension, Starbucks will consider up to 1 year extension. You may request an extension by 
contacting Starbucks via FoodShareUS@Starbucks.com, using the subject line: “FY25 Capacity 
Building Grant Extension Request: <Insert Food Bank Name>”.  
• Final Report due July 30, 2025, covering the 9-month grant period of 10/1/24 – 6/30/25. 
 
For Food Banks that receive under $10,000: 
• Final Report due July 30, 2025, covering the 9-month grant period of 10/1/24 – 6/30/25.

Additionally, there will be four (4) quarterly surveys to assess operational execution and two (2) 
questionnaires to gather holiday closure schedules and contact information.  
• The quarterly surveys will be dynamic surveys that ask the Program Lead to rate various 
components of the program and 3PL management. Survey results will be summarized for 
insights and improvement. 
• The semi-annual questionnaires will enable program execution and mitigation planning, as well 
as ensure communication channels are accurate.

Communication Requirements 
Grant amount is $10,000 or more 
If your food bank is awarded a grant of $10,000 or more, you are required to recognize Starbucks at 
least one (1) time during the grant period using social media and online platforms to help promote the 
partnership (i.e., Instagram, Twitter & Facebook posts, website promotion, electronic communication 
with donors, volunteers, other constituents). Please reference the FoodShare Communications Toolkit 
for guidance, this will be available on your food bank’s Versaic homepage.  
 
All grantees who receive $10,000 or more will be asked to report on donor recognition efforts made 
throughout the grant period, demonstrating the impact of both food donations and funding support.

Grant amount is less than $10,000   
If your food bank is awarded a grant less than $10,000, you are strongly encouraged to recognize 
Starbucks for this grant award during the grant period using social media and online platforms to help 
promote the partnership.  
 
Starbucks Store and 3PL Provider Engagement 
Engaging with FoodShare at the local level is essential to program success. Your food bank engagement 
is core to fostering a strong relationship with your local Starbucks stores and 3PL providers in support of 
the FoodShare program. We will be asking you to report on the # of engagements you’ve had with both 
Starbucks stores and 3PL providers at the end of the grant period.  
 
We’ve included a list of suggestions on ways you can engage with your Starbucks stores and 3PL 
providers that include but are not limited to the following ideas:  
• For all food banks: 
o Connect with Regional Directors or District Managers for your area. They are a conduit 
for stewardship and to improve FoodShare execution. 
o Ask local Starbucks store managers if they can host you in-store to connect directly with 
community members and raise awareness of your role in the community. 
o Offer opportunities for Starbucks partners to volunteer at the food bank or a partner 
agency. 
o If you have an on-site meeting space for external groups, invite the local Starbucks team 
or regional leaders to host their meeting at your food bank and provide a tour of your 
facility.  
o Provide Starbucks stores with a fact sheet about your food bank so they understand the 
impact enabled by your food bank and how they are supporting your mission. Provide 
agency impact stories, stats, or map of distribution points in your community.  
o Invite Starbucks store contacts to attend any food bank celebrations or events.  
o If/when you are seeking board members, consider inviting Starbucks partners to provide 
board-level support. 
• For food banks that receive donations via Starbucks backhaul model: 
o Stay connected with your 3PL team (General Manager, Transportation Manager, 
Operations), they are essential to FoodShare daily delivery. 
o Offer opportunities for Starbucks 3PL provider employees to volunteer at the food bank 
or a partner agency. 
o Provide 3PL provider partners with a fact sheet about your food bank so they 
understand the impact enabled by your food bank and how they are supporting your 
mission. Provide agency impact stories, stats, or map of distribution points in your 
community. 
o Ask to attend a staff meeting at a 3PL distribution center to say thank you, provide 
impact metrics year-to-date, encourage their impact on the community, introduce 
yourself and explore other opportunities to collaborate for community betterment. 
o Invite your 3PL provider contacts to attend any food bank celebrations or events.
