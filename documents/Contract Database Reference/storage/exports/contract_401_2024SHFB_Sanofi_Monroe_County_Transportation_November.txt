Mission and History of Organization  
The Community Action Committee of the Lehigh Valley (CALV) was established in 1965 as a part of Lyndon 
B. Hohnson’s War on Poverty.  The mission of CALV is “to improve the quality of life in the Lehigh Valley by 
building a community in which all people have access to economic opportunity, the ability to pursue that 
opportunity, and a voice in the decisions that affect their lives.” Today, CALV operates 13 programs and 3 
subsidiary corporations. As a Community Action Agency (CAA), CALV is committed to ensuring that the 
community plays an active role in identifying needs, developing, and implementing solutions to improve 
the quality of life in the region. Toward that end, CAAs have a tripartite Board of Directors comprised of 
at least one-third of its members from the low-income community, one-third from the public sector, and 
one-third from the private sector.  
The mission of Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast Pennsylvania is to obtain 
food and distribute it to people in need through area non-profits and to provide resources for education 
and advocacy to end hunger. We provide nutritious food, offer nutrition education, advocate to end food 
insecurity, dismantle barriers to create access, and work to attain food justice. We respect and preserve 
the dignity of each of our neighbors.  
Second Harvest, a program of Community Action Lehigh Valley, began its mission to end food insecurity 
in 1982, as the Lehigh Valley Food Bank. Initially, distributions were made from the back of a rented truck 
to a network of about 15 nonprofit agencies. Since September 2015, Second Harvest Food Bank has been 
operating from a 65,000 square-foot warehouse in East Allen Township.

Annually, each program of Community Action, including Second Harvest, develops a work plan with

Action Board of Directors. Progress is monitored at the six-month mark, and all performance targets are 
fully evaluated at the end of the program year.
