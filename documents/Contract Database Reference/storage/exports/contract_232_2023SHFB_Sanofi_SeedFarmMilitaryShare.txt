*Project TitleSecond Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast PA 
*Project DescriptionSecond Harvest Food Bank, a member of Feeding America – the
nation’s largest charitable food network, serves Lehigh,
Northampton, Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne counties. Last
year, Second Harvest distributed 14.7 million pounds of food to
our network of 200+ nonprofit agencies, (food pantries, schools,
meal centers, shelters, childcare agencies, addiction treatment
programs), a 44% increase from the previous year. Included in this
total is 6.7 million pounds of fresh produce, perishable protein,
and fresh dairy products (46% of total distribution). Annually, we
rescue more than 3 million pounds of food from ending up in a
landfill.
On average, the Second Harvest network serves more than 87,000
people each month - a 52% increase from last year. The pantries

within the Second Harvest network are operated almost solely
through the work of community volunteers. Our food distribution
is a model of neighbors helping neighbors.
In addition to food distribution to our network, Second Harvest
operates several programs aimed at improving the health of our
community and working toward a more just and equitable local
food system. A contribution from Sanofi will support two of our
programs, MilitaryShare and The Seed Farm.
Our MilitaryShare program provides a monthly supply of shelf
stable and fresh foods to military families in need. The Seed Farm
is a dynamic urban farming program which features an incubator
farm program, farm training opportunities for local farmers and
the public, an urban community gardens initiative, and direct
vegetable production for food pantries.

*Describe the societal need(s)
your program/project addresses
and its target population.
Feeding America estimates that 20% of people served through
the charitable food network are veterans or have a veteran in
their household. Further, according to data from the U.S. Census,
there are over 59,000 veterans living within Second Harvest’s six-
county service territory and 5% are living at or below the poverty
level, meaning that more than 3,100 veterans are struggling to
make ends meet.
Active military members and veterans are one of the most
difficult groups to reach through the emergency food network,
citing pride as the biggest barrier. The USDA, Feeding America,
and regional food banks, have created food distribution
programs that break down these barriers. Our MilitaryShare
program is a part of this nationwide effort.

Our MilitaryShare program provides 500+ military members in
need with a monthly share of fresh and shelf-stable food.
MilitaryShare improves health outcomes and eases financial
strain among low-income active duty, reserve, and retired
military members and their families.
Because there can be no access to nourishing food without
farms, The Seed Farm works to support food production at all
levels, from home gardeners to community gardens to local
farmers, with an emphasis on getting nutritious and locally-
grown food to under-resourced communities. Strong local food
systems equal food security.
In the past year, Second Harvest has seen a 52% increase in the
number of people served. Inflation has a devastating impact and
the need for affordable, nutritious food is great. To bring fresh,
healthy food into under-resourced areas, The Seed Farm
operates its Growing Partners and Farm to Pantry programs. Our
Growing Partners community garden program increases access
to nutritious foods for residents of under-resourced urban
centers and Farm to Pantry provides fresh, local, culturally
relevant foods to neighbors facing food insecurity throughout
our six-county service area.
 
*How will your program have a
positive impact on your target
population
The promotion of health equity is urgently necessary.
Populations facing food insecurity are strongly correlated with
those that have been impacted by systemic racism and economic
exploitation. An equitable society is one in which health equity is
realized and everyone has access to food that is nutritious,
affordable, and culturally connected.
Second Harvest is committed to providing the resource of food to
people who need it, with an emphasis on healthier, fresh options
whenever possible.
Poverty, and therefore food insecurity, are social determinants of
health. People living in low-income households are
disproportionately diagnosed with diet-related illnesses, making
it nearly impossible for people to thrive. When funds are tight,
one of the first things cut from a household budget is nutritious
food (fresh produce, fresh dairy, and meat), which are more
expensive than their highly processed, and less nutritious,
counterparts. Too often, the only fresh, healthy foods that a
family has access to comes from our food pantries. It is
imperative that Second Harvest continues to provide nourishing
food to all, because our community is healthy only when all of

our neighbors are well-nourished and thriving. Our programs,
MilitaryShare and The Seed Farm play a critical role in our food
distribution and health equity efforts.
 
*How do you measure success?Annually, each program of Community Action, including Second
Harvest, develops a work plan with measurable performance
targets that guide the activities of our programs and are designed
to have a positive impact on the community. Performance targets
are monitored and evaluated by the Community Action Board of
Directors. Progress is monitored at the six-month mark, and all
performance targets are fully evaluated at the end of the
program year.
Additionally, Second Harvest solicits feedback from its partner
agencies and food pantry participants, in the form of formal
surveys, conversational feedback, and committee work. The
results and feedback guide Second Harvest’s strategic planning,
program development, and service provision. We are working
toward a more participant-centric model of operation. We are
taking the necessary, often tough, steps to shift power to the
people we serve.
