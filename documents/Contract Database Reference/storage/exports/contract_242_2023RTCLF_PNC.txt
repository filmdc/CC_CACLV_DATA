*Organization Mission
The mission of the Rising Tide Community Loan Fund (RTCLF) is to identify small business and community
development credit needs that may stifle the creation of economic opportunity in low- to moderate-income
communities of the Lehigh Valley and to meet those needs with affordable credit products. 
*Organization History
RTCLF is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit corporation and a subsidiary of the Community Action Lehigh Valley. Formed in
2001 and certified by the U.S. Treasury as a Community Development Financial Institution, RTCLF was
established to provide accessible and flexible financing options for business owners whose risk factors make it
difficult to obtain funds from traditional lenders.
Since 2012, RTCLF has administered and managed the City of Bethlehem’s Small Business Loan Fund, which
provides entrepreneurs with access to up to $100,000 for property or equipment on condition that one full time
equivalent position is created for every $35,000 that is borrowed. In 2019, RTCLF began administering and
managing the Lehigh and Northampton Counties Revolving Loan Fund (LNCRLF). The LNCRLF offers small
businesses in the Lehigh Valley access to mezzanine financing for their start-up and expansion projects.
RTCLF originally served only Lehigh and Northampton Counties, but expanded its territory to also serve Carbon,
Monroe, and upper Bucks counties in 2015 in response to requests for assistance. RTCLF added a line of credit
product to provide entrepreneurs with access to $3,000 to $15,000 in working capital.
In response to the pandemic, RTCLF has delivered $249 million in COVID-19 emergency grant funds as a
member of the PA CDFI network, along with 16 other community development financial institutions, to
businesses across the commonwealth. During the 2022-2023 fiscal year, RTCLF disbursed $1,818,384 in loans,
bringing the total to date to over $11,795,445; approved 25 loans, 15 of which were made to women-owned
businesses, 17 were made to minority-owned businesses, and 23 were to businesses owned by low-to-moderate
income individuals and/or located in low-to-moderate-income census tracts.

*Organization Purpose
RTCLF promotes economic opportunity and development in targeted neighborhoods throughout Northampton,
Lehigh, Carbon, Monroe, and upper Bucks counties. Rising Tide envisions a future with self-sustaining
community neighborhoods thriving throughout the greater Lehigh Valley. Rising Tide aims to achieve this vision
in three distinct ways. Primarily, Rising Tide empowers low-income residents to become self-sustaining by
creating their own jobs. Residents are encouraged to use their own specialized skill set to construct meaningful
employment for themselves. Secondly, a thriving small business developed and maintained by a low-income
community resident has the potential of employing additional community members. This can become critically
important as the community begins to feel that it has a stake in the success of the business. Finally, Rising Tide
attempts to bring businesses to target areas of the cities where economic development is of the utmost
importance. A proliferation of strategically placed small businesses can revitalize a neighborhood and build a
strong sense of community among residents.

*Addressing PNC Foundation's Funding Priorities
Rising Tide devotes its efforts entirely to economic development in Lehigh, Northampton, Carbon, Monroe, and
upper Bucks counties with the goal of enhancing the overall quality of life in the region through neighborhood
revitalization. More specifically, Rising Tide addresses the community development aspect of economic
development by: 1) offering technical and marketing assistance to, and loan programs for, small businesses
located in low- and moderate-income areas; and 2) supporting small businesses that are started by and employ
low- and moderate-income individuals. 
*Problem to be Addressed
Rising Tide serves Lehigh, Northampton, Carbon, and Monroe counties and 21 municipalities in upper Bucks
County. This large, diverse region with a population of over one million people has a median household income
of approximately $73,091. Economic inequality is pervasive throughout the region. According to 2017-2021
American Community Survey estimates, approximately 10.4% of households in the target market are below the
poverty level. There are several census tracts with poverty rates exceeding 50% throughout the target market.
Many low- to moderate-income entrepreneurs lack access to capital. According to the FDIC, 20.7% of
households are underbanked in the Allentown Bethlehem Easton MSA where 93% of our target population lives
(2017). A 2016 study from the Federal Reserve Bank of New York’s Liberty Street Economics blog shows that
people in low-income tracts are 80% more likely to live in a banking desert than their counterparts in higher
income tracts. When branches close, new small-business lending falls by 40% in low-income neighborhoods.
The main reasons for microenterprise loan denial are insufficient credit history and insufficient collateral. The
Federal Reserve Bank's 2022 Small Business Credit Survey found that in the last 12 months, 52% of businesses
obtained loans in response to financial challenges. Of businesses that did not apply for assistance, 44%
responded that they did not think their business would qualify. Of “discouraged” firms that did not apply, 56%
cited weak businesses financials, and 32% cited strict lender requirements as their reason for not applying for
the funding they need.

*Project/Program Description
Rising Tide provides financing to business owners whose risk factors make it difficult to obtain funds from
traditional lenders. Rising Tide serves prospective, start-up, and established businesses in Lehigh, Northampton,
Carbon, Monroe and upper Bucks counties through loans for leasehold improvements, the purchase of
equipment/machinery, marketing costs, working capital and inventory purchases as well as one-on-one technical
assistance on business plans, marketing strategies, cash flow analysis and projections. Rising Tide also

As the only CDFI headquartered in the Lehigh Valley, Rising Tide has an expansive understanding of the capital
and capacity gaps facing low- to moderate-income entrepreneurs in the area and adapts financial products and
services to suit the needs of individual qualified borrowers. Other sources of loans in the target market are
typically more rigid and less responsive to the variable needs of borrowers. Many lenders are reluctant to work
with individuals with past credit problems or little collateral. Rising Tide works with current and potential
borrowers to create business operating budgets, break-even analyses and other projections. Staff works with
potential borrowers to improve credit scores. As a CDFI, at least 60% of the loans provided and at least 60% of
the total dollars loaned must go to businesses operated by LMI owners or to businesses that are located within
an LMI census area in order to maintain CDFI certification.
Rising Tide’s loan committee evaluates requests of a detailed business plan and financial projections developed
with assistance from staff. Successful loan applicants must be current on all existing credit obligations but past
credit problems do not necessarily disadvantage an applicant. Collateral is important, although not always
required. 
*Evaluation
RTCLF's director develops an annual work plan of goals and objectives. Evaluations are conducted twice per
year to measure program effectiveness and outcomes. This evaluation is carefully analyzed by Community
Action's Program Development and Evaluation Committee as well as the board of RTCLF.
The director completes reports that monitor the success of goals and objectives in the work plan. The long-term
impact of the programs and services of RTCLF are measured using DownHome loan software and reported
annually to the United States Department of the Treasury.

*Sustainability
RTCLF anticipates increased loan applications and approvals due to expansion. This will generate new interest
income to strengthen organizational sustainability as well as the ability to develop innovative products in
response to emerging needs in the target market.
By performing careful due diligence on loan applications, RTCLF further shores up program sustainability. Loans
are reviewed and rated based on policies and procedures outlined in the RTCLF lending policies. Due diligence
is ultimately the responsibility of the loan committee. It is also practiced by the RTCLF director and staff at two
Community Action subsidiary community development corporations which provide education and technical
support to entrepreneurs. Final loan decisions are made by the loan committee and reported to the board of
directors. Once loans are approved and disbursed, the director carefully monitors repayment. When
entrepreneurs fall behind, the director proactively engages with them and, if necessary, initiates collections
related to their loan collateral.
RTCLF also remains sustainable by adjusting in response to community needs. The recent expansion of
marketing and technical assistance services, including the rejuvenation of a Marketing Council to serve the
business community, is just one example of how the RTCLF has developed new products and services to
provide greater opportunities for entrepreneurs. RTCLF also has strong working relationships with other small
business support providers; these relationships are leveraged to meet the diverse needs of entrepreneurs
served by RTCLF.
As a subsidiary of Community Action Lehigh Valley, RTCLF benefits from the stability of an established, larger
organization that maximizes limited resources. Community Action provides management, administrative, and
systems support including administrative, fiscal, human resources, fundraising and grant writing, organizational
planning, research, and program development and evaluation. 
*Community Involvement History
Community Action Lehigh Valley is the leading regional advocate for low- and moderate-income people in the
Lehigh Valley and as such has actively engaged and mobilized thousands of residents in the fight against
poverty since 1965. Rising Tide has a history of actively reaching out to financial institutions, small business
owners, and prospective entrepreneurs to educate them about lending opportunities for small business launch or
expansion.
As a subsidiary of Community Action, Rising Tide formerly collaborated with two other subsidiaries—Community
Action Development Allentown (CADA) and Community Action Development Bethlehem (CADB)—in providing
marketing and technical assistance to businesses. However, under the strategic direction of Community Action’s
Executive Director, it was determined that Rising Tide would gradually assume full responsibility for provision of
these services through the creation of a new Business Unit, a transition that was formalized in July 2020. Rising
Tide’s full-time staff has now grown from two to four people, including a new Marketing and Communications
Coordinator. With our dramatically increased capacity, we will be able to expand our outreach into the community
to ensure that aspiring entrepreneurs get the help they need to be successful.
Rising Tide still closely partners with CADA and CADB in many endeavors. CADA and CADB enhance the
success of Rising Tide both by providing development services that are beyond the capacity of Rising Tide and
by acting as an additional presence in the communities served. With offices in downtown Allentown and south
Bethlehem, prospective and existing business owners have the opportunity to access walk-in services in
locations that may be more convenient and accessible to them.

*Fundraising Plan
Rising Tide has a strong history of raising private and public support to meet strategic goals. The Rising Tide has
received, and will continue to receive, Community Development Block Grant funding through the City of
Allentown, as well as federal funding through both the CDFI Fund and the Community Service Block Grant
program. Community Action’s executive director also serves as the executive director of Rising Tide. Community
Action’s annual budget has grown from $500,000 to over $30 million. This experience will position Rising Tide to
continue developing partnerships with businesses and foundations throughout our target market. Current donors
will continue to be engaged with the work of the organization and this pool of supporters will continually be
cultivated and expanded.
Rising Tide raises its entire operating budget each fiscal year, less any earned income it receives through
interest payments and loan fees. Rising Tide receives fundraising services through a management contract with
Community Action which includes: identification of funding opportunities; communication with funders;
management of a funding calendar; writing and submission of grant proposals; tracking progress of funding
requests in a database; and ensuring contract compliance by assisting with contractual obligations, conducting
internal program monitoring, and assisting with external monitoring.
