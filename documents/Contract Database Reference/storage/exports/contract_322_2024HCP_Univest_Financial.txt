Historical Involvement  
Community Action Committee of the Lehigh Valley, Inc. (AKA Community Action Lehigh 
Valley (CALV)) was incorporated on December 20, 1965 as a Pennsylvania not-for-profit 
corporation and designated as a 501(c)3 organization to work with and for low-income 
residents of the Lehigh Valley to combat poverty. Its programs have grown and evolved 
over the past 58 years and have had a strong impact on the local community. In 1970, CALV 
established the first Head Start program in the area, educating 250 students, and provided 
17 migrant farm workers with housing. In 1980, CALV established the Low-Income Home 
Energy Assistance Program, operated a 24-hour emergency hotline, and delivered heating 
fuel to residents in crisis. Lehigh Valley Food Bank began distributing food from a rented 
truck in 1984, and Lehigh Valley Confederation on the Homeless launched. In the 1990s, 
CALV opened transitional long-term housing for families pursuing an education, and 
CALV’s Task Force on Economic Opportunity started supporting minority entrepreneurs 
through loans. The Sixth Street Shelter expanded to assist more families experiencing 
homelessness, and CALV began administering PPL’s OnTrack program. The Home 
Ownership Counseling Program (HOCP), which includes the First Time Homebuyer 
Seminars, started in 1992. Rising Tide Community Loan Fund launched in 2000, and CALV

began offering foreclosure prevention counseling in 2004. In 2005, CALV mobilized 
residents for the start of the Main Street and Greenway projects, as well as Second Harvest's 
Backpack Buddies initiative. The Mortgage Foreclosure Court Diversion Program with both 
Lehigh and Northampton counties started in 2009. In 2011, the Land Trust put its first home 
on the market. In 2013, the Sixth Street Shelter expanded to provide housing units for 12 
additional families. In 2016, the Campaign for Racial and Ethnic Justice launched, and in 
2017, a neighborhood partnership program in the Slate Belt started. CALV also developed a 
comprehensive housing revitalization initiative in 2018, Community Action Homes, to 
increase the amount of safe and affordable housing for low- to moderate-income 
households in the Lehigh Valley through acquisition/rehab/resale, owner-occupied rehab, 
and investor-owned rehab. In 2019, Color Outside the Lines, a 5-7-year racial diversity and 
inclusion plan, launched to ensure that everyone in the Lehigh Valley has equal access to 
opportunity.

Description  
The Housing Counseling Program (HCP) is a HUD - and PHFA-approved housing 
counseling program of Community Action Lehigh Valley, which offers a number of 
services to work with families in the Lehigh Valley who hope to achieve 
homeownership and avoid foreclosure. HCP’s mission is to provide low- to 
moderate-income individuals and families with a continuum of coordinated 
financial counseling services in one location in order to encourage and assist them 
to build personal assets, to increase and preserve homeownership, and to develop 
neighborhood wealth for the benefit of the whole community.  
Programs offered by HCP: 
• Home Ownership Counseling Program (HOCP) consists of seminars for first -
time homebuyers, pre-settlement counseling and one-on-one pre-purchase 
counseling services. The participants have a unique opportunity to maximize 
their knowledge of homeownership, cover the basics of effective budgeting 
and plan to prepare themselves for purchasing their first homes. The sessions 
are offered both in English and Spanish in consideration of the cultures and 
demographics that make up the Lehigh Valley; 
• The Foreclosure Mitigation Counseling Program provides counseling services 
to homeowners in default, at risk of default, or facing foreclosure; 
• The Mortgage Foreclosure Diversion Program is a mediation program in the 
Lehigh and Northampton County court systems that is available to owners of 
single-family homes in foreclosure; 
• The Homeowners’ Emergency Mortgage Assistance Program (HEMAP) 
includes foreclosure mitigation services, provided by a Housing Counselor, to 
homeowners of single-family homes who are in default and who receive an 
ACT 91 Notice; 
• Financial counseling and financial education are offered to pre-purchase 
counseling participants. These services are also offered to participants of 
Community Action Lehigh Valley’s Ferry Street transitional housing 
apartments to promote the sustainability of participants’ self-sufficiency and 
decrease the likelihood that participants will return to homelessness.

Impact 
Donation Beneficiaries  
HCP’s services help families, in particular those who are low- to moderate-income, 
achieve homeownership and avoid foreclosure. Not only does this benefit individual 
families, but promoting and maintaining homeownership among low - to 
moderate-income households is also an investment in a community’s 
neighborhoods. Homeowners move less frequently than renters and are more likely 
to develop strong ties to their surrounding community. Homeowners invest 
financially and personally in their individual properties and neighborhoods. As 
property crimes affect homeowners directly, homeowners are motivated to deter 
crime and implement crime-prevention programs. Additionally, neighborhoods 
influence other homeowners to improve their properties, resulting in a better 
overall quality of the community. Therefore, the increase in homeownership and 
decrease in foreclosure will positively impact the Lehigh Valley through increased 
property values and pride in neighborhoods and decreased transience, crime, and 
strain on local government resources.
