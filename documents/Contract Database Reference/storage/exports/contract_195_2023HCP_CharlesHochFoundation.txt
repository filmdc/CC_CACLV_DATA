Dear Directors of the Charles H. Hoch Foundation, 
 
We envision a future in which all neighborhoods in the Lehigh Valley are healthy, safe, and thriving places 
where children are free to play and adults are actively engaged in their community. We wish that all our 
neighborhoods were places where people were proud to live. While we know this is unfortunately not the 
case for all Lehigh Valley residents, we also know that increasing and preserving homeownership 
opportunities leads to stable communities. 
 
Increasing homeownership is an essential component of any effort to promote neighborhood stability and 
revitalize communities facing economic challenges. Homeowners move less frequently than renters, and 
therefore become rooted to their neighborhoods, resulting in stronger communities. Homeowners are 
invested financially and personally in their individual properties and their surrounding neighborhoods. As 
property crimes affect homeowners directly, homeowners are motivated to deter crime and implement 
crime-prevention programs. Additionally, neighbors influence other homeowners to take care of their 
properties, elevating community-wide pride in neighborhoods.  
 
At Community Action Lehigh Valley (CALV), it is our duty to make sure that low- to moderate-income 
families interested in homeownership are well equipped to become homebuyers and are well supported in 
maintaining their homes. The Housing Counseling Program (HCP), a HUD- and PHFA-approved housing 
counseling program of CALV, offers a number of programs to help Lehigh Valley families achieve 
homeownership and avoid foreclosure. It is HCP’s mission to provide low- to moderate-income individuals 
and families with a continuum of coordinated financial counseling services in one location in order to 
encourage and assist them to build personal assets, to increase and preserve homeownership, and to develop 
neighborhood wealth for the benefit of the whole community.  
 
There is no other program in the Lehigh Valley that offers comprehensive home ownership counseling, 
including a first-time homebuyer seminar, pre-purchase and pre-settlement counseling, financial education, 
and foreclosure mitigation and assistance programs comprised of individual counseling and a diversion 
program held in civil court. HCP is directed by Ms. Susan Gottwald, with the assistance of three full-time 
Housing Counselors and an Administrative Assistant. HCP offers the following services:  
 
Home Ownership Counseling Program (HOCP) 
 
HCP has been operating the HOCP for over 30 years. HOCP offers counseling services and homeownership 
education to low- to moderate-income prospective homebuyers, enabling participants to evaluate the pros

and cons of homeownership and become successful homeowners. HOCP’s mission is to expand 
homeownership opportunities, particularly for minorities and persons with low- to moderate-incomes; to 
educate prospective first-time homebuyers to enable them to make an informed decision regarding 
homeownership; to assist those who proceed through the process of purchasing a home and provide the 
resources to promote successful homeownership; and to inform the related industries about the needs of the 
community. 
 
HOCP consists of three parts: first-time homebuyer seminars, pre-settlement counseling, and one-on-one 
pre-purchase counseling. 
 
First-time Homebuyer Seminars: Housing Counselors conduct seven first-time homebuyer seminars each 
year throughout the community, usually in areas of greatest need, such as low-income communities in the 
region’s urban areas. Curriculum topics include: Budgeting Your Money; Your Credit Report; Credit 
Establishment/Re-Establishment; Affordability; Working With Your Lender; Mortgage Terminology; 
Mortgage Process; Debt-to-Income Ratio; Mortgage Loans; Predatory Lending; Working With a Realtor; 
Home Inspection; Homeowners Insurance; Settlement Day; Avoiding Foreclosure; and Local 
Homeownership Programs. Seminars are offered in English and Spanish. Speakers for the seminars rotate 
into the schedule and include representatives from local lending institutions, realtors, home inspection 
agencies, insurance agencies, and settlement companies. 
 
Pre-Settlement Counseling: Housing Counselors provide small group and one-on-one pre-settlement 
counseling sessions in English and Spanish. Pre-settlement counseling is held on a weekly basis, and both 
two-hour and four-hour sessions are offered. Through pre-settlement counseling, participants learn about 
the closing process, financial management/budgeting, predatory lending, Fair Housing laws, homeowners 
insurance, home inspections, title insurance, home repair/maintenance and energy conservation, and how 
to avoid foreclosure and review mortgage loan documents. 
 
One-on-One Pre-Purchase Counseling: Housing Counselors provide face-to-face pre-purchase 
counseling sessions. The sessions are usually one to two hours in length and are offered on a daily basis. 
Individuals have the opportunity to develop a budget and action plan, review their credit report, and learn 
about mortgage documentation, the pre-qualification process, and mortgage programs. Housing Counselors 
also provide pre-purchase financial/rental counseling to participants at the Turner Street and Ferry Street 
Apartments, CALV’s long-term transitional housing program, to promote the sustainability of participants’ 
self-sufficiency and decrease the likelihood that participants will return to homelessness.  
 
HCP leverages community partnerships to ensure the success of the HOCP and its participants. 
Northampton County’s First-Time Homebuyer Program provides subordinate loans to eligible first-time 
homebuyers subject to completion of the first-time homebuyer seminar or pre-settlement counseling. 
Participants using First Front Door funds for down payment and closing cost assistance can take an HCP 
four-hour pre-settlement counseling session. HCP involves experts from the community at the first-time 
homebuyer seminars, such as home inspectors, lenders, realtors, and home insurance agents.  
 
Speakers for the first-time homebuyer seminars rotate into the schedule and include representatives from 
lending institutions (such as Truist, Wells Fargo, TD Bank, Fulton Bank, ESSA Bank & Trust, Embassy 
Bank, Firstrust, First Northern Bank & Trust, Provident Bank, and Prosperity Home Mortgage); home 
inspection agencies (such as Second Sight Home Inspections, Advantage Inspection Service, CDA 
Inspection Services, and Smart Home Services LLC); realtors (such as Century 21 Ramos Realty, Weichert, 
and Keller Williams); homeowners insurance agencies (such as State Farm Insurance, Valera Agency, and 
Arbor Insurance Group); and settlement companies (such as First United Land Transfer and The Settlement 
Company). HCP continues to form new partnerships and recruit new speakers as needed.

HCP staff members work with the HCP Advisory Board to boost program participation in an effort to reach

Financial Education  
 
The Housing Counselors teach a financial education course utilizing the Financial Education and Coaching 
Initiative (FECI) curriculum from the Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency (PHFA). This course is taught 
in four 1 1/2 -hour sessions, or two 3-hour sessions, for a total of 6 hours. Session topics include: Start on 
Solid Ground: Budget; Maximize Earnings; Taxes (Earnings); Map Your Money; Stash Your Cash 
(Savings/Investing); Make Your Money Work (For You); Don’t Risk Your Future; Borrow to Grow. This 
course is also offered to residents of CALV’s long-term transitional housing units at Turner Street and Ferry 
Street Apartments. 
 
Foreclosure Mitigation Counseling Program 
 
HCP began offering Foreclosure Mitigation Counseling services in 2004. Housing Counselors provide 
foreclosure mitigation counseling to homeowners in default of their mortgages or for whom default is 
imminent. Counseling consists of loan document review, budget work-up, budget verification, review of 
credit report, discussion of the options available to avoid foreclosure or cure the default, development of an 
action plan to achieve the desired option(s), and referrals to other local agencies and services. Housing 
Counselors assist homeowners in putting together a request to the loan servicer for loan modification, 
forbearance, repayment plan, or other foreclosure alternatives. In addition, Housing Counselors discuss 
possible mortgage modification frauds with homeowners, and any suspected cases are referred to HUD.   
 
HCP benefits from marketing conducted by the Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency (PHFA), HUD, and 
local elected representatives. PHFA provides advertising and marketing for foreclosure counseling services 
and identifies local housing counseling agencies, like Community Action Lehigh Valley, in its newspaper 
and billboard advertisements. Consumers who call PHFA or check its website are provided phone numbers 
for local housing counseling agencies. 
 
Mortgage Foreclosure Diversion Program 
 
In 2009, Community Action Lehigh Valley expanded its efforts to preserve home ownership in the Lehigh 
Valley by partnering with the county civil courts to launch the Mortgage Foreclosure Diversion program in 
Lehigh and Northampton counties. This program assists homeowners in Lehigh and Northampton counties 
who have received a foreclosure complaint. Those who are hoping to avoid foreclosure are “diverted” to a 
conciliation process in civil court. During the conciliation process, the foreclosure proceeding is stayed.  
Homeowners receive foreclosure mitigation counseling (described above), and the homeowner has an 
opportunity, with the help of Housing Counselors and attorneys, to attempt to resolve the mortgage 
problems and avoid foreclosure. 
 
Homeowners’ Emergency Mortgage Assistance Program (HEMAP) 
 
Assisting homeowners in default who have received an ACT 91 Notice with the completion of a 
Homeowners’ Emergency Mortgage Assistance Program (HEMAP) application is a responsibility of HCP 
Housing Counselors. A Housing Counselor also provides comprehensive counseling to the delinquent

2021-2022 Program Year Outcomes  
 
• 55: The number of families who purchased a home with assistance from the Housing Counseling 
Program that we were able to contact.  
 
• Conducted seven seminars, each providing eight hours of HUD-approved education, that were 
attended by 199 prospective homebuyers, 175 of whom received certificates of completion. Three 
seminars were conducted in Spanish and four in English. All seven seminars were held virtually. 
 
• Provided default and delinquency counseling to 23 financially distressed homeowners through the 
Northampton County and Lehigh County Mortgage Foreclosure Diversion Programs and saved 14 
households from foreclosure through loan modifications, repayment plans, partial claims, and applying 
for rescue funds. 
 
2022-2023 Program Year Goals and Performance Targets 
 
GOAL 1 The Housing Counseling Program (HCP) will increase homeownership, particularly 
for households of color and low- to moderate-income households. 
 
Performance Targets: 
 
A. One hundred seventy-five (175) prospective homebuyers will improve their understanding of the 
complexity of home-buying and the challenges and benefits of homeownership as demonstrated by 
their completion of and graduation from one (1) of seven (7) seminars in Allentown, Bethlehem, 
Easton, and remotely (four in English, three in Spanish).   
 
B. Fifty (50) prospective homebuyers will receive pre-purchase counseling, which can include 
understanding the mortgage process, establishing and/or how to repair credit, creating a budget and 
action plan, and understanding predatory lending.  
 
C. Fifty (50) prospective homebuyers will receive pre-settlement counseling before their 
settlement/closing date and will purchase a home.
