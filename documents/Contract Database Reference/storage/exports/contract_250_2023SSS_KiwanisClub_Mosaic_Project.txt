PRINCIPAL SERVICES (describe) 
 
Sixth Street Shelter assists families experiencing homelessness secure safe housing through family-centered services, in-
house programming, and referrals to community resources while advocating to end homelessness and housing insecurity 
in the Lehigh Vally. Located in Center City Allentown, Sixth Street Shelter provides safe, short-term housing for families 
with at least one child under the age of 18 experiencing homelessness. The property has 25 private apartments, a Family 
Resource Center, playground, and full-choice food pantry for residents. The Sixth Street Shelter is unique because it is 
the only shelter in the City where entire families of any configuration (including those with fathers and teenaged boys, 
multi-generational families, and those from the LGBTQ+ community) stay together in their own apartments.  
 
For many families, the stay at the Sixth Street Shelter enables them to begin to stabilize their income and address the 
problems that led to their homelessness. The families who come to the Shelter do so for a variety of reasons: low-paying 
jobs, lack of affordable housing, mental and/or physical health crises, domestic violence, and lack of access to reliable 
transportation, healthcare, and/or childcare. The Shelter provides each family with a fully furnished apartment and 
supportive services, including individualized case management. Staff work to connect them with the necessary resources 
to address their individual needs, such as financial literacy and parenting classes, access to childcare and healthcare, and 
mental health support. During their residency, the family develops a Family Service Plan with their caseworker, setting 
household, budgeting, educational, and vocational goals and seeking suitable housing and employment. We have started 
a Benchmark Family Care Initiative, which aims to ensure that all residents have or obtain medical insurance and are 
connected to appropriate providers so that they can achieve a list of benchmarks related to their relevant medical, 
dental, and mental health care, as well as track financial goals. Other recent initiatives include adding a Housing Specialist 
to our staff to work with our residents and local landlords in order to identify affordable housing options and facilitate 
faster placements for families, and expanding our educational and vocational programming at the Sixth Street Shelter 
to increase residents’ earning capacity, which will help them to remain stably housed when they move out of the Shelter 
and into permanent housing. Staff maintain contact with the family following their move into permanent housing to 
offer additional assistance and determine how well they are continuing to move toward self-sufficiency.  
 
The Shelter and supportive services are critically important due to the detrimental impacts of homelessness on children. 
The First Focus Campaign for Children summarizes, “Studies show that housing instability and homelessness exert long-
term adverse physiological, academic, and economic effects on children.” Research from the National Center on Family 
Homelessness indicates that children experiencing homelessness are twice as likely to be food insecure, and, according 
to the U.S. Department of Education, have three times the rate of emotional and behavioral problems. The National 
Alliance to End Homelessness states that they experience more school mobility, have lower academic performance, and 
are more likely to experience separations from their families. Sixth Street Shelter works to keep families housed together 
and to offer greater stability and a supportive environment to children and their families while they navigate the 
challenges they face. Homelessness is often traumatic for children, and the Sixth Street Shelter provides resources and 
services to help mitigate these impacts and set families on a path out of homelessness. 
 
In our last fiscal year, the Sixth Street Shelter provided shelter to 52 families consisting of 196 people, including 123 
children (49 of whom were 5 and under). 94% of those families had a female head of household, and 67% were headed 
by a single mother. 87% of families qualified as Extremely Low-Income (0-30% Area Median Income) when they entered 
the Shelter, and 13% qualified as Low-Income (31-50% Area Median Income). Of last year’s families, 67% identified as 
Black or African American, 29% as White, and 4% as Native Hawaiian or Pacific Islander; and 35% identified as 
Hispanic/Latino. The Shelter endeavors to engage staff that reflect the community we serve and have lived expertise 
with homelessness.

SPECIFIC PURPOSE: 
 
The Sixth Street Shelter works to mitigate the negative impacts of homelessness and poverty on children and families, and 
art therapy can serve an important role in this process. There are healing components that can come through art, including 
via the media and materials used. The Shelter plans to create a mosaic wall on the outside of our Family Resource Center 
as a welcoming sign to all those who pass by and enter the doors. The Family Resource Center is an appropriate location 
for this mosaic because much of the supportive programming we offer families takes place in this space. Children residing 
at the Shelter will take part in the completion of this project, which will simultaneously teach them about mosaics as a 
form of art and also allow them to add their voice to the project and create a lasting imprint on the Shelter. 
 
This project is a physical manifestation of our ideals to promote family togetherness, inclusivity, and healing through the 
arts. Shelter staff will lead the project, and children of all ages can take part in the collaborative efforts of the installation. 
Shelter residents will contribute to the design of the mosaic, and all children will have the opportunity to engage in the 
hands-on aspect of applying the mosaic tiles. This will engage their interest as well as their tactile sensibilities, while at the 
same time emphasizing a sense of community and inclusivity by working alongside one another towards a common goal. 
We will use a combination of repurposed and eco-friendly materials to create a work that will be a continuous source of 
pride, demonstrating resilience much like that of our residents and of the City.
