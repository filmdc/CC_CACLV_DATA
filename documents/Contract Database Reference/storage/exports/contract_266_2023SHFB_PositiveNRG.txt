Please describe your organization's mission
and purpose
The mission of Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and 
Northeast Pennsylvania is to obtain food and distribute it to people in 
need through area non-profits and to provide resources for education 
and advocacy to end hunger. We provide nutritious food, offer nutrition 
education, advocate to end food insecurity, dismantle barriers to create 
access, and work to attain food justice. We respect and preserve the 
dignity of each of our neighbors.
Describe the service(s) your organization
provides
Second Harvest Food Bank, a member of Feeding America – the 
nation’s largest charitable food network, serves Lehigh, Northampton, 
Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne counties. Last year, Second Harvest 
distributed 14.7 million pounds of food to our network of 200+ nonprofit 
agencies (food pantries, schools, meal centers, shelters, childcare 
agencies, addiction treatment programs), a 44% increase from the 
previous year. Included in this total is 6.7 million pounds of fresh 
produce, perishable protein, and fresh dairy products (46% of total 
distribution). Annually, we rescue more than 8 million pounds of food 
from ending up in a landfill.

On average, the Second Harvest network serves more than 87,000 
people each month - a 52% increase from last year. The pantries within 
the Second Harvest network are operated almost solely through the work 
of community volunteers. Our food distribution is a model of neighbors 
helping neighbors. 
In addition to food distribution to our network, Second Harvest operates 
several programs aimed at improving the health of our community and 
working toward a more just and equitable local food system. We 
administer the Pennsylvania Senior Food Box Program (PASB). PASB 
works to improve the health of low-income seniors by supplementing 
their diets with shelf-stable nutritious foods. Eligible participants include 
low-income individuals who are at least 60 years old and whose 
household income is at or below 130% of the U.S. poverty level. We 
work to supplement boxes with healthy, fresh items when possible. 
Currently, Second Harvest provides senior food boxes to nearly 2,350 
seniors across 49 sites each month.
Our MilitaryShare program works “to serve those who served us” by 
providing access to nutritious food, with a goal of improving health 
outcomes and easing financial strain among low-income active duty, 
reserve, and retired military members and their families. MilitaryShares 
provide military members in need with a monthly share of fresh and 
shelf-stable food. Currently, we are able to provide 500 shares each 
month. 
Our MilitaryShare program works “to serve those who served us” by 
providing access to nutritious food, with a goal of improving health 
outcomes and easing financial strain among low-income active duty, 
reserve, and retired military members and their families. Our 
MilitaryShare program provides 500+ military families with share of fresh 
and shelf-stable food each month. 
Our SNAP (Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program) outreach 
coordinators work with local county offices of Health and Human 
Services to offer SNAP application assistance. Our SNAP coordinators 
offer dedicated personal application support to ensure that all eligible 
participants receive needed benefits.
Second Harvest operates The Seed Farm, a 42-acre training farm. 
Because there can be no access to nourishing food without farms, The 
Seed Farm works to support food production at all levels, from home 
gardeners to community gardens to local farmers, with an emphasis on 
getting nutritious and locally-grown food to under-resourced 
communities. Strong local food systems equal food security.
We also offer a dynamic community-based nutrition education program, 
which includes the provision of Share Our Strength’s Cooking Matters® 
program - free cooking classes for parents, kids and childcare providers. 
Participants learn about cooking, meal prep, grocery shopping, food 
budgeting, and nutrition.

How will you measure results/impact of
program?
Annually, each program of Community Action, including Second Harvest, 
develops a work plan with measurable performance targets that guide 
the activities of our programs and are designed to have a positive impact 
on the community. Performance targets are monitored and evaluated by 
the Community Action Board of Directors. Progress is monitored at the 
six-month mark, and all performance targets are fully evaluated at the 
end of the program year. Work plan objectives to be accomplished by 
June 30, 2024, include:
GOAL 1: SHFB will source enough food to serve our six-county region, 
prioritizing fresh, nutritionally dense and neighbor-responsive food.   
Performance Targets:
A.        Source 15,000,000 pounds of food for distribution to our 
agencies.
B.        Source 6,000,000 pounds of fresh, perishable produce, protein 
and dairy.
C.        Donate fresh produce grown at The Seed Farm to the pantry 
network.

D.        Ensure that less than 15% of Second Harvest monthly (ranked) 
inventory is composed of “choose rarely” foods monthly. 
E.        Maintain donated product at 55% or more of total pounds 
sourced. 
F.        Increase agency enabled pickups by 7% over FY2023 pounds.
Goal 2: SHFB will work with our network of 200+ agencies to deliver food 
for distribution to neighbors facing food insecurity, focusing on equitable 
distribution and vulnerable populations.
Performance Targets:
A.        Distribute 13,280,00 pounds of food to people in need.
B.        Provide (at a minimum) 53.8 Meals Per Person in Need (MPIN) in 
each of the six counties. 
C.        Provide a three-day supply of nutritious food each month to 2,450 
senior citizens who qualify for the PA Senior Box program. 
D.        Provide 560 Military Share boxes per month, with coverage in 
each county served by SHFB.  
E.        Distribute 500,000 meals through school pantries. 
F.        Pilot Service Insights-Meal Connect at 10 pantries.
Goal 3: SHFB will actively work to empower neighbors and partner 
agencies, by providing technical assistance, resources, and educational 
opportunities.
 
Performance Targets:
A.        Provide SNAP (formerly Food Stamps) application assistance for 
at least 300 households. 
B.        Engage neighbors through nutrition education programming, 
including cooking instruction, taste-testing and Healthy Pantry Initiative 
programming. 
C.        Engage at least 50% of network pantry directors in training and 
technical assistance programming. 
D.        Engage volunteers and community members with Growing 
Partners programming. 
E.        Provide at least 45.5 hours of farm management training to new 
farmers on skills/knowledge areas identified as critical to new farmer 
success. 
F.        Offer 2 training opportunities (workshop, course, or 
demonstration) in collaboration with at least one partner organization, 
such as Penn State Extension, Pennsylvania Association for Sustainable 
Agriculture, or PA FarmLink.
GOAL 4:   SHFB will comply with food safety and contractual obligations 
of Feeding America      to ensure the integrity of the food bank and 
partner agencies.

Performance Targets:
A.        Monthly assessments of warehouse facilities will comply with AIB 
standards.  
B.        Agency monitoring will occur consistently throughout the year to 
confirm compliance with contract requirements, including verification that 
agencies are obtaining eligibility determination from food recipients.  
C.        Maintain accurate inventory within less than 2% error. 
Additionally, Second Harvest solicits feedback from our partner agencies 
and food pantry participants, in the form of formal surveys, 
conversational feedback, and committee work. The results and feedback 
guide Second Harvest’s strategic planning, program development, and 
service provision. We are working toward a participant-centric model of 
operation. We are taking the necessary, often tough, steps to shift power 
to the people we serve.
