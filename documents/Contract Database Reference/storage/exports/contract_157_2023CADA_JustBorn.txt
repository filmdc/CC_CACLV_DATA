Organization Background
 Primary Goals and Objectives	 
 
Community Action Lehigh Valley was created in 1965 to work with and for low-income residents
of the Lehigh Valley to combat poverty. Community Action Development Allentown was
incorporated on September 7, 1993, as a subsidiary community and economic development
corporation of Community Action Lehigh Valley.
Community Action Development Allentown’s governing body is a Board of Directors made up of
neighborhood residents, representatives from local government, and professionals from the
banking community and local funding sources. By design, the Board draws half its membership
from the neighborhood and includes representatives from traditionally underrepresented groups.
Relying particularly on the life experiences and practical knowledge of neighborhood residents,
the Board sets policy, investigates and approves projects, and provides guidance for planning and
development. Strong community involvement on the Board of Directors leads to programming
that represents the best interests of the neighborhood.
Community Action Development Allentown has historically worked in the City of Allentown within
a focus area known as the downtown. Currently, there are 38,296 residents who live in the focus
area served by Community Action Development Allentown. The focus area is densely populated
and home to 31.6% of residents in the City of Allentown. The community is more racially and
ethnically diverse than the City overall; 57% of residents in the focus area are Hispanic/Latino
and 16.3% are Black or African American.

There are many hurdles to financial autonomy for youth in Allentown. The City of Allentown’s
“Allentown Vision 2030: Comprehensive & Economic Development Plan” includes principles of
increasing access to training and skill building as well as to foster small business and
entrepreneurship. According to the Allentown Vision 2030 plan, creating a robust entrepreneurial
ecosystem means ensuring that anyone can start and grow a business, regardless of income
level. Entrepreneurs and small businesses need resources and support to start their business,
grow and maintain that business, and obtain financial success. By providing comprehensive
assistance to the most economically vulnerable, especially those youth who do not wish to pursue
a traditional college degree but instead enter the workforce after graduating high school,
Allentown’s youth will have greater opportunity to achieve financial mobility and stability.

This program will provide business trainings and entrepreneurial mentorship opportunities for
low-income youth aged 15-21 residing in the City of Allentown, specifically the downtown/Center
City area. The entrepreneurship classes are held twice a week. One session per week features a
small business owner who presents their story about how they got started. The second weekly
session focuses on technical aspects of launching a business, including topics like setting up an
LLC, filing for a fictitious name, acquiring a business license, and opening a business account. The
Youth Entrepreneurship Program will offer multiple 8-week sessions both virtually and in-person,
based around the Allentown School District’s calendar to align with holidays and summer break.

In the Allentown School District, 55% of the student body identifies as Hispanic or Latino, and
10% identifies as Black. Nearly 75% of Allentown School District students come from low-income
families. These students face challenges and trauma including poverty, racism, and a lack of
opportunity not seen in more affluent areas of the Lehigh Valley.
Educational disparities exist when comparing City of Allentown residents to other residents of
Lehigh County and the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania. Residents of the City of Allentown have
educational attainment levels that are significantly lower than Pennsylvania averages in every
category of degree completion: high school graduation, some college or associate degree,
bachelor’s degree, and graduate degree. These attainment levels are even more significant when
comparing the target neighborhood to the city. A much larger proportion of target neighborhood
residents (29.2%) did not complete high school in comparison to city residents (19.4%).
Dropout rates and graduation rates in Allentown are cause for alarm. According to recent data
made available by the Pennsylvania Department of Education, Allentown School District’s dropout
rate of 3.14% is the highest in the Lehigh Valley—a rate more than double that of the second-
highest district.
A significant portion of the city’s teenagers and young adults are not enrolled in school or
working, leaving them with idle time and a need to find ways to earn money. In Allentown, 15.2%
of those aged 15 to 19 are not enrolled in school and 5.6% are completely “idle” meaning they
are neither enrolled in school nor employed. Areas with historically high poverty and
unemployment rates, coupled with low educational attainment and access to opportunity, feed
gang activity and violent crime.
Children in the community are affected by local violence, both directly and indirectly. The 2019
Community Health Needs Assessment (CHNA) conducted by Lehigh Valley Health Network (LVHN)
shows that of residents surveyed, 55.1% identified violence as an issue present in the
community. The CHNA indicates an even higher percentage response related to gang activity
being present (57.7%).
Based on data from the Lehigh County’s Regional Intelligence and Investigation Center (RIIC), as
of 2015, there were 30 gangs operating in the City of Allentown and surrounding municipalities,
with 70 subsets of those gangs. Unofficially, Allentown School District (ASD) staff are aware of
heavy recruitment in Allentown’s schools, beginning as early as second grade, and with at least
five active gangs at Francis D. Raub Middle School. School data, while technically anecdotal, has
been substantiated by staff at Promise Neighborhoods of the Lehigh Valley, a partner nonprofit
who has worked closely with the school administration for several years.
The American Community Survey 5-Year Estimates (2016-2020) indicate that in the City of
Allentown, 10.2% of the population received Supplemental Security Income (SSI), which is
defined by the Social Security Administration as being “designed to help aged, the blind, and
disabled people, who have little or no income; and provides cash to meet basic needs for food,
clothing, and shelter.” In Lehigh County that figure was 5.5%.
