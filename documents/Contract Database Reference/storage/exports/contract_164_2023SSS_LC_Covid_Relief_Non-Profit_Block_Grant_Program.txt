Community need that will be addressed and additional expenses incurred due to COVID 19: 
 
The Sixth Street Shelter has seen a marked increase in cases of bed bugs since the onset of the COVID-19 
pandemic, which has impacted shelter operations. Although social distancing helped to slow the spread 
of bed bugs nationwide during the pandemic due to strict travel restrictions, quarantines, and a general 
anxiety around meeting in large groups, practicing social distancing can be difficult or even impossible for 
individuals experiencing homelessness. Unsheltered families often stay wherever they can find a place 
regardless of the health risks, which can include exposure to COVID and/or bed bugs. Once a family is 
exposed to bed bugs, they then transport these to the Shelter, where they can easily spread among units. 
While the exterminator used to visit the Shelter twice a month, they now visit weekly to address the 
infestations. When the exterminator comes to treat a unit, the family may have to temporarily vacate, but 
families experiencing homelessness generally do not have alternative locations to stay. We have also had 
to rely more heavily on professional cleaning services to clean vacated units in order to maintain a safe, 
healthy environment. We are pursuing a variety of avenues through which to approach the bed bug 
problem exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic, and purchasing bed bug resistant furniture is another 
important step. 
Bed bugs can hide in mattresses, bed frames, and other furniture, but bed bug resistant furniture is made 
of materials that are unfavorable for bed bugs to climb on or burrow into, and it does not allow for hiding 
places to nest and lay eggs. Much of the furniture currently in our units is wood, and the porous nature of 
unfinished or roughly finished areas of this well-worn furniture can provide hiding spaces for bed bugs. 
Metal furniture, on the other hand, has a smooth finish and provides fewer hiding spaces. It also resists 
chemical damage and can be easily cleaned after bed bug treatments. Vinyl, in contrast to other fabrics, 
is durable, stain resistant, and easy to clean with common household products. The furniture we have 
selected is designed for easy extermination and cleaning so that we will not have to regularly replace 
furniture that becomes infested. It also means that furniture is easier to sanitize should there be another 
COVID surge.  
We plan to purchase 2 sofas and 6 love seats that are composed of strong metal frames and durable vinyl. 
The sofas will be for our larger units, and the love seats for the smaller units. We also plan to buy dinette 
sets for 12 of our units (12 tables and 34 chairs). These sets are made of maple rubber wood, which is 
durable, cost effective, and scratch resistant. Additionally, we will purchase 10 metal bunk bed frames. 
Through a separate grant, we were able to purchase bed bug resistant mattresses, so we already have the 
mattresses needed to complete these frames.  
Not only is this furniture durable enough to handle the wear and tear of multiple families residing in the 
unit each year, but it also provides a safer, healthier environment for families in terms of cleaning and 
sanitizing against the spread of viruses and bed bugs. Refurnishing the units will give families a clean, safe, 
and comfortable place to call home during their stay at the Sixth Street Shelter.
