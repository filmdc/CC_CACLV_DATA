Donor Overview 
Since 1986, Cargill has supported Feeding America with philanthropic funding through financial
investment, product donation, employee engagement, senior thought leadership, and grants to support a
variety of programs/initiatives such as research, produce, food safety, nudges, and community grants. 
In 2023, Cargill renewed its support of Feeding America via a three-year (FY23-FY25) $4.9M gift, this is
the second year of the three-year gift. 
 
 
Opportunity Context 
As part of that gift, Cargill committed funding for food bank staff to receive food safety training
certifications in the below areas. Awardees will receive $2,000 each to use toward food safety training
registration. 
 
In FY24, awardees will receive a standard award amount of $2,000 each to use toward registration. The
$2,000 will cover most, but not every training listed. While only the courses below are eligible use of
funds, awardees should enroll in the eligible courses that best fit their needs. 
 
The award total, $2,000 each, was determined based on the average award amount in previous cycles
of this opportunity. Up to $200 of grant funds may be used to offset costs associated with food safety
expenses.    
 
Partner food banks can consider re-granting funds to Partner State Associations (PSAs), Affiliate Food
Banks, Regional Distribution Organizations (RDOs) and/or agencies partners that handle and distribute
food. Please see Use of Funds section for more information on re-granting. 
 
To learn of more resources offered by Feeding America to assist your food bank in developing food
safety policies, programs, documentation, and best practices, visit the Food Safety section of
HungerNet. 
 
 
Training Options 
 Enrollment in courses is required by the end of the grant period. Ideally, courses are started by the
end of the grant period, but this is not required. It is important to the donor that the funds are spent by
the end of the grant period.

Understanding Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP): With many new workers joining the food
industry, their application of basic GMPs is essential to ensuring food safety. Unfortunately, they
don’t always understand the reasons for the numerous rules for working in a food manufacturing
operation. This quick 4-part series explains why those rules are in place, how they tie to the
GMPs, and why they’re so important to protecting the safety of the food supply. By understanding
these rules and how they are applied each day in the work environment, you will gain buy-in from
your workforce, promote accountability, and nurture a strong food safety culture in your
organization. 
Registration fee: $432 
Link to register: https://www.aibinternational.com/products/understanding-the-good-
manufacturing-practices-gmp/

you to produce safe, legal food products and meet your customers’ food safety expectations. This
course is available in individual modules which you can select the modules that best fit your

ServSafe Food Handler Guide for Food Banking & Online Video Companion Bundle:  To
support new food safety training requirements outlined in the Partner Food Bank Agreement,
awardees may use the full $2,000 on ServeSafe training. 
This is a comprehensive food handler training solution that delivers consistent food safety training
and meets the specific requirements of food bank employees, agency staff, and volunteers. This
easy-to-implement custom program has been tailored to match the quality and spirit of Feeding
America's network of food banks and agencies. This training is designed for food bank staff that
are not ServSafe Manager certified, regular volunteers, and food pantry staff. For staff who work in
a soup or community kitchen, they should still use the standard ServSafe Food Handler program.
This training is not an online training. The exams are found at the back of the workbooks and the
answer key is found on the ServSafe resource page on HungerNet. 
Registration fee:  May use the full $2,000  
Link to register: Log In/Create Account | ServSafe   
 
Your current User ID and Password will work if you are a registered user with ServSafe. If you are
not a registered user with ServSafe, you must create a new profile before receiving access to
Feeding America / ServSafe Portal. Also, you must show the tax-exempt information for your food
bank to not be charged tax. 
 
The Companion Instructional Video is designed to be used in conjunction with the workbooks. An
instructor can use the video to assist with teaching the material in a meeting or classroom setting
or use it to train individuals remotely. The Companion Instructional Video can be viewed with
groups or assigned to individuals After purchase, the guides will be shipped, and the purchaser will
receive an email with instructions to access the companion video keys. The Companion
Instructional Video codes are valid for 12 months from purchase. A video code can be viewed for
30 days after redemption. Instructor materials and other training resources that have been
developed specifically to support this customized training are found on the ServSafe Food Safety
page on HungerNet.

 Expectations & Registration 
If awarded, grantees are expected to register for safety training no later than the end of the grant period,
9/30/2024. Ideally, training is initiated or completed by the end of the grant period, but this is not a
requirement – only enrollment in the food safety training courses is a requirement. Registration is the
responsibility of the food bank.   
 
Grantees may utilize grant funding to register for food safety training courses in a way that best suits
their needs. For example, funding may be used to send more than one staff member to attend the same
training or send different staff members to each training course and so forth.  
 As you plan attendance, please keep in mind that for some courses, such as AIB, it is possible to have
multiple people watch the webinars within the allotted time frame (consider using webinars in a meeting
so more people can view them).The short quizzes at the end of each webinar could be done in a
group setting.While the additional attendees would not receive individual credit from AIB by
documenting who watched the training webinar and when, it would meet the AIB food safety training
requirement.
