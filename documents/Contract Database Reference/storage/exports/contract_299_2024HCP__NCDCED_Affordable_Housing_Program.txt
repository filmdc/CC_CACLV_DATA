17. As per the COUNTY's requirements under the laws of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, the 
following thresholds shall be used in determining the appropriate method for purchases made in 
whole or in part with the funds provided through this AGREEMENT: 
 
A.  Quoting requirements when the total expenditure in any one sale transaction, regardless of the 
number of individual items purchased and including any shipping costs during this transaction 
is as follows: 
 
•  $1 to $5,000 -- One quote, verbal or written 
•  $5,001 to $24,999 – Minimum three (3) written quotes. Written documentation must be 
attached to the transaction, and shall indicate which qualified vendor(s) were contacted, 
their responses, the date, and other pertinent information besides price, such as delivery, 
etc. 
•  Expenditures over $25,000 need to be procured utilizing a formally advertised public bid. 
 
Competitive sealed bids must be used to procure goods or services in excess of $25,000 for 
construction, reconstruction, demolition, alteration and/or repair, for acquisition of machinery 
and equipment, or for engagement of services of a professional consultant, other than 
architectural or engineering services.
 
Selecting a good or service through the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania’s cooperative 
purchasing program administered by the Department of General Services (DGS) Bureau of 
Procurement, the COSTARS Program, is an acceptable alternative to the methods listed above. 
 
In addition, prevailing wage rates apply to those projects with a total project budget cost which 
exceeds $25,000 as per Section 165-1 of “The Prevailing Wage Act.” 
 
B.  For procurement involving architecture or engineering (A/E) services, RECIPIENT may use 
competitive proposal procedures whereby competitors’ qualifications are evaluated and the most 
qualified competitor is selected, subject to negotiation of fair and reasonable compensation. In 
these instances, price is not used as a selection factor. Under this method, once the most-qualified 
firm is identified, only that firm is asked for a price proposal that is subject to negotiation of a 
fair and reasonable price. If negotiations with the selected firm are unsuccessful, this process is 
repeated with the next highest-ranked firm, until a fair and reasonably priced contract can be 
awarded. RECIPIENT must document the bases for its determination of the most qualified 
competitor and the reasonableness of the contract price. This qualifications-based approach to

B.7. What do you plan to accomplish with this project?  Doc ID: 4444a814dc3c31cac6044cb2f1c04e3f8ff656b8

Community Action Lehigh Valley strives to build healthy and safe neighborhoods in the Lehigh Valley, where children 
feel free to play and adults are actively engaged in community affairs. Community Action, as a HUD-approved housing 
counseling agency, believes in the value and long-term benefits of homeownership. This notion is supported by research 
findings that consistently demonstrate the importance of the housing market for both the economy and the financial 
benefits it can bring to homeowners. In addition to tangible benefits, homeownership positively impacts families, 
communities, and the country as a whole. Homeownership can result in the following outcomes: social stability, 
educational achievement, civic participation (including voting and volunteerism), and health benefits. Furthermore, 
homeownership has traditionally been fundamental to community wealth. It stabilizes property value and represents a 
fixed asset which is very important to a family’s ability to build assets and wealth. 
Community Action has a duty to ensure that low- to moderate-income families interested in homeownership are well-
equipped to become homebuyers and are well-supported in maintaining their homes. One of the obstacles to 
homeownership is often the uncertainty among potential homebuyers about the home buying and mortgage financing 
process and misperceptions about the housing market. Many low- to moderate-income families are not informed about 
or do not have access to information regarding the housing market or homeownership opportunities. 
 
Community Action’s Housing Counseling Program (HCP) provides services to low- to moderate-income families with 
income equal to or below 80% of the median household income. If awarded this grant, income eligible participants who 
reside in the county will be served through housing counseling initiatives, which primarily consist of the Home 
Ownership Counseling Program (HOCP). HCP’s Home Ownership Counseling Program addresses the goal of expanding 
and preserving homeownership opportunities, particularly for minorities and persons with low- to moderate-incomes. 
Potential first-time homebuyers are educated about the responsibilities, benefits, and burdens of homeownership, so 
they can make informed decisions regarding their housing goals.  
 
HCP will also provide financial counseling to homeowners delinquent on their mortgage or in foreclosure. Foreclosure 
assistance benefits homeowners by giving the homeowners an opportunity to make an agreement with their lenders, 
and homeowners who receive loan modifications are able to save their homes. It should also be noted that no one 
benefits from foreclosures. Homeowners lose their homes, lenders acquire the expense of maintaining the property, 
and often property owners residing near the foreclosed property lose property value as a result of foreclosures.  
B.8. How do you plan to accomplish it?  
Community Action Lehigh Valley (CALV) is a HUD- and PHFA-approved housing counseling agency, and the Housing 
Counseling Program (HCP) offers a number of services to help Lehigh Valley families achieve homeownership and avoid 
foreclosure. It is HCP’s mission to provide low- to moderate-income individuals and families with a continuum of 
coordinated financial counseling services in one location in order to encourage and assist them to build personal assets, 
to increase and preserve homeownership, and to develop neighborhood wealth for the benefit of the whole community. 
There is no other program in the Lehigh Valley that offers the same level of comprehensive homeownership counseling. 
HCP is directed by Ms. Susan Gottwald, with the assistance of three full-time Housing Counselors and an Administrative 
Assistant. 
 
HCP provides the Home Ownership Counseling Program (HOCP) to low- to moderate-income residents of Northampton 
County, which consists of first-time homebuyer educational seminars, pre-purchase counseling, and pre-settlement 
counseling. HCP offers foreclosure prevention services through the Foreclosure Mitigation Counseling Program and 
Mortgage Foreclosure Diversion Program to homeowners who face losing their homes due to economic challenges. 
Comprehensive financial counseling is part of these foreclosure programs. HCP also offers comprehensive counseling 
services to tenants to help them learn to budget effectively in order to meet their housing obligations and avoid 
eviction.  Doc ID: 4444a814dc3c31cac6044cb2f1c04e3f8ff656b8

Printed By: Sarah Ortiz on 1/19/2024 	7 of 15 
The Home Ownership Counseling Program (HOCP) consists of three parts: 
1) Group Education: Housing Counselors conduct seven First-Time Homebuyer Seminars throughout the community 
each year. Seminars are offered in English and Spanish. Speakers rotate into the schedule and include representatives 
from lending institutions, home inspection agencies, realtors, homeowners insurance agencies, and settlement 
companies. 
2) Pre-Settlement Counseling: Housing Counselors provide small group and one-on-one pre-settlement counseling in 
English and Spanish. Pre-settlement counseling is held by appointment, and both two-hour and four-hour sessions are 
offered. Participants learn about the closing process, financial management/budgeting, predatory lending, Fair Housing 
laws, homeowners insurance, home inspections, title insurance, home repair/maintenance and energy conservation, 
and how to avoid foreclosure and review mortgage loan documents. 
3) One-on-One Pre-Purchase Counseling: Housing Counselors provide face-to-face pre-purchase counseling sessions. The 
sessions are usually one to two hours in length and are offered on a daily basis. Individuals have the opportunity to 
develop a budget and action plan, review their credit report, and learn about mortgage documentation, the pre-
qualification process, and mortgage programs. 
 
Foreclosure Mitigation Counseling consists of loan document review, budget work-up, budget verification, review of 
credit report, discussion of the options available to avoid foreclosure or cure the default, development of an action plan 
to achieve the desired option(s), and referrals to other local agencies and services. Housing Counselors assist 
homeowners in putting together a request to the lender for loan modification, forbearance, repayment plan, or other 
foreclosure alternatives. In addition, Housing Counselors discuss possible mortgage modification frauds with 
homeowners and any suspected cases are referred to HUD. Foreclosure Mitigation Counseling appointments are 
generally held in person. The Housing Counselors also assist eligible homeowners who have received an Act 91 Notice 
from their lender with submitting an application for assistance to the Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency’s 
Homeowners’ Emergency Mortgage Assistance Program (HEMAP). The Housing Counselors also provide counseling. 
HEMAP appointments are conducted in-person. 
 
The Mortgage Foreclosure Diversion Program assists homeowners in Lehigh and Northampton counties who have 
received a foreclosure complaint. Those who are hoping to avoid foreclosure are “diverted” to a conciliation process in 
civil court. During the conciliation process, the foreclosure proceeding is stayed. Homeowners receive foreclosure 
mitigation counseling (described above), and the homeowner has an opportunity, with the help of Housing Counselors 
and attorneys, to try to resolve the mortgage problems and avoid foreclosure.

HCP expects to have a positive impact on the community by supporting increased homeownership and preventing 
foreclosures. Because a homebuyer has a stake in their own property, as well as their community, owning a house

rooted to their neighborhood, which results in stronger communities. They are more invested financially and personally 
in their properties and neighborhoods. As property crimes affect homeowners directly, they are motivated to deter 
crime and implement crime-prevention programs. Additionally, neighbors influence new homeowners to improve their 
properties, which results in a better quality of the community overall.

Budget Narrative 
 
Susan Gottwald, Director: A total of $5,000 will be used to support the salary and benefits of the Director 
to supervise the comprehensive homeownership counseling services, including pre-purchase and 
foreclosure prevention counseling services, collect and analyze data, directly assist participants as needed, 
and engage in reporting to funders.
 
 
Jeanette Velez, Housing Counselor: A total of $8,000 will be used to support the salary and benefits of 
the Housing Counselor to conduct HOCP seminars and to provide pre-settlement counseling, pre-purchase 
counseling, and foreclosure prevention counseling.
 
 
Annette Santiago, Housing Counselor: A total of $22,000 will be used to support the salary and benefits 
of the Housing Counselor to conduct HOCP seminars and to provide pre-settlement counseling, pre-
purchase counseling, and foreclosure prevention counseling. A greater amount of funding has been 
requested for this Housing Counselor, as they are the lead Housing Counselor for the Northampton County 
Mortgage Foreclosure Diversion Program. 
 
Rayne Schnabel, Housing Counselor: A total of $8,000 will be used to support the salary and benefits of 
the Housing Counselor to conduct HOCP seminars and to provide pre-settlement counseling, pre-purchase 
counseling, and foreclosure prevention counseling. 
  
Carmen Arroyo, Administrative Assistant: A total of $7,000 will be used to support the salary and 
benefits of the Administrative Assistant to respond to phone calls and work with prospective clients of HCP 
to set up group sessions and appointments with housing counselors.
