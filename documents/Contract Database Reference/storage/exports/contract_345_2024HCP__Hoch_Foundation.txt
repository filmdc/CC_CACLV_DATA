Dear Directors of the Charles H. Hoch Foundation,
Housing costs have risen drastically in the Lehigh Valley since the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, and The Morning Call reported on the affordable housing crisis in the region through a series of articles in 2023. The Lehigh Valley is on an economic upswing and is a desirable place to live, drawing new residents, but many low- to moderate-income residents are being squeezed out by rising housing costs. Figures from the Greater Lehigh Valley Realtors for December 2023 indicate that the median sales price for a single-family home in the region is approximately $300,000. While the median value of owner-occupied units in the area is below that at $258,000, 25.5% of homeowners in the Lehigh Valley with a mortgage and 17.2% of homeowners without a mortgage are cost-burdened, meaning that they spend more than 30% of their income on housing costs (American Community Survey 5-Year Estimates 2018-2022). Given the high costs of homes locally and the currently elevated mortgage rates, prospective first-time homebuyers in the Lehigh Valley can greatly benefit from the education and support offered by Community Action Lehigh Valley’s Housing Counseling Program.
Increasing homeownership is an essential component of any effort to promote neighborhood stability and revitalize communities facing economic challenges. Homeowners move less frequently than renters, and therefore become rooted to their neighborhoods, resulting in stronger communities. Homeowners are invested financially and personally in their individual properties and their surrounding neighborhoods. As property crimes affect homeowners directly, homeowners are motivated to deter crime and implement crime-prevention programs. Additionally, neighbors influence other homeowners to take care of their properties, elevating community-wide pride in neighborhoods. 
At Community Action Lehigh Valley (CALV), it is our duty to make sure that low- to moderate-income families interested in homeownership are well equipped to become homebuyers and are well supported in maintaining their homes. The Housing Counseling Program (HCP), a HUD- and PHFA-approved housing counseling program of CALV, offers a number of programs to help Lehigh Valley families achieve homeownership and avoid foreclosure. It is HCP’s mission to provide low- to moderate-income individuals and families with a continuum of coordinated financial counseling services in one location in order to encourage and assist them to build personal assets, to increase and preserve homeownership, and to develop neighborhood wealth for the benefit of the whole community. 
There is no other program in the Lehigh Valley that offers comprehensive homeownership counseling, including a first-time homebuyer seminar, pre-purchase and pre-settlement counseling, financial education, and foreclosure mitigation and assistance programs comprised of individual counseling and a diversion program held in civil court. HCP is directed by Ms. Susan Gottwald, with the assistance of three full-time Housing Counselors and an Administrative Assistant. HCP offers the following services: 
Home Ownership Counseling Program (HOCP)
HCP has been operating HOCP for over 30 years. HOCP offers counseling services and homeownership education to low- to moderate-income prospective homebuyers, enabling participants to evaluate the pros and cons of homeownership and become successful homeowners. HOCP’s mission is to expand homeownership opportunities, particularly for minorities and persons with low- to moderate-incomes; to educate prospective first-time homebuyers to enable them to make informed decisions regarding homeownership; to assist those who proceed through the process of purchasing a home and provide the resources to promote successful homeownership; and to inform the related industries about the needs of the community.
HOCP consists of three parts: first-time homebuyer seminars, pre-settlement counseling, and one-on-one pre-purchase counseling.
First-Time Homebuyer Seminars: Housing Counselors conduct seven first-time homebuyer seminars each year throughout the community, usually in areas of greatest need, such as low-income communities in the region’s urban areas. Curriculum topics include: budgeting your money; your credit report; credit establishment/re-establishment; affordability; working with your lender; mortgage terminology; mortgage process; debt-to-income ratio; mortgage loans; predatory lending; working with a realtor; home inspection; homeowners insurance; settlement day; avoiding foreclosure; and local homeownership programs. Seminars are offered in English and Spanish. Speakers for the seminars rotate into the schedule and include representatives from local lending institutions, realtors, home inspection agencies, insurance agencies, and settlement companies.
Pre-Settlement Counseling: Housing Counselors provide small group and one-on-one pre-settlement counseling sessions in English and Spanish. Pre-settlement counseling is held on a weekly basis, and both two-hour and four-hour sessions are offered. Through pre-settlement counseling, participants learn about the closing process, financial management/budgeting, predatory lending, Fair Housing laws, homeowners insurance, home inspections, title insurance, home repair/maintenance and energy conservation, and how to avoid foreclosure and review mortgage loan documents.
One-on-One Pre-Purchase Counseling: Housing Counselors provide in-person pre-purchase counseling sessions. The sessions are usually one to two hours in length and are offered on a daily basis. Individuals have the opportunity to develop a budget and action plan, review their credit report, and learn about mortgage documentation, the pre-qualification process, and mortgage programs. 
HCP leverages community partnerships to ensure the success of the HOCP and its participants. Northampton County’s First-Time Homebuyer Program provides subordinate loans to eligible first-time homebuyers subject to completion of the first-time homebuyer seminar or pre-settlement counseling. Participants using First Front Door funds for down payment and closing cost assistance can take an HCP four-hour pre-settlement counseling session. HCP involves experts from the community at the first-time homebuyer seminars, such as home inspectors, lenders, realtors, and home insurance agents. 
Speakers for the first-time homebuyer seminars rotate into the schedule and include representatives from lending institutions (such as Truist, Wells Fargo, TD Bank, Fulton Bank, ESSA Bank &amp; Trust, Embassy Bank, Firstrust, First Northern Bank &amp; Trust, Provident Bank, and Prosperity Home Mortgage); home inspection agencies (such as Second Sight Home InspectionsomH, Advantage Inspection Service, CDA Inspection Services, and Smart Home Services LLC); realtors (such as Century 21 Ramos Realty, Weichert, and Keller Williams); homeowners insurance agencies (such as State Farm Insurance, Valera Agency, and Arbor Insurance Group); and settlement companies (such as First United Land Transfer, Edge Settlement Associates, and The Settlement Company). HCP continues to form new partnerships and recruit new speakers as needed.
HCP staff members work with the HCP Advisory Board to boost program participation in an effort to reach as many potential first-time homebuyers as possible. The HCP Advisory Board includes lenders, realtors, home insurance agents, and community organizations. Outreach techniques for HCP’s services include emails, press releases, posting flyers in the community, community websites, Facebook, and speaking at and participating in community events. In addition, Advisory Board members and community agencies circulate flyers to potential first-time homebuyers. 
Financial Education &amp; Financial Counseling
The Housing Counselors teach a financial education course utilizing the Financial Education and Coaching Initiative (FECI) curriculum from the Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency (PHFA). Session topics include: starting a solid budget; maximizing earnings; taxes (earnings); how to map your money; stashing your cash (saving/investing); and how to make your money work for you. Financial education and counseling are also offered to residents of CALV’s long-term transitional housing units at Ferry Street Apartments to promote the sustainability of participants’ self-sufficiency and decrease the likelihood that participants will return to homelessness.
Foreclosure Mitigation Counseling Program
HCP began offering Foreclosure Mitigation Counseling services in 2004. Housing Counselors provide foreclosure mitigation counseling to homeowners in default of their mortgages or for whom default is imminent. Counseling consists of loan document review, budget work-up, budget verification, review of credit report, discussion of the options available to avoid foreclosure or cure the default, development of an action plan to achieve the desired option(s), and referrals to other local agencies and services. Housing Counselors assist homeowners in putting together a request to the loan servicer for loan modification, forbearance, repayment plan, or other foreclosure alternatives. In addition, Housing Counselors discuss possible mortgage modification frauds with homeowners, and any suspected cases are referred to HUD.  
HCP benefits from marketing conducted by the Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency (PHFA), HUD, and local elected representatives. PHFA provides advertising and marketing for foreclosure counseling services and identifies local housing counseling agencies, like Community Action Lehigh Valley, in its newspaper and billboard advertisements. Consumers who call PHFA or check its website are provided phone numbers for local housing counseling agencies.
Mortgage Foreclosure Diversion Program
In 2009, Community Action Lehigh Valley expanded its efforts to preserve homeownership in the Lehigh Valley by partnering with the county civil courts to launch the Mortgage Foreclosure Diversion program in Lehigh and Northampton counties. This program assists homeowners in Lehigh and Northampton counties who have received a foreclosure complaint. Those who are hoping to avoid foreclosure are “diverted” to a conciliation process in civil court. During the conciliation process, the foreclosure proceeding is stayed.  Homeowners receive foreclosure mitigation counseling (described above), and the homeowner has an opportunity, with the help of Housing Counselors and attorneys, to attempt to resolve the mortgage problems and avoid foreclosure.
Homeowners’ Emergency Mortgage Assistance Program (HEMAP)
HCP Housing Counselors assist homeowners in default who have received an ACT 91 Notice with the completion of a Homeowners’ Emergency Mortgage Assistance Program (HEMAP) application. A Housing Counselor also provides comprehensive counseling to the delinquent homeowner. When a request is approved, the Housing Counselor may meet with the homeowner to assist them in understanding the proposal and their rights and obligations. If a request is denied, the Housing Counselor may discuss other alternatives and programs available to the homeowner.
2022-2023 Program Year Outcomes 
45: The number of families who purchased a home with assistance from the Housing Counseling Program that we were able to contact. 
Conducted seven seminars, each providing eight hours of HUD-approved education, where 181 participants received certificates of completion. Three seminars were conducted in Spanish and four in English. Five seminars were held virtually, one seminar was held in Easton, and one in Bethlehem.
Provided default and delinquency counseling to 65 financially distressed homeowners through the Northampton County and Lehigh County Mortgage Foreclosure Diversion Programs and saved 34 households from foreclosure through loan modifications, repayment plans, partial claims, and applying for rescue funds. Twenty-one of those cases remained under review for potential modifications.
2023-2024 Program Year Goals and Performance Targets
GOAL 1: The Housing Counseling Program will increase homeownership, particularly for households of color and low- to moderate-income households.
Performance Targets:
A.	One hundred seventy-five (175) prospective homebuyers will improve their understanding of the complexity of home-buying and the challenges and benefits of homeownership as demonstrated by their completion of and graduation from one (1) of seven (7) seminars in Allentown, Bethlehem, Easton, and remotely (four in English, three in Spanish).  
B.	Fifty (50) prospective homebuyers will receive pre-purchase counseling, which can include understanding the mortgage process, reviewing the credit report, creating a budget and action plan, understanding predatory lending, and working toward the goal of purchasing a home. 
C.	Fifty (50) prospective homebuyers will receive pre-settlement counseling before their settlement/closing date and will purchase a home. 
GOAL 2: The Housing Counseling Program will provide an array of services that protect homeowners from losing their homes due to economic distress.
Performance Targets:
Provide loss mitigation services to forty (40) homeowners in default who have received an Act 91 Notice by assisting them in applying for the Homeowners’ Emergency Mortgage Assistance Program. 
Provide loss mitigation services to fifteen (15) financially distressed homeowners who are behind on their mortgage payments and are in danger of losing their home to foreclosure.
Provide comprehensive housing counseling services and advocacy in a court setting to prevent foreclosure for forty (40) financially distressed homeowners that have been served with an urgent notice and complaint from the Northampton County Court of Common Pleas. Sixteen (16) Northampton County homeowners receiving comprehensive housing counseling services and advocacy in a court setting to prevent foreclosure will avoid the loss of their home. 
Provide comprehensive housing counseling services and advocacy in a court setting to prevent foreclosure to forty (40) financially distressed homeowners that have been served with an urgent notice and complaint from the Lehigh County Court of Common Pleas. Sixteen (16) Lehigh County homeowners receiving comprehensive housing counseling services and advocacy in a court setting to prevent foreclosure will avoid the loss of their home. 
GOAL 3: Homebuyers and renters will become financially “literate” in order to promote the sustainability of participants’ self-sufficiency.
Performance Targets:
Provide financial counseling to ten (10) Ferry Street participants.
Provide a financial education course to ten (10) pre-purchase counseling participants, Home Ownership Savings Account participants, and/or financial counseling participants.
