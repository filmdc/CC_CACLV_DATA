PROPOSED PROJECTS FOR NPP YEAR 3 (FY 2023 -2024) (Amended) 
  
The NPP Steering Committee has identified the following priorities for implementation in YEAR THREE: 
  
• Housing Affordability and Quality  
• Public and Green Spaces  
• Communication and Connectivity  
  
From January through March 2023, the working committees of Southside’s Tomorrow met to discuss the 
development of projects for CADCB’s Neighborhood Partnership Program. With the recommendations 
from the plan’s community input process and of staff from Community Action Development Corporation 
of Bethlehem, the Steering Committee drafted a schedule of activities for the third year. In April 2023, 
Steering Committee members deliberated on these proposed activities and arrived at the following projects 
to align with funding priorities for implementation in FY 2023-2024 (NPP Year 3): 
 
HOUSING AFFORDABILITY AND QUALITY ($38,000) 
Façade Improvements ($28,000) 
Due to the large shift toward renter-occupied housing in south Bethlehem and community concerns, as well 
as data indicating a lack of quality housing stock, efforts are needed to stabilize homeownership and 
improve the housing stock. There is evidence that investment in façade improvements has a stabilizing 
impact on a neighborhood by restoring its historic architecture and enhancing property values of homes that 
have façade work and those that surround it. CADCB will complete residential façade improvements and 
efforts will be made to coordinate with Community Action Lehigh Valley’s Community Action Homes 
program to leverage funds and pair façade improvements with owner-occupied housing rehab and 
acquisition-rehab-resale for low- to moderate-income families in a targeted geographic area. 
Objective #1: By September 30, 2024, at least two (2) façade improvements will be completed in targeted 
areas of south Bethlehem. 
Festival Unbound: Most Beautiful Home, Maybe ($10,000) 
Festival UnBound will continue its focus on fostering a vibrant, healthy, equitable, and inclusive community 
through embracing and raising the voices of those often marginalized in society. Through this five-day event 
of art generation, performance, and conversation, Touchstone Theatre aims to creatively generate the 
understanding necessary to move our community forward. Festival UnBound is comprised of a series of 
varied performances and happenings. Supporting these core events is a series of auxiliary panel discussions, 
public meals, social media exchanges, surveys, workshops, and post-performance talkbacks. In this iteration 
of the program, the festival will focus on the local ramifications of the national housing crisis, engaging 
policy makers, advocates, and activists, as well as local residents in round-table discussions about issues of 
housing inequities and how to address them. 
Objective #2: By September 30, 2024, 5,000 members of the Lehigh Valley community will be engaged 
through Festival UnBound’s programming. 
Objective #3: By September 30, 2024, Festival UnBound will yield a net positive change in response to 
survey questions regarding shifting perceptions of the Bethlehem community.

Objective #4: By September 30, 2024, Festival UnBound will forge channels of communication between 
previously siloed groups within the Bethlehem community, as well as between community members and 
decision-makers (such as local government representatives and officials). 
 
PUBLIC AND GREEN SPACES ($93,267) 
Community Cleanups ($1,500) 
CADCB will host monthly community cleanups from October to November 2023 and April through 
September 2024. Each cleanup will start in a different public space and will convene volunteers to pick up 
trash and beautify the public space before expanding into the surrounding neighborhoods. In addition, 
committee members will identify one location for a "dumpster day," in which a dumpster will be provided 
to haul away significant quantities of waste that occupy public or heavily transited areas of the Southside. 
Objective #5: By September 30, 2024, at least eight (8) neighborhood cleanups will be held at public 
locations throughout the Southside. 
Objective #6: By September 30, 2024, at least 75 Southside residents will participate in neighborhood 
cleanups. 
Community Garden Improvements ($3,500) 
To enhance the programming at Martin Luther King Jr. Park, infrastructure upgrades are being implemented 
in the community garden. This year, CADB is allocating funds to install a deer fence around the garden to 
replace the aging wooden barrier. This new fence will shield the cultivated produce from deer and various 
pests. The initiative stems from regular discussions with community members active in the gardening 
program, notably with Nia Watson, Head Gardener. 
Objective #5: By September 30, 2024, one deer fence will be installed at Martin Luther King Jr. Park. 
Green Space Maintenance and Preservation ($5,000) ($6,000) 
Regular maintenance is required to ensure that the physical improvements achieve their original goals in 
the areas of neighborhood beautification and placemaking. On an annual basis, Lopes International Park 
requires grass cutting, weeding, shrub pruning and snow removal. CADCB contracted with the owner of 
the land several years ago, with the agreement that CADCB would continue to maintain the park in 
exchange for use of the land. This project will ensure regular maintenance of public spaces including parks 
and community gardens as well as engaging residents to adopt a local park or segment of the Greenway. 
Funding will purchase materials needed by residents to maintain beautification and maintenance of parks. 
Objective #7: By September 30, 2024, Lopes International Park and Tranquility Park will be maintained 
throughout the 2023-2024 program year. 
Objective #8: By September 30, 2024, at least 50 plants or shrubs will be installed. 
Objective #9: By September 30, 2024, at least two (2) community gardens in south Bethlehem will be 
expanded and improved. 
Objective #10: By September 30, 2024, at least 30 children and youth will be engaged to participate in 
enrichment activities at a community garden.

Greenway Healthy Fresh Produce ($5,000) 
The Kellyn Foundation has been serving the Southside with fresh local and non-local produce since 2016. 
Through their Mobile Market, they have provided a source of nutritious food to south Bethlehem residents 
by operating on the South Bethlehem Greenway. In the coming year, Kellyn will initiate the coordination 
of a larger South Bethlehem Community Market, engaging local vendors and community members. The 
expanded market will provide additional support for combating food insecurity and increase non-resident 
visits to the Southside, increasing foot traffic for local businesses. 
Objective #11: By September 30, 2024, an expanded South Bethlehem Community Market will be planned. 
Greenway Public Mini Meadows ($5,000) 
This project will create public gardens along selected areas of the South Bethlehem Greenway. These areas 
are currently occupied by weeds and brush that are difficult to maintain throughout the year. Installing a 
public mini meadow will beautify the space and provide a location for residents to gather and interact with 
plants and wildlife. Abundant flowers, a variety of textures, trees providing shade, and low-maintenance 
design will help to maximize the garden’s impact on the community.  
Objective #12: By September 30, 2024, one (1) mini meadow will be installed along the South Bethlehem 
Greenway. 
Parham Park Landscaping ($13,500) 
This project will provide landscaping improvements at Parham Park on Fourth Street between Taylor and 
Polk Streets. Parham Park, situated adjacent to Touchstone Theatre, serves as a connecting pathway from 
Fourth Street, a main thoroughfare, to the Greenway's outdoor stage. The landscaping improvements aim 
to enhance the aesthetics of the park, creating an inviting and vibrant atmosphere that encourages 
pedestrians to utilize the space. By beautifying the park, the project will improve pedestrian’s sense of 
safety, increasing walkability and foot traffic. Additionally, the enhanced landscaping will help promote 
the use of the outdoor stage as a versatile venue for events, further enhancing the community's 
engagement with the park. 
Objective #13: By September 30, 2024, Parham Park will undergo landscaping improvements beautifying 
the space and increasing walkability.  
Plant Therapy is Real ($6,750) 
The “Plant Therapy is Real” program aims to promote personal wellness through the practice of plant care. 
By integrating nature and greenery into our living spaces, individuals can experience numerous benefits 
such as stress relief, anxiety reduction, and improved mood. Exposure to greenery has proven to evoke 
happiness, abundance, and relaxation. This initiative will provide plant therapy sensory spaces in three 
affordable housing communities and workshops on the benefits of plant therapy. Subsequent months will 
feature repeat plant therapy and wellness sessions, encouraging participants to embrace both indoor and 
outdoor green spaces, with added physical activities to promote mindfulness and exercise. 
Objective #14: By September 30, 2024, at least three (3) plant therapy sensory spaces will be installed in 
affordable housing communities. 
Objective #15: By September 30, 2024, at least three (3) workshops will be held to teach the benefits of 
plant therapy and green spaces. 
Saturdays in the Park ($5,000)

The Saturdays in the Park program aims to address the lack of access to quality mental health care and 
support in the community. Recognizing that the demand for mental health care exceeds the available 
infrastructure, the program focuses on prevention and strengthening protective factors for individuals 
experiencing high levels of stress, anxiety, depression, and trauma. The project organizes regular pop-ups 
in community green spaces, offering community members opportunities to reduce stress, foster 
connections, develop skills, and access community resources. 
Objective #16: By September 30, 2024, at least seven (7) pop-up workshops will be held in local parks. 
Sidewalk and Tree Mini Grants ($14,000) 
The Public and Green Spaces Mini Grants project is an initiative by Community Action Development 
Bethlehem to address the need for increased tree coverage in south Bethlehem. The program offers flexible 
funding to support various projects related to tree planting, including installation, maintenance, and 
sidewalk repair. Through the allocation of Southside Tomorrow funding for these mini-grants, residents of 
south Bethlehem can apply for small grants throughout the year to address urgent needs in their 
neighborhoods. By addressing the need for more trees and greenery, the program contributes to improving 
the cleanliness and appearance of neighborhoods, thus enhancing the overall community image. 
Community Action Development Bethlehem will provide administrative support with the City Forester's 
office to ensure this program's effectiveness. 
Objective #13: By September 30, 2024, at least 2 mini grants will be disbursed to south Bethlehem residents 
to support tree planting and other green neighborhood beautification efforts. 
Southside Ambassadors Extension ($25,000) 
This project will continue funding for the Southside Ambassadors extension area from Taylor Street to 
Hayes Street along the Fourth Street “4 Blocks International” business corridor, in addition to the north-
south side streets between Third and Fourth Streets. For years, business owners and residents complained 
about trash and leaf debris along Fourth Street, and overgrown brush on side streets. Since January 2016, 
the Southside Ambassadors have provided “clean” services to the extension area through Hayes Street. 
From January through December 2017, the Southside Ambassadors removed 29,098 pounds of trash from 
the extension area, and removed 39 instances of graffiti, made 292 contacts with business owners, and 
provided 429 instances of hospitality assistance. A community meeting was held to gather feedback and 
provide information to the extension area community about the Southside Ambassadors program, and 
resident and business owner responses were overwhelmingly positive. Since then, the Southside 
Ambassadors have invested in equipment to improve leaf and snow removal, installed cigarette butt 
containers throughout the neighborhood, worked with businesses to remove trash cans from the street, and 
taken care of pocket parks. 
Objective #17: By September 30, 2024, the Southside Ambassadors Program will maintain the 
neighborhood along Fourth Street between Taylor and Hayes streets, as well as along north-south side 
streets between Third and Fourth streets. 
Southside Community Gardening ($18,000) 
Community gardens are a widely recognized strategy for improving urban food access, engaging 
community members in collaborative projects that build community pride and preserving green spaces for 
public use and enjoyment. However, successful community gardens require ongoing maintenance and 
support from committed gardeners to function effectively. Through programming offered on a twice weekly 
basis, community gardeners work to engage community members in learning the basics of gardening while 
providing free produce to local residents. To support and expand community gardens during the 2023

growing season, funding will compensate a head gardener, as well as assist in purchasing equipment and 
supplies needed to expand activities and educational opportunities. 
Objective #18: By September 30, 2024, at least three (3) existing community gardens will be supported, 
maintained, and developed as centers for education and community engagement.  
Lynfield Community Center Garden ($8,517) 
In order to foster ownership of public green space and combat food insecurity, the Lynfield Community 
Center will refresh, plant, and tend its community and pollinator gardens. An additional composter and two 
additional pollinator garden modules will be added this year to expand the capacity for residents to connect 
with their shared green space and learn about the importance of pollinators and returning nutrients to the 
soil. Youth attending the Lynfield Community Center summer activities will connect to this public green 
space through education and activities surrounding the gardens. 
Objective #19: By September 30, 2024, one (1) vegetable and pollinator garden will be planted and 
maintained. 
 
COMMUNICATION AND CONNECTIVITY ($55,233) 
“Big Plans!” Workshop Series ($4,000) 
This project, “Big Plans!”, addresses vocational ambition and encouragement through the applied arts. The 
goal of this project is to create an alternative option to the usual lectures, stories, and video presentations 
on this subject. These events will offer a physical and personal approach to addressing the future workforce, 
one that will become an important moment in the student’s life. Just as important is the effect public 
testimony – in the form of poetry, theater, puppetry, and music – will have on the peer group audience 
attending a culminating assembly program. The question-and-answer session following the student 
performances will have a great impact on the students in the audience. 
Objective #20: By September 30, 2024, at least two (2) schools on the Southside will be engaged to host 
the Big Plans! series of workshops and public assembly program. 
Objective #21: By September 30, 2024, at least 200 students will participate in an in-school workshop. 
Change Agents: Community Pop-up Workshops ($5,000) 
Starting in August 2024, Lehigh University Art Galleries (LUAG) will present “Change Agents”, the latest 
in an ongoing series of community-centered public exhibitions. In addition to being displayed across 
campus gallery spaces, this selection of diverse images and videos will be mounted on a series of billboards

PROPOSED PROJECTS FOR NPP YEAR 3 (FY 2023 -2024) (Original) 
  
The NPP Steering Committee has identified the following priorities for implementation in YEAR THREE: 
  
• Housing Affordability and Quality  
• Public and Green Spaces  
• Communication and Connectivity  
  
From January through March 2023, the working committees of Southside’s Tomorrow met to discuss the 
development of projects for CADCB’s Neighborhood Partnership Program. With the recommendations 
from the plan’s community input process and of staff from Community Action Development Corporation 
of Bethlehem, the Steering Committee drafted a schedule of activities for the third year. In April 2023, 
Steering Committee members deliberated on these proposed activities and arrived at the following projects 
to align with funding priorities for implementation in FY 2023-2024 (NPP Year 3): 
 
HOUSING AFFORDABILITY AND QUALITY ($38,000) 
Façade Improvements ($28,000) 
Due to the large shift toward renter-occupied housing in south Bethlehem and community concerns, as well 
as data indicating a lack of quality housing stock, efforts are needed to stabilize homeownership and 
improve the housing stock. There is evidence that investment in façade improvements has a stabilizing 
impact on a neighborhood by restoring its historic architecture and enhancing property values of homes that 
have façade work and those that surround it. CADCB will complete residential façade improvements and 
efforts will be made to coordinate with Community Action Lehigh Valley’s Community Action Homes 
program to leverage funds and pair façade improvements with owner-occupied housing rehab and 
acquisition-rehab-resale for low- to moderate-income families in a targeted geographic area. 
Objective #1: By September 30, 2024, at least two (2) façade improvements will be completed in targeted 
areas of south Bethlehem. 
Festival Unbound: Most Beautiful Home, Maybe ($10,000) 
Festival UnBound will continue its focus on fostering a vibrant, healthy, equitable, and inclusive community 
through embracing and raising the voices of those often marginalized in society. Through this five-day event 
of art generation, performance, and conversation, Touchstone Theatre aims to creatively generate the 
understanding necessary to move our community forward. Festival UnBound is comprised of a series of 
varied performances and happenings. Supporting these core events is a series of auxiliary panel discussions, 
public meals, social media exchanges, surveys, workshops, and post-performance talkbacks. In this iteration 
of the program, the festival will focus on the local ramifications of the national housing crisis, engaging 
policy makers, advocates, and activists, as well as local residents in round-table discussions about issues of 
housing inequities and how to address them. 
Objective #2: By September 30, 2024, 5,000 members of the Lehigh Valley community will be engaged 
through Festival UnBound’s programming. 
Objective #3: By September 30, 2024, Festival UnBound will yield a net positive change in response to 
survey questions regarding shifting perceptions of the Bethlehem community.

Objective #4: By September 30, 2024, Festival UnBound will forge channels of communication between 
previously siloed groups within the Bethlehem community, as well as between community members and 
decision-makers (such as local government representatives and officials). 
 
PUBLIC AND GREEN SPACES ($93,267) 
Community Cleanups ($1,500) 
CADCB will host monthly community cleanups from October to November 2023 and April through 
September 2024. Each cleanup will start in a different public space and will convene volunteers to pick up 
trash and beautify the public space before expanding into the surrounding neighborhoods. In addition, 
committee members will identify one location for a "dumpster day," in which a dumpster will be provided 
to haul away significant quantities of waste that occupy public or heavily transited areas of the Southside. 
Objective #5: By September 30, 2024, at least eight (8) neighborhood cleanups will be held at public 
locations throughout the Southside. 
Objective #6: By September 30, 2024, at least 75 Southside residents will participate in neighborhood

Green Space Maintenance and Preservation ($5,000) 
Regular maintenance is required to ensure that the physical improvements achieve their original goals in 
the areas of neighborhood beautification and placemaking. On an annual basis, Lopes International Park 
requires grass cutting, weeding, shrub pruning and snow removal. CADCB contracted with the owner of 
the land several years ago, with the agreement that CADCB would continue to maintain the park in 
exchange for use of the land. This project will ensure regular maintenance of public spaces including parks 
and community gardens as well as engaging residents to adopt a local park or segment of the Greenway. 
Funding will purchase materials needed by residents to maintain beautification and maintenance of parks. 
Objective #7: By September 30, 2024, Lopes International Park and Tranquility Park will be maintained 
throughout the 2023-2024 program year. 
Objective #8: By September 30, 2024, at least 50 plants or shrubs will be installed. 
Objective #9: By September 30, 2024, at least two (2) community gardens in south Bethlehem will be 
expanded and improved. 
Objective #10: By September 30, 2024, at least 30 children and youth will be engaged to participate in 
enrichment activities at a community garden.  
Greenway Healthy Fresh Produce ($5,000) 
The Kellyn Foundation has been serving the Southside with fresh local and non-local produce since 2016. 
Through their Mobile Market, they have provided a source of nutritious food to south Bethlehem residents 
by operating on the South Bethlehem Greenway. In the coming year, Kellyn will initiate the coordination 
of a larger South Bethlehem Community Market, engaging local vendors and community members. The 
expanded market will provide additional support for combating food insecurity and increase non-resident 
visits to the Southside, increasing foot traffic for local businesses.

Objective #11: By September 30, 2024, an expanded South Bethlehem Community Market will be planned. 
Greenway Public Mini Meadows ($5,000) 
This project will create public gardens along selected areas of the South Bethlehem Greenway. These areas 
are currently occupied by weeds and brush that are difficult to maintain throughout the year. Installing a 
public mini meadow will beautify the space and provide a location for residents to gather and interact with 
plants and wildlife. Abundant flowers, a variety of textures, trees providing shade, and low-maintenance 
design will help to maximize the garden’s impact on the community.  
Objective #12: By September 30, 2024, one (1) mini meadow will be installed along the South Bethlehem 
Greenway. 
Parham Park Landscaping ($13,500) 
This project will provide landscaping improvements at Parham Park on Fourth Street between Taylor and 
Polk Streets. Parham Park, situated adjacent to Touchstone Theatre, serves as a connecting pathway from 
Fourth Street, a main thoroughfare, to the Greenway's outdoor stage. The landscaping improvements aim 
to enhance the aesthetics of the park, creating an inviting and vibrant atmosphere that encourages 
pedestrians to utilize the space. By beautifying the park, the project will improve pedestrian’s sense of 
safety, increasing walkability and foot traffic. Additionally, the enhanced landscaping will help promote 
the use of the outdoor stage as a versatile venue for events, further enhancing the community's 
engagement with the park. 
Objective #13: By September 30, 2024, Parham Park will undergo landscaping improvements beautifying 
the space and increasing walkability.  
Plant Therapy is Real ($6,750) 
The “Plant Therapy is Real” program aims to promote personal wellness through the practice of plant care. 
By integrating nature and greenery into our living spaces, individuals can experience numerous benefits 
such as stress relief, anxiety reduction, and improved mood. Exposure to greenery has proven to evoke 
happiness, abundance, and relaxation. This initiative will provide plant therapy sensory spaces in three 
affordable housing communities and workshops on the benefits of plant therapy. Subsequent months will 
feature repeat plant therapy and wellness sessions, encouraging participants to embrace both indoor and 
outdoor green spaces, with added physical activities to promote mindfulness and exercise. 
Objective #14: By September 30, 2024, at least three (3) plant therapy sensory spaces will be installed in 
affordable housing communities. 
Objective #15: By September 30, 2024, at least three (3) workshops will be held to teach the benefits of 
plant therapy and green spaces. 
Saturdays in the Park ($5,000) 
The Saturdays in the Park program aims to address the lack of access to quality mental health care and 
support in the community. Recognizing that the demand for mental health care exceeds the available 
infrastructure, the program focuses on prevention and strengthening protective factors for individuals 
experiencing high levels of stress, anxiety, depression, and trauma. The project organizes regular pop-ups 
in community green spaces, offering community members opportunities to reduce stress, foster 
connections, develop skills, and access community resources.

Objective #16: By September 30, 2024, at least seven (7) pop-up workshops will be held in local parks. 
Southside Ambassadors Extension ($25,000) 
This project will continue funding for the Southside Ambassadors extension area from Taylor Street to 
Hayes Street along the Fourth Street “4 Blocks International” business corridor, in addition to the north-
south side streets between Third and Fourth Streets. For years, business owners and residents complained 
about trash and leaf debris along Fourth Street, and overgrown brush on side streets. Since January 2016, 
the Southside Ambassadors have provided “clean” services to the extension area through Hayes Street. 
From January through December 2017, the Southside Ambassadors removed 29,098 pounds of trash from 
the extension area, and removed 39 instances of graffiti, made 292 contacts with business owners, and 
provided 429 instances of hospitality assistance. A community meeting was held to gather feedback and 
provide information to the extension area community about the Southside Ambassadors program, and 
resident and business owner responses were overwhelmingly positive. Since then, the Southside 
Ambassadors have invested in equipment to improve leaf and snow removal, installed cigarette butt 
containers throughout the neighborhood, worked with businesses to remove trash cans from the street, and 
taken care of pocket parks. 
Objective #17: By September 30, 2024, the Southside Ambassadors Program will maintain the 
neighborhood along Fourth Street between Taylor and Hayes streets, as well as along north-south side 
streets between Third and Fourth streets. 
Southside Community Gardening ($18,000) 
Community gardens are a widely recognized strategy for improving urban food access, engaging 
community members in collaborative projects that build community pride and preserving green spaces for 
public use and enjoyment. However, successful community gardens require ongoing maintenance and 
support from committed gardeners to function effectively. Through programming offered on a twice weekly 
basis, community gardeners work to engage community members in learning the basics of gardening while 
providing free produce to local residents. To support and expand community gardens during the 2023 
growing season, funding will compensate a head gardener, as well as assist in purchasing equipment and 
supplies needed to expand activities and educational opportunities. 
Objective #18: By September 30, 2024, at least three (3) existing community gardens will be supported, 
maintained, and developed as centers for education and community engagement.  
Lynfield Community Center Garden ($8,517) 
In order to foster ownership of public green space and combat food insecurity, the Lynfield Community 
Center will refresh, plant, and tend its community and pollinator gardens. An additional composter and two 
additional pollinator garden modules will be added this year to expand the capacity for residents to connect 
with their shared green space and learn about the importance of pollinators and returning nutrients to the 
soil. Youth attending the Lynfield Community Center summer activities will connect to this public green 
space through education and activities surrounding the gardens. 
Objective #19: By September 30, 2024, one (1) vegetable and pollinator garden will be planted and 
maintained.
