*Organization ProfileCommunity Action Lehigh Valley (CALV), located in Bethlehem, Pennsylvania, was incorporated in 1965 as a
501(c)(3) non-profit corporation to work with and for low-income residents of the Lehigh Valley in order to
combat poverty. CALV administers programs that meet basic social needs while encouraging self-sufficiency;
works toward the economic and community development of low-income neighborhoods; engages in community
planning and advocacy efforts prioritizing community participation; and establishes partnerships with community
organizations, local government, and the private sector. Our areas of impact include: advocacy; business start-
up and development; food access and nutrition; housing; neighborhood revitalization; and youth programs.
Under the leadership of Executive Director Dawn Godshall, over 100 employees provide services with an
operating budget of over $20 million, including four subsidiaries.
CALV has provided seminars and counseling to potential first-time homebuyers since 1992. In 2004 we began
to offer foreclosure counseling, and a mortgage foreclosure court diversion program was added in 2009. These
services are provided through our Housing Counseling Program (HCP). CALV is acknowledged as a leading
agency in its provision of services to current and potential homeowners, having been recognized by the
Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency (PHFA). CALV received a Community Partnership Award in 2014, a Best
Agency Award in 2018, and a Financial Education and Coaching Award, Funding Partnership Award, and Best
Agency Award in 2020 from PHFA for work done through HCP. Last fiscal year, 181 individuals completed a
First-Time Homebuyer Seminar, 39 prospective homebuyers received pre-purchase counseling and 41 received
pre-settlement counseling, 51 homeowners received assistance in applying to the Homeowners' Emergency
Mortgage Assistance Program, and 65 financially distressed homeowners received assistance through our
mortgage foreclosure diversion program. 
*Type of FundingProgrammatic Grant
 
*Executive Summary of Program / ProjectThe Housing Counseling Program (HCP) is a HUD- and PHFA-approved housing counseling program that offers
services to help Lehigh Valley families achieve homeownership and avoid foreclosure. It is HCP's mission to
provide low- to moderate-income individuals and families with a continuum of coordinated financial counseling
services in one location in order to encourage and assist them to build personal assets, to increase and
preserve homeownership, and to develop neighborhood wealth for the benefit of the whole community. There is
no other program in the Lehigh Valley that offers the same level of comprehensive homeownership counseling.
Research has consistently shown the long-term financial benefits to homeowners. In addition to tangible
financial benefits, homeownership brings substantial social benefits to families and communities.
Homeownership has a positive impact on the following outcomes: social stability, educational achievement, civic
participation including voting and volunteerism, and health outcomes. Moreover, homeownership has
traditionally been fundamental to community wealth. Homeownership stabilizes property values and is a fixed
asset that is very important to a family's ability to build assets and "wealth." HCP's educational services prepare
participants to be mortgage ready, avoid predatory lending, and avoid potential foreclosure on the homes they
eventually purchase.
Promoting and maintaining homeownership among low- to moderate-income households is an effective strategy
to encourage the accumulation of individual wealth, as well as an investment in a community's neighborhoods.
Homeowners move less frequently than renters and are more likely to develop strong ties to their surrounding
community. Homeowners invest financially and personally in their individual properties and neighborhoods.
Additionally, neighborhoods influence other homeowners to improve their properties, resulting in a better overall
quality of the community.

* Problem StatementMany low- to moderate-income families do not see homeownership as an option. Uncertainty about the home
buying and mortgage financing process and misperceptions about the housing market can deter potential
homebuyers from pursuing homeownership. Some obstacles potential homebuyers face include credit issues,
inadequate savings for a down payment and closing costs, and lack of education regarding the home-buying
process itself. Also, foreclosure moratoriums and forbearances have come to an end. 
* Program Activities and Implementation StrategyThe Housing Counseling Program (HCP) is a HUD- and PHFA-approved Housing Counseling agency that
provides low- to moderate-income first-time homebuyers with services to achieve homeownership and avoid
foreclosure. Through its programs and outreach activities, HCP stresses the importance of good credit and
effective budgeting and planning strategies in the home purchasing process. We teach program participants
about their rights and obligations as mortgagors and homeowners to best prepare them for homeownership.
The Homeownership Counseling Program (HOCP) offers counseling services and homeownership education to
prospective homebuyers, enabling participants to evaluate the pros and cons of homeownership and become
successful homeowners. HOCP's mission is to expand homeownership opportunities, particularly for minorities
and persons with low- to moderate-incomes; to educate prospective first-time homebuyers to enable them to
make informed decisions regarding homeownership; to assist those who proceed through the process of
purchasing a home and provide the resources to promote successful homeownership; and to inform the related
industries about the needs of the community. HOCP consists of three parts: group education; pre-settlement
counseling, and pre-purchase counseling.

HCP also provides services to homeowners who become delinquent on their home mortgage loan. The
Homeowners' Emergency Mortgage Assistance Program (HEMAP) provides foreclosure mitigation services to
homeowners of single-family homes in the Lehigh and Northampton County court systems who receive an ACT
91 Notice. The Mortgage Foreclosure Diversion Program is a mediation program in the Lehigh and Northampton
County court systems available to single-family homeowners in foreclosure. In addition to the individualized
counseling sessions, the Housing Counselor submits a workout packet to the lender's attorney and attends
conciliation hearings with the participant to advocate on their behalf. 
Timeline and Incremental MilestonesCALV operates on a July 1 - June 30 fiscal year. HCP administers the Home Ownership Counseling Program
and foreclosure prevention services year-round in an effort to meet the ongoing needs of the community.
HCP conducts seven First-Time Homebuyer Seminars a year (four in English; three in Spanish) to
accommodate our target population. They are held in January, March, May, June, September, October, and
November. Traditionally, seminars have been held in central locations, usually in areas of greatest need, such
as low-income communities in the region's urban areas, which are accessible to the target population. During
the pandemic, seminars were only held virtually. Moving forward, HCP plans to schedule both in-person and
virtual seminars each year to meet the different needs and preferences of the community. To ensure the
maximum number of participants are reached, HCP partners with other community organizations where the
target population typically seeks services. 
* Goals and Outcomes HCP's mission is to provide low- to moderate-income individuals and families with a continuum of coordinated
financial counseling services in one location to encourage and assist them to build personal assets, to increase
and preserve homeownership, and to develop neighborhood wealth for the benefit of the whole community.
Homeownership brings substantial social benefits to both families and communities. Research also
demonstrates the detriment that foreclosure can cause to individuals, families, and the broader community. As
the foreclosure moratoriums and forbearances have come to an end, we are seeing a continuous rise in the
number of foreclosure cases in the Lehigh Valley.
Each year, a Work Plan is developed with measurable goals and performance targets that guide program
activities and are designed to have a positive impact on program participants and the larger community. The
Work Plan is evaluated twice yearly to measure effectiveness of services and program outcomes.
