Organization Background
 Primary Goals and Objectives 
 
Community Action’s Racial and Ethnic Justice Program brings together a wide
diversity of people, resources, and perspectives to push back on the bigotry and
racism that is all to pervasive in our community. The overarching goal of the
Racial and Ethnic Justice program is to ensure that every person has equal
access to opportunity. Recently, we officially launched our Color Outside the
Lines initiative, a five-to-seven-year plan for closing the racial equity and wealth
gaps in the Lehigh Valley.
The mission of the Color Outside the Lines Initiative is to engage the Lehigh
Valley in conversations about race and ethnicity that eliminate barriers to
fairness and opportunity, strengthens our community, and leaves no one behind.
 
 Key Achievements 
 
Engaged 1,053 individuals across Lehigh, Northampton, and Monroe counties in
conversations, trainings, and workshops focused on cultural humility, implicit
bias, intersectionality, and racial equity.
688 people participated in Cultural Humility and Implicit Bias trainings.
299 people participated in Racial Conversations.
48 people participated in Intersectionality Workshops.

What need in the community are you trying to address with this specific
program?
 
 
Color Outside the Lines will work towards racial and ethnic equity by addressing
the symptoms of the historical exclusion of Black, Indigenous, Peoples of Color,
and all other underserved communities. Community Action Lehigh Valley
believes that racial and ethnic equity is realized when race can no longer be
used to predict life outcomes, and the outcomes for all groups are improved.
Thus, our collective collaboration is united behind a vision to make the Lehigh
Valley a community that celebrates its diversity through developing mechanisms
to open doors rather than close them. We are creating a plan to ensure that
everyone has equitable access to opportunity. 
The Color Outside the Lines blueprint was developed through a series of
informed community-wide conversations focusing on race, inclusion, humility,
empathy, and cooperation. These conversations involved hundreds of people
through forums, workshops, informal gatherings, community meetings, focus

 Please describe the population that will benefit from this program. 
 
The Lehigh Valley is a diverse region, and is home to over 840,000 people. In
the Lehigh Valley, racial disparities are glaringly evident when analyzing
economic data. In the Lehigh Valley metropolitan area, the poverty rate for
people who identify as white alone, non-Hispanic or Latino is 6.6%. That figure
is 18.7% for Black residents, and 24.1% for Hispanic or Latino residents. In
Allentown, Bethlehem, and Easton, the Lehigh Valley’s three most populous
cities, these figures are even more stark. In Allentown, the city with the Lehigh
Valley’s most impoverished areas, the poverty rate for white, non-Hispanic or
Latino residents is 14.9%; for Black residents, it is 23.9%; for Hispanic or Latino
residents, it is 32.3%.
Employment indicators also point to evidence of racial disparities. The
unemployment rate in the Lehigh Valley metropolitan area is 5.5%. White, non-
Hispanic or Latino residents have a lower unemployment rate at 4.5%, while
Black and Hispanic or Latino residents each have significantly higher rates at
8.8% and 8.5%, respectively. This pattern holds true in each of the cities of
Allentown, Bethlehem, and Easton: white residents have lower unemployment
rates than the city as a whole, while Black and Hispanic or Latino residents have
higher unemployment rates than the city as a whole.
Educational attainment levels are also lower for Black and Hispanic or Latino
residents in the Lehigh Valley when compared to white residents. Of white, non-
Hispanic or Latino residents, 92.6% have graduated at least high school, and
31.8% have attained a bachelor’s degree or higher. For Black residents, those
figures drop to 88% and 19.5%, respectively. For Hispanic or Latino residents,
those figures drop even further to a respective 74.9% and 13.2%. Educational
attainment levels are clear predictors of future earnings. Lehigh Valley residents
