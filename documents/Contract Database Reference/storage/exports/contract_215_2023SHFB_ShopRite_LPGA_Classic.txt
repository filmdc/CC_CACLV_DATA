Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast Pennsylvania  	1 
 
 
Fresh Food Distribution Program Description 
2023 ShopRite LPGA Classic Grant Opportunity 
 
Access to nutritious food for under-resourced populations is an immediate need. Nationally, the 
emergency food network is serving historic volumes of people. Current inflation, coupled with the close 
of federally-funded COVID-related supplemental programs, is causing many families to visit food pantries 
for the first time. Often, the only fresh, healthy foods a family facing food insecurity has access to come 
from the emergency food network.   
 
The mission of the Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast Pennsylvania is “to 
obtain food and distribute it to people in need through area nonprofits, and to provide resources for 
education and advocacy to end hunger.” We distribute nutritious food, break barriers to create access, 
provide nutrition education, advocate to end food insecurity, and work to attain food justice. Second 
Harvest respects and preserves the dignity of every person served.  
 
Second Harvest, a member of Feeding America – the nation’s largest charitable food network, serves 
Lehigh, Northampton, Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne counties. Annually, Second Harvest distributes 
more than 11 million pounds of food to our network of 200+ nonprofit agencies, (food pantries, schools, 
meal centers, shelters, childcare agencies, addiction treatment programs). Last year, 41% of this food was 
fresh produce, perishable protein, and fresh dairy. 1.9 million pounds of food was rescued from ending 
up in a landfill.  
 
Currently, the Second Harvest network serves more than 100,000 people each month. We anticipate the 
number of people in need of food will continue to rise. And, we will continue to provide the fresh, healthy, 
and culturally relevant foods families need to thrive.  
 
The Need(s) To Be Addressed 
Access to nutritious food is an immediate need. According to Feeding America’s Map the Meal Gap 2020 
(the most recent year for which data is available), the food insecurity rate for Second Harvest’s service 
area is 10.9% (102,790 people). The rate is higher for children, at 16.8% (34,850 children). Populations 
facing food insecurity are strongly correlated with those that have been impacted by systemic racism and 
economic exploitation. The food insecurity rate across the Second Harvest service area is higher for Black 
(16%) and Hispanic (20%) individuals, as compared to their white counterparts (7%).

A pre-pandemic study found that following the USDA’s MyPlate Dietary Guidelines would cost a family of 
four between $1,000-$1,200 a month ($12,000-$14,400 annually) depending on the age of the family 
members. For a comparison, the average middle-income family in the United States spends roughly 
$6,224 on food each year with the average lower-income family spending even less at roughly $3,862 per 
year. Current inflation is having a devastating impact. According to the USDA, the cost of groceries rose 
11.8% from December 2021 to December 2022. The cost of specific items rose even higher – the price of 
eggs increased by 60%. Eating healthy food consistently is simply out of reach for many families. 
The Second Harvest network of member agencies continues to serve historic volumes of people – 
currently 100,000+ each month. Second Harvest plays an integral role in ensuring all people have access 
to the healthy foods needed to thrive.  
 
Activities/Services and Program Guidelines 
Annually, Second Harvest distributes more than 11 million pounds of food to our network of 200+ 
nonprofit agencies. Last year, 41% of this food was fresh produce, perishable protein, and fresh dairy. 
 
Our focus is always on providing fresh, nutritious, and culturally relevant foods. In 2021, Second Harvest 
developed a Food and Nutrition Policy and began implementing Healthy Eating Research (HER) Guidelines 
to inform food sourcing priorities. HER is a project of the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation and has been 
adopted by Feeding America as guiding principle. Since the onset of implementation, SHFB has made 
significant strides in improving the nutritional content of our inventory of food, including food that is 
distributed to our network and food that Second Harvest provides directly through community programs.   
In addition to food distribution to our network, Second Harvest operates several programs aimed at 
improving the health of our community and working toward a more just and equitable local food system. 
We administer the Pennsylvania Senior Food Box Program (PASB). PASB works to improve the health of 
low-income seniors by supplementing their diets with shelf-stable nutritious foods. We supplement the 
boxes with healthy, fresh items when possible. Currently, Second Harvest provides senior food boxes to 
nearly 2,250 seniors across 76 sites each month. 
 
Our MilitaryShare program works “to serve those who served us” by providing access to nutritious food, 
with a goal of improving health outcomes and easing financial strain among low-income active duty, 
reserve, and retired military members and their families. MilitaryShare provides 500 shares of fresh and 
shelf-stable food each month.   
 
We also offer a dynamic community-based nutrition education program, which includes the provision of 
Share Our Strength’s Cooking Matters® program. The curriculum provides free cooking classes for adults 
and children. Participants learn about cooking, meal prep, grocery shopping, food budgeting, and 
nutrition.

Population To Be Served 
Our target population is low-income individuals and families in need of food who reside within Second 
Harvest’s service area: Carbon, Lehigh, Monroe, Northampton, Pike, Wayne counties. 
 
 
Project/Program Timeline  
Second Harvest operates on a July 1-June 30 fiscal year. We distribute fresh foods to our network of 
member agencies year-round.  
 
 
Evaluation Criteria 
Evaluation is a crucial part of our programming - measurable outcomes make it possible to gauge what is 
working and what needs to be modified to successfully serve our community. 
 
Evaluation of all Community Action programs, including Second Harvest, begins with recordkeeping and 
data collection. Annually, Second Harvest develops a work plan with measurable performance targets that 
guide the activities of our programs. Targets are monitored and evaluated by the Community Action Board 
of Directors. Progress is monitored at the six-month mark, and all performance targets are fully evaluated 
at the end of the program year.  
 
Additionally, Second Harvest solicits feedback from its partner agencies and food pantry participants, in 
the form of formal surveys, conversational feedback, and committee work. The results and feedback guide 
Second Harvest’s strategic planning, program development, and service provision. We are working toward 
a more participant-centric model of operation. We are taking the necessary, often tough, steps to shift 
power to the people we serve.
