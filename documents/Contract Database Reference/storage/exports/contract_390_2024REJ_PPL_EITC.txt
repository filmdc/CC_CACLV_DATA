Mission Statement and
History
Community Action Lehigh Valley, located in Bethlehem,
Pennsylvania, is a non-profit corporation that works to
combat poverty through its mission to "improve the quality
of life in the Lehigh Valley by building a community in which
all people have access to economic opportunity, the ability
to pursue that opportunity, and a voice in the decisions that
affect their lives." The history of Community Action Lehigh
Valley started in 1964, with the formation of a steering
committee of the first Economic Action Program with an
annual budget of $34,535 in Bethlehem. On December 20,
1965, when Sargent Shriver led the Office of Economic
Opportunity and launched the nationwide Community
Action Program, Community Action Lehigh Valley was
officially incorporated as a 501(c)(3) non-profit corporation.
Today, Community Action Lehigh Valley has an operating
budget of $ 20,648,330, including four subsidiaries.
Community Action Lehigh Valley administers programs that
meet basic social needs while encouraging self-sufficiency;
works toward the economic and community development
of low-income neighborhoods; engages in community
planning and advocacy efforts prioritizing community
participation; and establishes partnerships with community
organizations, local government, and the private sector.
Community Action Lehigh Valley offers community service
programs that offer assistance with homelessness, food
insecurity, homeownership, foreclosure prevention,
financial literacy, weatherization services, and youth-
centered programs that work to empower young women

and provide support to first-generation college students.
Community Action Lehigh Valley also offers community and
economic development programs that focus on affordable
housing, community empowerment, and small business
development that includes technical assistance to
prospective and existing entrepreneurs as well as
microloans through our CDFI subsidiary corporation.

*Program TitleBridging the College Admissions Gap

Request Summary Community Action Lehigh Valley is respectfully requesting
PPL Electric Utilities support for the Bridging the College
Admissions Gap programs. Each of these programs strive to
educate and empower underserved youth to reach their
potential.
SHE (Self-Esteem, Health, Education) is an educational
empowerment program that assists middle and elementary
school girls in developing life skills that promote personal
well-being, healthy relationships, resilience, teamwork,
leadership, and academic success.
Generation Next is a college and career readiness, access,
and completion program. The program works with
historically underserved high school students to achieve
college admission, integrate into college life, and complete
a post-secondary program.
The target population of each of these programs is low-
income, first-generation, students of color.

Desired ResultsThe primary goals of Generation Next and SHE as listed in
the 24-25 Annual Work Plan are as follows:
Generation Next
Increase the number of college/university acceptances
received by Generation Next students.
Increase student's self-awareness of personality traits,
strengths, and interests that may inform their career paths.
Increase exposure to different career paths and tools
needed for success.
Increase Generation Next students understanding of
financial literacy in the context of higher education and
adult life.
SHE
Promote positive self-esteem and self-efficacy amongst the
SHE students.
Promote positive self-image as well as general health and
hygiene practices to ensure overall well-being and self-care
among SHE students.
Increase educational opportunities amongst SHE students.
Foster community service, civic engagement, and
leadership skills.  
Targets and MilestonesGeneration Next Performance Targets:
50 Generation Next students will receive free SAT prep
courses.
At least 75% of juniors participating in the Generation Next
program will apply to a college or university.

At least 75% of Generation Next students will complete a
self-assessment.
Program staff will develop 1 comprehensive document
outlining the details of new curriculum.
Host at least 10 career exploration sessions with diverse
speakers.
At least 75% of Generation Next students create and/or
optimize their LinkedIn profiles.
At least 75% of Generation Next students will attend one

At least 75% of Generation Next students will attend one

SHE Performance Targets:
Provide at least 4 lessons or activities focusing on personal

Provide at least 4 lessons on health and hygiene practices
to the SHE students.
Invite one community partner or guest speaker to discuss a
health and hygiene related topics.
Provide at least 4 lessons on mental health topics to the
SHE students.
Provide at least 4 STEAM-related activities to the SHE
students throughout the program year.
Host at least 10 career exploration sessions with diverse
speakers.
At least 50% of SHE students will attend one session on
financial literacy topics.
At least 50% of SHE students will attend one session on

At least 4 SHE students will participate in one public

At least 10 SHE students will participate in one community

Measurement of SuccessAgency-wide, evaluation and measurement of success
begins with Community Action Lehigh Valley's Strategic Plan
that defines Community Action Lehigh Valley's mission,
values, strategic assumptions, strategic direction, and five-
year strategy for mission implementation. To ensure that
Community Action Lehigh Valley's strategic plan is carried
out, the agency develops an Annual Work Plan for each
agency program. Community Action Lehigh Valley's Annual
Work Plan for Generation Next and SHE consists of
measurable goals and performance targets that guide the
services and activities. The Work Plan is evaluated twice
yearly to measure the effectiveness of services and
program outcomes. This evaluation is conducted by the
Program Development and Evaluation Committee of
Community Action Lehigh Valley's Board of Directors and is
then approved by Community Action Lehigh Valley's full
Board of Directors. Program directors are responsible for
the on-going recordkeeping and data collection, which
enables us to track our progress during each program year
and afterward.  
People ServedIn order to be admitted to the Generation Next or SHE
program, students need to meet two of the following three
criteria: be a prospective first-generation college graduate,
identify as a member of a historically marginalized racial or
ethnic group, and/or be eligible for free or reduced-cost
lunch.
In the 2024-2025 school year, Generation Next and SHE will
serve 290 and 60 students, respectively. 
Staff Expertise Winslow Mason serves as the Program Director for the
Generation Next and SHE programs. He provides staff
support for projects that address racial and ethnic
disparities. Mr. Mason has a Bachelor's degree in
Journalism and Communications and a Master of Science in
Organizational Development. Staff members are

responsible for the development and delivery of the
Generation Next curriculum.
Amanda Starner serves as the Program Coordinator for the
Generation Next program. She has Bachelor of Arts in
Secondary Education and seven years of experience
teaching English at the high school level.
Olga Torres serves as the SHE Program Coordinator with
the responsibility of implementing and evaluating the SHE
program. She has over 20 years of experience working with
family youth, including areas of family services, behavioral
health, and trauma-informed systems of care.  
Budgetary InformationThe total budget for the Generation Next and SHE
programs for FY23-24 totals $411,623. The breakdown of
revenue sources includes the following: Individual/Small
Business Contributions ($152,123), Corporate Business
Grants ($112,500), Foundation/ Trust Grants ($50,000) and
Program Service Fees ($97,000).
Funding from PPL Electric Utilities will be used solely for
direct program costs related to the Generation Next and
SHE programs and would represent 9% of our anticipated
corporate/business revenue. For FY24-25, the Generation
Next and SHE programs are anticipated to account for less
than 2% of the agency's overall budget. 
Program ContinuityCommunity Action Lehigh Valley employs a multi-pronged
approach to sustainability and fund development. Both
Generation Next and SHE are sustained by a multi-faceted
development strategy that includes funding from
corporations, foundations, government entities, and
individual donors. CALV's Executive Director is responsible
for personal solicitation of funds. Community Action's
Planning Department of five staff carry out fund
development activities for the organization, primarily in the
form of grant-writing and contract compliance. In addition,
Community Action's Fund Development team is responsible
for the private fund-raising activities. Year after year,
Community Action has continued to expand its services,
grow its budget, and add staff to fulfill the needs of each
program.  
Program Promotion Efforts	Throughout the school year the director of the Generation
Next and SHE programs provides Community Action's

Marketing and IT departments with material to disseminate
on the programs' Facebook and Instagram pages. Posts
have included updates about fieldtrips, events, and
classroom activities.
Additionally, Community Action issues an Annual Report
each year highlighting the accomplishments of each of its
programs. The Annual Report is then distributed to the
community after being released at the Annual Meeting
event. This event is highly regarded in the Lehigh Valley and
is attended by over 100 community members, stakeholders
and funders. During the event, prominent speakers discuss
the accomplishments of Community Action from the past
year and new initiatives for the following year are revealed.
Community Action Lehigh Valley is open to discussing with
PPL how we can promote PPL's support of these programs,
should PPL provide funding. 
PPL Employee InvolvementPPL employees have several avenues for involvement in
both the Generation Next and SHE programs. Firstly, they
can volunteer as chaperones during college visits. By
accompanying students on these trips, PPL employees
provide valuable guidance, helping to broaden students'
horizons and expose them to higher education
opportunities.
Additionally, PPL employees can share their knowledge and
experiences by volunteering as guest speakers. As guest
speakers, they have the opportunity to inspire and educate
students by discussing their career paths. This allows them
to provide valuable insights into various professions and
industries, empowering students to explore different career
options and make informed decisions about their futures.
For those interested in more sustained involvement, PPL
employees can participate in the Generation Next or SHE
Advisory Board. This board is composed of dedicated
community leaders who meet regularly to provide guidance
for the program's direction.
