Second Harvest Food Bank of Lehigh Valley and Northeast PA 
School Pantry Program Description 
Wawa Fly Beyond Program 
About Second Harvest: 
Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast PA is a program of Community 
Action Lehigh Valley, the region’s leading anti-poverty organization, and a member of Feeding 
America – the nation’s largest charitable food network. Second Harvest serves Lehigh, 
Northampton, Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne counties.  
Last year, Second Harvest distributed 14.7 million pounds of food to our network of 200+ 
nonprofit agencies (food pantries, schools, meal centers, shelters, childcare agencies, senior 
centers, veterans organizations), a 44% increase from the previous year. Included in this total is 
6.7 million pounds of fresh produce, perishable protein, and fresh dairy products (46% of total 
distribution). Last year, we distributed more than 3 million pounds of fresh produce.  
On average, the Second Harvest network serves more than 90,000 people each month - a 52% 
increase from last year. The pantries within the Second Harvest network are operated almost solely 
through the work of community volunteers. Our food distribution is a model of neighbors helping 
neighbors. 
School Pantry Program: 
Second Harvest works to ensure all children have access to healthy foods so they can succeed and 
thrive in school and their communities. Across our six-county service region, 12% of children 
(25,470) face food insecurity – a rate on-par with the national average. Those rates can be much 
higher when drilling down to the community-level. Our efforts to combat child hunger involve 
many community partnerships and we focus on providing nutritious food, cooking and nutrition 
education, and connecting children to other resources.  
So far this calendar year (2024), we are providing food to an average of 23,188 children per month 
– a 5% increase over the same period in 2023. This reflects the challenging post-COVID economic 
landscape, but our efforts to locate food pantries in schools that provide additional resources and 
support to children and families in need have increased our reach substantially.  
Second Harvest has a sharp focus on building out our school pantry program. Many of our 
traditional food pantries operated by faith-based organizations are sustained by an aging volunteer 
network, and future service provision lies in question. Establishing food pantries associated with 
schools allows us to provide sustainability to our charitable food network, reduces stigma, and 
ultimately allows us to reach more children and families.  
Presently, there are seven school pantry sites within the Second Harvest network of agencies, with 
two school sites currently in the onboarding phase, set to open within the coming months.

2 
 
Funding through Wawa’s Fly Beyond Program will allow us to sustain our school pantry 
distribution costs and will supplement our food purchasing budget to specifically source kid-
friendly fruits and veggies that are easy to consume with minimal preparation.   
Dedicated Personnel: 
Our Agency Relations Team coordinates our school pantry program. Our Agency Relations Team 
consists of two Agency Relations Coordinators, with our Community Services Manager providing 
oversight. Our Agency Relations Coordinators serve as the liaison between the Second Harvest 
network of agencies (including schools) and Second Harvest. Our Coordinators support our agency 
partners to ensure that they can provide food to their constituents with dignity, safely, and 
efficiently.  
Produce sourcing: 
Last year, Second Harvest distributed more than 3 million pounds of fresh produce. Our food 
sourcing team consists of two staff members who solicit food donations from donors and 
coordinate food purchases from vendors and producers. Produce often arrives as unscheduled 
donations resulting from logistic challenges at local warehouses and retail stores. We have also 
built strong partnerships and receive consistent donations through various local outlets. One 
example includes Bowery Farming, a local indoor vertical and hydroponic producer of greens and 
berries.  
Second Harvest participates in USDA’s The Emergency Food Assistance Program (TEFAP), which 
provides commodities to food distribution agencies (usually food banks) through state offices. 
Second Harvest receives fresh, refrigerated, and frozen fruits and vegetables via TEFAP.  
Additionally, Second Harvest participates in the Mid-Atlantic Regional Cooperative (MARC), a 
member-led regional produce cooperative of Feeding America Food Banks serving the states from 
Maine to Virginia. The MARC provides an outlet for surplus product from farmers, wholesalers, 
and importers, aggregates the product, and distributes it to food banks. This enables Second 
Harvest to receive 5-10 different commodities per load, which enhances variety of our inventory 
and our ability to offer culturally-relevant foods. 
Donations, TEFAP commodity, and food purchase through the MARC help supplement our 
distribution; however, much of our produce is sourced directly from local growers and producers 
utilizing federal and state funding programs. The Local Food Purchase Assistance Cooperative 
Agreement Program (LFPA) is a federal program, the purpose of which is to maintain and improve 
food and agricultural supply chain resiliency. This program enables Second Harvest to link 
agencies directly to farms to receive locally grown produce.  
The Pennsylvania Agricultural Surplus System (PASS) is a program of Pennsylvania Department 
of Agriculture. Since 2010, PASS has helped to support Pennsylvania’s agricultural industry 
statewide – making connections between production agriculture and the charitable food system. 
Utilizing PASS funds, Second Harvest can purchase fresh foods in bulk directly from local

growers. The PASS and LFPA programs have dramatically increased access to fresh, nutritious 
foods for people facing food insecurity.

Fly Beyond Key Issue Grant 
 
Background 
In 2023, Wawa partnered with Second Harvest Food Bank of Central Florida to unveil a 
$1 million pilot aimed at reducing hunger with increased access to fresh, nutritious 
options. The pilot increased the amount of fresh produce distributed in the Summer Food 
Service and School Market programs and reached 53,000 households experiencing food 
insecurity. The program name “Fly Beyond” emphasizes the difference that good, 
nutritious food can make in building healthy habits, supporting the success of students 
and helping families thrive.  The Wawa Foundation is now expanding the program and 
partnering with Second Harvest to share results and learnings as a model for other food 
banks to inspire future programs to expand their reach and provide fresh, nutritious 
options in innovative ways. 
Criteria 
Fly Beyond Key Issue Grant will support our community by inspiring goodness through food and fighting food insecurity 
while also providing access to more nutritious and healthy meal options for youth. The Wawa Foundation invites our food 
bank partners in the markets we serve in to apply for financial grants that meet program criteria.  This program also provides 
a way to inspire, raise awareness and drive action within our community. 
 
To be considered, organizations must be invited and meet the following criteria: 
• Existing Check-Out Hunger food bank partner 
• Qualified, 501(c)(3) non-profit status 
• Providing service in Wawa’s existing market area 
• Align with programs listed below to implement or expand access to fresh, nutritious meal options with funding: 
o School Partnership Program– providing additional fresh produce and nutritious food options with school 
partnership programs, such as pantries or markets 
o School Snack Program – supporting schools or enrichment programs with nutritious options 
o Weekend Meal Programs – supporting schools, enrichment programs and expanding overall distribution 
• To participate, food banks would be required to have a combination of the following: 
o Good relationship with a produce provider 
o Standing school program already in place 
o Good relationship with schools and a dedicated staff member that solicits them 
o Capacity to deliver the produce 
• Grants can range from $10,000 to $250,000 depending on program, criteria met and reach 
 
*All other requirements of The Wawa Foundation must be met. 
 
March 1 – March 31:  Submission Process 
All submissions should include the following: 
• Description of organization’s mission, purpose and services that align with the Fly Beyond criteria 
• ‘Fly Beyond Key Issue Grant’ must be included in the proposal title for it to be considered with this program 
• Organizations should include if they would be interested in partnering on a ‘matching program’ with details on 
communication plan to inspire and engage community partners and potentially expand funding 
• Please submit by clicking here and using invitation code FB2024.

The Wawa Foundation Announces Fly Beyond® Key Issue Grants to Fight Hunger and Inspire Goodness with Food 
Check-Out Hunger Partners are Invited to Apply for Funds for Qualified Programs; Virtual Information Session: 3/11/24 
 
Wawa, PA (March 1, 2024) The Wawa Foundation today announced a new key issue grant fund named “Fly Beyond” aimed 
at providing underserved youth with increased access to more nutritious and healthier meal and snack options. Fly Beyond 
Key Issue Grants can range between $10,000 and $250,000 and are available for qualified partners with existing or new 
programs aligned with this goal.  The Wawa Foundation has earmarked more than $1 million for Fly Beyond Key Issue Grants 
as part of a set of signature initiatives to make more fresh, nutritious meals available through its food bank partners.   
 
Who Can Apply:    
Current community partner nonprofits who are part of the Feeding America food bank network and a current or future Check-
Out Hunger food bank partner of The Wawa Foundation can apply for Fly Beyond Key Issue Grants. All requests for grants 
must be designed to support new or existing programs that provide greater access to more nutritious meal and snack options 
to youth in underserved areas through school or community-based programs. 
 
Virtual Information Session on March 11 at 11:00 a.m.  
Members of The Wawa Foundation and partner Second Harvest Food Bank of Central Florida will host a live, virtual 
information session to provide more details and answer questions about programs that could qualify for Fly Beyond grants.  
Registration is required in advance and partners will receive a registration link via email. 
 
Submission Process is from March 1 – March 31 
Submissions will be accepted only at www.thewawafoundation.org. All submissions should include the following:   
• Description of organization’s mission, purpose and services that align with the Fly Beyond criteria 
• Fly Beyond Key Issue Grant must be included in the proposal title for it to be considered with this program 
• Organizations should include if they would be interested in partnering on a ‘matching program’ with details on 
communication plan to inspire and engage community partners and potentially expand funding 
 
The Wawa Foundation will be reviewing requests and announcing grant recipients in May.  
 
“The Fly Beyond key issue grant is a significant effort to fight food insecurity in our communities and will focus on programs 
emphasizing that good, nutritious food can build healthy habits, support the success of students and help them thrive,” said 
Elizabeth Simeone, President of The Wawa Foundation. “In addition to providing consistent access to nutritious and healthy 
meals for kids, we hope to inspire others, raise awareness and drive action within the communities we serve. Fly Beyond 
grants will be used to fund initiatives that have the greatest impact to reach more youth and achieve their greatest potential.”
