Please answer the following questions: 
 
1.  What is the specific mission of your agency? 
 
Located in Center City Allentown, the Sixth Street Shelter provides safe, short-term housing for 
low-income families with at least one child under the age of 18 experiencing homelessness. Our 
mission is to assist families experiencing homelessness secure safe housing through family-
centered services, in-house programming, and referrals to community resources while advocating 
that affordable housing is a human right.  
 
The mission of Community Action Lehigh Valley is to improve the quality of life in the Lehigh Valley 
and surrounding areas by building a community in which all people have access to economic 
opportunity, the ability to pursue that opportunity, and a voice in the decisions that affect their lives. 
 
 
2.  What are the funding sources for your agency? 
 
The Sixth Street Shelter is sustained by a multi-faceted development strategy and has a nearly 40-
year history of making our fundraising goals and supporting our program. The Shelter staff meet 
regularly with the development and grant writing teams to discuss funding opportunities and to 
ensure we are on target to meet our goals. We receive funding from corporations, foundations, 
faith-based organizations, local, state and federal government entities, and individual donors. 
 
 
3.  Has your agency experienced any significant change in funding (increase/decrease) in the 
past year? If so, briefly describe the effects of this change on your agency’s needs.

2 
While we received an influx of federal funding through the CARES Act in prior years due to the 
pandemic, those funding streams have now tapered off, and we continue to seek additional funds 
to fill that gap. We have also seen a decrease in private donations in recent years. At the same 
time, the community need for services and our expenses have increased, particularly given the 
lack of affordable housing and impacts of inflation, which strains our resources. 
 
 
4.  Have you seen an increase in the need for your services during the last year? Describe 
briefly.  
 
The persistent affordable housing crisis in the Lehigh Valley contributes to housing insecurity and 
homelessness across the region. The rise in rental prices and increasing requirements of landlords 
in recent years have made it more challenging to secure housing for our families. This means that 
families need additional time at the Shelter and enhanced supports to secure housing. As families 
stay longer, we are able to assist fewer families each year, though each family who enters our 
doors receives intensive case management. We also see an increased need for mental health 
support to help our families achieve greater stability. 
 
We are very grateful for community support from Asbury United Methodist Church and others, 
which is invaluable to providing for the needs of our families at the Shelter each year. We hope to 
continue our partnership with Asbury in Mission again this year. 
 
 
5.  Describe any changes to your program and/or services during 2023. Are any changes 
expected in 2024? 
 
In 2023 we expanded our supportive services for residents to help them on their path toward 
greater stability. Housing is a key social determinant of health, as it influences one’s mental and 
physical health, well-being, and quality of life. Recognizing this link between housing and health, 
we started a Benchmark Family Care Initiative, which aims to ensure that all residents have or 
obtain medical insurance and are connected to appropriate providers so that they can achieve a 
list of benchmarks related to their relevant medical, dental, and mental health care, as well as 
track financial goals. We continue to expand our educational and vocational programming at the 
Sixth Street Shelter to increase residents’ earning capacity, which will help them to remain stably 
housed when they move out of the Shelter and into permanent housing. This includes our new 
HiSET program, which enables participants to earn their high school equivalency diploma online at 
their own pace. Launched in May 2023, our HiSET program has already had its first graduate. We 
also added a Housing Innovation Specialist (HIS) to our staff in 2023 to work with our residents 
and local landlords in order to identify affordable housing options and facilitate faster placements 
for families. The HIS engages in outreach and fosters positive relationships with landlords to build 
a roster of landlords ready to rent to our families. 
 
In 2024 we plan to expand our landlord engagement efforts and continue to strengthen our 
relationships with local landlords. We recognize that landlords may view clients with a history of 
homelessness as high risk, particularly if they have a poor credit history and/or prior evictions. A 
strong tenant education program can help address landlord concerns. We are in the process of 
developing a tenant education program that would provide residents with skills and supports they 
will need to be good tenants. This program will help to create long-term tenants, which reduces 
landlord costs and serves as an incentive for landlords to work with residents who have completed

the program. We are also in the process of hiring a Case Manager Supervisor, who will provide 
oversight and leadership for our case management team as they provide supportive services and 
work to secure safe, affordable housing for our families. 
 
 
6.  What ways do you see for Asbury United Methodist Church to be in partnership with your 
agency in 2024? 
 
Contributions of time, funds, and material goods from communities of faith help the Sixth Street 
Shelter offer families the resources they need as they work toward self-sufficiency. Asbury United 
Methodist Church is among the most generous here, with both monetary support and supplies, 
including recent donations of much-needed pillows and cleaning supplies for our families. Many 
families enter the Shelter with very few belongings, and we provide them with everything they 
need for daily living, including housewares, linens and bedding, cleaning supplies, paper goods, 
and sometimes even food. The Shelter buildings are constantly in use, with each apartment 
housing up to six families per year. Maintenance expenses, both routine and unexpected, place a 
burden on our budget. We also need volunteers to assist with a variety of tasks outlined in the 
attached Volunteer Info Sheet. We would welcome volunteers from Asbury United Methodist 
Church to work in partnership with the Shelter in any capacity that fits their skills and interests.
