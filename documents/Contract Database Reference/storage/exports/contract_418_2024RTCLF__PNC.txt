*Organization Mission
The mission of the Rising Tide Community Loan Fund (RTCLF, or Rising Tide) is to identify small business and
community development credit needs that may stifle the creation of economic opportunity in low- to moderate-
income communities of the Lehigh Valley and to meet those needs with affordable credit products. 
*Organization History
RTCLF is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit corporation and a subsidiary of Community Action Lehigh Valley. Formed in 2001
and certified by the U.S. Treasury as a Community Development Financial Institution (CDFI), RTCLF was
established to provide accessible and flexible financing options for business owners whose risk factors make it
difficult to obtain funds from traditional lenders.
Since 2012, RTCLF has administered and managed the City of Bethlehem’s Small Business Loan Fund, which
provides entrepreneurs with access to up to $100,000 for property or equipment on condition that one full time
equivalent position is created for every $35,000 that is borrowed. In 2019, RTCLF began administering and
managing the Lehigh and Northampton Counties Revolving Loan Fund (LNCRLF). The LNCRLF offers small
businesses in the Lehigh Valley access to mezzanine financing for their start-up and expansion projects.
RTCLF originally served only Lehigh and Northampton Counties, but expanded its territory to also serve Carbon,
Monroe, and upper Bucks counties in 2015 in response to requests for assistance. RTCLF added a line of credit
product to provide entrepreneurs with access to $3,000 to $15,000 in working capital.
In response to the pandemic, RTCLF delivered $249 million in COVID-19 emergency grant funds as a member
of the PA CDFI network, along with 16 other community development financial institutions, to businesses across
the commonwealth. Since its formation in 2001, Rising Tide has awarded 335 loans to 274 unique businesses,
totaling $12,774,050 in disbursed loan funds. Of those loans, 195 have been awarded to women-owned
businesses, 131 have been award to minority-owned businesses, and 226 have been awarded to low-to-
moderate income entrepreneurs or to businesses located in a low-to-moderate income census tract. Rising
Tide’s loans have led to the creation of at least 423 jobs, and the retention of at least 909 jobs.

*Organization Description
Rising Tide serves prospective, start-up, and established businesses in Lehigh, Northampton, Carbon, Monroe
and upper Bucks counties by providing a full range of one-to-one business technical assistance and small
business loans to business owners whose resources and opportunities for growth are limited. Rising Tide offers
four loan products: start-up businesses are eligible to apply for up to $50,000; businesses in operation at least
one year are eligible for the Small Business Opportunity Loan Fund, which has a maximum loan amount of
$150,000; a line of credit is available in amounts from $3,000 to $15,000; and in partnership with the City of
Bethlehem, businesses located within the city limits are eligible to apply for up to $100,000 under the Bethlehem
Small Business Loan Fund.
Rising Tide also partners with the City of Allentown to help facilitate a self-forgiving loan program for
entrepreneurs committed to creating jobs in the Neighborhood Improvement Zone. The Bethlehem and
Allentown loan programs require borrowers to create jobs for low- to moderate-income people. Proceeds from a
Rising Tide loan may be used for lease-hold improvements, the purchase of equipment/machinery, marketing
costs, working capital, and inventory purchases. Rising Tide also provides technical and marketing assistance to
current and prospective entrepreneurs and business owners, both of which are tailored to meet the needs of the
individual. Technical assistance may include helping entrepreneurs develop a business plan, create financial
projections, and prepare their loan application. Marketing assistance may include website development, social
media marketing, graphic design, and branding.
Rising Tide is funded through loans, grants, and private contributions. Sources include local, state, and federal
government funds, bank grants, businesses grants, and individual donations.
 
*Organization Purpose
Rising Tide promotes economic opportunity and development in targeted neighborhoods throughout
Northampton, Lehigh, Carbon, Monroe, and upper Bucks counties. Rising Tide envisions a future with self-
sustaining community neighborhoods thriving throughout the greater Lehigh Valley. Rising Tide aims to achieve
this vision in three distinct ways. Primarily, Rising Tide empowers low-income residents to become self-
sustaining by creating their own jobs. Residents are encouraged to use their own specialized skill-set to
construct meaningful employment for themselves. Secondly, a thriving small business developed and maintained
by a low-income community resident has the potential of employing additional community members. This can
become critically important as the community begins to feel that it has a stake in the success of the business.
Finally, Rising Tide attempts to bring business to target areas of the cities where economic development is of the
utmost importance. A proliferation of strategically placed small businesses can revitalize a neighborhood and
build a strong sense of community among residents.

*Addressing PNC Foundation's Funding Priorities
Rising Tide devotes its efforts entirely to economic development in Lehigh, Northampton, Carbon, Monroe, and
upper Bucks counties with the goal of enhancing the overall quality of life in the region through neighborhood
revitalization. More specifically, Rising Tide addresses the community development aspect of economic
development by: 1) offering technical and marketing assistance to, and loan programs for, small businesses
located in low- and moderate-income areas; and 2) supporting small businesses that are started by and employ
low- and moderate-income individuals. 
*Problem to be Addressed
Rising Tide serves Lehigh, Northampton, Carbon, and Monroe counties and 21 municipalities in upper Bucks
County. This large, diverse region with a population of over one million people has a median household income
of approximately $78,597. Economic inequality is pervasive throughout the region. According to 2018-2022
American Community Survey 5-year estimates, approximately 10.4% of households in the target market are
below the poverty level. There are several census tracts with poverty rates exceeding 50% throughout the target
market.
Since the COVID-19 pandemic, small businesses have continued to face challenges that highlight the
importance of CDFI lending. The Federal Reserve Bank’s 2023 Small Business Credit Survey shows that 77% of
firms report the rising cost of goods and services as their primary financial challenge, and 29% reported credit
availability as their primary financial challenge. CDFI’s and online lenders were the only two credit sources that
received more applications from firms with a medium/high credit risk than firms with low credit risk. CDFI’s were
significantly more likely to fully approve these loans than were online lenders.
The main reasons for microenterprise loan denial are insufficient credit history and insufficient collateral. The
Federal Reserve Bank’s 2022 Small Business Credit Survey found that in the last 12 months, 52% of businesses
obtained loans in response to financial challenges. Of businesses that did not apply for assistance, 44%
responded that they did not think their business would qualify. Of “discouraged” firms that did not apply, 56%
cited weak businesses financials, and 32% cited strict lender requirements as their reason for not applying for
the funding they need.

*Project/Program Description
Rising Tide provides financing to business owners whose risk factors make it difficult to obtain funds from
traditional lenders. Rising Tide serves prospective, start-up, and established businesses in Lehigh, Northampton,
Carbon, Monroe and upper Bucks counties through loans for leasehold improvements, the purchase of
equipment/machinery, marketing costs, working capital and inventory purchases as well as one-on-one technical
assistance on business plans, marketing strategies, cash flow analysis and projections. Rising Tide also
manages the City of Bethlehem’s Small Business Loan Fund as well as the Lehigh and Northampton Revolving
Loan Fund.
As the only CDFI headquartered in the Lehigh Valley, Rising Tide has an expansive understanding of the capital
and capacity gaps facing low- to moderate-income entrepreneurs in the area and adapts financial products and
services to suit the needs of individual qualified borrowers. Other sources of loans in the target market are
typically more rigid and less responsive to the variable needs of borrowers. Many lenders are reluctant to work
with individuals with past credit problems or little collateral. Rising Tide works with current and potential
borrowers to create business operating budgets, break-even analyses and other projections. Staff works with
potential borrowers to improve credit scores. As a CDFI, at least 60% of the loans provided and at least 60% of
the total dollars loaned must go to businesses operated by LMI owners or to businesses that are located within
an LMI census area in order to maintain CDFI certification.
Rising Tide’s Loan Committee evaluates requests of a detailed business plan and financial projections
developed with assistance from staff. Successful loan applicants must be current on all existing credit obligations
but past credit problems do not necessarily disadvantage an applicant. Collateral is important, although not
always required.

*Sustainability
RTCLF anticipates increased loan applications and approvals due to a favorable economic climate as the move
further away from the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic. This will generate new interest income to strengthen
organizational sustainability as well as the ability to develop innovative products in response to emerging needs
in the target market.
By performing careful due diligence on loan applications, RTCLF further shores up program sustainability. Loans
are reviewed and rated based on policies and procedures outlined in the RTCLF lending policies. Due diligence
is ultimately the responsibility of the loan committee. Final loan decisions are made by the loan committee and
reported to the board of directors. Once loans are approved and disbursed, the director carefully monitors
repayment. When entrepreneurs fall behind, the director proactively engages with them and, if necessary,
initiates collections related to their loan collateral.
RTCLF also remains sustainable by adjusting in response to community needs. The recent expansion of
marketing and technical assistance services, including the rejuvenation of a marketing committee to serve the
business community, is just one example of how the RTCLF has developed new products and services to
provide greater opportunities for entrepreneurs. RTCLF also has strong working relationships with other small
business support providers; these relationships are leveraged to meet the diverse needs of entrepreneurs
served by RTCLF.
As a subsidiary of Community Action Lehigh Valley, RTCLF benefits from the stability of an established, larger
organization that maximizes limited resources and provides a backbone of federal funding through the
Community Services Block Grant program. Community Action also provides management, administrative, and
systems support including administrative, fiscal, human resources, fundraising and grant writing, organizational
planning, research, and program development and evaluation.

*Community Involvement History
Community Action Lehigh Valley is the leading regional advocate for low- and moderate-income people in the
Lehigh Valley and as such has actively engaged and mobilized thousands of residents in the fight against
poverty since 1965. Rising Tide has a history of actively reaching out to financial institutions, small business
owners, and prospective entrepreneurs to educate them about lending opportunities for small business launch or
expansion.
As a subsidiary of Community Action, Rising Tide formerly collaborated with two other subsidiaries—Community
Action Development Allentown (CADA) and Community Action Development Bethlehem (CADB)—in providing
marketing and technical assistance to businesses. However, under the strategic direction of Community Action’s
Executive Director, it was determined that Rising Tide would gradually assume full responsibility for provision of
these services, a transition that was formalized in July 2020. Rising Tide’s full-time staff has now grown from two
to four people, including a Marketing and Communications Coordinator. With our dramatically increased capacity,
we have expanded our outreach into the community to ensure that aspiring entrepreneurs get the help they need
to be successful.
Rising Tide still closely partners with CADA and CADB in many endeavors. CADA and CADB enhance the
success of Rising Tide both by providing development services that are beyond the capacity of Rising Tide and
by acting as an additional presence in the communities served. With offices in downtown Allentown and south
Bethlehem, prospective and existing business owners have the opportunity to access walk-in services in
locations that may be more convenient and accessible to them. 
*Fundraising Plan
Rising Tide has a strong history of raising private and public support to meet strategic goals. The Rising Tide has
received, and will continue to receive, federal funding through the CDFI Fund, Community Development Block
Grant funding administered by local municipalities, and the Community Service Block Grant program.
Community Action’s Executive Director also serves as the Executive Director of Rising Tide. Community Action’s
annual budget has grown from $500,000 to over $30 million. This experience will position Rising Tide to continue
developing partnerships with businesses and foundations throughout our target market. Current donors will
continue to be engaged with the work of the organization and this pool of supporters will continually be cultivated
and expanded.
Rising Tide raises its entire operating budget each fiscal year, less any earned income it receives through
interest payments and loan fees. Rising Tide receives fundraising services through a management contract with
Community Action which includes: identification of funding opportunities; communication with funders;
management of a funding calendar; writing and submission of grant proposals; tracking progress of funding
requests in a database; and ensuring contract compliance by assisting with contractual obligations, conducting
internal program monitoring, and assisting with external monitoring.

*Addressing PNC Foundation's Funding Priorities
Rising Tide devotes its efforts entirely to economic development in Lehigh, Northampton, Carbon, Monroe, and
upper Bucks counties with the goal of enhancing the overall quality of life in the region through neighborhood
revitalization. More specifically, Rising Tide addresses the community development aspect of economic
development by: 1) offering technical and marketing assistance to, and loan programs for, small businesses
located in low- and moderate-income areas; and 2) supporting small businesses that are started by and employ
low- and moderate-income individuals. 
*Problem to be Addressed
Rising Tide serves Lehigh, Northampton, Carbon, and Monroe counties and 21 municipalities in upper Bucks
County. This large, diverse region with a population of over one million people has a median household income
of approximately $78,597. Economic inequality is pervasive throughout the region. According to 2018-2022
American Community Survey 5-year estimates, approximately 10.4% of households in the target market are
below the poverty level. There are several census tracts with poverty rates exceeding 50% throughout the target
market.
Since the COVID-19 pandemic, small businesses have continued to face challenges that highlight the
importance of CDFI lending. The Federal Reserve Bank’s 2023 Small Business Credit Survey shows that 77% of
firms report the rising cost of goods and services as their primary financial challenge, and 29% reported credit
availability as their primary financial challenge. CDFI’s and online lenders were the only two credit sources that
received more applications from firms with a medium/high credit risk than firms with low credit risk. CDFI’s were
significantly more likely to fully approve these loans than were online lenders.
The main reasons for microenterprise loan denial are insufficient credit history and insufficient collateral. The
Federal Reserve Bank’s 2022 Small Business Credit Survey found that in the last 12 months, 52% of businesses
obtained loans in response to financial challenges. Of businesses that did not apply for assistance, 44%
responded that they did not think their business would qualify. Of “discouraged” firms that did not apply, 56%
cited weak businesses financials, and 32% cited strict lender requirements as their reason for not applying for
the funding they need.

*Project/Program Description
Rising Tide provides financing to business owners whose risk factors make it difficult to obtain funds from
traditional lenders. Rising Tide serves prospective, start-up, and established businesses in Lehigh, Northampton,
Carbon, Monroe and upper Bucks counties through loans for leasehold improvements, the purchase of
equipment/machinery, marketing costs, working capital and inventory purchases as well as one-on-one technical
assistance on business plans, marketing strategies, cash flow analysis and projections. Rising Tide also
manages the City of Bethlehem’s Small Business Loan Fund as well as the Lehigh and Northampton Revolving
Loan Fund.
As the only CDFI headquartered in the Lehigh Valley, Rising Tide has an expansive understanding of the capital
and capacity gaps facing low- to moderate-income entrepreneurs in the area and adapts financial products and
services to suit the needs of individual qualified borrowers. Other sources of loans in the target market are
typically more rigid and less responsive to the variable needs of borrowers. Many lenders are reluctant to work
with individuals with past credit problems or little collateral. Rising Tide works with current and potential
borrowers to create business operating budgets, break-even analyses and other projections. Staff works with
potential borrowers to improve credit scores. As a CDFI, at least 60% of the loans provided and at least 60% of
the total dollars loaned must go to businesses operated by LMI owners or to businesses that are located within
an LMI census area in order to maintain CDFI certification.
Rising Tide’s Loan Committee evaluates requests of a detailed business plan and financial projections
developed with assistance from staff. Successful loan applicants must be current on all existing credit obligations
but past credit problems do not necessarily disadvantage an applicant. Collateral is important, although not
always required.

*Sustainability
RTCLF anticipates increased loan applications and approvals due to a favorable economic climate as the move
further away from the impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic. This will generate new interest income to strengthen
organizational sustainability as well as the ability to develop innovative products in response to emerging needs
in the target market.
By performing careful due diligence on loan applications, RTCLF further shores up program sustainability. Loans
are reviewed and rated based on policies and procedures outlined in the RTCLF lending policies. Due diligence
is ultimately the responsibility of the loan committee. Final loan decisions are made by the loan committee and
reported to the board of directors. Once loans are approved and disbursed, the director carefully monitors
repayment. When entrepreneurs fall behind, the director proactively engages with them and, if necessary,
initiates collections related to their loan collateral.
RTCLF also remains sustainable by adjusting in response to community needs. The recent expansion of
marketing and technical assistance services, including the rejuvenation of a marketing committee to serve the
business community, is just one example of how the RTCLF has developed new products and services to
provide greater opportunities for entrepreneurs. RTCLF also has strong working relationships with other small
business support providers; these relationships are leveraged to meet the diverse needs of entrepreneurs
served by RTCLF.
As a subsidiary of Community Action Lehigh Valley, RTCLF benefits from the stability of an established, larger
organization that maximizes limited resources and provides a backbone of federal funding through the
Community Services Block Grant program. Community Action also provides management, administrative, and
systems support including administrative, fiscal, human resources, fundraising and grant writing, organizational
planning, research, and program development and evaluation.

*Community Involvement History
Community Action Lehigh Valley is the leading regional advocate for low- and moderate-income people in the
Lehigh Valley and as such has actively engaged and mobilized thousands of residents in the fight against
poverty since 1965. Rising Tide has a history of actively reaching out to financial institutions, small business
owners, and prospective entrepreneurs to educate them about lending opportunities for small business launch or
expansion.
As a subsidiary of Community Action, Rising Tide formerly collaborated with two other subsidiaries—Community
Action Development Allentown (CADA) and Community Action Development Bethlehem (CADB)—in providing
marketing and technical assistance to businesses. However, under the strategic direction of Community Action’s
Executive Director, it was determined that Rising Tide would gradually assume full responsibility for provision of
these services, a transition that was formalized in July 2020. Rising Tide’s full-time staff has now grown from two
to four people, including a Marketing and Communications Coordinator. With our dramatically increased capacity,
we have expanded our outreach into the community to ensure that aspiring entrepreneurs get the help they need
to be successful.
Rising Tide still closely partners with CADA and CADB in many endeavors. CADA and CADB enhance the
success of Rising Tide both by providing development services that are beyond the capacity of Rising Tide and
by acting as an additional presence in the communities served. With offices in downtown Allentown and south
Bethlehem, prospective and existing business owners have the opportunity to access walk-in services in
locations that may be more convenient and accessible to them. 
*Fundraising Plan
Rising Tide has a strong history of raising private and public support to meet strategic goals. The Rising Tide has
received, and will continue to receive, federal funding through the CDFI Fund, Community Development Block
Grant funding administered by local municipalities, and the Community Service Block Grant program.
Community Action’s Executive Director also serves as the Executive Director of Rising Tide. Community Action’s
annual budget has grown from $500,000 to over $30 million. This experience will position Rising Tide to continue
developing partnerships with businesses and foundations throughout our target market. Current donors will
continue to be engaged with the work of the organization and this pool of supporters will continually be cultivated
and expanded.
Rising Tide raises its entire operating budget each fiscal year, less any earned income it receives through
interest payments and loan fees. Rising Tide receives fundraising services through a management contract with
Community Action which includes: identification of funding opportunities; communication with funders;
management of a funding calendar; writing and submission of grant proposals; tracking progress of funding
requests in a database; and ensuring contract compliance by assisting with contractual obligations, conducting
internal program monitoring, and assisting with external monitoring.
