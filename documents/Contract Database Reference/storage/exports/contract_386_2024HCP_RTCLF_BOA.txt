*Detailed Grant Description
Community Action's mission is to improve the quality of life in the Lehigh Valley and surrounding areas by building a community in which all people have
access to economic opportunity, the ability to pursue that opportunity, and a voice in the decisions that affect their lives. Our programs work to combat
poverty in areas with significant demonstrated need. In the Lehigh Valley metropolitan area, the unemployment rate is 5.5%, and the poverty rate is
10.4%. These figures are notably higher in the densely populated cities. In Allentown, the unemployment rate is 9.2% and the poverty rate is 21.4%. In
Bethlehem, the unemployment rate is 5.6% and the poverty rate is 18.3%.
These economic struggles are compounded b y an inflated housing market, and low- to moderate-income residents are being squeezed out by rising
housing costs. According to a March 2024 report by RentCafe, the Lehigh Valley is the third most competitive small rental market in the USA. Figures from
the Greater Lehigh Valley Realtors for May 2024 indicate that the median sales price for a single-family home in the area is approximately $321,800, an
8% increase over the prior year. In the Lehigh Valley, 50.5% of renters, 25.5% of homeowners with a mortgage, and 17.2% of homeowners without a
mortgage are cost-burdened, meaning that they spend more than 30% of their income on housing costs (ACS 5- Year Estimates 2018-2022). There is a
critical shortage of affordable housing in the Lehigh Valley, and the issue will persist until addressed by a dedicated coalition of local governments, housing
developers, and non-profit community organizations.
The Bank of America Foundation's generous contribution will enable these three programs to continue assisting local residents in obtaining and
maintaining affordable, safe housing through the Community Action Homes initiative and the Housing Counseling Program, as well as supporting existing
and prospective entrepreneurs through the Rising Tide Community Loan Fund. 
Additional Information
The mission of the Rising Tide Community Loan Fund, a federally-certified Community Development Financial Institution (CDFI) is to identify small
business and community development credit needs that stifle the creation of economic opportunity in low- to moderate-income communities of the Lehigh
Valley and to meet those needs with affordable credit products.
The mission of the Housing Counseling Program is to provide low- to moderate-income families with a continuum of coordinated services in one location to
better meet their housing and consumer credit needs and to build individual and community wealth.
Community Action Homes is a comprehensive housing revitalization initiative that focuses resources in targeted areas of Allentown, Bethlehem,
Northampton County, and Lehigh County to undertake: acquisition/rehab/resale; owner-occupied rehab; investor-owned rehab, and owner-occupied,
investor-owned and commercial facades. These impro vements aim to enhance the health, well-being, and quality of life of residents, increase property
values, and create wealth for the residents and revenue for the cities and counties.
Program Accomplishments 2022-2023:
Community Action Homes:
Coordinated rehabilitation activities for 42 low-income households in Lehigh and Northampton counties, emphasizing roof and HV AC system replacements,
energy efficiency improvements, and mobility repairs, ensuring low-income homeowners can remain comfortable in their homes for the long term and
afford utility bills.
Completed 12 façade improvements to residential and commercial properties in Allentown, Bethlehem, and the Slate Belt.
Sold 3 completed rehabilitated homes to low-income buyers.
Managed the renovation of 3 homes anticipated for sale.
Acquired 1 home in Allentown from the Allentown Redevelopment Authority, along a key gateway route to Center City.
Continued site design and financing work for the development of 5 townhomes to be sold to low-income buyers on a high-visibility adaptive reuse site in
Glendon Borough.
Launched the Whole-Home R epairs Program for Lehigh County, managing a funding allocation of $2.7 million. This high-profile state program made
funding available for low-income homeowners to receive a variety of needed home improvements, and additionally provided funding for workforce
development.
Housing Counseling Program:
Conducted 7 seminars providing 8 hours of HUD-approved education, attended by 181 prospective homebuyers, who then received certificates of
completion.
Provided pre-purchase counseling to 39 prospective homebuyers and pre-settlement counseling to 41 prospective homebuyers.
8 active savers took part in the Home Ownership Savings Account Program.
3 participants completed a 6-hour financial education course.
51 homeowners received assistance applying to the Homeowners' Emergency Mortgage Assistance Progr am.
Provided default and delinquency counseling to 65 financially distressed homeowners.
Saved 34 households from foreclosure through loan modifications.
17 delinquent homeowners received loss mitigation services.
15 delinquent homeowners received assistance applying to the PA Homeowner Assistance Fund.
Rising Tide Community Loan Fund:
Dispersed over $1.8 million in loans.
Approved 25 loans to 24 businesses; 15 were made to women-owned businesses, 20 were made to minorit y-owned businesses, and 23 were to
businesses owned by low-to-moderate-income individuals and/or located in low-to-moderate-income census tracts.
Provided free-of-charge marketing services to over 60 businesses, including but not limited to: web design, logo design, social media assistance, and
marketing materials development.

Administered the Lehigh and Northampton Counties R evolving Loan Fund and provided a $50,000 loan to a local restaurant.

*Evaluating and Measuring Impact
Community Action Lehigh Valley's activities are driven by a board-approved strategic plan that defines Community Action Lehigh Valley 's mission, values,
strategic assumptions, strategic direction, and three-year strategy for mission implementation. To ensure that Community Action Lehigh Valley's strategic
plan is effectively carried out, the agency develops an annual work plan that consists of measurable goals and performance targets that guide the services
and activities for every one of Community Action Lehigh Valley's programs and subsidiaries. The annual work plan is evaluated twice a year by the
agency's Board of Directors to measure the effectiveness of services and program outcomes. Communit y Action Lehigh Valley staff documents services
provided using comprehensive client files and ClientTrack software. In addition, some programs utilize program-specific databases. Community Action
Lehigh Valley's small business lending arm uses a specialized database to monitor and track lending activity and housing counselors use Rx Office.
Success is also measured through contract compliance activities and monitoring conducted by Community Action Lehigh Valley's planning, compliance,
and fiscal departments. Community Action Lehigh Valley's program participants are also given the opportunity to evaluate the program to help Community
Action Lehigh Valley to evaluate the effectiveness of programs. For example, those who participate in the Home Ownership Counseling seminars are ask ed
to complete a survey. This data is analyzed and reviewed.
