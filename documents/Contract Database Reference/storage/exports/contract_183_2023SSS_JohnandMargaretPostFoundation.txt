*Project TitleSixth Street Shelter 
*Project/Program DescriptionThe Sixth Street Shelter, a program of Community Action Lehigh Valley (CALV),
helps families with children work their way out of homelessness. A 60- to 90-day
stay in the Shelter enables families to begin to stabilize their income and address
the problems that led to their homelessness. The property has 25 private
apartments, a Family Resource Center, and playground. Specifically, the Shelter
helps families set and reach their goals through intensive and effective case
management, in-house programming, and referrals to appropriate services.
Funding would help sustain the important work of the Shelter by providing general
operating funds to support all activities. Funding for general operations is
particularly important now, as federal emergency funding authorized during the
pandemic is eliminated, even though Lehigh Valley residents continue to face
barriers caused by affordable housing and rental shortages, job loss, and inflation. 
*Common Goals The Sixth Street Shelter shares a common mission with the John A. and Margaret
Post Foundation -- to improve the quality of life for people and society in general.
The Sixth Street Shelter works with families experiencing homelessness to help
them on their way to self-sufficiency. It is the only shelter in the area where entire
families of any configuration (including those with fathers and teenaged boys,
multi-generational families, and those from the LGBTQ+ community) stay together
in their own apartments. In addition to the comprehensive services provided to
Shelter residents, the Sixth Street Shelter has become a resource center for former
residents and people from the surrounding neighborhood. People regularly drop
by looking for information regarding community programs and resources, which
are always readily available.

Project Details
 
*Project/Program ObjectivesThe Sixth Street Shelter assists families experiencing
homelessness secure safe housing through family-centered
services, in-house programming, and referrals to community
resources while advocating to end homelessness and
housing insecurity in the Lehigh Valley. The property has 25
private apartments, a Family Resource Center, playground,
and full-choice food pantry for residents. A 60- to 90-day stay
in the Shelter enables families to begin to stabilize their
income and address the problems that led to their
homelessness. The families who come to the Sixth Street
Shelter do so for a variety of reasons: low-paying jobs, lack of
affordable housing, domestic violence and mental and/or
physical health crises, and lack of access to reliable
transportation, healthcare, and childcare. The Shelter
provides each family with a fully furnished apartment and
supportive services, including individualized case
management. Staff work to connect them with the necessary
resources to address their individual needs, such as financial
literacy and parenting classes, access to childcare and
healthcare, and mental health support. During their
residency, the family develops a Family Service Plan with their
caseworker, setting household, budgeting, educational, and
vocational goals and seeking suitable housing and
employment. We have also started a new Benchmark Family
Care Initiative, which aims to ensure that all residents have or
obtain medical insurance and are connected to appropriate
providers so that they can achieve a list of benchmarks
related to their relevant medical, dental, and mental health
care. Staff maintain contact with the family following their
move into permanent housing to offer additional assistance
and determine how well they are continuing to move toward
self-sufficiency. It costs approximately $3,300 per month to
provide a family with a furnished apartment, case
management, and supportive services. A $20,000 grant
would support two families each for a three-month stay at
the Sixth Street Shelter.
The ongoing impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic continue to
exacerbate the challenges our families face, as there is a
disproportionate impact on low-income members of our
community. The pandemic has also intensified the already

detrimental impacts of homelessness on children. The First
Focus Campaign for Children summarizes quite clearly,
"Studies show that housing instability and homelessness
exert long-term adverse physiological, academic, and
economic effects on children." Research from the National
Center on Family Homelessness indicates that children
experiencing homelessness are twice as likely to be food
insecure, and, according to the U.S. Department of Education,
have three times the rate of emotional and behavioral
problems. The National Alliance to End Homelessness states
that they experience more school mobility, have lower
academic performance, and are more likely to experience
separations from their families. Sixth Street Shelter works to
keep families housed together and to offer greater stability
and a supportive environment to children and their families
while they navigate the challenges they face. Homelessness is
often traumatic for children, and the Sixth Street Shelter
provides resources and services to help mitigate these
impacts and set families on a path out of homelessness. 
*Implementation PlanThe Sixth Street Shelter is a long-standing program and
operates year-round to meet the needs of local families. The
Sixth Street Shelter has an Annual Work Plan that outlines
goals and measurable objectives crafted to have a positive
impact on both the Shelter's residents and the broader
community. In order to achieve these goals, Shelter staff will
work with the families to provide intensive case management
and supportive services. During their residency at the Shelter,
families will: 1) work on the issues that contributed to their
homelessness by developing a Family Service Plan with the
Shelter's staff to set household, budgeting, educational, and
vocational goals; 2) look for suitable housing with the support
of their Case Manager; 3) learn job-seeking skills in our
Family Resource Center; 4) meet with their Case Manager
regularly to assess their progress in achieving the
benchmarks set for their medical and dental care; and 5)
attend classes and workshops provided by Shelter staff and
local partners. Topics for these sessions include healthy
relationships, parenting, health and wellness, and financial
literacy, among others. Shelter staff will also follow up with
former residents to check on their continued progress and
offer additional assistance. 
*Measuring SuccessEach year, a Work Plan is developed with measurable goals
and performance targets that guide program activities. In
order to measure effectiveness of services and outcomes, the
work plan is evaluated twice yearly by the Program
Development & Evaluation Committee of CALV's Board of
Directors and is then approved by CALV's full Board. In
addition, Sixth Street Shelter utilizes the Homeless
Management Information System (HMIS) and ClientTrack to
track and analyze participant data. The Shelter also tracks
qualitative data to document the positive long-term impact of
the Shelter in the community. Staff conduct follow-up
evaluations with former residents that can be located at 3-,
6-, and 12-month intervals to ascertain their continued
progress.
The Sixth Street Shelter has set the following performance
targets for our current fiscal year: 1) At least 100 families will
reside at the Shelter and develop Family Service Plans based
on their housing, educational and financial goals; 2) 50% of
families will save money toward rent or a security deposit
upon leaving the Shelter; 3) 75% of families will achieve their
Family Service Plan goals; 4) 100% of families will create a
written budget to understand credit and money
management as a part of their goal plan; 5) 40% of families
will move into affordable housing upon leaving.  
*CollaborationThe Sixth Street Shelter partners with many government
entities and non-profit agencies to ensure that residents
receive the services and gain the skills they will need as part
of a comprehensive plan to help them to become self-
sufficient. The Shelter has a nearly 30-year partnership with
Lehigh County Office of Children and Youth Services, and

many other local organizations contribute to programming
for our residents. For instance, ESSA Bank leads financial
literacy classes, CareerLink assists with job searches and
resume writing, and Highmark Wholecare facilitates
"Wellness Friday" sessions for both Shelter residents and
community members. The Shelter is also a member of the
Lehigh Valley Regional Homeless Advisory Board. The
LVRHAB is a consortium of representatives from City and
County governments, social service agencies, veterans
support groups, faith-based and other community
organizations, homeless advocates, homeless or formerly
homeless people, representatives from legislators, and other
interested parties with the mission to prevent and end
homelessness in the Lehigh Valley. 
*Project/Program BackgroundSince 1984, the Sixth Street Shelter, a program of Community
Action Lehigh Valley, has been offering services to help
prepare families for the challenges of a difficult job and
rental market that too often result in homelessness. It is the
only family shelter in the region that admits families of any
configuration (with at least one child under 18) and houses
them in their own private apartments. The Sixth Street
Shelter has proven to be a vital resource in the community,
and while it has been operating for almost 40 years, the
program has adapted to the changing needs of the
community. For instance, there is currently a severe shortage
of available, affordable housing units locally, and landlords
have been very selective in whom they rent to. Our
caseworkers report that they struggle to find housing for
their clients despite having rental assistance and supportive
services available. This means that residents are experiencing
longer stays in the Shelter before moving into permanent
housing. We recently added a Housing Innovation Specialist
to our staff to work with our residents and local landlords in
order to identify affordable housing options and facilitate
faster placements for families.

Project/Program TimelineContingent upon the timing of receipt of funding, we

measure the effectiveness of services and program
outcomes. This evaluation is conducted by the Program
Development & Evaluation Committee of CALV's Board of

ascertain their continued progress. All of this information is
logged for future reference in order to help the program
monitor its efficiency and community impact over time.
