*Mission StatementEstablished in 1965, Community Action Lehigh Valley (CALV)
is an anti-poverty nonprofit that strives to improve the
quality of life in Lehigh Valley and surrounding areas by
building a community in which all people have access to
economic opportunity, the ability to pursue that
opportunity, and a voice in the decisions that affect their
lives.
Community Action administers programs that aim to meet
basic social needs while encouraging self-sufficiency; works
toward the economic and community development of low-
income neighborhoods; engages in community planning
and advocacy efforts prioritizing community participation;
and establishes partnerships with community
organizations, local government, and the private sector.
Our areas of impact include advocacy, business start-up
and development, food access and nutrition, housing,
neighborhood revitalization, and youth. Key programs
include:
1. Sixth Street Shelter: Provides short- and long-term
shelter for homeless families.
2. Housing Counseling: Provides education, counseling, and
assistance regarding home ownership, predatory lending,
and mortgage foreclosure.
3. Community Action Homes: Preserves and creates,
rehabilitates, and improves the quality of affordable

housing for low-income families,
4. Weatherization: Offers solutions to home heating/cooling
problems through counseling and weatherization.
5. Second Harvest Food Bank: Offers food assistance to the
hungry.
6. The Campaign for Racial and Ethnic Justice: Promotes
inclusion, equity, and justice through community dialogue,
leadership development, relationship-building, network
creation, and increased access to resources.
7. SHE: Works with girls transitioning into middle school by
empowering them to find their own voices, be independent,
and make informed choices for their futures.
8. Slate Belt Rising: Revitalizes the Slate Belt area,
promoting and enhancing the lives of its residents.
We are committed to economic and community
development because of the belief that people have the
best chance of reaching their full potential when their
communities are places where people want to live, work,
and play. The agency's economic and community
development work is conducted by its subsidiary
corporations. Community Action Development Allentown
and Community Action Development Bethlehem work with
residents to revitalize their communities. The Rising Tide
Community Loan Fund, a federally-certified community
development financial institution, provides microloans and
technical and marketing assistance to businesses that other
organizations or businesses do not provide. Lehigh Valley
Community Land Trust increases the supply of permanent
affordable housing for low- to moderate-income
households by working with Community Action Homes.  
*Organizational HistoryCommunity Action Lehigh Valley, located in Bethlehem,
Pennsylvania, was established in December 1965 as a 501(c)
(3) non-profit corporation dedicated to addressing poverty
among low-income residents of the Lehigh Valley, with a
focus on the poorest neighborhoods.
Its programs have grown and evolved over the past 58
years and have had a strong impact on the local

community. In 1970, CALV established the first Head Start
program in the area, educating 250 students, and 17
migrant farm workers were provided with housing. In 1980,
CALV established the Low-Income Home Energy Assistance
Program, operated a 24-hour emergency hotline, and
delivered heating fuel to residents in crisis. Lehigh Valley
Food Bank began distributing food from a rented truck in
1984, and Lehigh Valley Confederation on the Homeless
launched. In the '90s, CALV opened transitional long-term
housing for families pursuing an education, and CALV's
Task Force on Economic Opportunity started supporting
minority entrepreneurs through loans. Sixth Street Shelter
expanded to assist more families, and CALV began
administering PPL's OnTrack program. Rising Tide
Community Loan Fund launched in 2000. In 2005, CALV
mobilized residents for the start of the Main Street and
Greenway projects, as well as Second Harvest's Backpack
Buddies initiative. In 2011, the Land Trust put its first home
on the market. In 2013, Sixth Street Shelter expanded to
add 12 additional apartments for families. In 2016, the
Campaign for Racial and Ethnic Justice launched, and in
2017, a neighborhood partnership program in the Slate Belt
started. CALV also developed a comprehensive housing
revitalization initiative, Community Action Homes. Attaining
CHDO (Community Housing Development Organization)
status in 2018 enabled CALV to broaden its scope from
housing repair to development. Today, Community Action
Homes (CAH) implements comprehensive housing
strategies, including Acquisition/Rehab/Resale, Owner-
occupied Housing Rehab, and Façade Improvement, to
facilitate low-to-moderate income households' access to
and retention of quality, affordable homes. In 2019, Color
Outside the Lines, a 5-7-year racial diversity and inclusion
plan, was launched to ensure that everyone in the Lehigh
Valley has equal access to opportunity.
CALV has a proven track record of service expansion,
annual revenue growth, and sound fiscal management,
consistently operating without budget deficits. Our funding
sources include fees, individual donations, philanthropic
contributions, corporate sponsorships, foundation grants,
and government support.

*Brief Project DescriptionThis housing project aims to promote homeownership,
preserve and improve the quality of affordable housing,
and increase the affordable housing stock. We focus
resources on our service areas (City of Allentown, City of
Bethlehem, Northampton County, and Lehigh County) to
undertake housing counseling, housing
acquisition/rehab/resale, owner-occupied housing
rehabilitation, and façade improvement. This enhances
housing stability, overall health, and quality of life of the
families we assist. 
*Needs to Be AddressedHousing costs have risen drastically in the Lehigh Valley
since the onset of the pandemic. The Lehigh Valley is a
desirable place to live, drawing new residents, but many
low- to moderate-income residents are being squeezed out
by rising housing costs. Figures from the Greater Lehigh
Valley Realtors for December 2023 indicate that the median
sales price for a single-family home in the area is
approximately $300,000. While the median value of owner-
occupied units in the area is below that at $258,000, 25.5%
of homeowners in the Lehigh Valley with a mortgage and
17.2% of homeowners without a mortgage are cost-
burdened, meaning that they spend more than 30% of their
income on housing costs (American Community Survey 5-
Year Estimates 2018-2022). Given the high costs of homes

locally and the currently elevated mortgage rates,
prospective first-time homebuyers can greatly benefit from
the education and support offered by CALV's Housing
Counseling Program. There is no other local program that
offers comprehensive homeownership counseling,
including a first-time homebuyer seminar, pre-purchase
and pre-settlement counseling, financial education, and
foreclosure mitigation and assistance programs comprised
of individual counseling and a diversion program held in
civil court. Older homes are a more affordable option but
also incur higher maintenance costs. Community Action
Homes assists low-income households in rehabilitating
their homes to ensure the health and safety of residents. 
*Project Goals &
Outcomes
Each year, a Work Plan is developed with measurable goals
and performance targets that guide program activities and
are designed to have a positive impact on program
participants and the larger community. The Work Plan is
evaluated twice yearly to measure effectiveness of services
and program outcomes. HCP and CAH have set the
following performance targets for FY 2023-2024:
Goal 1: Increase homeownership, particularly for
households of color and low- to moderate-income
households
Output 1: 175 prospective homebuyers will participate in
one of seven seminars; 50 will participate in pre-purchase
counseling services; 50 will receive pre-settlement
counseling.
Goal 2: Provide services that protect homeowners from
losing their homes due to economic distress
Output 1: 40 homeowners receiving an Act 91 Notice will
receive loss mitigation services and assistance in applying
for HEMAP.
Output 2: 80 homeowners facing foreclosure will receive
comprehensive housing counseling and advocacy in a court
setting.
Outcome 1: 32 homeowners facing foreclosure will avoid
the loss of their home.

Goal 3: Preserve affordable housing for low-to moderate-
income households
Output: rehabilitate 35 homes by enhancing energy
efficiency, replacing roofs and/or HVAC, removing lead and
reducing blight, and other essential repairs in Lehigh and
Northampton Counties. 
*Outcome Measurement Community Action's Strategic Plan (2024-2026) is where
agency-wide evaluation and measurement of success
begins. Our strategic plan defines our mission, values,
strategic assumptions, strategic direction, and three-year
strategy for mission implementation. In order to ensure
that Community Action's strategic plan is carried out, the
agency develops an Annual Work Plan. Community Action's
Annual Work Plan consists of measurable goals and
performance targets that guide the services and activities
for each of Community Action's programs and subsidiaries.
It is evaluated twice yearly to measure the effectiveness of
services and program outcomes. This evaluation is
conducted by the Program Development and Evaluation
Committee of Community Action's Board of Directors and is
then approved by Community Action's full Board of
Directors.
Program directors submit a monthly report of program
activities to Community Action's Executive Director. The
Executive Director is responsible for reviewing the
accomplishments of the program and presenting a monthly
report on agency activities to Community Action's Board of
Directors. The report serves the purpose of monitoring
program performance on a routine basis and ensuring that
staff activities toward achieving program objectives are on
schedule.
Success is also measured through contract compliance
activities conducted by Community Action's Fiscal, Planning,
and Compliance Departments. They ensure contractual
obligations are met.

*Beneficiary Outreach CALV's Housing Program has been serving the Lehigh Valley
community for over 30 years. Our established partnerships
with community leaders, organizations, and local
government representatives enable us to effectively reach
out to low- to moderate-income households seeking
homebuyer and housing rehabilitation resources. Referral
systems from local municipalities (Northampton County,
Lehigh County, the City of Allentown, and the City of
Bethlehem) are in place to conduct intake and assessment
processes. Outreach efforts are also coordinated with
community organizations where the target population
seeks services.

The Housing Counseling Program (HCP) conducts seven
First-Time Homebuyer Seminars a year (four in English;
three in Spanish) to accommodate our target population.
Traditionally, seminars have been held in central locations,
usually in areas of greatest need, such as low-income
communities in the region's urban areas, which are
accessible to the target population. During the pandemic,
these seminars were only held virtually. Moving forward,
HCP plans to schedule both in-person seminars and virtual
seminars each year to meet the different needs and
preferences of the community.
To reach as many potential first-time homebuyers and low-
to-moderate-income households as possible, HCP and
Community Action Home (CAH) also work with their
Advisory Boards, which consist of members from lenders,
realtors, home insurance agents, and community
organization representatives.
CALV promotes its housing programs through various
channels, including social media (website, Facebook,
Instagram), posting flyers, emails, press releases, speaking
at and participating in various community events, and
ensuring the target community has ways to reach us. To
increase accessibility, our website allows users to translate
the site to English, Spanish, Arabic, Chinese and
Portuguese. Both HCP and CAH have bilingual staff
members, ready to assist clients.

Description
At CALV, it is our duty to ensure that low- to moderate-
income families interested in homeownership are well
equipped to become homebuyers and are well supported
in maintaining their homes.
The Housing Counseling Program (HCP), a HUD- and PHFA-
approved housing counseling program of CALV, offers a
number of programs to help Lehigh Valley families achieve
homeownership and avoid foreclosure. It is HCP's mission
to provide low- to moderate-income individuals and
families with a continuum of coordinated financial
counseling services in one location in order to encourage
and assist them to build personal assets, to increase and
preserve homeownership, and to develop neighborhood
wealth for the benefit of the whole community. Through its
programs and outreach activities, HCP stresses the
importance of good credit and effective budgeting and
planning strategies in the home purchasing process. We
teach our program participants about their rights and
obligations as mortgagors and homeowners so to best
prepare them for homeownership.
Community Action Homes (CAH) assists low- to moderate-
income owner-occupied households in improving their
homes to be financially sustainable, safe, healthy, and
environmentally conscious. Starting with home
assessments, the rehabilitation work helps homeowners
bring their properties into compliance with health and
safety standards and increase comfort in the home.
Preserving affordable housing provides safety and long-
term stability for low- to moderate-income households
while, at the same time, supporting community
development. It enables the target communities to have
access to employment, education, and other opportunities
to improve their lives.
Research has consistently shown the importance of the
housing market on the economy and the long-term
financial benefits to homeowners. In addition to tangible
financial benefits, homeownership brings substantial social
benefits to families, communities, and the country.
Homeownership has a positive impact on the following
outcomes: social stability, educational achievement, civic

participation including voting and volunteerism, and health
outcomes. Moreover, homeownership has traditionally
been fundamental to community wealth. Homeownership
stabilizes property values and is a fixed asset that is very
important to a family's ability to build assets and "wealth."
HCP's educational services prepare participants to be
mortgage ready, avoid predatory lending, and avoid
potential foreclosure on the homes they eventually
purchase.
Promoting and maintaining homeownership among low- to
moderate-income households is an effective strategy to
encourage the accumulation of individual wealth, as well as
an investment in a community's neighborhoods.
Homeowners move less frequently than renters and are
more likely to develop strong ties to their surrounding
community. Homeowners invest financially and personally
in their individual properties and neighborhoods. As
property crimes affect homeowners directly, homeowners
are motivated to deter crime and implement crime-
prevention programs. Additionally, neighborhoods
influence other homeowners to improve their properties,
resulting in a better overall quality of the community.
Investing in homeownership is an investment in the entire
community.
