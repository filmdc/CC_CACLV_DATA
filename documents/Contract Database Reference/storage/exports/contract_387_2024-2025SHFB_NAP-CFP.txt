Detailed Project Description 
 
Program Information 
The mission of Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast Pennsylvania is “to obtain food and 
distribute it to people in need through area nonprofits, and to provide resources for education and advocacy to end 
hunger.” We work to eliminate food insecurity by providing nutritious food to those in need, dismantling access 
barriers, and advocating for health and racial equity. Second Harvest respects and preserves the dignity of every person 
served. Second Harvest is a program of Community Action Lehigh Valley and a member of Feeding America.  
  
In fiscal year 2024, Second Harvest is on course to distribute more than 16 million pounds of food -- or 13.3 million 
meals -- to our network of more than 200 nonprofit partner agencies (food pantries, schools, meal centers, shelters, 
childcare agencies, senior centers, veterans organizations). Included in this total is 7.3 million pounds of fresh produce, 
perishable protein, and fresh dairy products (46% of total distribution). Annually, we rescue more than 8 million 
pounds of food from ending up in a landfill.   
  
Currently, the Second Harvest network serves, on average, more than 95,000 individuals each month. Second Harvest 
will continue to provide the fresh, nutritious, and culturally relevant foods families need to thrive.   
 
A. PLACE 
Second Harvest Food Bank serves food pantries across a six-county region in eastern Pennsylvania: Lehigh, 
Northampton, Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne counties. Our service area extends across a mix of rural, suburban, 
and urban environments, including three, class 3 cities: Allentown, Bethlehem, and Easton. The region includes the 
Lehigh Valley, Pocono Mountains, and borders with New York state and New Jersey on the eastern and northern 
borders. 
Target Area 
 
As of 2020, Second Harvest’s six-county service area was home to an estimated 1,030,274 people.

B. PROBLEM 
Food Insecurity and Food Access  
The data shows a disturbing trend. Prior to the COVID-19 pandemic (2019), food insecurity across our service area 
was estimated to impact 9.7% of the population. Through the pandemic and subsequent economic crisis, those rates 
have steadily increased, and statistics reporting from our partner agencies indicate the problem is only growing.  
In the past three years, the number of people seeking food at a Second Harvest partner agency increased 115% from 
an average of 45,256 individuals per month in fiscal year 2022 to an average of 97,405 individuals per month in fiscal 
year 2024. Partner agencies saw a 103% increase in the average number of seniors seeking food during the same time 
period, and among children younger than 18 years old, there was a 111% increase in the average number who received 
charitable food.  
A pre-pandemic study found that following the USDA’s MyPlate Dietary Guidelines would cost a family of four 
between $1,000-$1,200 a month ($12,000-$14,400 annually) depending on the age of the family members. For a 
comparison, the average middle-	income family in the United States spends roughly $6,224 on food each year with the 
average lower-income family spending even less at roughly $3,862 per year. Inflation continues to have a devastating 
impact. According to USDA’s Economic Research Service, U.S. food prices rose by 25% from 2019 to 2023. Eating 
healthy food consistently is simply out of reach for too many families. More people need access to affordable fresh, 
nutritious foods.

facing food insecurity in 2022. That number represents 11.7% of the population, but examining the data for particular

Diet-Related Health Outcomes 
 
When funds are tight, one of the first things cut from a household budget is healthy food (including fresh fruit and 
vegetables, eggs, milk and other dairy, and meat), which are more expensive than their highly processed counterparts. 
Food insecurity and poverty are social determinants of health and poor health outcomes make it nearly impossible for 
people to thrive. According to the Food Research and Action Center (FRAC), “people living in or near poverty have 
disproportionately worse health outcomes and less access to health care than those who do not.” Feeding America 
reports that people facing food insecurity are “disproportionally affected by diet-sensitive chronic diseases,” including 
diabetes and high blood pressure, and are more likely to experience mental health challenges. In addition to the 
negative impact on their health, children experiencing food insecurity are more likely to struggle in school and social 
situations, both of which can have long-term consequences.  
 
Many factors are contributing to the growth in food insecurity across our region and the nation, including high housing 
costs and the impacts of inflation on the cost of basic goods and services. Healthy food is expensive. Families visit 
our pantries to bridge the gap between what is needed and what can be afforded at the grocery store. By not having to 
spend as much on groceries, families can allocate those dollars to other expenses, such as rent, utilities, childcare, or 
medication. Eliminating the need to spend money on groceries might mean that a family can keep a roof over their 
head or keep the heat and electricity on. Second Harvest will remain poised to meet the rising demand.

C. PROJECT 
Scope of Work, Activities, and Timeline 
 
Second Harvest Food Bank operates on a July 1 – June 30 fiscal year and distributes food and provides programming 
and services year-round. Second Harvest is a member of Feeding America – the nation’s largest charitable food 
network. Second Harvest serves Lehigh, Northampton, Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne counties. Last year, Second 
Harvest distributed 14.7 million pounds of food to our network of 200+ nonprofit agencies (food pantries, schools, 
meal centers, shelters, childcare agencies, senior centers, veterans organizations), a 44% increase from the previous 
year. Included in this total is 6.7 million pounds of fresh produce, perishable protein, and fresh dairy products (46% 
of total distribution). Annually, we rescue more than 8 million pounds of food from ending up in a landfill.   
  
On average, the Second Harvest network serves more than 95,000 people each month. The pantries within the Second 
Harvest network are operated almost solely through the work of community volunteers. Our food distribution is a 
model of neighbors helping neighbors.   
 
In addition to food distribution to our network, Second Harvest operates several programs aimed at improving the 
health of our community and working toward a more just and equitable local food system. We administer the 
Pennsylvania Senior Food Box Program (PASB). PASB works to improve the health of low	-income seniors by 
supplementing their diets with shelf-stable nutritious foods. Eligible participants include low-income individuals who 
are at least 60 years old and whose household income is at or below 130% of the U.S. poverty level. We work to 
supplement boxes with healthy, fresh items when possible. Currently, Second Harvest provides senior food boxes to

Partnerships 
 	Second Harvest works closely with local government, businesses, community organizations, and leaders to implement 
our food distribution and service provision. 	Our primary partners are the 200 nonprofits that make up our charitable 
food network. They are unequivocally the backbone of our operation and make it possible for us to reach over 95,000 
people each month.  
As a member of the Feeding America network, we gain access to resources that help support our daily operations and 
guide best practices. Similarly, our membership with Feeding Pennsylvania (a consortium of nine Feeding America-
affiliated food banks across the state) has been crucial to our program development and service provision efforts. We 
further benefit from our affiliation with Feeding Pennsylvania in that we purchase food through their Mid-	Atlantic 
Regional Cooperative (MARC) initiative, a regional produce distribution system. Additionally, we have strong 
partnerships with many local farms that result in our ability to purchase fresh, local products at reasonable prices. 
 
Organizational Capacity and Sustainability of the Organization 
Second Harvest, a program of Community Action Lehigh Valley, began its mission to end food insecurity in 1982, as 
the Lehigh Valley Food Bank. Initially, distributions were made from the back of a rented truck to a network of about 
15 nonprofit agencies. Since September 2015, Second Harvest Food Bank has been operating from a 65,000 square-
foot warehouse in East Allen Township, which includes 96,000 cubic feet of space for refrigerated and frozen foods. 
We have distributed food to meet the needs of the community for more than 40 years. There has never been a pause 
in our distribution efforts, even during the height of the COVID-19 pandemic.

and are designed to have a positive impact on the community and the people we serve. Data on the number of people 
served through our partner network are reported by the agencies on a monthly basis and aggregated in our inventory 
software, Primarius 2. Reports of pounds sourced and distributed are also generated through Primarius 2. We track a 
number of data points through this system, including specific poundage for specific food types, such as fresh produce, 
perishable proteins, and dairy.
