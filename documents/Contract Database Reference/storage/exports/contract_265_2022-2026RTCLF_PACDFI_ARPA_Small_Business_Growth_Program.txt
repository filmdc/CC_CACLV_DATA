Background Overview 
 
The Pennsylvania CDFI Network received a grant of $4 million of American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds 
under the State and Local Fiscal Recovery Funds (SLFRF) program through the Pennsylvania Department 
of Community and Economic Development (DCED).  These funds are being used for the Small Business 
Growth Program. 
 
These grant funds can be used for grants, loans, or technical assistance for small businesses or nonprofit 
organizations. 
 
The Network allocated the funds as follows to each CDFI: 
 
$184,000 for lending or grants to small businesses or nonprofits 
     48,941 for technical assistance to small businesses or nonprofits 
$232,941 Total to each CDFI 
 
The entire budget is as follows:  
 
               $3,959,997  (Grants to 17 CDFIs) 
                     40,003   (Network Administration) 
               $4,000,000  Grant Total 
 
Each CDFI is expected to assist at least 5 businesses with loans/grants and at least 30 businesses with 
technical assistance with these funds. 
 
This document outlines the primary requirements imposed by the federal SLFRF to guide CDFIs in 
designing and describing their project. 
 
 
Eligible Businesses (Impacted) 
 
Small businesses with less than 500 employees or, if applicable, the size established by the SBA 
Administrator for the industry.  Eligible businesses must have been impacted, which can be 
demonstrated as experiencing financial hardship such as declines in revenues, increased costs, financial 
insecurity, or periods of business closure. 
 
CDFIs must maintain documentation or records to support an assessment of how businesses receiving 
assistance were affected by the negative economic impacts of the pandemic and how the funds will be 
used to mitigate these impacts. The Treasury Department has not issued guidance or procedures on 
documentation of how funds will mitigate the negative economic impact.  The federal reporting only 
requires that CDFIs identify the number of businesses and amounts expended on impacted businesses.

Eligible Businesses (Disproportionally Impacted) 
 
Small businesses (also less than 500 employees or meeting SBA requirement for selected industries) 
operating in a Qualified Census Tract are presumed to be disproportionally impacted.  Qualified Census 
Tracts (QCTs) are the same as QCTs defined for the federal Low Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC).  Here 
is a link to determine where QCTs are located:  https://www.huduser.gov/portal/sadda/sadda_qct.html  
 
This group of eligible business is different in that the business does not need to demonstrate impacts 
and may be a startup firm that was not in operation prior to the pandemic (Feb 2020). 
 
CDFIs must maintain documentation or records to support an assessment that businesses operate in a 
QCT. There is no guidance on how to document that a business is located in a QCT.  The federal 
reporting only requires that CDFIs identify the number of businesses and amounts expended on 
disproportionately impacted businesses. 
 
(NOTE:  QCTs use a different definition of income levels than the SLFRF definition of Low and Moderate 
Income.) 
 
 
Eligible Uses  
 
• Funds may be used for loans or grants to mitigate financial hardship such as declines in revenues 
or impacts of periods of business closure. Specific examples identified by U.S. Treasury 
Department include assistance such as supporting payroll, and benefits, costs to retain 
employees, and mortgage, rent, utility, and other operating costs. Also, Treasury allows use of 
the funds for:  COVID-19 prevention or mitigation tactics, such as physical plant changes to 
enable social distancing, enhanced cleaning efforts, barriers or partitions, or COVID-19 
vaccination, testing, or contact tracing programs. 
 
• Funds may be used for providing technical assistance.  While not defined by statute or 
regulation, DCED instructed the PA CDFI Network to include that definition in materials 
submitted to DCED. That definition is as follows:   
 
Technical assistance and business counseling services include a variety of services, such 
as business planning, marketing, accounting, finance, and strategy assistance to keep 
businesses viable. CDFIs may also provide support completing financing applications and 
obtaining the necessary business documents to keep their business compliant.  Many 
small businesses are unable to secure clients or financing because they lack the 
education, skills, or resources to perform certain management tasks. CDFIs are able to 
support business owners and their staff with these business function supports.

Obligation and Expenditure 
 
The SLFRF Final Rule requires funds must be used to cover “costs incurred” between March 3, 2021, 
and December 31, 2024.  Funds must be obligated by this date.   
 
This time restriction is clearly applicable to the initial government recipient (DCED).  Negative 
economic impacts may have occurred prior to this time period which can be considered in 
determining eligible businesses.   However, there is no specific guidance as to whether a grant or 
loan can be directed for covering a business or nonprofit’s costs prior to March 3, 2021. The DCED 
contract allows costs incurred from March 1, 2020 through December 31, 2024.

Program Income Provisions 
 
Repayment of principal and interest during the performance period (until December 31, 2022) are 
treated somewhat differently.  While both must be reused for any of the eligible purposes, reuse of 
principal repayments is subject to same timing restrictions on the use of funds as the original use of the 
funds.  Interest payments are not subject to the timing restrictions.   
 
Principal repayments that are re-issued in new loans must either mature or be forgiven by December 31, 
2026.  If re-loaned principal funds do not mature by December 31, 2026, the CDFI must only use those 
principal funds for the subsidy cost of the loan as described in “Term of Loans” above. 
 
Therefore, CDFIs must account for principal and interest repayments separately.

PA Prevailing Wage Act 
 
The DCED contract imposes use of the Pennsylvania Prevailing Wage Act wage rates for ARPA funds.   
 
While this added requirement has the potential to impact use of the funds for capital and construction 
project uses, there are exemptions you should be aware of.  First, and most significantly, funds provided 
as a loan to a business, do not trigger state prevailing wage rates.  Such projects are exempt.  Second, if 
you provide small grants, those projects may also be exempt from state prevailing wage rates.  The use 
of the grant must be for projects in which the total project cost (including all other funding sources, not 
just your grant) is $25,000 or less.  Project costs exceeding $25,000 will trigger state prevailing wage 
rates if the funds are provided to the business as a grant. 
 
The federal law creating ARPA did not impose federal Davis-Bacon wage rates, but U.S. Treasury is 
encouraging “recipients to adhere to strong labor standards, including project labor agreements and
