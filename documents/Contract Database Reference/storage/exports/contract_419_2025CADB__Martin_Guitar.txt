REPORT TO THE MARTIN GUITAR CHARITABLE FOUNDATION – FY23-24
Year-End Evaluation of Annual Work Plan 2023-2024
Community Action Development Bethlehem
Goal 1: Serve the Bethlehem community as a community hub for information and referral services, neighborhood advocacy, and community problem-solving:
Performance Outcomes:
128 Bethlehem residents received information and referral services.
16 residents were referred to New Bethany&#039;s Housing Advocate program for rental assistance and/or housing search support.
111 south Bethlehem residents were engaged in neighborhood meetings to identify and discuss community concerns.
CADB staff have participated in 8 initiatives external to the organization including the Most Beautiful Lehigh Valley event planning, Choice Neighborhoods Steering Committee, the Bethlehem Opening Doors Steering Committee, Nurture Nature Center&#039;s CREATE Resilience, Better Blocks for Bethlehem committees, the Southside Arts District Advisory Committee, and the Southside Community Center planning process, among others.
CADB staff have spoken at 9 events, including City Council meetings, conferences, and summits.
Goal 2: Improve the quality of Bethlehem’s housing stock by implementing targeted rehabilitation programs in partnership with Community Action Homes:
Performance Outcomes:
3 residential façades improvements were completed on the Southside.
3 residential facades improvements were completed on the Northside.
5 south Bethlehem households were referred to Community Action Homes and/or City of Bethlehem housing rehabilitation programs.
Goal 3: Advocate for affordable, quality, and stable housing for all City of Bethlehem residents through community organizing, trainings, policy advocacy, and program development:
Performance Outcomes:
1 training regarding landlord-tenant law and Fair Housing was held at the Southside Lofts for 10 individuals.
CADB staff provided ongoing support for the Alley House pilot program. 
CADB staff identified two opportunities to support program and/or policy development to address the housing crisis in Bethlehem – the Choice Neighborhoods Community Ambassador training program and development of a landlord/tenant mediation program.
CADB funded Touchstone Theatre’s artist residency “The Most Beautiful Home…Maybe?”, which was designed to foster community conversations surrounding the housing crisis.
Goal 4: Preserve the integrity of public and green spaces in south Bethlehem while investing in practical and creative utilization of those spaces to foster community engagement:
Performance Outcomes:
3 gardens were supported through the 2023 growing season (MLK, Esperanza, and Lynfield).
200 native plants were installed at Madison Park in September 2023.
A pollinator garden was installed on the South Bethlehem Greenway.
98 residents have been engaged through gardening and outdoor wellness activities. 
Southside Ambassadors have provided continuous cleaning services to the south Bethlehem neighborhood.
19 cleaning and weeding sessions of Lopes International Park were provided by landscaping services.
9 neighborhood cleanup events were held.
80 residents and business owners have been engaged in neighborhood cleanups.
800 residents were engaged through 4 community building events.
The Southside Tomorrow NPP supported the Kellyn Foundation’s ongoing produce distribution on the Greenway.
Goal 5: Create, maintain, and build on existing initiatives that enhance community connectivity:
Performance Outcomes:
Two interns worked in July and August on developing original content for the “Southside Proud” marketing campaign.
16 posts have been shared on “Southside Proud” social media platforms.
75 contacts have been added to the neighborhood contact list.
Funding was provided to support 3 youth programs – the Shooting Stars Basketball program, the Push Ahead Skateboarding program and the Lynfield Community Center.
56 youth were engaged through the 3 youth programs during summer 2023.
The Southside Tomorrow NPP funded the installation of a bike rack at New Bethany Ministries to improve access to the Mollard Hospitality Center and the New Bethany Food Pantry.
The Southside Tomorrow NPP funded “Talento Local” the Hispanic Center Lehigh Valley’s senior music ensemble.
Goal 6: Support the development of leadership and community problem-solving skills, knowledge, and abilities among Bethlehem residents to enhance civic engagement and equitable representation in community decision-making processes:
Performance Outcomes:
The Southside Tomorrow NPP funded a comprehensive survey conducted by YWCA Bethlehem focusing on program development to increase leadership opportunities.
CADB Staff secured funding from the City of Bethlehem Choice Neighborhoods program and launched a pilot leadership development program in Pembroke in spring 2024.

THE MARTIN GUITAR CHARITABLE FOUNDATION  
The Martin Guitar Charitable Foundation was established in 1996 by Christian Frederick Martin IV to serve the charitable interests of 
Martin Guitar Company. The Foundation supports 501(c)(3) nonprofit organizations with programs in Foundation target areas. 
The Foundation’s core principles are to support Martin Guitar’s engagement with non-profit music, arts, education, and environmental 
action organizations. The Foundation does not fund political or religious organizations or activity. The Trustees manage the 
Foundation’s assets conservatively to allow for long term planning, and seek best value from Foundation grant-making for the people 
and causes that their grantees serve. 
The Foundation supports the best work of effective 501(c)(3) nonprofit organizations offering programs in one or more of the 
following areas: 
• Performance. Participation, and education in acoustic steel string guitar music 
• Community activity in the Nazareth, PA region 
• Study and preservation of America’s acoustic guitar heritage 
• Arts education programs emphasizing acoustic guitar 
• Educational institutions in the Lehigh Valley 
• Promotion of arts and culture, mainly in the Lehigh Valley 
• Help for the poor, hungry, sick, and homeless in the Lehigh Valley 
• Environmental action related to guitar-making materials and processes 
The Foundation does not provide any Martin Guitar products or make grants to individuals. 
The Foundation accepts requests for grant funding during the period of June 1 to August 31. Requests must be received before 
September 1 to be considered for funding.  Foundation grants are typically given in the range of $1,000 to $10,000. Please make 
your request no longer than two pages. Please include: 
• A brief overview of the organization, mission statement and total budget for the organization 
• Description of the program seeking funding and the impact that program will have on the greater community that you serve.  
Please include a budget for the program and the amount you are requesting.  
• Prior grantees should submit a one page report on the impact their prior grant has had on the community and how the 
foundation has been recognized throughout the year. 
• Please include contact name, position, organization name, organization address, phone number, website address and phone 
number. 
• 501(c)(3) determination letter. 
• List of current board members and their affiliation. 
• First two pages of the organization’s 990 
 
Renewal of Foundation grants is not automatic. Requests from prior year grantees should be sent prior to September 1 and should 
include a separate one-page report of how the Foundation’s funds were used, the impact of Foundation support, and how the 
Foundation was recognized. Please email your materials to martinguitarfoundation@gmail.com or to C.F. Marin Inc., Attn: Martin 
Guitar Charitable Foundation, 510 Sycamore Street, PO Box 329, Nazareth, PA 18064. 
 
We receive many requests for funding. Telephoning or emailing company representatives concerning grant requests will not increase 
the likelihood of funding. Our decision process is competitive, and we are not able to fund every request.
