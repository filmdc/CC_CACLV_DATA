Mission and History of Organization  
The Community Action Committee of the Lehigh Valley (CALV) was established in 1965 as a part of Lyndon 
B. Johnson’s War on Poverty.  The mission of CALV is “to improve the quality of life in the Lehigh Valley by 
building a community in which all people have access to economic opportunity, the ability to pursue that 
opportunity, and a voice in the decisions that affect their lives.” Today, CALV operates 13 programs and 3 
subsidiary corporations. As a Community Action Agency (CAA), CALV is committed to ensuring that the 
community plays an active role in identifying needs, developing, and implementing solutions to improve 
the quality of life in the region. Toward that end, CAAs have a tripartite Board of Directors comprised of 
at least one-third of its members from the low-income community, one-third from the public sector, and 
one-third from the private sector.  
The mission of Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast Pennsylvania is to obtain 
food and distribute it to people in need through area non-profits and to provide resources for education 
and advocacy to end hunger. We provide nutritious food, offer nutrition education, advocate to end food 
insecurity, dismantle barriers to create access, and work to attain food justice. We respect and preserve 
the dignity of each of our neighbors.  
Second Harvest, a program of Community Action Lehigh Valley, began its mission to end food insecurity 
in 1982, as the Lehigh Valley Food Bank. Initially, distributions were made from the back of a rented truck 
to a network of about 15 nonprofit agencies. Since September 2015, Second Harvest Food Bank has been 
operating from a 65,000 square-foot warehouse in East Allen Township.

Programs and Services 
Second Harvest Food Bank, a member of Feeding America – the nation’s largest charitable food network, 
serves Lehigh, Northampton, Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne counties. Last year, Second Harvest 
distributed 14.7 million pounds of food to our network of 200+ nonprofit agencies, (food pantries, schools, 
meal centers, shelters, childcare agencies, addiction treatment programs), a 44% increase from the 
previous year. Included in this total is 6.7 million pounds of fresh produce, perishable protein, and fresh 
dairy products (46% of total distribution). Annually, we rescue more than 3 million pounds of food from 
ending up in a landfill.  
 
On average, the Second Harvest network serves more than 87,000 people each month - a 52% increase 
from last year. The pantries within the Second Harvest network are operated almost solely through the 
work of community volunteers. Our food distribution is a model of neighbors helping neighbors.  
 
In addition to food distribution to our network, Second Harvest operates several programs aimed at 
improving the health of our community and working toward a more just and equitable local food system. 
We administer the Pennsylvania Senior Food Box Program (PASB). PASB works to improve the health of 
low-income seniors by supplementing their diets with shelf-stable nutritious foods. Eligible participants 
include low-income individuals who are at least 60 years old and whose household income is at or below 
130% of the U.S. poverty level. We work to supplement boxes with healthy, fresh items when possible. 
Currently, Second Harvest provides senior food boxes to nearly 2,350 seniors across 49 sites each month. 
Our MilitaryShare program works “to serve those who served us” by providing access to nutritious food, 
with a goal of improving health outcomes and easing financial strain among low-income active duty, 
reserve, and retired military members and their families. Our MilitaryShare program provides 500+ 
military families with a share of fresh and shelf-stable food each month.  
Our SNAP (Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program) outreach coordinators work with local county 
offices of Health and Human Services to offer SNAP application assistance. Our SNAP coordinators offer 
dedicated personal application support to ensure that all eligible participants receive needed benefits. 
We offer dynamic, community-based nutrition education. We provide free cooking classes and 
demonstrations for parents, kids and childcare providers where participants learn about cooking, meal 
prep, grocery shopping, food budgeting, and nutrition. Through our Healthy Pantry Initiative (HPI), a 
program of Feeding Pennsylvania, our HPI Coordinator works with pantry coordinators and volunteers to 
encourage consumption of nutritious and fresh foods, and also provides direct nutrition education to 
pantry clients. 
Additionally, Second Harvest operates The Seed Farm, a 42-acre training farm. Because there can be no 
access to nourishing food without farms, The Seed Farm works to support food production at all levels, 
from home gardeners to community gardens to local farmers, with an emphasis on getting nutritious and 
locally-grown food to under-resourced communities. Strong local food systems equal food security.

Monroe County Food Insecurity 
Monroe County has a population of 168,128. According to Feeding America’s Map the Meal Gap (2022), 
one in nine Monroe County residents faces food insecurity. This means that more than 19,220 individuals 
(11.7%) do not have certain access to adequate food throughout the year. 
Child Nutrition 
• 17.8% children in Monroe County face food insecurity. 
• An estimated 5,830 children face food insecurity, with 64% of children in households earning less 
than 185% of the federal poverty level facing food insecurity and 36% of children in households 
earning more than 185% of the federal poverty level facing food insecurity. 
Senior Nutrition 
• Data from Feeding America’s State of Senior Hunger 2021 report estimates that in Pennsylvania 
4.7% of seniors are food insecure with 1.7% facing extremely high levels of food insecurity.
2 
While 
the data set does not drill down to the county level, U.S. Census data show 3,081 adults older than 
age 65 (9.6%) live below the poverty level in Monroe County. 
Racial and Ethnic Disparities 
• Over 60% of the Monroe County population is non-Hispanic white; however, food insecurity is 
higher among Hispanic (19%) residents, compared with 10% of white residents and 13% of Black 
residents. 
SNAP 
• Map the Meal Gap 2022 estimates that 41% of food insecure Monroe County residents are above 
the income threshold to qualify for SNAP benefits or other food assistance programs (200% of the 
federal poverty level), while 59% are below the threshold and may qualify for supplemental 
nutrition benefits and programs. 
 
Monroe County Impact 
 
Second Harvest distributes food to 20 emergency food sites (pantries and meal centers), six senior box 
sites, and two MilitaryShare sites within Monroe County. Collectively, these agencies continue to serve a 
rising number of people.  
Overall, the number of people served at Monroe County agencies increased by 53% from FY 2022 to FY 
2023. On average, pantries were serving 17,308 people per month in FY23, compared with 11,297 per

month in FY22. At meal centers, the number of people served increased drastically by 108% from 1,997 
people to 4,164 people during the same period.  
From FY 2022 to FY 2023, Second Harvest increased food distribution to Monroe County by 29%, from 
2,916,504 lbs. in FY22 to 3,766,192 lbs. in FY23 [meal equivalent = 2,430,420 meals vs. 3,138,493 meals, 
respectively).  
 
 
Goals, Measurable Outcomes, and Evaluation 
Annually, each program of Community Action, including Second Harvest, develops a work plan with 
measurable performance targets that guide the activities of our programs and are designed to have a 
positive impact on the community. Performance targets are monitored and evaluated by the Community 
Action Board of Directors. Progress is monitored at the six-month mark, and all performance targets are 
fully evaluated at the end of the program year.  
Additionally, Second Harvest solicits feedback from its partner agencies and food pantry participants, in 
the form of formal surveys, conversational feedback, and committee work. The results and feedback guide 
Second Harvest’s strategic planning, program development, and service provision. We are working toward 
a more participant-centric model of operation. We are taking the necessary, often tough, steps to shift 
power to the people we serve.   
 
Partnership with Sanofi Pasteur 
Funding from Sanofi will allow us to cover transportation costs to distribute food to our Monroe County 
partners. We are grateful for the opportunity to submit this proposal and look forward to growing our 
partnership with Sanofi. We know our community will benefit greatly.
