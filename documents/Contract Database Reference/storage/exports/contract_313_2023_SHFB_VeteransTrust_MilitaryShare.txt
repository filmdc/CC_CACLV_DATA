The Community Action Committee of the Lehigh Valley (CALV) was established in 1965. The mission of 
CALV is “to improve the quality of life in the Lehigh Valley by building a community in which all people 
have access to economic opportunity, the ability to pursue that opportunity, and a voice in the decisions that 
affect their lives.” Today, CALV operates 3 subsidiary corporations and 13 programs, including Second 
Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast Pennsylvania. CALV is governed by a tripartite 
Board of Directors comprised of at least one-third from the low-income community, one-third from the public 
sector, and one-third from the private sector. 
 
The mission of Second Harvest Food Bank is to obtain food and distribute it to people in need through area 
non-profits and to provide resources for education and advocacy to end hunger. We provide nutritious food, 
offer nutrition education, advocate to end food insecurity, dismantle barriers to create access, and work to 
attain food justice. We respect and preserve the dignity of each of our neighbors.  
Second Harvest began its mission to end food insecurity in 1982, as the Lehigh Valley Food Bank. Initially, 
distributions were made from the back of a rented truck to a network of about 15 nonprofit agencies. The 
Food Bank has grown and evolved, and since September 2015, Second Harvest has been operating from a 
65,000 square-foot warehouse in East Allen Township. 
 
Second Harvest, a member of Feeding America – the nation’s largest charitable food network, serves Lehigh, 
Northampton, Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne counties. Last year, Second Harvest distributed 14.7 million 
pounds of food to our network of 200+ nonprofit agencies (food pantries, schools, meal centers, shelters, 
childcare agencies, senior centers, veterans organizations), a 44% increase from the previous year. Included 
in this total is 6.7 million pounds of fresh produce, perishable protein, and fresh dairy products (46% of total 
distribution). Annually, we rescue more than 8 million pounds of food from ending up in a landfill.  
 
On average, the Second Harvest network serves more than 87,000 people each month - a 52% increase from 
last year. The pantries within the Second Harvest network are operated almost solely through the work of 
community volunteers. Our food distribution is a model of neighbors helping neighbors.  
 
In addition to food distribution to our network, Second Harvest operates several programs aimed at improving 
the health of our community and working toward a more just and equitable local food system. We administer 
the Pennsylvania Senior Food Box Program (PASB). PASB works to improve the health of low-income 
seniors by supplementing their diets with shelf-stable nutritious foods. Eligible participants include low-
income individuals who are at least 60 years of age and whose household income is at or below 130% of the 
U.S. poverty level. We work to supplement boxes with healthy, fresh items when possible. Currently, Second 
Harvest provides senior food boxes to nearly 2,350 seniors across 49 sites each month. 
 
Our SNAP (Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program) outreach coordinators work with local county 
offices of Health and Human Services to offer SNAP application assistance. Our SNAP coordinators offer 
dedicated personal application support to ensure that all eligible participants receive needed benefits.

Support from the Pennsylvania Veteran’s Trust Fund will allow Second Harvest to continue to grow our 
MilitaryShare program, ensuring we will reach more military families in need.  
 
Feeding America estimates that 20% of people served through the charitable food network are veterans or 
have a veteran in their household. Further, according to U.S. Census Bureau’s American Community Survey 
(2022), there are 37,342 veterans living within Second Harvest’s six-county service territory and 6% are 
living at or below the poverty level, meaning that more than 2,185 veterans are struggling to make ends meet, 
and most likely do not have the funds to purchase nutritious, fresh foods for themselves and their families.  
 
According to Feeding America, as many as 24% of active-duty service members and their families experience 
food insecurity.  Service members are often required to move from base to base, making it difficult for 
spouses to maintain consistent work, leaving many families to rely on a single income. The family budget is 
tight when relying on one low-ranking military salary. One-third of students at Department of Defense 
schools were eligible for free and reduced-price meals during the 2018-2019 school year. 
 
Additionally, though many military families face food insecurity, few qualify for federal food assistance 
programs. When determining SNAP eligibility, housing allowances are considered income for service 
members. According to the USDA, only 2% of active-duty service members qualify for SNAP benefits, but 
about 7% of military members experience food insecurity. 
 
Military families and veterans are one of the most difficult groups to reach through the emergency food 
network, citing pride as the biggest barrier. To address this need, the USDA, Feeding America, and regional 
food banks have created food distribution programs that break down these barriers. Second Harvest’s 
MilitaryShare program is a part of this nation-wide effort and was designed to address the food insecurity 
needs of military families to promote health and financial stability. 
 
The mission of Second Harvest’s MilitaryShare program is “to serve those who served us” by providing 
access to nutritious food, which will assist in improving health outcomes and easing financial strain. When 
funds are tight, often the first thing to be cut from the family budget is groceries, especially higher-priced 
nutritious foods. By supplying struggling military families with healthy food, it saves room in the monthly 
budget for other expenses such as housing, utilities, and medical bills. By not having to worry about the cost 
of food, it might mean that families don’t have to make the tough choice between paying their rent and 
keeping food on the table. 
 
Each monthly MilitaryShare contains a box of shelf-stable foods, weighing roughly 20 pounds, with 
additional fresh products such as eggs, milk, meat, and a 10-pound box of produce. Shelf-stable foods include 
canned fruits, vegetables, beans and meats, peanut butter, juices, cereals, rice, and pasta, with a focus on 
whole grains and low-sugar products. Each share weighs between 35-40 pounds.  
 
Second Harvest partners with our food pantry member agencies, local veterans’ groups, county veterans’ 
affairs departments and military and reserve outlets to conduct outreach. Referrals from such organizations 
can make the difference between those in need getting connected with our program or remaining unaware.
