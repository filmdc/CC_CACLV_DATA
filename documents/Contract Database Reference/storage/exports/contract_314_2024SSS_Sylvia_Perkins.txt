Describe the specific purpose for which any grant funds awarded from this foundation will 
be used:  
Funds would be used to purchase furniture for several apartments at the Sixth Street Shelter. We have 
been replacing damaged and worn items with items that are bed bug resistant and easy to clean and 
sanitize without causing damage to the furniture. Not only is this furniture durable enough to handle the 
wear and tear of multiple families residing in the unit each year, but it also provides a safer, healthier 
environment for families in terms of cleaning and sanitizing against the spread of viruses and bed bugs. 
This will give families a clean, safe, and comfortable place to call home during their stay at the Sixth 
Street Shelter. 
The Sixth Street Shelter has seen a marked increase in cases of bed bugs in recent years, which has 
impacted shelter operations and increased our maintenance costs. Unsheltered families often stay 
wherever they can find a place regardless of the health risks, which can include exposure to bed bugs. 
Once a family is exposed to bed bugs, they then transport these to the Shelter, where they can easily 
spread among units. The furniture we have selected is designed for easy extermination and cleaning so 
that we will not have to regularly replace furniture that becomes infested. 
Describe how a grant to your organization would further the foundation’s mission and the 
priorities of the foundation.  
The Sixth Street Shelter shares a common goal with the Sylvia Perkin Perpetual Charitable Trust: to 
work towards public health and welfare. The Shelter provides intensive case management to all families 
who reside here, helping them on their path to self-sufficiency. The Shelter also needs to be a safe and

healthy environment for children and families experiencing homelessness. These funds would support us 
in ensuring that the furniture in the apartments is clean and sanitary, and will help to mitigate instances 
of bed bugs. 
Describe the objectives of the project or program to be funded:  
The Sixth Street Shelter assists families experiencing homelessness secure safe housing through family-
centered services, in-house programming, and referrals to community resources while advocating that 
affordable housing is a human right. Located in Center City Allentown, the property has 25 private 
apartments, a Family Resource Center, playground, and full-choice food pantry for residents. A stay at 
the Shelter enables families to begin to stabilize their income and address the problems that led to their 
homelessness. The Shelter provides each family with a fully furnished apartment and supportive 
services, including individualized case management. Staff work to connect them with the necessary 
resources to address their individual needs, such as financial literacy and parenting classes, access to 
childcare and healthcare, and mental health support. During their residency, the family develops a 
Family Service Plan with their caseworker, setting household, budgeting, educational, and vocational 
goals and seeking suitable housing and employment. We have started a new Benchmark Family Care 
Initiative, which aims to ensure that all residents have or obtain medical insurance and are connected to 
appropriate providers so that they can achieve a list of benchmarks related to their relevant medical, 
dental, and mental health care, as well as track financial goals. Staff maintain contact with the family 
following their move into permanent housing to offer additional assistance and determine how well they 
are continuing to move toward self-sufficiency.  
For this project specifically, the objective is to provide a clean, healthy, and comfortable environment for 
our residents as they work towards their goals. This entails having the proper furniture in each apartment 
to support the residents’ health and comfort and to ease the challenges of upkeep on Shelter staff. Every 
apartment should provide a sense of stability and a feeling of home for the family, as these are spaces 
where they spend time together. Residents also learn the importance of maintaining their space and 
furniture in preparation for renting, and this furniture is easy to maintain. This furniture will also 
mitigate the instances of bed bugs and decrease our expenditures on extermination, deep-cleaning 
services, and replacing damaged furniture.  
Describe the implementation plan for the project or program. Include at least three specific 
actions your organization will take in order to achieve results:  
Upon receipt of funding, we would first place an order with Institutional Bedz LLC for the furniture we 
have selected. We have ordered furniture from them in the past, which will help to streamline the 
process. When the furniture arrives, we would prioritize apartments that are in greatest need of new 
items to replace worn-out and/or soiled furniture. Then we would arrange to have the proper staff and/or 
volunteers ready to assemble anything if necessary and place the furniture accordingly in each 
apartment. We would follow up by inspecting the furniture periodically to confirm that it is meeting our 
needs and the families’ needs in terms of comfort, utility, and durability. 
What criteria does (or will) your organization use to measure the success of the project or 
program?  
Success of this project will be measured by whether the furniture is purchased, delivered, assembled, 
and placed in the apartments within the grant timeline and within budget. More broadly, this project will 
contribute to reaching our overall performance targets for the Sixth Street Shelter in our current fiscal

year: 1) At least 85 families will reside at the Shelter and develop Family Service Plans based on their 
housing, educational and financial goals; 2) 50% of families will save money toward rent or a security 
deposit upon leaving the Shelter; 3) 75% of families will achieve their Family Service Plan goals; 4) 
100% of families will create a written budget to understand credit and money management as a part of 
their goal plan; 5) 25% of families will move into affordable housing upon leaving.  
Each year, a work plan is developed with measurable goals and performance targets that guide program 
activities. In order to measure effectiveness of services and outcomes, the work plan is evaluated twice 
yearly by the Program Development & Evaluation Committee of Community Action’s Board of 
Directors and is then approved by Community Action’s full Board. In addition, Sixth Street Shelter 
utilizes the Homeless Management Information System (HMIS) and ClientTrack to track and analyze 
participant data. The Shelter also tracks qualitative data to document the positive long-term impact of 
the Shelter in the community. Staff conduct follow-up evaluations with former residents that can be 
located at 3-, 6-, and 12-month intervals to ascertain their continued progress. 
Briefly describe any formal or informal collaborative ventures your organization has 
established (or will establish) with other entities serving similar purposes that may be 
relevant to this grant request.  
The Sixth Street Shelter partners with many government entities and non-profit agencies to ensure that 
residents receive the services and gain the skills they will need as part of a comprehensive plan to help 
them to become self-sufficient. The Shelter has a nearly 30-year partnership with the Lehigh County 
Office of Children and Youth Services, and many other local organizations contribute to programming 
for our residents. For instance, First Northern Bank leads financial literacy classes, CareerLink assists 
with job searches and resume writing, Highmark Wholecare facilitates “Wellness Friday” sessions for 
both Shelter residents and community members, and Vision To Learn provides free comprehensive eye 
exams and prescription glasses for children. The Shelter is also a member of the Lehigh Valley Regional 
Homeless Advisory Board. The LVRHAB is a consortium of representatives from city and county 
governments, social service agencies, veterans support groups, faith-based and other community 
organizations, homeless advocates, homeless or formerly homeless people, representatives from 
legislators, and other interested parties with the mission to prevent and end homelessness in the Lehigh 
Valley. Julie Shoults, a Planner for Community Action Lehigh Valley, currently serves on the LVRHAB 
Governing Board. 
If this grant relates to an ongoing project or program, how long has the project or program 
been operating?  
Since 1985, the Sixth Street Shelter, a program of Community Action Lehigh Valley, has been offering 
services to help prepare families for the challenges of a difficult job and rental market that too often 
result in homelessness. It is the only family shelter in the region that admits families of any 
configuration (with at least one child under 18) and houses them in their own private apartments. The 
Sixth Street Shelter has proven to be a vital resource in the community, and while it has been operating 
for almost 40 years, the program has adapted to the changing needs of the community. For instance, 
there is currently a severe shortage of available, affordable housing units locally, and landlords have 
been very selective in whom they rent to. Our caseworkers report that they struggle to find housing for 
their clients despite having rental assistance and supportive services available. This means that residents 
are experiencing longer stays in the Shelter before moving into permanent housing. We have added a 
Housing Innovation Specialist to our staff to work with our residents and local landlords in order to

identify affordable housing options and facilitate faster placements for families. We have also been 
expanding our educational and vocational programming at the Sixth Street Shelter to increase residents’ 
earning capacity, which will help them to remain stably housed when they move out of the Shelter and 
into permanent housing.

Demographics Comments:

The Eastern Pennsylvania Continuum of Care (CoC) manages the Coordinated Entry (CE) system for individuals 
experiencing or at imminent risk of homelessness in the region. The CoC reported 1,055 CE enrollments in Lehigh 
County in 2022, almost double the next highest county enrollment of 533. This demonstrates the great need for 
homelessness services in Lehigh County, where the Sixth Street Shelter is located.  
 
Last fiscal year, 52 families consisting of 196 people (including 123 children, 49 of whom were five and under) 
found refuge at Sixth Street Shelter. Many heads of households that the Shelter serves are unemployed or 
underemployed, or recipients of SSI and SSDI whose low incomes cannot support even what HUD has determined 
as Fair Market Rent for an apartment in the Lehigh Valley. Others have come to the end of their unemployment or 
welfare benefits and are poorly equipped educationally and vocationally to find jobs in a changing labor market. 
Some face additional challenges, such as a disability or experiencing domestic violence.  
 
Ninety-four percent of the families who resided at the Shelter last fiscal year had a female head of household, and 
67% were headed by a single mother. Eighty-seven percent of families qualified as Extremely Low-Income (0-30% 
Area Median Income) when they entered the Shelter, and 13% qualified as Low-Income (31-50% Area Median 
Income). Of last year’s families, 67% identified as Black or African American, 29% as White, and 4% as Native 
Hawaiian or Pacific Islander; and 35% identified as Hispanic/Latino. The Shelter endeavors to engage staff that 
reflect the community we serve and have lived expertise with homelessness.
