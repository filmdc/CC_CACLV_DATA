*Organization Mission
The mission of Community Action Committee of the Lehigh Valley, Inc. (AKA Community Action Lehigh Valley) is
"to improve the quality of life in the Lehigh Valley by building a community in which all people have access to
economic opportunity, the ability to pursue that opportunity, and a voice in the decisions that affect their lives." 
*Organization History
Community Action Committee of the Lehigh Valley, Inc. (AKA Community Action Lehigh Valley (CALV)) was
incorporated on December 20, 1965 as a Pennsylvania not-for-profit corporation and designated as a 501(c)3
organization to work with and for low-income residents of the Lehigh Valley to combat poverty. Its programs have
grown and evolved over the past 57 years and have had a strong impact on the local community. In 1970, CALV
established the first Head Start program in the area, educating 250 students, and 17 migrant farm workers were
provided with housing. In 1980, CALV established the Low-Income Home Energy Assistance Program, operated
a 24-hour emergency hotline, and delivered heating fuel to residents in crisis. Lehigh Valley Food Bank began
distributing food from a rented truck in 1984, and Lehigh Valley Confederation on the Homeless launched. In the
‘90s, CALV opened transitional long-term housing for families pursuing an education, and CALV’s Task Force on
Economic Opportunity started supporting minority entrepreneurs through loans. Sixth Street Shelter expanded to
assist more families, and CALV began administering PPL’s OnTrack program. Rising Tide Community Loan Fund
launched in 2000. In 2005, CALV mobilized residents for the start of the Main Street and Greenway projects, as
well as Second Harvest's Backpack Buddies initiative. In 2011, the Land Trust put its first home on the market. In
2013, Sixth Street Shelter expanded to provide housing for 12 additional families. In 2016, the Campaign for
Racial and Ethnic Justice launched, and in 2017, a neighborhood partnership program in the Slate Belt started.
CALV also developed a comprehensive housing revitalization initiative, Community Action Homes. In 2019, Color
Outside the Lines, a 5-7-year racial diversity and inclusion plan, launched to ensure that everyone in the Lehigh
Valley has equal access to opportunity. 
*Organization Description
CALV offers food for the hungry through Second Harvest Food Bank and short- and long-term shelter for
homeless families through Sixth Street Shelter and Turner and Ferry Street Apartments. The Housing
Counseling Program (formerly Community Action Financial Services) is a HUD and PHFA-approved housing
counseling program that increases self-sufficiency, financial literacy, and asset-building by providing education
and counseling for home ownership and mortgage foreclosure. Weatherization offers solutions to home heating
problems through direct financial assistance, counseling, and weatherization of residences. The Campaign for
Racial and Ethnic Justice works with vulnerable youth to instill a perpetual sense of purpose through intentional
PNC Foundation

and creative implementation of its core principles-Respect, Responsibility, Connections, Consistency. SHE (She
Has Everything) empowers young women transitioning into middle school to become independent and change
the power dynamic. Slate Belt Rising is a multi-municipality initiative that works on revitalizing the Slate Belt to
improve the lives of residents. Each of these programs acts as a non-duplicative resource in the Lehigh Valley.
Economic and community development work is conducted by its subsidiary corporations. Community Action
Development Corporations of Allentown and Bethlehem work with residents to revitalize their communities.
Rising Tide Community Loan Fund is a federally certified community development financial institution that
provides microloans and technical and marketing assistance to small businesses. Community Action Homes is a
comprehensive housing revitalization initiative that increases the affordable housing stock in the Lehigh Valley
through acquisition/rehab/resale, owner-occupied rehab, and rehab of residential and commercial facades.
Lehigh Valley Community Land Trust increases the supply of permanent affordable housing for low- to moderate-
income households by working with Community Action Homes. 
*Organization Purpose
CALV's mission statement, adopted in June 1993, reflects the understanding of the Board of Directors and the
staff that CALV will strive to include its constituents in full partnership with the agency, melding service provision,
community education, advocacy, and engagement.
CALV recognizes that people in crisis cannot move toward a vision of individual achievement while their
immediate needs - hunger, homelessness, or other dire predicament - remain unresolved. Foremost among
CALV's goals is to help individuals empower themselves and learn to use available resources to overcome
current and future obstacles that stand in the way of their progress. Thriving individuals and families are the
foundation of thriving businesses and communities.
Programs that are typically referred to as "human services" are important, but no level of "human services"
funding can solve problems such as the lack of jobs and lack of availability of decent, affordable housing in safe,
functioning neighborhoods that are close to places of employment. CALV will seek out the best tools to combat
those problems.
Equally important, as an agency, CALV places high priority on creating and maintaining strong communities
across the Lehigh Valley. The mission is not to help people escape impoverished neighborhoods, but to stand
squarely on the side of uplifting neighborhoods.
CALV's anti-poverty agenda emphasizes economic opportunity; encourages personal responsibility; builds
assets; strengthens neighborhoods; grows the tax bases of urbanized municipalities; encourages the
accumulation of wealth, including ownership of homes and businesses; and creates positive role models.
Perhaps most importantly, CALV stresses civic participation as the highest form of self-sufficiency, with power -
political power and self-determination - at the top of the pyramid.

*Addressing PNC Foundation's Funding Priorities
CALV addresses 4 funding priorities:
Affordable Housing: Housing Counseling Program (HCP) (formerly Community Action Financial Services) offers
programs to help low-to moderate- income families achieve homeownership. Potential homeowners are
educated on the importance of good credit, budgeting, the home buying process and their rights and obligations
as homeowners. HCP also assists families who are experiencing financial challenges and face foreclosure.
Community Action Homes, a comprehensive housing revitalization initiative, focuses resources to undertake
acquisition/rehab/resale, owner-occupied and investor-owned rehab, and facades. Lehigh Valley Community
Land Trust provides permanent affordable housing for low- to moderate-income households.
Community Development: Community Action Development Corporations of Allentown and Bethlehem and Slate
Belt Rising work with residents to revitalize the community. Rising Tide Community Loan Fund, a federally
certified community development financial institution, provides microloans and technical and marketing
assistance to small businesses.
Community Services: Second Harvest Food Bank provides food to people in need. The Sixth Street Shelter
provides safe and supportive temporary housing for homeless families. Weatherization provides energy
education and residential weatherization. She Has Everything (SHE) and Campaign for Racial and Ethnic
Justice (CREJ) work with vulnerable youth to change both the gender and racial power dynamic to instill a
perpetual sense of purpose.
Revitalization and Stabilization: CALV is committed to an anti-poverty agenda that emphasizes economic
opportunity; encourages personal responsibility; strengthens neighborhoods; grows the tax bases of urbanized
municipalities; encourages accumulation of wealth, including ownership of homes and businesses; and creates
positive role models. Most importantly, CALV stresses civic participation as the highest form of self-sufficiency. 
*Problem to be Addressed
Lack of Affordable Housing:
In 2022, HUD listed the Fair Market Rent (FMR) for the Allentown-Bethlehem-Easton MSA as $1,196 for a 2-
bedroom unit, which is projected to increase by $200 to $1,396 in 2023. According to the National Low Income
Housing Coalition report titled “Out of Reach 2022,” the income needed to afford the FMR for a 2-bedroom unit is
$47,840, or an hourly wage of $23. A head of household would have to work 3.2 full-time jobs at minimum wage
to afford a 2-bedroom FMR apartment. This is simply out of reach for low-income members of the community.

Low Homeownership:
According to the 2016-2020 American Community Survey, 64.7% of housing units in Lehigh County are owner-
occupied and 35.3% of the housing units are renter-occupied. In Northampton County, 71.2% of housing units
are owner-occupied and 28.8% are renter-occupied. Some of the obstacles potential homebuyers face include
credit issues, inadequate savings for a down payment and closing costs, as well as the lack of education
regarding the home buying process itself. Lehigh Valley residents who could potentially purchase a house
continue to pay rent that is higher than a mortgage payment. This trend continues because residents do not
perceive homeownership as an attainable goal for a variety of reasons, including a lack of education. However,
because a homebuyer has a stake in their own property, as well as their community, owning a house strengthens
the sense of community in a neighborhood.
Foreclosure:
Foreclosures experienced by individual homeowners typically impact the neighborhood in three significant ways:
1) declining property values and deterioration; 2) increased transience which leads to crime activity; and 3) local
government fiscal hardship. HCP continues to serve a great need in Lehigh and Northampton counties in the
area of default and delinquency counseling for impacted homeowners with increasing demand for services
provided by the mortgage diversion program in both counties.

*Project/Program Description
The Housing Counseling Program (HCP) (formerly Community Action Financial Services) is a HUD & PHFA-
approved housing counseling program offering services to work with families hoping to achieve homeownership
and avoid foreclosure. HCP’s mission is “to provide low- to moderate-income individuals and families with a
continuum of coordinated financial counseling services in one location in order to encourage and assist them to
build personal assets, increase and preserve homeownership, and to develop neighborhood wealth for the
benefit of the whole community.”
The Home Ownership Counseling Program consists of 7 seminars for first-time homebuyers (75% of whom are
LMI), pre-settlement counseling, and one-on-one pre-purchase counseling. Participants maximize their
knowledge of homeownership, learn to budget, and plan to purchase their first homes. Seminars are offered in
both English and Spanish.
The Homeowners’ Emergency Mortgage Assistance Program (HEMAP) provides foreclosure mitigation services
to homeowners of single-family homes in the Lehigh and Northampton County court systems who receive an
ACT 91 Notice.
Mortgage Foreclosure Diversion Program is a mediation program in the Lehigh and Northampton County court
systems available to single-family homeowners in foreclosure. 
*Evaluation
The Housing Counseling Program (HCP) will increase homeownership, stabilize neighborhoods, and build
community wealth, particularly for minorities and low- to moderate-income households, through the provision of
homeownership opportunities.
• 175 prospective homebuyers will participate in 1 of 7 seminars conducted in Allentown, Bethlehem and Easton
(4 in English, 3 in Spanish); 50 prospective homebuyers will participate in pre-purchase counseling services; and
50 prospective homebuyers will receive pre-settlement counseling before their settlement/closing date and will
purchase a home.
• 30 financially distressed homeowners who received an Act 91 Notice will receive loss mitigation services.
• 80 financially distressed homeowners that have been served with an urgent notice and complaint from the
Northampton or Lehigh County Court of Common Pleas will be provided with comprehensive housing counseling
services and advocacy in a court setting. 32 homeowners will avoid the loss of their home.
HCP’s expected accomplishments come from its Annual Work Plan, which is evaluated twice yearly to measure
the effectiveness of services and program outcomes. In addition, HCP’s Director submits a monthly report of
program activities to CALV’s Executive Director, which is presented to the Board of Directors. The report allows
for monitoring program performance on a routine basis and assures that staff activities toward achieving program
objectives are on schedule. HCP also prepares and submits regular progress reports on its goals and services
according to HUD, PHFA and CDBG guidelines and receives periodic monitoring visits conducted by HUD,
PHFA, the City of Allentown, and the City of Bethlehem.

*Sustainability
HCP strives to provide continuous, high-quality and sustainable services. In order to achieve sustainability, HCP
implements a three-dimensional approach:
1. Fund Development: HCP is sustained by a multi-faceted development strategy. HCP receives funding from
corporations, foundations, government entities, and individual donors. CALV’s Executive Director is responsible
for personal solicitation of funds. The Planning Department is responsible for identifying new funding
opportunities, managing a 12-month calendar of due dates for proposals and reports for existing donors, writing
and submitting strategic grant proposals to corporations, foundations and government entities, and tracking the
progress of funding requests in an internal database.
2. Contract Compliance: As a non-profit, CALV values responsible stewardship of the funds entrusted and the
resources available. Toward that end, CALV’s Planning and Fiscal Departments and Compliance Director
conduct contract compliance activities. The Fiscal Department is responsible for meeting all contractual fiscal
obligations. The Compliance Director and Planning Department provide contract compliance oversight by
ensuring that program directors are familiar with their contracts, program objectives and reporting requirements.
The Planning Department also conducts periodic internal monitoring of all programs that includes periodic review
of client files, data collection processes and reports. The Planning Department also assists program directors in
preparing for monitoring visits by funders.
3. HCP Advisory Board: The Advisory Board includes lenders, realtors, home insurance agents and community
organizations. The Advisory Board meets bi-monthly to review the operations and budget of the program and
make suggestions for improvements, as needed, based on market trends and industry best practices. 

*Community Involvement History
HCP leverages community partnerships to ensure the success of the Home Ownership Counseling Program
(HOCP) and its participants. Neighborhood Housing Services of the Lehigh Valley offers down payment and
closing cost assistance subject to homebuyers graduating from the HOCP first-time homebuyer seminar or pre-
settlement counseling. Recently, HCP began partnering with the City of Bethlehem to administer the Bethlehem
Homebuyer Assistance Program (BHAP), which provides down payment and closing cost assistance to low- to
moderate-income first-time homebuyers within the City of Bethlehem. Lenders, home inspectors, realtors and
homeowners insurance agents speak at the first-time homebuyer seminars (such as Truist, Wells Fargo,
Santander Bank, TD Bank, Fulton Bank, ESSA Bank & Trust, Embassy Bank, Second Sight Home Inspections,
Advantage Inspection Service, CDA Inspection Services, Century 21 Ramos Realty, Weichert, Keller Williams,
State Farm Insurance, Valera Agency, and Arbor Insurance Group). HCP continues to form new partnerships and
recruit new speakers as needed. Additionally, lenders, realtors, home insurance agents and community agencies
make up the HCP Advisory Board. HCP staff members work with the Advisory Board to boost program
participation in an effort to reach as many potential first-time homebuyers as possible. Advisory Board members
and non-profit agencies assist with outreach for the first-time homebuyer seminar by circulating flyers to potential
first-time homebuyers. 
*Fundraising Plan
HCP is sustained by a comprehensive development strategy. HCP receives funding from a variety of sources.
Revenue sources include Community Development Block Grant funds from the cities of Allentown and
Bethlehem, the County of Northampton, Lehigh Affordable Housing Trust Fund, Northampton County Affordable
Housing Program, Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency (PHFA), Community Services Block Grant (CSBG),
the United States Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD), and local contributions from private
foundations, lending institutions, housing agencies, real estate agencies, title companies, insurance companies,
and individuals.
Funding proposals are written by CALV’s Planning staff with the consultation of the Director of Housing
Counseling, the Executive Director of CALV and CALV’s Fiscal Department. Additionally, the Executive Director
is responsible for personal solicitation of funds to support the program.
