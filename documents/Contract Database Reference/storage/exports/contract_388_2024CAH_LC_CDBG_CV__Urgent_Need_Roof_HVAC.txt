This written strategy describes the method for the phased environmental review of urgent 
housing rehabilitation activities as detailed in the Tier 1 Broad-Level Environmental Review (Tier 
1 ER) project description.  	Each site will be evaluated using this Tier 2 Site-Specific Environmental 
Review (Tier 2 ER) strategy and checklist.  The Tier 2 ER must be used ONLY for activities described 
in the attached project description.  All documentation for the compliance factors included in 
the site-specific review must be completed prior to committing funds for the individual sites. 
A. Airport Hazards: 
There are no military airports within Lehigh County or within 15,000 feet of Lehigh 
County.  There are three (3) FAA regulated civil airports within Lehigh County or within 	2,500 feet of the county boundary – Lehigh Valley International Airport (ABE), Allentown 
Queen City Municipal Airport (XLL), and Slatington Airport (69N).  Dwelling units located 
within a Runway Protection Zone/Clear Zone (RPZ/CZ) will not be eligible for assistance 
under this urgent need rehabilitation program. 
The Tier 2 ER for each project site shall include a map illustrating the distance between 
the project site and the end of the nearest runway of the closest civilian airport, which 
will either be Dulles International Airport (IAD) or the Leesburg Executive Airport (JYO).  If 
the project site is within 2,500 feet of one of the noted civilian airports, the Tier 2 ER must 
include a map demonstrating that the project site is not located in a Runway Protection 
Zone / Clear Zone (RPZ/CZ) of either of the respective identified civilian airports.  Federal 
assistance may not be used for rehabilitation of a structure located in an RPZ/CZ. 
Documentation confirming compliance under this factor must be included in Attachment 
A of the Tier 2 Site -Specific ER.

The County’s Housing Rehabilitation Assistance Program does not fall under one of the

24 CFR Part 55, per Executive Orders 11988 and 13690, distinguishes rehabilitation within 
a FFRMS floodplain as either “minor improvements” or “substantial improvements.”  A 
“Substantial Improvement” is any repair, reconstruction, modernization, or improvement 
of a structure, including a manufactured housing unit, the cost of which equals or exceeds 
50% of the market value of the structure either, a.) Before the improvement or repair is 
started; or, b.) If the structure has been damaged, and is being restored, before the 
damage occurred 
[55.2(b)(12)]. 
The County shall use one of the following methods when determining whether a property 
receiving urgent housing rehabilitation is located in the FFRMS F	loodplain and include the 
determination and documentation in Attachment D. 
• Climate-Informed Science Approach (CISA).  	If using a local tool, data, or resources, 
ensure that the FFRMS elevation is higher than would have been determined using 
the 0.2 PFA or the FVA. The Federal Flood Standard Support Tool available online 
should be used to document this determination. 
• 0.2-Percent-Annual Chance Floodplain Approach (0.2PFA).  Where FEMA has defined 
the 0.2- percent-annual-chance floodplain, the FFRMS floodplain is the area that 
FEMA has designated as within the 0.2-percent-annual- chance floodplain. The FEMA 
Flood Map Service Center available online should be used to document this 
determination. 
• Freeboard Value Approach (FVA).  If neither CISA nor 0.2-PFA is available, for non-
critical actions, the FFRMS floodplain is the area that results from adding two feet to 
the base flood elevation as established by the effective FIRM or FIS or - if available - a 
FEMA- provided preliminary or pending FIRM or FIS or advisory base flood elevations, 
whether regulatory or informational in nature. However, an interim or preliminary 
FEMA map cannot be used if it is lower than the current FIRM or FIS. The Federal 
Flood Standard Support Tool available online should be used to document this 
determination. 
When using the Federal Flood Standard Support Tool available online, the Service 
Criticality for this urgent housing rehabilitation 	activity is be “Non-Critical” [see 24 CFR 
Part 55.2(b)(3)(i)].  The expected service life must be at least 30 years out in accordance 
with HUD related guidance.

E. Historic Preservation: 
The County of Lehigh 	will consult with the Pennsylvania State Historic Preservation Officer 
(PA SHPO) for each site -specific property to be demolished and cleared.  The PA SHPO will 
decide if the property is historic or if it is contributory to a historic district.  If the identified 
property is considered by the PA SHPO to be historic or contributory and the action of 
urgent housing rehabilitation 	of the subject property is determined to be an “Adverse 
Effect,” urgent housing rehabilitation assistance may not proceed using federal funds 
under this environmentally cleared activity. 
The following historic review determination and supporting documentation must be 
included Attachment E of the Tier 2 Site -Specific ER. 
• “NO EFFECT” – Letter from PA SHPO that concurs with the County’s determination of 
''No Effect" on historic properties, with documentation on 1) the undertaking and the 
Area of Potential Effect (APE), including photographs, maps, and drawings, as 
necessary, 2) steps taken to identify historic properties, 3) the basis for determining 
that no historic properties are present or affected, 4) evidence of tribal consultation 
if required; and 5) copies or summaries of any views provided by consulting parties 
and the public.  If the PA SHPO has not responded to a properly documented request 
for concurrence within 30 days of receipt of the request, the request will be 
documented, and the lack of response made part of the ERR. 
• “NO ADVERSE EFFECT” – Letter from PA SHPO that concurs with the County’s finding 
of ''No Adverse Effect” on historic properties, with documentation on 1) the 
undertaking and the Area of Potential Effect (APE), including photographs, maps, and 
drawings, as necessary, 2) steps taken to identify historic properties, 3) affected 
historic properties (including characteristics qualifying them for the National Register 
of Historic Places), 4) the undertaking's effects on historic properties, 5) why the 
criteria of adverse effect were not applicable (Section 800.5), 6) evidence of tribal 
consultation if required, and 7) copies or summaries of any views provided by 
consulting parties and the public.

F. Noise Abatement and Control: 
The County may rehabilitate housing units within proximity of the 24 CFR Part 51, Subpart 
B potential noise generators; specifically, within 1,000 of a major roadway, 3,000 feet of 
a railroad, or 15 miles of an airport. The civilian airports within the 15-mile radius of 
potential project sites in Lehigh 	County are the Lehigh Valley International Airport (ABE), 
Allentown Queen City Municipal Airport (XLL), and Slatington Airport (69N). 
The owners of properties to be rehabilitated that are located within the proximity of a 
major roadway, railroad, and/or airport noise generator(s) will be notified that the noise 
generated nearby may exceed the average acceptable noise level as determined by HUD. 
If any of the urgent housing rehabilitation projects are located in a noise sensitive area of 
the County, the owner will be notified of the implication of the location and may be 
required to sign a standard notification form.  	The level of noise will be determined, and 
noise mitigation measures will be encouraged in the rehabilitation of the homes that have 
elevated noise levels, but final discretion will depend on the homeowner. 
The Tier 2 ER must contain at least the completed HUD Noise Abatement and Control 
Worksheet, maps illustrating the proximity of the project site to potential noise 
generators, and HUD’s Day/Night Noise Level Electronic Assessment Tool if the project 
site is within one or more of the potential noise generator distance thresholds. 
Documentation confirming compliance under this factor must be included in Attachment 
F of the Tier 2 Site-Specific ER.

This written strategy describes the method for the phased environmental review of urgent housing rehabilitation activities as detailed in the Tier 1 Broad-Level Environmental Review (Tier 1 ER) project description.  Each site will be evaluated using this Tier 2 Site-Specific Environmental Review (Tier 2 ER) strategy and checklist.  The Tier 2 ER must be used ONLY for activities described in the attached project description.  All documentation for the compliance factors included in the site-specific review must be completed prior to committing funds for the individual sites.
Airport Hazards:
There are no military airports within Lehigh County or within 15,000 feet of Lehigh County.  There are three (3) FAA regulated civil airports within Lehigh County or within 2,500 feet of the county boundary – Lehigh Valley International Airport (ABE), Allentown Queen City Municipal Airport (XLL), and Slatington Airport (69N).  Dwelling units located within a Runway Protection Zone/Clear Zone (RPZ/CZ) will not be eligible for assistance under this urgent need rehabilitation program.
The Tier 2 ER for each project site shall include a map illustrating the distance between the project site and the end of the nearest runway of the closest civilian airport, which will either be Dulles International Airport (IAD) or the Leesburg Executive Airport (JYO).  If the project site is within 2,500 feet of one of the noted civilian airports, the Tier 2 ER must include a map demonstrating that the project site is not located in a Runway Protection Zone / Clear Zone (RPZ/CZ) of either of the respective identified civilian airports.  Federal assistance may not be used for rehabilitation of a structure located in an RPZ/CZ.
Documentation confirming compliance under this factor must be included in Attachment A of the Tier 2 Site-Specific ER.
Flood Insurance:
Applicants whose property to be rehabilitated is located in the 1-Percent-Annual Chance Floodplain Special Flood Hazard Area will be required to purchase and maintain a policy under the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP).  The amount of Flood Insurance Coverage must be equal to the total project cost (less the estimated land cost) or to the maximum limit of coverage made available by the NFIP.  Property owners must maintain coverage for the life of the subsidy tied to the rehabilitation of the property or the life of the property if funds are granted.  The Tier 2 ER must contain one of the following:
Documentation supporting the determination that the project does not require flood insurance or is excepted from flood insurance.
A FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map (FIRMette) showing that the project is not located in a 1-Percent-Annual Chance Floodplain Special Flood Hazard Area.
A FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map (FIRMette) showing that the project is located in a 1-Percent-Annual Chance Floodplain Special Flood Hazard Area along with a copy of the flood insurance policy declaration or a paid receipt for the current annual flood insurance premium and a copy of the application for flood insurance in the review.
Federal assistance may not be used for rehabilitation of a structure located in a floodway.
Documentation confirming compliance under this factor must be included in Attachment B of the Tier 2 Site-Specific ER.
Contamination and Toxic Substance:
The County of Lehigh may rehabilitate homes that contain toxic substances such as lead-based paint, asbestos, radon, mold, etc.  Each site must be inspected and tested for hazards and nuisances, including soil and water if applicable, prior to finalizing the rehabilitation scope of work and committing to the project.  At a minimum, the dwelling unit must be inspected for lead-based paint, asbestos, radon, mold, etc. and related report(s) and testing results must be included in the Tier 2 ER.  Information will be gathered, and remediation will occur to ensure that the site is safe for the intended users of the project as required at 24 CFR 58.5(i).  All Federal and State lead-based paint and asbestos regulations must be followed.  The contractor will be required to perform all requisite notifications and obtain all necessary permits related to lead-based paint and/or asbestos remediation.  As needed, additional testing and mitigation will be done on a site-specific basis.
For structures where asbestos-containing materials (ACMs) must be properly removed, transported, and disposed as a part of rehabilitation, the contractor must provide copies of contractor’s ACM certification, Asbestos Abatement and Demolition/Renovation Notification Form, permitting, disposal manifest, etc.  Said documentation must be included in Attachment C of the Tier 2 ER.
The Tier 2 ER will include a U.S. EPA online generated NEPAssist map and report identifying Recognized Environmental Conditions (RECs) within 0.5 miles of the project site.  The Tier 2 ER must also contain the completed HUD Contamination and Toxic Substances Worksheet for the property and one of the following to document compliance:
Evidence supporting a determination that the identified hazard(s) will not affect health and safety of the occupants or conflict with the intended use of the site, including any mitigation measures used.
Evidence supporting a determination that the identified hazard(s) will not affect health and safety of the occupants or conflict with the intended use of the site, including any mitigation measures used.
Documentation that the proposed project site has been cleaned up according to EPA or State standards for residential properties, which requires a letter of &#039;&#039;No Further Action&#039;&#039; (NFA) or similar documentation.
In addition, the Tier 2 ER must include the following:
Documentation supporting the determination that lead-based paint is or is not present and will or will not be disturbed during rehabilitation.  If present and will be disturbed during rehabilitation, documentation that safe renovations procedures are planned and have been ensured.
Documentation supporting the determination that asbestos-containing materials (ACM) are or are not present.  If present, documentation that the hazard has been properly mitigated.
Documentation supporting the determination that elevated levels of radon are or are not present (4 pCi/L or more).  If present, documentation that the hazard has been properly mitigated.
Documentation supporting the determination that any other contamination, toxic substance nuisance is or is not present.  If present, documentation that the hazard(s) has/have been properly mitigated.
Documentation confirming compliance under this factor must be included in Attachment C of the Tier 2 Site-Specific ER.
Floodplain Management:
The County’s Housing Rehabilitation Assistance Program does not fall under one of the 24 CFR Part 55 compliance exemptions at § 55.12.  The County may rehabilitate housing units in the Federal Floodplain Risk Management Standard (FFRMS) floodplain.  However, Federal funds cannot be used under the County’s Housing Rehabilitation Assistance Program for projects located in a floodway.
The 8-Step Decision-Making Process required under 24 CFR 55.20 for this urgent housing rehabilitation has been completed and made a part of the Tier-1 Broad-Level Review.  The Early and Final Floodplain Notices required under the 8-Step Process were published under and made a part of the Tier-1 Board-Level Review for all properties identified for urgent housing rehabilitation located in a FFRMS Floodplain.
24 CFR Part 55, per Executive Orders 11988 and 13690, distinguishes rehabilitation within a FFRMS floodplain as either “minor improvements” or “substantial improvements.”  A “Substantial Improvement” is any repair, reconstruction, modernization, or improvement of a structure, including a manufactured housing unit, the cost of which equals or exceeds 50% of the market value of the structure either, a.) Before the improvement or repair is started; or, b.) If the structure has been damaged, and is being restored, before the damage occurred [55.2(b)(12)].
The County shall use one of the following methods when determining whether a property receiving urgent housing rehabilitation is located in the FFRMS Floodplain and include the determination and documentation in Attachment D.
Climate-Informed Science Approach (CISA).  If using a local tool, data, or resources, ensure that the FFRMS elevation is higher than would have been determined using the 0.2 PFA or the FVA.  The Federal Flood Standard Support Tool available online should be used to document this determination.
0.2-Percent-Annual Chance Floodplain Approach (0.2PFA).  Where FEMA has defined the 0.2-percent-annual-chance floodplain, the FFRMS floodplain is the area that FEMA has designated as within the 0.2-percent-annual-chance floodplain.  The FEMA Flood Map Service Center available online should be used to document this determination.
Freeboard Value Approach (FVA).  If neither CISA nor 0.2-PFA is available, for non-critical actions, the FFRMS floodplain is the area that results from adding two feet to the base flood elevation as established by the effective FIRM or FIS or - if available - a FEMA-provided preliminary or pending FIRM or FIS or advisory base flood elevations, whether regulatory or informational in nature. However, an interim or preliminary FEMA map cannot be used if it is lower than the current FIRM or FIS.  The Federal Flood Standard Support Tool available online should be used to document this determination.
When using the Federal Flood Standard Support Tool available online, the Service Criticality for this urgent housing rehabilitation activity is be “Non-Critical” [see 24 CFR Part 55.2(b)(3)(i)].  The expected service life must be at least 30 years out in accordance with HUD related guidance.
Documentation confirming compliance under this factor must be included in Attachment D of the Tier 2 Site-Specific ER.
Historic Preservation:
The County of Lehigh will consult with the Pennsylvania State Historic Preservation Officer (PA SHPO) for each site-specific property to be demolished and cleared.  The PA SHPO will decide if the property is historic or if it is contributory to a historic district.  If the identified property is considered by the PA SHPO to be historic or contributory and the action of urgent housing rehabilitation of the subject property is determined to be an “Adverse Effect,” urgent housing rehabilitation assistance may not proceed using federal funds under this environmentally cleared activity.
The following historic review determination and supporting documentation must be included Attachment E of the Tier 2 Site-Specific ER.
“NO EFFECT” – Letter from PA SHPO that concurs with the County’s determination of &#039;&#039;No Effect&quot; on historic properties, with documentation on 1) the undertaking and the Area of Potential Effect (APE), including photographs, maps, and drawings, as necessary, 2) steps taken to identify historic properties, 3) the basis for determining that no historic properties are present or affected, 4) evidence of tribal consultation if required; and 5) copies or summaries of any views provided by consulting parties and the public.  If the PA SHPO has not responded to a properly documented request for concurrence within 30 days of receipt of the request, the request will be documented, and the lack of response made part of the ERR.
“NO ADVERSE EFFECT” – Letter from PA SHPO that concurs with the County’s finding of &#039;&#039;No Adverse Effect” on historic properties, with documentation on 1) the undertaking and the Area of Potential Effect (APE), including photographs, maps, and drawings, as necessary, 2) steps taken to identify historic properties, 3) affected historic properties (including characteristics qualifying them for the National Register of Historic Places), 4) the undertaking&#039;s effects on historic properties, 5) why the criteria of adverse effect were not applicable (Section 800.5), 6) evidence of tribal consultation if required, and 7) copies or summaries of any views provided by consulting parties and the public.
Documentation confirming compliance under this factor must be included in Attachment E of the Tier 2 Site-Specific ER.

Noise Abatement and Control:
The County may rehabilitate housing units within proximity of the 24 CFR Part 51, Subpart B potential noise generators; specifically, within 1,000 of a major roadway, 3,000 feet of a railroad, or 15 miles of an airport. The civilian airports within the 15-mile radius of potential project sites in Lehigh County are the Lehigh Valley International Airport (ABE), Allentown Queen City Municipal Airport (XLL), and Slatington Airport (69N).
The owners of properties to be rehabilitated that are located within the proximity of a major roadway, railroad, and/or airport noise generator(s) will be notified that the noise generated nearby may exceed the average acceptable noise level as determined by HUD. If any of the urgent housing rehabilitation projects are located in a noise sensitive area of the County, the owner will be notified of the implication of the location and may be required to sign a standard notification form.  The level of noise will be determined, and noise mitigation measures will be encouraged in the rehabilitation of the homes that have elevated noise levels, but final discretion will depend on the homeowner.
The Tier 2 ER must contain at least the completed HUD Noise Abatement and Control Worksheet, maps illustrating the proximity of the project site to potential noise generators, and HUD’s Day/Night Noise Level Electronic Assessment Tool if the project site is within one or more of the potential noise generator distance thresholds.
Documentation confirming compliance under this factor must be included in Attachment F of the Tier 2 Site-Specific ER.
