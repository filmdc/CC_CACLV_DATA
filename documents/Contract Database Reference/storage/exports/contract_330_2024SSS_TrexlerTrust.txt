How the need will be met  
The Sixth Street Shelter assists families experiencing homelessness secure safe housing through family-centered 
services, in-house programming, and referrals to community resources while advocating that affordable housing 
is a human right. Located in Center City Allentown, the Sixth Street Shelter provides safe, short-term housing 
for families of any configuration experiencing homelessness. The property has 25 private apartments, a Family 
Resource Center, a playground, and a full-choice food pantry for residents. 
 
The Shelter provides each family with a furnished apartment and supportive services, including individualized 
case management. Staff work to connect them with the necessary resources to address their individual needs, such 
as financial literacy and parenting classes, access to childcare and healthcare, and mental health support. The 
Shelter has a comprehensive network of community partners where we can refer residents for additional support. 
During their residency, the family develops a Family Service Plan with their case manager, setting household, 
budgeting, educational, and vocational goals and seeking suitable housing and employment. Our Benchmark 
Family Care Initiative aims to ensure that all residents have or obtain medical insurance and are connected to 
appropriate providers so that they can achieve a list of benchmarks related to their relevant medical, dental, and 
mental health care, as well as track financial goals. We have expanded our educational and vocational 
programming at the Shelter to increase residents’ earning capacity, which will help them to remain stably housed 
when they move out of the Shelter and into permanent housing. Following the family’s move back into the 
community, the Shelter staff maintain contact with them whenever possible to offer additional assistance and to 
determine how well they are continuing to move toward self-sufficiency.  
 
Methods and Measurement Standards for Success 
Objectives to be accomplished by June 30, 2024 include: Goal: Sixth Street Shelter will provide short-term 
transitional housing, case management and other supportive services for families with children who are 
experiencing homelessness and help them achieve their self-sufficiency goals. 
 
Performance Targets: 
A. 85 families will reside at the Shelter and will develop Family Service Plans based on their housing, 
educational, and financial goals; 
B. 50% of families will save money toward rent or a security deposit upon leaving the Shelter; 
C. 75% of families will achieve their Family Service Plan goals; 
D. 100% of families will create a written budget to understand credit and money management as part of their 
goal plan; 
E. 15% of families will be referred to CareerLink and obtain services to assist with vocational development 
and the job search; 
F. 25% of families will move into affordable housing upon leaving the Shelter. 
 
Each year, a Work Plan is developed with measurable goals and performance targets (listed above) that guide the 
activities of the program and are designed to have a positive impact on Shelter families and the larger community. 
The Work Plan is evaluated twice a year and then approved by Community Action’s full Board of Directors. 
 
The Sixth Street Shelter utilizes the Homeless Management Information System (HMIS) to track and analyze 
data. HMIS is used by the Eastern Pennsylvania Continuum of Care/Lehigh Valley Regional Homeless Advisory 
Board (of which the Shelter is an active member) and administered by the Pennsylvania Department of 
Community and Economic Development. Sixth Street Shelter also participates in Connect to Home: the

Coordinated Entry (CE) System of the Eastern PA Continuum of Care, which coordinates access, assessment, 
prioritization and referral for housing and supports for people experiencing and at imminent risk of homelessness 
in the community. A component of the CE System is designed to capture data on people experiencing 
homelessness across the Lehigh Valley and develop community-wide strategies to meet that need. The information 
gathered from HMIS becomes the beginning of a confidential case file that contains basic information on the 
family and documents the history of their progression through the program; all such files are always secured in 
locked filing cabinets. Sixth Street Shelter also utilizes a client management information system called 
ClientTrack (which interfaces with HMIS) to track information not encompassed by HMIS.  
 
In addition to the data collection methods noted above, Sixth Street Shelter gathers qualitative data as part of a 
strategy to document the positive long-term impact of the Shelter in the community. The Shelter is able to track 
the progress of former residents because staff check in and continue to work with them. One example is Yasmin 
and her husband Ben (not their real names), who arrived at the Shelter with their two sons after a wrongful eviction 
left them homeless and couch-surfing. Determined to move forward, they worked hard to save $3,000. Their case 
manager helped to secure a unit for the family at a local apartment complex, arrange school transportation for the 
boys, and ensure that they had necessities for their new place. The family’s commitment and the support of the 
Shelter team enabled the family to settle into their new home, finally finding stability after a year of uncertainty.  
 
How the organization is best positioned to meet the need  
The Sixth Street Shelter has been helping families experiencing homelessness since 1984. It is the largest family 
shelter in the City of Allentown. It is unique because it is the only shelter in the region where entire families of 
any configuration (including those with fathers and teenaged boys, multi-generational families, and those from 
the LGBTQ+ community) stay together in their own private apartments. The assistance detailed above helps each 
family begin to address the challenges that led to their homelessness. We added a Housing Innovation Specialist 
to our staff to work with local landlords to identify affordable housing options and facilitate faster placements for 
families. The Sixth Street Shelter participates in the community effort to end homelessness as an active member 
of the Lehigh Valley Regional Homeless Advisory Board and the Eastern Pennsylvania Continuum of Care.
