Description: 
St. Paul’s United Church of Christ works for “righteousness, justice, and peace” in our community and 
has been a vital partner to Community Action Lehigh Valley for many years. The Sixth Street Shelter, a 
long-standing program of Community Action, also pursues these values by providing an essential service 
to the community as a place for families experiencing homelessness to find a temporary home and 
supportive services as they increase their self-sufficiency and stability on their path forward.  
 
Located in Center City Allentown, the Sixth Street Shelter provides safe, short-term housing for low-
income families with at least one child under the age of 18 who have become homeless. The property has 
25 private apartments, a Family Resource Center, playground, and full-choice food pantry for residents. 
The Sixth Street Shelter is unique because it is the only shelter in the area where entire families of any 
configuration (including those with fathers and teenaged boys, multi-generational families, and those from 
the LGBTQ+ community) stay together in their own apartments. Each family is afforded the dignity of 
having their own kitchen, bathroom, and bedroom rather than having to share facilities with other families, 
as is common with traditional shelters. Through coordinated efforts as part of the Lehigh Valley Regional 
Homeless Advisory Board (LVRHAB), the Sixth Street Shelter meets the needs of the community by 
providing shelter housing specifically for families. 
 
For many families, the stay at the Sixth Street Shelter enables them to begin to stabilize their income and 
address the problems that led to their homelessness. The families who come to the Shelter do so for various

reasons: low-paying jobs, lack of affordable housing, mental and/or physical health crises, domestic 
violence, and lack of access to reliable transportation, healthcare, and/or childcare. The Shelter provides 
each family with a fully furnished apartment and supportive services, including individualized case 
management. Staff work to connect them with the necessary resources to address their individual needs, 
such as financial literacy and parenting classes, access to childcare and healthcare, and mental health 
support. The Shelter has a comprehensive network of community partners where we can refer residents 
for additional services and resources. During their residency, the family develops a Family Service Plan 
with their caseworker, setting household, budgeting, educational, and vocational goals and seeking 
suitable housing and employment. We have started a new Benchmark Family Care Initiative, which aims 
to ensure that all residents have or obtain medical insurance and are connected to appropriate providers so 
that they can achieve a list of benchmarks related to their relevant medical, dental, and mental health care, 
as well as track financial goals. Other recent initiatives include adding a Housing Innovation Specialist to 
our staff to work with our residents and local landlords in order to identify affordable housing options and 
facilitate faster placements for families, and expanding our educational and vocational programming at 
the Sixth Street Shelter to increase residents’ earning capacity, which will help them to remain stably 
housed when they move out of the Shelter and into permanent housing. Staff maintain contact with the 
family following their move into permanent housing to offer additional assistance and determine how well 
they are continuing to move toward self-sufficiency.  
 
Housing is a key social determinant of health, as it influences one’s mental and physical health, well-
being, and quality of life. The negative health impacts of homelessness are well documented, including 
short- and long-term adverse health outcomes. According to the Housing Alliance of Pennsylvania, 
“Experiencing homelessness leads to new and exacerbates existing health problems,” through the lack of 
access to “clean water, refrigeration, shelter from the elements, and safety from harm.” Long-term impacts 
include chronic illness, over-reliance on costly and acute health care services, and significantly higher 
morbidity. The National Low Income Housing Coalition cites that “People experiencing homelessness die 
on average 12 years sooner than people in the general population in the U.S.” Case Managers at the Shelter 
work to ensure that all residents have or obtain medical insurance and are connected to appropriate health 
care providers. We collaborate with local partners and health professionals to offer educational sessions 
on healthy eating and general wellness, and provide access to free health services, such as vaccinations 
and comprehensive eye exams and prescription glasses. Each family has access to an on-site, full-choice 
food pantry and their own kitchen in their apartment so that they can store fresh foods and prepare their 
own meals. These supportive services offered to residents aim to increase knowledge, answer questions, 
facilitate access to resources, and improve health outcomes, which sets a foundation for achieving stable 
housing and greater well-being. Homelessness is often traumatic, and the Sixth Street Shelter provides 
resources and services to help mitigate these impacts and set families on a path out of homelessness. 
 
We are seeking a grant from St. Paul’s UCC for our Supportive Services Fund for residents. These funds 
are used for a variety of expenses that may arise for residents during their stay at the Shelter. This may 
include tuition assistance, books and course materials, computers, rental application fees, establishing 
renters’ insurance, the acquisition or replacement of documents (Social Security cards, birth certificates, 
and driver’s licenses), childcare, food, transportation costs, hygiene items, cleaning supplies, emergency 
assistance, and diapers and other supplies for children. In short, the Supportive Services Fund removes 
barriers to self-sufficiency to assist our families in securing housing and achieving greater stability.

Since 1985, the Sixth Street Shelter has been helping families on their path out of homelessness. In 
addition to the comprehensive services provided to Shelter residents, Sixth Street Shelter has become a 
resource center for former residents and people from the surrounding neighborhood. Community members 
drop by regularly looking for information regarding community programs and resources, which are always 
readily available. We also welcome volunteers from the community. The Sixth Street Shelter has proven 
to be a vital resource in the community, and we value our partners who help us in this endeavor.
