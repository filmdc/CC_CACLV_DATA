*Mission Statement and
History
Community Action Development Corporation of Bethlehem,
doing business as Community Action Development
Bethlehem (CADB), is a subsidiary of Community Action
Committee of the Lehigh Valley (CALV) located in south
Bethlehem, Pennsylvania. Since its incorporation on
October 6, 1997, CADB has been dedicated to improving the
quality of life in Bethlehem through resident-led
neighborhood revitalization initiatives focused on
community-identified priorities.
CADB's mission states: "Based on a belief in economic and
social justice, CADB improves the quality of life in south
Bethlehem by fostering economic opportunity, promoting
community development, and empowering residents to
actively participate in the decision-making process
regarding the future of our diverse community."

*Organization's Major
Achievements
In 2001, CADB applied for, and was recognized as, a
Neighborhood Partnership Program (NPP) through the
Commonwealth of Pennsylvania Department of Community
and Economic Development. Southside Vision 2014 made
great progress toward transforming the south Bethlehem
community through strategic partnerships. In addition to
$1,942,602 contributed by corporate partners, Southside
Vision leveraged over $14 million in private and public
funds.
In 2014, CADB was granted a new NPP, titled Southside
Vision 20/20, which committed corporate funding of
$200,000 per year for six years. Like its predecessor, this

new plan was created through a comprehensive public
input process and identified five areas in which to focus:
economic sustainability, housing, public spaces, safety and
well-being, and community engagement and
communication.
In 2021, CADB was approved for a new 6-year NPP, titled
Southside Tomorrow 2021-2027, which committed
corporate funding of $325,000 per year for six years. This
new plan was created through a comprehensive public
input process and review of existing community plans,
which led to the identification of equitable community
development as the theme of the new plan and the
following focus areas: housing revitalization, green and
public spaces, and communication and connectivity.
Year 3 of the Southside Tomorrow program recently
concluded, and the projects prioritized through extensive
committee and public input processes for Year 4 are
currently underway.

Description
CADB is a community development organization dedicated
to working with the residents and business owners of south
Bethlehem to create sustainable economic development
and community revitalization. It is located at 409 E. 4th St.
in south Bethlehem, in a neighborhood known as the Four
Blocks International area.
Staff work diligently to develop relationships with and
among residents and stakeholders of south Bethlehem.
They learn about neighborhood needs from the residents,
identify residents interested in taking an active role in
ownership of their neighborhoods, develop neighborhood
leaders, and organize residents to use their collective power
to solve neighborhood problems. The goal for CADB staff is
to empower the individuals of south Bethlehem to have a
greater voice in their community. In addition, staff provide
direct support to low-to moderate-income tenants and
homeowners facing housing challenges.
Currently, three staff members carry out the work of CADB:
the Director, the Tenant Advocate and Organizer, and the
Administrative and Community Resource Coordinator.
CADB also subcontracts with CALV's Community Action
Homes program to provide the construction-end of CADB's
façade work. CADB staff is overseen by the Associate
Executive Director for Community Services and the
Executive Director of CALV. Leadership is provided by the
Board of Directors, which is comprised of Southside
residents, small business owners, and area civic leaders
interested in improving the quality of life in south
Bethlehem.

*Goals and ObjectivesThrough the Southside Tomorrow program, CADB carries
out a number of projects that are created by and for the
residents of south Bethlehem. These projects align with the
following goals and strategies outlined in the Southside
Tomorrow Master Plan:
Housing Affordability & Quality:
Goal:
South Bethlehem will have a variety of quality, affordable
renter- and owner-occupied housing options and
encourage development that meets the needs and wants of
current and new residents, alike.
Strategies:
-Provide opportunities to engage and empower the
community to have a voice in the decisions regarding new
development and accessibility of affordable housing.
-Provide opportunities for mixed-use, adaptive reuse
development to increase quality, affordable rental housing.
-Provide opportunities for adaptive reuse development and
rehabilitation to increase and maintain quality, affordable
owner-occupied housing.
-Invest in data collection initiatives that expand an
understanding of how past, present, and future
development in south Bethlehem impacts its residents.
Public & Green Spaces

Goal:
Preserve the integrity of existing public and green spaces
while investing in practical and creative utilization of those
spaces to foster community engagement while addressing
community concerns.
Strategies:
-Implement neighborhood programs that connect residents
with opportunities that build a sense of creative
placemaking and community pride with communal spaces.
-Utilize green spaces to address concerns of food insecurity
and neighborhood access to fresh fruits and vegetables.
-Address community concerns for youth engagement by
expanding opportunities that connect youth with
public/green spaces.
-Invest in community image by improving upon avenues for
cleanliness and appearance.
Communication & Connectivity
Goal:
Build a sense of collective community identity through
modernized initiatives that enhance residents' belonging,
pride, and fellowship.
Strategies:
-Create, maintain, and build on existing initiatives that
enhance community connectivity and unification.
-Foster neighborhood connectedness through community
building events and celebratory gatherings.
-Continue and enhance community input process to further
garner ongoing community feedback.  
*Identify ProblemIn 1943, at the height of World War II, Bethlehem Steel
employed over 31,000 people in the city and nearly 300,000
nationwide. The Southside was a key commercial
destination, the streets bustling with shoppers, throughout
the 1900s. When Bethlehem Steel ceased production in
1995 (and declared bankruptcy in 2003), it left a community

in crisis. High-paying manufacturing jobs were replaced by
low-paying retail and service jobs, particularly in south
Bethlehem.
Today, south Bethlehem remains a community of diverse
income and ethnicities. However, there has been an
increase in new development to south Bethlehem since
2012, the majority of which are luxury apartments. With
housing costs and development rising in south Bethlehem,
along with a decrease in the availability of affordable
housing options, this has caused increased strain on low- to
moderate-income residents who worry that they may no
longer be able to afford their housing costs or have the
proper amenities that correspond with their wants and
needs.
The income of Bethlehem is lower than the state median;
this is even more pronounced in south Bethlehem.
According to the American Community Survey 5-Year
Estimates (2018-2022), the median household income of
$46,230 for south Bethlehem considerably lagged behind
that of the city ($62,072), Northampton County ($82,201),
and the state ($73,170). Furthermore, the household
median income for south Bethlehem's five census tracts
was only $46,230 (109), $27,743 (110), $70,536 (111)
$38,927 (112), and $55,018 (113) respectively. In south
Bethlehem, 25.7% of residents live below the poverty level
(compared to 18.3% in Bethlehem and 9.2% in
Northampton County). Census tracts 110, 111, and 112 in
south Bethlehem are considered by the U.S. Census Bureau
as "poverty areas," which are defined as "census tracts or
block numbering areas (BNA's) where at least 20 percent of
residents were below the poverty level."
This reality furthers the concern that the
interconnectedness of the community may be lost if there
is not a focus and emphasis placed upon Equitable
Community Development, which is grounded in community
engagement, guided by the voices that live and work in
south Bethlehem, and driven by the vision and needs of
those who are most affected by the changing landscape of
the district. Therefore, CADB seeks to address these
economic disparities through its community and economic
development efforts.  

*Background and RationaleCADB's Southside Tomorrow is a unifying, equitable
community development initiative that will enhance the
quality of life, change the power dynamic, and build a
meaningful relationship between the community and south
Bethlehem's continued development. Southside Tomorrow
presents a framework by which CADB will provide equitable
community development opportunities through housing
revitalization, public and green spaces, and communication
and connectivity, which are three focus areas that have
been prioritized through a comprehensive public input
process and review of existing community plans. CADB was
recently approved for the fourth year of a six-year
Neighborhood Partnership Program (NPP) in partnership
with the Pennsylvania Department of Community and
Economic Development.
Southside Tomorrow ensures that residents are not only
included in spaces where decisions are made that affect the
livelihood of their community, but that their collective
power is affirmed and respected. After all, sustainable
development depends on the existence of a capable civic
body, whose determined involvement in the social life
requires community empowerment. By improving housing
affordability and quality, public and green spaces, and
communication and connectivity through community
empowerment, engagement, and ownership, it is this plan's
hope that south Bethlehem is able to maintain and build on
its unique assets of inclusivity and diversity to modernize
and preserve the integrity of the south Bethlehem
community in an equitable manner.  
*Alignment with our
Guidelines
Community Action Development Bethlehem (CADB) works
with residents to create sustainable economic development
and community revitalization. CADB carries out its mission
through community organizing activities and services that
foster community ownership. Community ownership

problem-solving processes that affect the community.
Leadership is provided by the Board of Directors of CADB,
which is comprised of Southside residents, small business
owners, and civic leaders. The goal of CADB staff is to
empower residents, many of whom are low-to-moderate-

encouraging and assisting them to attain more economic
independence.
As part of the six-year plan, Southside Tomorrow, CADB will
build on the revitalization work done in south Bethlehem
and further focus on equitable community development,
emphasizing housing revitalization, green and public
spaces, and communication and connectivity.

Implementation and Evaluation
*Timeline and MilestonesIn March of each year, the working committees of
Southside Tomorrow meet to discuss the development of
projects for CADCB's new Neighborhood Partnership
Program. With the recommendations from the plan's
community input process and of CADB staff, the Steering
Committee drafts a schedule of the activities for the
following program year. In April, Steering Committee
members deliberate on these proposed activities and arrive
at the projects to be implemented. Once this process is
completed, CADB is charged with the implementation of
each project between the close of the fiscal year (June 30th)
and September 30th of the following year. 
*Measuring SuccessEvaluation of all Community Action programs begins with
recordkeeping and data collection, which enables
Community Action to track the progress of program

participants during the time they were assisted by the
agency and afterward.
Agency wide, evaluation and measurement of success
begins with Community Action's Strategic Plan (2024-2026)
that defines Community Action's mission, values, strategic
assumptions, strategic direction, and three-year strategy for
mission implementation. To ensure that the strategic plan
is carried out, the agency develops an Annual Work Plan.
Community Action's Annual Work Plan consists of
measurable goals and performance targets that guide the
services and activities for every one of Community Action's
programs and subsidiaries. It is evaluated twice yearly to
measure the effectiveness of services and program
outcomes. This evaluation is conducted by the Program
Development and Evaluation Committee of Community
Action's Board of Directors and is then approved by
Community Action's full Board of Directors.
Success is also measured through contract compliance
activities conducted by Community Action's Planning,
Compliance, and Fiscal Departments. The Fiscal
Department is responsible for meeting all contractual fiscal
obligations that may include submission of time sheets,
invoices, and fiscal reports to funders. The Planning and
Compliance Departments provide contract compliance
oversight by ensuring that program directors are familiar
with their contracts, program objectives, and reporting
requirements. The Planning and Compliance Departments
also conduct periodic internal monitoring of all programs
that includes review of client files, data collection processes,
and reports, and assist the program director in preparing
for monitoring visits by funders. Lastly, the performance of
all staff is evaluated annually by their immediate
supervisors.

*Sustainability PlanCADB and its parent organization, CALV, employ a multi-
pronged approach to sustainability that includes:
Fund Development: Both programs are sustained by a
multi-faceted development strategy that includes funding
from corporations, foundations, government entities, and
individual donors. CALV's Executive Director is responsible
for personal solicitation of funds. The Planning Department
is responsible for identifying new funding opportunities,
managing a 12-month calendar of due dates for proposals
and reports for existing donors, writing and submitting
strategic grant proposals to corporations, foundations, and
government entities, and tracking the progress of funding
requests in an internal database.
Contract Compliance: CALV values responsible stewardship
of the funds entrusted to us and the resources available to
us. CALV's Planning and Fiscal Departments conduct
contract compliance activities. The Fiscal Department is
responsible for meeting all contractual fiscal obligations.
Planning provides contract compliance oversight by
ensuring that program directors are familiar with their
contracts, program objectives, and reporting requirements.
Planning also conducts periodic internal monitoring of all

programs, including periodic review of client files, data
collection processes, and reports.
CADB Board: CADB has a board of directors that consists of
community residents, local business owners, nonprofit
organizations, representatives from Lehigh University,
Northampton Community College, the City of Bethlehem,
and contributors. The Board meets regularly to review the
operations and budget of the program and make
suggestions for improvements as needed.
