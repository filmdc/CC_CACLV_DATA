*Mission Statement and
History
The mission of Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and
Northeast Pennsylvania is to obtain food and distribute it to
people in need through area non-profits and to provide resources
for education and advocacy to end hunger. We provide nutritious
food, offer nutrition education, advocate to end food insecurity,
dismantle barriers to create access, and work to attain food
justice. We respect and preserve the dignity of each of our
neighbors.

Second Harvest, a program of Community Action Lehigh Valley,
began its mission to end food insecurity in 1982, as the Lehigh
Valley Food Bank. Initially, distributions were made from the back
of a rented truck to a network of about 15 nonprofit agencies.
Since September 2015, Second Harvest Food Bank has been
operating from a 65,000 square-foot warehouse in East Allen
Township.
Community Action Lehigh Valley (CALV) was established in 1965 as
a part of Lyndon B. Johnson's War on Poverty. The mission of CALV
is to improve the quality of life in the Lehigh Valley by building a
community in which all people have access to economic
opportunity, the ability to pursue that opportunity, and a voice in
the decisions that affect their lives. Today, CALV operates 13
programs and 3 subsidiary corporations. Annually, CALV provides
services to 132,000+ individuals.

*Project/Program DescriptionSecond Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast PA
(SHFB) is a program of Community Action Lehigh Valley, and a
member of Feeding America -- the nation's largest charitable food
network. SHFB serves Lehigh, Northampton, Carbon, Monroe,
Pike, and Wayne counties.
Last year, SHFB distributed 14.7 million pounds of food to our
network of 200+ nonprofit agencies, a 44% increase from the
previous year. Included in this total is 6.7 million pounds of fresh
produce, perishable protein, and fresh dairy products (46% of total
distribution). Annually, we rescue more than 8 million pounds of
food from ending up in a landfill. On average, the SHFB network
serves 87,000+ people each month - a 52% increase from last year.
Access to nutritious food for under-resourced populations is an
immediate need. Nationally, the emergency food network is
serving historic volumes of people. Current inflation is causing
many families to visit food pantries for the first time. Often, the
only fresh, nutritious foods a family facing food insecurity has
access to come from food pantries.
The distribution of fresh and nutritious foods is tantamount to our
mission, and we continue to make great strides in sourcing and
distributing more of these foods. As part of our larger nutrition
strategy, we developed and implemented a Food and Nutrition

Policy based on Healthy Eating Research (HER) guidelines (which
were created in partnership between the Robert Wood Johnson
Foundation, Duke University, and the charitable food network).
Our Nutrition Policy prioritizes and deprioritizes food by
nutritional content. Our Nutrition Policy now informs all aspects of
our food sourcing and distribution strategies.
In addition to food distribution to our network, SHFB operates
several programs aimed at improving the health of our community
and working toward a more just and equitable local food system.
This includes our nutrition education program, SNAP outreach,
supplemental food programs for seniors and military families, our
farm business incubator program, our community garden
program, and growing produce for direct distribution to pantries.

*Beneficiaries On average, the SHFB network serves 87,000+ people each month
- a 52% increase from last year. More than half of the people
served are children and seniors. Our pantry network provides
food to households with incomes at or below 185% of the federal
poverty level. This equates to an annual income of $55,500 for a
household of four.
Access to nutritious food for under-resourced populations is
critical. According to Feeding America's Map the Meal Gap 2021,
the food insecurity rate for SHFB service area is 8.9% (90,970
people). The rate is higher for children, at 12% (25,470 children).
Populations facing food insecurity are strongly correlated with
those that have been impacted by systemic racism and economic
exploitation. The food insecurity rate across the SHFB service area
is higher for Black (15%) and Hispanic (18%) individuals, as
compared to their white counterparts (7%).

It is essential that SHFB continues to provide nutritious food to
low-income and under-resourced communities, because often the
only fresh, nutritious foods that families have access to come from
our food pantries.
 
Implementation and Evaluation
*Measuring SuccessAnnually, each program of Community Action, including SHFB,
develops a work plan with measurable performance targets that
guide the activities of our programs and are designed to have a
positive impact on the community. Performance targets are
monitored and evaluated by the Community Action Board of
Directors. Progress is monitored at the six-month mark, and all
performance targets are fully evaluated at the end of the program
year. Work plan objectives to be accomplished by June 30, 2024,
include:
GOAL 1: SHFB will source enough food to serve our six-county
region, prioritizing fresh, nutritionally dense and neighbor-
responsive food.  
Performance Targets:
A. Source 15,000,000 pounds of food for distribution to our
agencies.
B. Source 6,000,000 pounds of fresh, perishable produce, protein
and dairy.
C. Donate fresh produce grown at The Seed Farm to the pantry
network.
D. Ensure that less than 15% of SHFB monthly (ranked) inventory
is composed of "choose rarely" foods monthly. 
E. Maintain donated product at 55% or more of total pounds
sourced.
F. Increase agency enabled pickups by 7% over FY2023 pounds.
Goal 2: SHFB will work with our network of 200+ agencies to
deliver food for distribution to neighbors facing food insecurity,
focusing on equitable distribution and vulnerable populations.
Performance Targets:
A. Distribute 13,280,00 pounds of food to people in need.

B. Provide (at a minimum) 53.8 Meals Per Person in Need (MPIN) in
each of the six counties.
C. Provide a three-day supply of nutritious food each month to
2,450 senior citizens who qualify for the PA Senior Box program. 
D. Provide 560 Military Share boxes per month, with coverage in
each county served by SHFB. 
E. Distribute 500,000 meals through school pantries.
F. Pilot Service Insights-Meal Connect at 10 pantries.
Goal 3: SHFB will actively work to empower neighbors and partner
agencies, by providing technical assistance, resources, and
educational opportunities.
Performance Targets:
A. Provide SNAP (formerly Food Stamps) application assistance for
at least 300 households.
B. Engage neighbors through nutrition education programming,
including cooking instruction, taste-testing and Healthy Pantry
Initiative programming.
C. Engage at least 50% of network pantry directors in training and
technical assistance programming.
D. Engage volunteers and community members with Growing
Partners programming.
E. Provide at least 45.5 hours of farm management training to new
farmers on skills/knowledge 	areas identified as critical to new
farmer success.
F. Offer 2 training opportunities (workshop, course, or
demonstration) in collaboration with at least one partner
organization, such as Penn State Extension, Pennsylvania
Association for Sustainable Agriculture, or PA FarmLink.
GOAL 4: SHFB will comply with food safety and contractual
obligations of Feeding America to ensure the integrity of the food
bank and partner agencies.
Performance Targets:
A. Monthly assessments of warehouse facilities will comply with
AIB standards. 
B. Agency monitoring will occur consistently throughout the year
to confirm compliance with contract requirements, including

verification that agencies are obtaining eligibility determination
from food recipients.
C. Maintain accurate inventory within less than 2% error.
Additionally, SHFB solicits feedback from our partner agencies and
food pantry participants, in the form of formal surveys,
conversational feedback, and committee work. The results and
feedback guide SHFB's strategic planning, program development,
and service provision. We are working toward a participant-centric
model of operation. We are taking the necessary, often tough,
steps to shift power to the people we serve.
