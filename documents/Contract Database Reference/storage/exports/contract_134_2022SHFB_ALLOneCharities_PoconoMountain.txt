Narrative of the Project/Program. Include a summary of organization&#039;s history, goals &amp;achievements. Max 2,000 words*
Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast Pennsylvania (SHFB or Second Harvest), a long-standing program of Community Action Lehigh Valley (CALV), began our mission to end food insecurity in October 1982, as the Lehigh Valley Food Bank. Initially, distributions were made from the back of a rented truck through a network of about 15 nonprofit member agencies. In the mid-1980s, we changed our name, became a member of what is now Feeding America, and expanded our territory to six counties – Carbon, Lehigh, Monroe, Northampton, Pike and Wayne. Eventually, Second Harvest outgrew the facility we occupied for 22 years and, in September 2015, relocated to our 65,000 square-foot warehouse in East Allen Township in Northampton County.
CALV, Second Harvest’s parent agency, was incorporated on December 20, 1965, as a Pennsylvania not-for-profit corporation and designated as a 501(c)3 organization to work with and for the low-income residents of the Lehigh Valley to combat poverty. The mission of CALV is “to improve the quality of life in the Lehigh Valley by building a community in which all people have access to economic opportunity, the ability to pursue that opportunity, and a voice in the decisions that affect their lives.” CALV stives to meet basic needs while encouraging self-sufficiency; work towards the economic and community development of low-income neighborhoods; engage in community planning and advocacy efforts with community participation; and establish partnerships with community organizations, local government, and the private sector.
Through a network of 200+ nonprofit community partners in our six-county service region (including food pantries, meal centers, shelters, childcare agencies, senior living centers, and addiction treatment programs), Second Harvest currently serves over 70,000 people per month. In addition to food distribution, Second Harvest offers supplemental nutrition programs for children and seniors, SNAP (formerly known as Food Stamps) outreach and application assistance, Share Our Strength’s Cooking Matters®, and advocacy initiatives. Second Harvest also distributes supplemental food to 340 active duty, reserve, and veteran military personnel on a monthly basis through its MilitaryShare program. Finally, Second Harvest operates an incubator farm to train a new generation of farmers, while also increasing the amount of fresh food in the local economy.
SHFB distributes nutritious food, breaks barriers to create access, provides nutrition education, advocates to end food insecurity, and works to attain food justice. Our mission is “to obtain food and distribute it to people in need through area non-profits, and to provide resources for education and advocacy to end hunger.” In the last fiscal year, Second Harvest: 
Distributed 10 million pounds of food through our network, 35% of which was fresh produce, perishable protein, and fresh dairy products.
Distributed 23,873 SUNShine (“Serving Under-Nourished Seniors”) supplemental food packages to low-income seniors, which was made possible by the federally-funded Commodities Supplemental Food Program.
Assisted 174 households in completing Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) applications and provided assistance with bureaucratic procedures in the application process to 338 additional households.
Conducted 11 Cooking Matters classes that teach cooking, nutrition and budgeting skills to low-income families and seniors for 158 participants at locations throughout Lehigh and Northampton counties.
The mission of MilitaryShare is “to serve those who served us” by providing access to nutritious food, with a goal of improving health outcomes and easing financial strain among low-income active duty, reserve, and retired military members and their families. The goal of the MilitaryShare program is to provide military members in need with a monthly share of fresh and shelf-stable food. In the last year (July 2021-June 2022), Second Harvest has distributed a cumulative 2,228 shares and 43,903 pounds of fresh food through MilitaryShare. The program operates at seven sites throughout all six counties that Second Harvest serves; specific locations were selected due to their concentrations of active duty and reserve personnel and veterans. Participants can be veterans, reserve, or retired from any branch of the military. To participate, an individual’s income must be at or below 150% of the Federal Poverty Line. Each month, qualifying military members receive a share of fresh and shelf-stable food in addition to their usual food pantry distribution. Each share contains fresh produce, meat, dairy, and eggs, as well as an assortment of shelf-stable foods such as pasta, beans, peanut butter, oatmeal, and canned meats, fruits and vegetables. 
The program has grown rapidly since the beginning of 2022, reflecting an overall increase in need among veterans. In January 2022, Second Harvest distributed 180 shares amounting to nearly 4,000 pounds of fresh food. By May 2022—just four months later—total distribution had increased to 340 shares and 6,120 pounds of fresh food per month, approximately doubling the size of the program. While we are excited that MilitaryShare has been able to grow dramatically to keep pace with increased need, we recognize that it is still not enough, and we will continue to deploy our funding and resources as efficiently as possible to ensure maximum impact.

Describe the regional need, issue, or opportunity that your proposal addresses. What is the level of involvement of the community-at-large?*
SHFB currently serves 70,000 people a month, which is up 58% since January 2022. Inflation, coupled with the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, is having a tremendous impact on families. For people experiencing food insecurity, the dual blow of these two crises has exacerbated pre-existing challenges of access and affordability.
These challenges are especially pronounced for veterans. The MilitaryShare model was developed to address the specific challenges faced by military members, and there is significant potential to expand the MilitaryShare network of pantries within Monroe County. According to the U.S. Census Bureau’s American Community Survey 5-Year Estimates (2016-2020), there are over 11,000 veterans in Monroe County and 7.4% of them live in poverty. That means that nearly 1,000 veterans a month in Monroe County are struggling with sufficient access to nutritious food for themselves and their families. Active-duty military members and their families are among the fastest growing populations living in poverty. For veterans between the ages of 18-34 in Monroe County, over 14% are living at or below the poverty level and that number is rising. According to the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA), active and reserve military members and veterans are one of the most difficult groups to reach through the emergency food network due to their pride. To address this need, the USDA, Feeding America, and other regional food banks have created food distribution programs that break down these barriers. Second Harvest’s MilitaryShare is a part of this effort and was designed to address the food insecurity needs of veterans and other military personnel to promote health and financial stability. 
Second Harvest partners with our member agencies, local veterans’ groups, county veterans’ affairs departments and military and reserve outlets to distribute MilitaryShare through pantries and other sites in each county, with a focus on areas with concentrations of active duty and reserve personnel and veterans. The most effective outreach can be accomplished by meeting veterans where they are. That is why Second Harvest is making a concerted push to partner with more veterans’ organizations, with a recent focus on American Legions. Referrals from such organizations can make the difference between those in need getting connected with our program and being able to put food on the table or remaining unaware.  
Currently, the food bank only has one partner organization for its MilitaryShare program in Monroe County—Feeding Families Ministry in Tobyhanna, PA. However, funding from PMCF would make it possible to expand our partnerships and distributions within Monroe County, thus serving a larger portion of the veteran community. Future potential partners include Valor Clinic Foundation in Kunkletown, PA and the American Legion located in Stroudsburg, PA.

Describe all collaborative relationships (financial and programmatic) with other organizations for this project/program.*
Second Harvest partners with our member agencies, local veterans’ groups, county veterans’ affairs departments and military and reserve outlets to distribute MilitaryShare through pantries in each county, with a focus on areas with concentrations of active duty and reserve personnel and veterans. By working with organizations that have existing relationships with military members, Second Harvest is able to break down barriers and establish trust with military members.

Having regular access to our data expertly analyzed and presented to us means that we make informed decisions regarding improvements to our service provision. Further, our ability to connect with our foodbanking peers, and understand national trends, means that we are often able to prepare in time to meet the changing needs of our own local community. Second Harvest also takes advantage of grant opportunities through Feeding America that are not available to food banks outside the network.
Our staff is incredibly engaged in the community and have deep connections with pantry directors, volunteers, and the people we serve. The open communication lines between what is happening “on the ground” allows us to pivot programming to address unforeseen challenges and needs, while the quantitative data we collect at the food bank allows us to understand whether those needs are being met. Clear channels for communication are paramount to our work. Although staff capacity is limited – particularly at a time when we are seeing such a rapid increase in demand – all staff, including our MilitaryShare Coordinator, keep others updated regularly with anecdotal and observational data.

Success will be measured by the successful expansion of the MilitaryShare program to provide 100 shares monthly at Feeding Families and develop new partnerships with other Monroe County veterans’ organizations such as Valor Foundation Clinic in Kunkletown, PA and the American Legion in Stroudsburg, PA.
