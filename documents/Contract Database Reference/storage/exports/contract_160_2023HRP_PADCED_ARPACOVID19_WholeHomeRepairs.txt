The purpose of this document is to provide DCED’s general understanding of the administration of the 
Federal State and Local Fiscal Recovery Fund (SLFRF) through the Pennsylvania (DCED) Whole-Home 
Repairs Program. While we can speak directly to the Pennsylvania fiscal code enacting the Whole-
Home Repairs program, many of the questions addressed within this document can only be answered 
through the guidance of the U.S. Treasury (the SLFRF office). It’s the responsibility of each grantee 
(county agency) to administer their programs in accordance with the SLFRF rules and regulations. 
May 17, 2023 – WHR Monthly Agency Meeting Q&A 
COVID Impact 
 How do we verify covid impact if an applicant just needs a new roof, for example? 
Covid impact will need to be demonstrated by following the SLFRF Overview of Final 
Rule guidance (Framework for Eligible Uses Beyond Those Enumerated – Appendix B, 
page 32).  Since this guidance comes directly from the Federal Government and not 
DCED, we are asking the agencies to work with their internal council to implement the 
SLFRF guidance as it is written.   
 
 If the 80% AMI is greater than 300% FPI, do we need to determine COVID impact? 
Yes, if the 80% AMI is greater than the 300% FPI proof of Covid Impact will need to be 
provided. 
Income Limits 
 For tenant income being 60% of PHFA PennHomes Limits – 
o When will those limits be updated?  
The 2023 PHFA PennHomes Limits are to set to be updated and effective 6/15/23 
o How long will we use the 2022 limits?  
The 2022 limits can be used through the date the change becomes effective.  Any 
application utilizing WHR funds received on or after the 2023 effective date should 
use the 2023 limits. 
 If a non-profit (or any other public assistance program) provides a letter stating a client 
qualifies for a program with income thresholds that are below the 80% AMI is that sufficient 
documentation? 
Yes, this is sufficient.  As with all WHR recipients for WHR grant funding, this letter will 
be supplemented by an income self-certification form which will show the total 
household income.  The self-certification form should be reviewed by the WHR agency 
to ensure that the household income is being self-certified at or below 80% AMI.

 If the household income changes to above the 80% AMI before the rehabilitation starts, 
would the applicant still be eligible for assistance? 
The Income Eligibility Verification Sign-Off process has been removed from the DCED 
WHR Eligibility & Income Verification Guidelines.  Agencies should confirm that the 
client meets eligibility during the initial application period only. 
Income Verification 
 Can tenants be income certified using categorical eligibility as well? 
Moving forward, tenants will only be required to self-certify household income.  No 
other income verification process will be required.   
 Is the self-income verification form the same as the zero-income form? 
No, the zero-income form would not be adequate to use in place of the self-income 
verification form. 
 If we collect actual income information (copy of paystubs, social security benefits, etc.) do 
we need a separate form for the homeowner to complete? Are copies of income documents 
sufficient? 
Homeowners must complete a self-certification of annual income form.  This is in 
addition to the documentation stated in the Eligibility Guidelines under Methods of 
Proof. 
 Is the goal for the income verification to verify last 12 months of income, current income, or 
anticipated income? 
The intent is to have the homeowner complete an annual income self-certification 
form and then supplement that form with two-months of income documentation 
supporting the income as it’s been self-certified.  With this method, it’s the intent to 
be forward looking at the time of the application, with the primary goal to establish 
the household’s annual income at application.    
 
 Do federal and state tax returns count as the past 12 months of income?  
As discussed in the previous answer, the intent is to verify annual household income at 
the time of application.  For this reason, we will not allow for the use of federal tax 
returns.

 Does the household income include only adults 18 and over or does it include all household 
income like SSI for a 12-year-old child? 
Household income should include all household income including public benefits (such 
as SSI) for children.  Earned income or unemployment compensation for minors under 
the age of 18 (or full-time high school students) may be excluded from this process.  
This rule was implemented in conjunction with DOE’s recent rule update to the way 
income is measured through the Weatherization Assistance Program and is intended 
to exclude part-time income earned by children within the household.  
Liens 
 Do we have to put a lien on the homeowner’s property after the work is done? 
For landlords receiving a forgivable loan, yes. 
For an owner-occupied homeowner, this is not a requirement of WHR Program, but 
agencies are free to implement this stipulation as they administer the program in their 
county.  DCED has provided no input as to whether this practice should be considered 
by a county WHR agency as it’s at the sole distraction of the local agency.  
Loans 
 Do the loan forgiveness dates go backwards from the loan received date? If they are 
forward, will the landlords make payments up until that 15-year period? If they pay it off, 
then qualify for forgiveness, are we expected to just cut them a check? 
The WHR loan forgiveness criteria will be monitored for the 15-years following the 
landlord’s receipt of WHR funds.  Because of the nature of the forgivable loan, it’s 
expected that no principal payments will be received by the WHR agency throughout 
the life of the loan.  If no principal is received throughout the life of the loan, no 
reimbursement will be owed to the landlord at year 15 if they successfully complete 
the monitoring period.      
Reporting 
 If we use categorical eligibility and do not have a specific income level, can we omit this 
from the reporting spreadsheet (both the detailed dollar income and the percent of AMI)? 
This information is a requirement.  Categorical eligibility is not a viable source for 
proof of income for this program.  If the method of proof obtained is documentation 
from another program that doesn’t provide the actual household income and only 
confirms the household is below the 80% AMI threshold, the household income 
information should be captured on the self-certification form and reported to DCED.

 On the reporting spreadsheet, the column header asks for the percent of AMI. Do you 
expect a specific percentage? 
Yes, the report should reflect the correct AMI for that household.  
 On the reporting spreadsheet, do you want the full address listed? 
Please include the property’s full address on the report.  
RFQ 
 Has there been any update on if we can RFQ or sole source a standing contractor so we can 
expedite delivery of projects and meet the timelines? 
No.  We continue to encourage all grantees to rely on the State and Local Fiscal 
Recovery Fund (SLFRF) guidance for policy questions not otherwise addressed by the 
PA legislation defining the Whole-Home Repairs Program.  As with any other federally 
funded program, the SLFRF policies should be adhered to in conjunction with the PA 
legislation.     
 Until further guidance is released in reference to the contractor process, are we able to 
follow our county procurement process to move construction along? 
It’s been the understanding of the WHR office that each WHR agency can follow their 
own procurement procedures when it comes to the selection of contractors.  This is 
because the homeowner (from DCED’s perspective) is a beneficiary of the funds rather 
than a grantee (with the grantee/subgrantee being the county agency or a 
subrecipient agency).  DCED has suggested that an RFP/RFQ may be appropriate if the 
WHR agency is attempting to select one General Contractor to manage a large portion 
of the repair work. 
If a grantee is selecting a subgrantee/subrecipient to assist in the administration of 
the WHR program, the following Bidding Requirements should be followed:  
If the Grantee is a political subdivision or other entity for which open and competitive 
bidding procedures have been established by law, the Grantee shall comply with those 
procedures if they are applicable to the project being funded with the grant funds.  
Otherwise, the Grantee shall comply with open and competitive bidding procedures in 
awarding any and all grants, subgrants, contracts, subcontracts or other agreements 
in excess of $10,000.00 for construction, reconstruction, demolition, alteration and/or 
repair, for acquisition of machinery and equipment, or for engagement of the services 
of a professional consultant, when said grants, subgrants, contracts, subcontracts or 
other agreements are funded in whole or at least 50% in part with funds made 
available under this Contract.  Open and competitive bidding procedures require the 
Grantee to obtain a minimum of three arm’s length bids from vendors capable of 
providing the goods and/or performing the services requested.  Arm’s length 
transactions occur when the parties to the transaction are not related to one another 
and each party is acting in its own self-interest.  The Grantor may require the Grantee 
to submit proof of compliance with said procedures, and failure to provide such proof

to the satisfaction of the Grantor may result in termination of the Contract and 
repayment of all or a portion of the funds available under this Contract.  Upon written 
request and for good cause shown, the Grantor may, at the Grantor's sole discretion, 
permit the Grantee to use an alternative procedure for solicitation of bids not 
inconsistent with law. 
 If we are nonprofit administering a local program rather than a municipal organization, can 
we follow our procurement policies?  
Please see previous answer.  
 In relationship to bids, are the homeowners able to get at least one bid for the work to be 
done and that is what is just for the project? We would not be bidding the project but rather 
receiving the bid from a contractor that the homeowner has chosen. 
The process of selecting a contractor to perform WHR repairs is at the discretion of the 
local agency.

development component? 
We plan to cover Workforce Development on the June call.

Eligibility Determination 
1. A dwelling unit is eligible for services if: 
a. The household income does not exceed 80% of the total area median

b. It is owned by a homeowner who meets any of the following criteria: 
i. An owner of record evidenced by a publicly recorded deed. 
ii. An owner-occupant of a manufactured home who leases a space in 
a manufactured home community. 
iii. An equitable owner who can demonstrate an ownership interest in 
a property as provided by law, including: 
1. A person who has inherited an interest in a property 
2. A person who has entered a contract to purchase the

3. A person who was the owner of record before a fraudulent 
conveyance of the property. 
4. A person who is a trust beneficiary and a person holding a 
partial ownership interest in a property such as tenancy by

c. It is owned by a small landlord who rents occupied affordable units to 
tenants at or below 60% of area median income, adjusted for household

PennHOMES countywide limits. A small landlord is a person who meets 
all of the following criteria: 
i. The person is a landlord. 
ii. The person has an ownership stake in no more than five properties
