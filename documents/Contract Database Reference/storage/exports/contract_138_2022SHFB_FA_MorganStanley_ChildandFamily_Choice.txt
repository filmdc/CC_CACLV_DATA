Program Overview
Donor Overview 
Since 2009, the Morgan Stanley Foundation has partnered with Feeding America to provide more than $41.7 million
in support of hunger-relief programs that deliver food assistance and healthy meals to children and families across
the country.  In 2020, the Morgan Stanley Foundation pledged $7.95 million over four (4) years to fund initiatives
including COVID Relief and Child and Family Feeding Programs.  As part of that pledge, a total of $5,137,500 is
allocated over three (3) years to support the expansion of Choice among Child and Family Feeding Programs.  
 
Opportunity Context  
This grant opportunity totaling $1,612,500 will support Year 2 of the Morgan Stanley Foundation Child & Family
Choice Grant and provides a unique opportunity for food banks to increase Choice in food distribution models while
maintaining COVID-19 health and safety protocols.  In addition, this grant will focus on increasing access to a
nutritious variety of food by addressing barriers families with children face in their communities and enhancing their
experience through Choice.  Based on research, there are four (4) key barriers that impact a family’s ability to
access food: awareness, stigma, physical access, and individual experience.  
Choice Model  
The Feeding America Network serves children and their families through direct services and programs and
traditional agency partners.  Giving neighbors the opportunity to choose among available food and food boxes at
distributions or pantry sites is considered a best practice.  Food banks report that Choice programs offer a more
dignified experience, reduce food waste, and increase access to food that is culturally sensitive and meets
dietary restrictions. However, most of this feedback is anecdotal and limited data exists to document the true
impact of the Choice model on food pantry operations and on neighbor experience. The COVID-19 pandemic
curtailed Choice offerings as many food banks shifted their School Pantries, Mobile Pantries, and other programs to
pre-packed food boxes that could be quickly distributed in a safe, socially distant manner.  As the pandemic
continues to affect food distribution efforts, this grant provides an opportunity to creatively and innovatively
incorporate elements of Choice to best serve children and their families, while abiding by COVID-19 safety protocols,
and to evaluate changes over one year.  
Strategic Overview
Our ability to provide food in every county nationwide is a testament to the strength and expanse of the Feeding
America network of nearly 200 food banks and more than 60,000 food pantries and meal programs. In our collective
pursuit to end hunger, we have charted a coordinated, streamlined course anchored by four core strategies - Feed,
Nourish, Empower and Unite - and the outcomes of each of these strategies will help guide us toward achieving our
vision: an America where no one is hungry. We will leverage advocacy, research, data and technology at both
the national level and by building the capacity and capabilities of the network. We will also center Equity, Diversity,
and Inclusion across all our work together.  
Commitment to Equity

To achieve our vision of a hunger-free America, we are committed to equity, ensuring fair treatment, access,
opportunity, and advancement for all people. We will identify and work towards solutions to eliminate social,
structural, and systemic inequities that contribute to food insecurity for individuals that have been historically
disadvantaged and/or adversely impacted by racial inequities. While there are many components to equity, racial
inequity has resulted in poorer outcomes for communities of color, including higher rates of food insecurity and
poverty, and worse health outcomes. By addressing racial equity, we position ourselves to make both a broad and
deep impact with the people who have historically been the most marginalized and systematically oppressed.Since
the start of the pandemic, food insecurity has increased, with communities of color being hardest hit: 
In 2020, 24.0% of Black, non-Hispanic individuals lived in a food-insecure household, compared to 19.2% in
2019. Black, non-Hispanic individuals were 3.2 times as likely to face hunger as white, non-Hispanic
individuals (7.6%). 
In 2020, 19.3% of Latino individuals lived in a food-insecure household, compared to 15.8% in 2019. Latino
individuals were 2.5 times as likely to face hunger as white, non-Hispanic individuals (7.6%). 
In 2020, 9.1% of individuals in the category “Other, non-Hispanic” lived in food insecure households,
compared to 9.0% in 2019. This category includes Asian American, Pacific Islander, Native American, and
multiracial individuals (non-Hispanic). Individuals represented by this category were 1.2 times as likely to face
hunger as white, non-Hispanic individuals (7.6%). 
In addition to communities of color, rural communities face significant barriers to accessing food: 
In 2019, rural counties made up 63% of all U.S. counties but represented 91% of counties with the highest
rates of food insecurity pre-pandemic. These high-levels of food insecurity are due in part to challenges
accessing food from both traditional and charitable sources, as well as limited job availability and job
opportunities that are more concentrated in low-wage industries.

maintaining COVID-19 health and safety protocols.

Increase equitable access to food for rural communities and/or communities of color.  
The learning objectives of this grant opportunity are as follows:

Evaluation 
Feeding America will partner with external evaluators to provide technical assistance and peer support throughout
the grant period.  In partnership with external evaluators and the Feeding America evaluation team, grantees will be
asked to implement a brief survey to be completed by partner agencies/program sites to examine changes to the
amount of Choice being offered and the impact of Choice on children and their families. 
Data collection will take place at two levels: 1) agencies/program sites and 2) neighbors. 
1) Food banks will gather information from their selected agencies/program sites twice over the course of the grant
period to measure the following:  
Level of Choice being offered 
Perceptions of food waste 
Barriers faced while offering Choice  
2) The Feeding America Evaluation team will provide support and training for food banks to execute survey
administration with a sample of neighbors participating in the program.  The survey will measure the following: 
Perceptions of food program/satisfaction 
Food waste
