Mission Statement (250 words): 
The mission of Community Action Lehigh Valley (also known as Community Action Committee of the 
Lehigh Valley) is to improve the quality of life in the Lehigh Valley and surrounding areas by building a 
community in which all people have access to economic opportunity, the ability to pursue that 
opportunity, and a voice in the decisions that affect their lives. 
Describe the services your organization provides (250 words): 
Community Action Lehigh Valley (CALV) administers programs that meet basic social needs while 
encouraging self-sufficiency, works toward economic and community development of low-income 
neighborhoods, engages in community planning and advocacy efforts and establishes partnerships. CALV 
tackles the following issues: food insecurity, housing insecurity, youth development, neighborhood 
revitalization and economic and business development.  
 
CALV’s Second Harvest Food Bank offers food for the hungry. 
 
Sixth Street Shelter provides transitional housing for homeless families. The Housing Counseling Program 
is a HUD- and PHFA-approved housing counseling agency that provides education, counseling and 
assistance regarding homeownership, predatory lending and mortgage foreclosure. Weatherization offers 
solutions to home heating problems through counseling and weatherization of residences. Community 
Action Homes increases quality affordable housing through acquisition/rehab/resale; owner-occupied and 
investor-owned rehabs; and owner-occupied, investor-owned and commercial facades. Lehigh Valley 
Community Land Trust sells affordable houses to low- to moderate-income residents. 
 
Youth development is accomplished through The Campaign for Racial and Ethnic Justice, which works 
with vulnerable youth to instill a perpetual sense of purpose. She Has Everything (SHE) empowers young 
women to be independent, confident and change the power dynamic.  
 
Neighborhood revitalization and economic and business development is accomplished through Slate Belt 
Rising, a multi-municipality initiative to revitalize Wind Gap, Bangor, Pen Argyl and Portland. Community 
Action Development of Allentown and Bethlehem work with residents to provide economic opportunities

and revitalize their communities. Rising Tide Community Loan Fund, a federally-certified community 
development financial institution, provides microloans and technical assistance to businesses that others do 
not.   
 
Describe any programs offered by your organization that address environmental justice and 
inequity, environmental disasters or climate change. (Input N/A if not applicable). 
 
Community Action Lehigh Valley’s housing rehabilitation program, Community Action Homes (CAH), 
focuses its efforts on low- to moderate-income households with an emphasis on increasing equity and 
environmental justice through the incorporation of health, safety, and energy-efficiency measures in all 
housing rehabilitation projects. Due to the Lehigh Valley’s old housing stock, there are often high levels of 
lead within the paint of older houses, specifically those older houses occupied by low- to moderate-income 
households. Due the negative health consequences of lead-based paint, seen most often in children who are 
Black and/or Hispanic as well as low- to moderate-income due to inequities, CAH administers the 
Bethlehem-Easton-Northampton County Lead Paint Hazard Program with a focus on this demographic. 
Through this program, CAH removes lead paint hazards within households with young children. Energy-
efficiency improvements made by CAH may include air sealing, insulation, efficient HVAC equipment, 
new Energy Star-certified windows, and ensuring proper ventilation for the home following ASHRAE 62.2 
2016 standards (bath fans with continuous capable functions and kitchen exhaust fans that work and are 
vented out properly. These additional health, safety, and energy-efficiency measures ensure low- to 
moderate-income owner-occupied households are not only able to live in safe and decent housing, but also 
financially sustainable and environmentally conscious housing. Furthermore, these efforts decrease 
transiency and stabilize communities.

Describe the community/neighborhood your organization serves, including its needs and challenges 
(250 words): 
The Lehigh Valley is a desirable place to live, drawing new residents, but many low-to-moderate income 
residents are being squeezed out by rising housing costs. Across the Lehigh Valley, 12.1% of families 
with children under 18 are living in poverty (American Community Survey 5-Year Estimates 2018-2022). 
Homelessness and housing insecurity are key issues in the region. The Eastern Pennsylvania Continuum 
of Care (CoC) manages the Coordinated Entry (CE) system for individuals experiencing or at imminent 
risk of homelessness in the region. The CoC reported 1,055 CE enrollments in Lehigh County in 2022, 
almost double the next highest county enrollment of 533. This demonstrates the great need for 
homelessness services in Lehigh County, where the Sixth Street Shelter is located.  
Affordable housing is another prime concern. Over half of renters and over a quarter of homeowners with 
a mortgage in the Lehigh Valley are cost-burdened, meaning that they spend more than 30% of their 
income on housing costs (ACS 2018-2022). RentCafe recently named the Lehigh Valley the third hottest 
small rental market in the country for small rental markets with a vacancy rate less than 4%, and figures

from the Greater Lehigh Valley Realtors for December 2023 indicate that the median sales price for a 
single-family home in the region is approximately $300,000. Local housing costs are simply out of reach 
for LMI individuals and families. 
Food insecurity is an additional challenge. According to Feeding America, one in ten Lehigh Valley 
residents face food insecurity, and one in six are kids.

Program/Project Description Summarize the program/project and describe the target population to be served. 
(maximum 250 words):  
The Housing Counseling Program (HCP) is a HUD- and PHFA-approved Housing Counseling agency that 
provides low- to moderate-income first-time homebuyers with services to prepare participants to be 
mortgage-ready, avoid predatory lending and avoid potential foreclosure on homes they eventually 
purchase. HCP also provides default and delinquency counseling for impacted homeowners through the 
mortgage diversion program and provides assistance with Homeowners Emergency Mortgage Assistance 
Program applications for homeowners who received an ACT 91 Notice. No other program in the Lehigh

Valley offers comprehensive home ownership counseling, including a first-time homebuyer seminar, pre-
purchase and pre-settlement counseling, financial education, and foreclosure mitigation and assistance 
programs comprised of individual counseling and a diversion program held in civil court.  
 
Located in Center City Allentown, Sixth Street Shelter (SSS) assists families experiencing homelessness 
secure safe housing through family-centered services, in-house programming, and referrals to community 
resources. SSS is the only shelter in the region where entire families of any configuration (including fathers 
and male teenage children) stay together in their own apartments. For many families, the stay in the Shelter 
enables them to begin to stabilize their income and address the problems that led to their homelessness. 
Each family receives individualized case management and develops a Family Service Plan to set household, 
budgeting, educational and vocational goals. Staff connect them with necessary resources to address their 
needs and locate housing. Following the family’s move into permanent housing, staff maintain contact to 
offer additional assistance and determine how well they are continuing to move toward self-sufficiency.

Results & Measurement. List the projected goals and outputs during the grant term. Describe the 
projected long-term outcomes of the grant activity. (max 250 words) 
Each year, a Work Plan is developed with measurable goals and performance targets that guide program 
activities and are designed to have a positive impact on program participants and the larger community. 
The Work Plan is evaluated twice yearly to measure effectiveness of services and program outcomes. HCP 
and SSS have set the following performance targets for FY 2023-2024: 
 
Goal 1: Increase homeownership, particularly for households of color and low- to moderate-income 
households 
Output 1: 175 prospective homebuyers will participate in one of seven seminars; 50 will participate in 
pre-purchase counseling services; 50 will receive pre-settlement counseling. 
Goal 2: Provide services that protect homeowners from losing their homes due to economic distress 
Output 1: 40 homeowners receiving an Act 91 Notice will receive loss mitigation services and 
assistance in applying for HEMAP. 
Output 2: 80 homeowners facing foreclosure will receive comprehensive housing counseling and 
advocacy in a court setting. 
Outcome 1: 32 homeowners facing foreclosure will avoid the loss of their home. 
Goal 3: Provide short-term transitional housing, case management and other supportive services for 
families with children who are experiencing homelessness 
Output 1: 85 families will develop Family Service Plans. 
Output 2: 50% of families will save money toward rent or security deposit. 
Output 3: 75% of families will achieve their Family Service Plan short-term goals. 
Output 4: 100% of families will create a written budget to understand credit and money management.

Outcome 1: 20% of families will move into stable housing upon leaving the Shelter.
