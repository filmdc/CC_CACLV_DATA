What is the name of your project or event?
Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast PA
Describe the project/event, its purpose & how it will be
implemented:
Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast PA is a 
program of Community Action Lehigh Valley, the region’s leading anti-
poverty organization, and a member of Feeding America – the nation’s 
largest charitable food network. Second Harvest serves Lehigh, 
Northampton, Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne counties. 
Last year, Second Harvest distributed 14.7 million pounds of food to 
our network of 200+ nonprofit agencies (food pantries, schools, meal 
centers, shelters, childcare agencies, senior centers, veterans 
organizations), a 44% increase from the previous year. Included in this 
total is 6.7 million pounds of fresh produce, perishable protein, and 
fresh dairy products (46% of total distribution). Annually, we rescue 
more than 8 million pounds of food from ending up in a landfill. 
On average, the Second Harvest network serves more than 87,000 
people each month - a 52% increase from last year. The pantries 
within the Second Harvest network are operated almost solely through 
the work of community volunteers. Our food distribution is a model of 
neighbors helping neighbors. 
In addition to food distribution to our network, Second Harvest 
operates several programs aimed at improving the health of our 
community and working toward a more just and equitable local food 
system. We administer the Pennsylvania Senior Food Box Program 
(PASB). PASB works to improve the health of low-income seniors by 
supplementing their diets with shelf-stable nutritious foods. Eligible 
participants include low-income individuals who are at least 60 years 
old and whose household income is at or below 130% of the U.S. 
poverty level. We work to supplement boxes with healthy, fresh items 
when possible. Currently, Second Harvest provides senior food boxes 
to nearly 2,350 seniors across 49 sites each month.
Our MilitaryShare program works “to serve those who served us” by 
providing access to nutritious food, with a goal of improving health 
outcomes and easing financial strain among low-income active duty, 
reserve, and retired military members and their families. Our 
MilitaryShare program provides 500+ military families with a share of 
fresh and shelf-stable food each month. 
Our SNAP (Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program) outreach 
coordinators work with local county offices of Health and Human 
Services to offer SNAP application assistance. Our SNAP coordinators 
offer dedicated personal application support to ensure that all eligible 
participants receive needed benefits.
We offer a dynamic community-based nutrition education program, 
which includes free cooking classes and demonstrations for parents, 
kids and childcare providers where participants learn about cooking, 
meal prep, grocery shopping, food budgeting, and nutrition. Through

our Healthy Pantry Initiative (HPI), a program of Feeding 
Pennsylvania, our HPI Coordinator works with pantry coordinators and 
volunteers to encourage consumption of nutritious and fresh foods, 
and also provides direct nutrition education to pantry clients. 
Additionally, Second Harvest operates The Seed Farm, a 42-acre 
training farm. Because there can be no access to nourishing food 
without farms, The Seed Farm works to support food production at all 
levels, from home gardeners to community gardens to local farmers, 
with an emphasis on getting nutritious and locally-grown food to under-
resourced communities. Strong local food systems equal food security.

The health and well-being of each of the people we serve is at the 
forefront of our food distribution, outreach, and education strategies. 
Poverty is tied to food insecurity. People living in low-income 
households are disproportionately diagnosed with diet-related 
illnesses, making it nearly impossible for people to thrive.
A community is not healthy until health equity is realized. Populations 
facing food insecurity are strongly correlated with those that have been 
impacted by systemic racism and economic exploitation. In the U.S., 
Native American (by 2.9 times), Black (by 2.4 times), and Latino (by 2 
times) households are more likely to be food-insecure than white 
households. Second Harvest advances equity by working to ensure 
that everyone has access to the foods needed to keep themselves and 
their families healthy.
