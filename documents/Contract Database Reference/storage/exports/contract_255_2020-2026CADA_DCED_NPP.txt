PROPOSED PROJECTS FOR YEAR 4 (FY 2023-2024) (Original) 
  
The Allentown’s CORE NPP Steering Committee will continue to focus on the following priorities for 
implementation in FY 2023-2024:  
  
 Education  
 Vocation  
 Health and Recreation  
 Citizen Engagement  
  
In March of 2020, the Steering Committee of Allentown’s CORE met to discuss the development of 
CADA’s new Neighborhood Partnership Program. With the recommendations of Community Action 
Lehigh Valley’s Executive Director, the now-retired Alan Jennings, as well as staff from Community 
Action Development Allentown, Promise Neighborhoods of the Lehigh Valley, the Cultural Coalition of 
Allentown (now Lehigh Valley Arts and Cultural Alliance), and Downtown Allentown Community 
Development Initiative, a first-year draft schedule of activities was presented to the Steering Committee for 
review. Over the summer, Steering Committee members deliberated on these proposed activities and 
arrived at the following projects for funding priorities for implementation in FY 2020-2021 (NPP Year 1). 
Following the COVID-19 pandemic and social distancing regulations, it was difficult to identify meaningful 
changes to be made to be our priorities moving into Year 2. The Steering Committee approved a motion to 
maintain the same proposed projects, including their financial allocations, in Year 2. This decision was 
predicated on the assumption that the second year of programming will yield significantly more usable data 
and experience moving forward than Year 1, which saw so much disruption following the COVID-19 
pandemic.  
  
Following a year of programming that saw less interruptions caused by the COVID-19 pandemic, Year 2 
of Allentown’s CORE allowed for a much more thorough understanding of how our programming addressed 
community needs. Thus, Year 3 of Allentown’s CORE featured some significant shifts in programming.  
  
One of the most obvious changes made to the Neighborhood Partnership Program in its third year was its 
new name: Allentown’s CORE. In April and May of 2021, an online contest was held where local youth 
were able to submit their ideas to rename the Neighborhood Partnership Program as they saw fit. The 
student with the winning submission was Jamari Rivera, aged 15.   
  
Another key change to Allentown’s CORE was the addition of the Allentown AfterZone initiative. Based 
on the model crafted by the Providence After School Alliance (PASA) in Providence, Rhode Island, the 
programs offered in the AfterZone would be integrated into the education goals of the Allentown School 
District, ensuring that each program supports the academic needs of each student. Hands-on learning is at 
the core of PASA’s model. Students choose their programs from program brochures, allowing them to 
become empowered learners with a sense of personal agency. Crucially, all AfterZone programming is 
entirely free for students. CADA acts as a connective hub, providing administrative oversight and 
monitoring of community organizations and service providers. The Allentown AfterZone provides each 
student with access to year-round, learning that engages young people in a way that excites them, stimulates 
their curiosity, and assists them in realizing their skills, talents, and abilities. Beginning with middle school 
students and integrating high school students in the near future, the Allentown AfterZone represents a city-
wide expanded learning system consisting of after-school, out-of-school, and summer activities.  
  
Following a year of highly successful programming, only small alterations to allocation amounts have been 
suggested for Year 4. While the allocation has not changed for the line item related to the acquisition of the

youth center, significant progress has been made towards the acquisition of our primary target site: the 
former Cleveland Elementary School building, located in the heart of the target neighborhood.  
  
Education ($140,000)   
  
Generation Next ($35,000)  
  
Generation Next is a strategy designed to help level the field and ensure that underserved students have 
increased access to opportunities to further their education. Students are recruited as freshman and continue 
with the program as they advance through college. Students must fit two of the following requirements: 
first generation college student, from a low-moderate income family and/or minority background. Program 
benefits include social/emotional intelligence building curriculum, SAT preparation and test costs, college 
campus visit, personal mentor, and access to discretionary funds.  
  
Objective #1: By September 30, 2024, CALV will facilitate Generation Next at William Allen High School 
for thirty (30) students and their parents/guardians.  
  
Objective #2: By September 30, 2024, CALV will ensure 90% retention rate for students in Generation 
Next.   
  
Objective #3: By September 30, 2024, CALV will ensure 90% of students in Generation Next demonstrate 
improved positive approaches toward learning, including improved attention skills.  
  
Objective #4: By September 30, 2024, 95% of students in Generation Next will be on pace to advance to 
the next grade.  
  
Allentown AfterZone ($105,000)  
  
The Allentown AfterZone program represents a city-wide expanded learning system consisting of after-
school, out-of-school, and summer activities. Some programs would take place in Allentown’s public 
schools, others at community partner locations throughout Allentown. The programs offered in the 
AfterZone are integrated into the education goals of the Allentown School District, ensuring that each 
program supports the academic needs of each school and student. CADA acts as a connective hub, providing 
administrative oversight and monitoring of community organizations and service providers.  
  
Objective #5: Continue to employ one (1) part-time Program Coordinator at 25 hours per week.   
  
Objective #6: Identify five (5) community partners to collaborate with on the development of AfterZone 
programming.  
  
Objective #7: Develop and launch a combination of five (5) AfterZone programs or events.  
  
Objective #8: Serve a minimum of seventy-five (75) youth through AfterZone programming and events.  
  
Objective #9: Utilize Allentown School District buildings for at least 80% of AfterZone programming and 
events.  
  
  
VOCATION ($30,000)

Youth Entrepreneurship Program ($30,000)  
  
This program will provide entrepreneurial mentorship opportunities for youth who want to learn more about 
starting/owning their own business in a field of their choice. CADA’s Director will recruit and pair local 
youth with an appropriate entrepreneur/small business owner.   
  
Objective #10: By September 30, 2024, CADA’s Director will recruit at least ten (10) local 
entrepreneurs/small business owners to the Youth Entrepreneurship Program.  
  
Objective #11: By September 30, 2024, CADA’s Director will pair at least ten (10) youth with local 
entrepreneurs/small business owners.  
  
Objective #12: By September 30, 2024, at least ten (10) youth will have a broader understanding of their 
future career goals based on their time in the Youth Entrepreneurship Program.  
  
  
HEALTH AND RECREATION ($190,000)  
  
Youth Center – Fourth-Year Costs (acquisition and renovations) ($100,000)  
  
During the fourth year of the Neighborhood Partnership Program, CADA staff will continue to work with 
the Allentown School District to finalize the acquisition of the identified youth center site. Costs will help 
cover acquisition costs as well as interim operating costs.  
  
Objective #13: By September 30, 2024, CADA will identify five (5) interim spaces where NPP activities 
can be held until the acquisition and creation of a new community center.  
  
Objective #14: By September 30, 2024, CADA will finalize the acquisition and begin the development of 
the identified youth center site located in Center City, Allentown.  
  
Youth Police Academy ($10,000)  
  
The Allentown Police Youth Academy, in partnership with the Allentown School District, will host a two-
week Youth Police Academy for thirty-five (35) to forty (40) students. The programs will provide 
classroom sessions with lectures, role plays and topical demonstrations by police officers in the areas of 
law, behavioral science, drug prevention and gang resistance. Field trips to a police department facility or 
city park will also be part of the curriculum. The second week of the program will include instruction at the 
Lehigh County Technical Institute, to further educate participating students in the field of law enforcement 
while continuing to strengthen the bond between students and officers.  
  
Objective #15: By September 30, 2024, CADA will provide program supplies for the Youth Police 
Academy that will serve, on average, forty (40) students over a four-week period during summer 2022.  
  
Creative Economy – Art Programming ($80,000)  
  
The Lehigh Valley Arts and Cultural Alliance, through the Artist-Neighbor Growth and Opportunity Project 
(TANGO) will provide community youth with art and cultural activities, including dance classes, video, 
multimedia projects, mural mentorship program, and creative entrepreneurship opportunities.

Objective #16: By September 30, 2024, the Lehigh Valley Arts and Cultural Alliance will offer a 
combination of yoga, Pilates, and dance classes to fifty (50) to seventy (70) students during the school year.  
  
Objective #17: By September 30, 2024, the Lehigh Valley Arts and Cultural Alliance will offer video and 
multimedia lab programming to twenty (20) to twenty-four (24) students.  
  
Objective #18: By September 30, 2024, the Lehigh Valley Arts and Cultural Alliance will offer twenty-
four (24) to thirty (30) students the opportunity to learn how to work and operate a recording studio.  
  
Objective #19: By September 30, 2024, the Lehigh Valley Arts and Cultural Alliance, in partnership with 
the Fine Feather Foundation, will conduct a 6 to 8-week summer camp focusing on the performing arts.  
  
Objective #20: By September 30, 2024, the Lehigh Valley Arts and Cultural Alliance will offer the Mural 
Arts Mentorship program to three (3) to four (4) students.  
  
  
CITIZEN ENGAGEMENT ($210,000)  
  
Community Outreach Workers ($95,000)  
  
The Community Outreach Workers of Promise Neighborhoods of the Lehigh Valley will be responsible for 
program outreach, connecting community youth with support services as well as building rapport with high-
risk youth. They serve as a link and support for youth through connections to community resources and 
participate, as necessary, in organizing strategic and inclusive responses to incidents of violence.  
  
Objective #21: By September 30, 2024, Promise Neighborhoods will continue to employ two (2) full time 
employees to serve as Community Outreach Workers.  
  
Objective #22: By September 30, 2024, Community Outreach Workers will have completed ongoing Cure 
Violence training and provide CADA with documentation of their trainings and certifications.  
  
Objective #23: By September 30, 2024, Community Outreach Workers will have assisted in the response 
to thirty-five to fifty (35-50) acts of violence within Allentown city limits. The Community Outreach 
Workers will provide documentation of their responses and the specific outcomes of their responses.  
  
James Lawson Freedom School ($30,000)  
  
The James Lawson Freedom School is a research-driven, best practice six-week summer enrichment 
program that partners low-income students and students struggling in school within the Allentown School 
District with area college students to create a dynamic intergenerational learning environment that builds 
the necessary literacy and leadership skills required for success in learning and life.   
  
Objective #24: By September 30, 2024, the James Lawson Freedom School will have provided a six-week 
summer literacy and cultural enrichment program to sixty-five (65) K-12 students in the Allentown School 
District footprint.  
  
Objective #25: By September 30, 2024, the James Lawson Freedom School will provide quality 
programming including high quality academic enrichment, parent and family involvement, civic 
engagement and social action, intergenerational servant leadership development, and nutrition, health, and 
mental health.

Objective #26: By September 30, 2024, based on pre- and post-testing, students of the James Lawson 
Freedom School will demonstrate increased academic performance in reading efficiency.   
  
Objective #27: By September 30, 2024, based on pre- and post-testing, students of the James Lawson 
Freedom School will maintain or gain in their instructional reading level.  
  
Coalition of Difference-Makers Discretionary Grants ($85,000)   
  
Objective #28: By September 30, 2024, the Coalition of Difference Makers will distribute $85,000 of 
discretionary funding to community organizations with the intent to build their capacity for long-term 
growth and success.  
  
Objective #29: By September 30, 2024, at least seventy-five (75) community youth will have been 
positively impacted through the projects funded by the Coalition of Difference Makers.  
  
  
ORGANIZATION AND MANAGEMENT ($80,000)  
  
Administration ($65,000)  
  
NPP funds will support the cost of the administration of the Allentown’s Future Neighborhood Partnership 
Program through the efforts of the Community Action Development Allentown and Community Action 
Lehigh Valley.   
  
Objective #30: By September 30, 2024, the Community Action Lehigh Valley and the Community Action 
Development Allentown will continue to provide proficient administration of the Neighborhood 
Partnership Program.  
  
  
Program Supplies ($15,000)  
  
NPP funds will support the cost of office and program supplies necessary to implement programming.  
  
Objective #31: Program supplies will be purchased and distributed to community partners as needed.

Generation Next ($35,000) ($28,000) 
  
Generation Next is a strategy designed to help level the field and ensure that underserved students have 
increased access to opportunities to further their education. Students are recruited as freshman and continue 
with the program as they advance through college. Students must fit two of the following requirements: 
first generation college student, from a low-moderate income family and/or minority background. Program 
benefits include social/emotional intelligence building curriculum, SAT preparation and test costs, college 
campus visit, personal mentor, and access to discretionary funds.  
  
Objective #1: By September 30, 2024, CALV will facilitate Generation Next at William Allen High School 
for thirty (30) students and their parents/guardians.  
  
Objective #2: By September 30, 2024, CALV will ensure 90% retention rate for students in Generation 
Next.   
  
Objective #3: By September 30, 2024, CALV will ensure 90% of students in Generation Next demonstrate 
improved positive approaches toward learning, including improved attention skills.  
  
Objective #4: By September 30, 2024, 95% of students in Generation Next will be on pace to advance to 
the next grade.  
  
Allentown AfterZone ($105,000) ($104,000) 
  
The Allentown AfterZone program represents a city-wide expanded learning system consisting of after-
school, out-of-school, and summer activities. Some programs would take place in Allentown’s public 
schools, others at community partner locations throughout Allentown. The programs offered in the 
AfterZone are integrated into the education goals of the Allentown School District, ensuring that each 
program supports the academic needs of each school and student. CADA acts as a connective hub, providing 
administrative oversight and monitoring of community organizations and service providers.

Youth Police Academy ($10,000)  
  
The Allentown Police Youth Academy, in partnership with the Allentown School District, will host a two-
week Youth Police Academy for thirty-five (35) to forty (40) students. The programs will provide 
classroom sessions with lectures, role plays and topical demonstrations by police officers in the areas of 
law, behavioral science, drug prevention and gang resistance. Field trips to a police department facility or 
city park will also be part of the curriculum. The second week of the program will include instruction at the 
Lehigh County Technical Institute, to further educate participating students in the field of law enforcement 
while continuing to strengthen the bond between students and officers.

James Lawson Freedom School ($30,000)  
  
The James Lawson Freedom School is a research-driven, best practice six-week summer enrichment 
program that partners low-income students and students struggling in school within the Allentown School 
District with area college students to create a dynamic intergenerational learning environment that builds 
the necessary literacy and leadership skills required for success in learning and life.   
  
Objective #24: By September 30, 2024, the James Lawson Freedom School will have provided a six-week 
summer literacy and cultural enrichment program to sixty-five (65) K-12 students in the Allentown School 
District footprint.

programming including high quality academic enrichment, parent and family involvement, civic 
engagement and social action, intergenerational servant leadership development, and nutrition, health, and 
mental health.
