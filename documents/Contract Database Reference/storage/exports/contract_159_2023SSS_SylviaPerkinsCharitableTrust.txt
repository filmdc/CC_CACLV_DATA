Describe the specific purpose for which any grant funds awarded from this foundation will be used:  
   
Funds would be used to purchase sofas and love seats for apartments at the Sixth Street Shelter. We plan 
to replace damaged and worn items with items that are bed bug resistant and easy to clean and sanitize 
without causing damage to the furniture. Not only is this furniture durable enough to handle the wear 
and tear of multiple families residing in the unit each year, but it also provides a safer, healthier 
environment for families in terms of cleaning and sanitizing against the spread of viruses and bed bugs.

This will give families a clean, safe, and comfortable place to call home during their stay at the Sixth 
Street Shelter. 
The Sixth Street Shelter has seen a marked increase in cases of bed bugs since the onset of the COVID-
19 pandemic, which has impacted shelter operations. Although social distancing helped to slow the 
spread of bed bugs nationwide during the pandemic due to strict travel restrictions, quarantines, and a 
general anxiety around meeting in large groups, practicing social distancing can be difficult or even 
impossible for individuals experiencing homelessness. Unsheltered families often stay wherever they 
can find a place regardless of the health risks, which can include exposure to bed bugs. Once a family is 
exposed to bed bugs, they then transport these to the Shelter, where they can easily spread among units. 
The furniture we have selected is designed for easy extermination and cleaning so that we will not have 
to regularly replace furniture that becomes infested. It also means that furniture is easier to sanitize 
should there be another COVID surge or similar health situation.  
Describe how a grant to your organization would further the foundation’s mission and the priorities of 
the foundation. 
   
The Sixth Street Shelter shares a common goal with the Sylvia Perkin Perpetual Charitable Trust: to 
work towards public health and welfare. The Shelter provides the opportunity and direction for all 
people to become self-sufficient, productive members of the community. The Shelter also needs to be a 
safe and healthy environment for children and families experiencing homelessness. These funds would 
support us in ensuring that the furniture in the apartments is clean and sanitary, and will help to mitigate 
instances of bed bugs.  
Describe the objectives of the project or program to be funded: 
   
The mission of the Sixth Street Shelter is to assist families experiencing homelessness secure safe 
housing through family-centered services, in-house programming, and referrals to community resources 
while advocating to end homelessness and housing insecurity in the Lehigh Valley. The property has 25 
private apartments, a Family Resource Center, playground, and full-choice food pantry for residents. A

60- to 90-day stay in the Shelter enables families to begin to stabilize their income and address the 
problems that led to their homelessness. The Shelter provides each family with a fully furnished 
apartment and supportive services, including individualized case management. Staff work to connect 
them with the necessary resources to address their individual needs, such as financial literacy and 
parenting classes, access to childcare and healthcare, and mental health support. During their residency, 
the family develops a Family Service Plan with their caseworker, setting household, budgeting, 
educational, and vocational goals and seeking suitable housing and employment. Staff maintain contact 
with the family following their move into permanent housing to offer additional assistance and 
determine how well they are continuing to move toward self-sufficiency. In addition to the 
comprehensive services provided to Shelter residents, the Sixth Street Shelter has become a resource 
center for former residents and people from the surrounding neighborhood. 
For this project specifically, the objective is to provide a clean, healthy, and comfortable environment 
for our residents as they work towards their goals. This entails having the proper furniture in each 
apartment to support the residents’ health and comfort and to ease the challenges of upkeep on Shelter 
staff. This furniture will also mitigate the instances of bed bugs and decrease our expenditures on 
extermination, deep-cleaning services, and replacing damaged furniture. 
Describe the implementation plan for the project or program. Include at least three specific actions your 
organization will take in order to achieve results: 
   
Upon receipt of funding, we would first place an order with Institutional Bedz LLC for the sofas and 
love seats we have selected. We have ordered furniture from them in the past, which will help to 
streamline the process. When the furniture arrives, we would prioritize apartments that are in greatest 
need of new items to replace worn-out and/or soiled furniture. Then we would arrange to have the 
proper staff and/or volunteers ready to assemble anything if necessary and place the furniture 
accordingly in each apartment. We would follow up by inspecting the furniture periodically to confirm 
that it is meeting our needs and the families’ needs in terms of comfort, utility, and durability. 
What criteria does (or will) your organization use to measure the success of the project or program?

Success of this project will be measured by whether the furniture is purchased, delivered, assembled, 
and placed in the apartments within the grant timeline and within budget. More broadly, this project will 
contribute to reaching our overall performance targets for the Sixth Street Shelter in our current fiscal 
year: 1) At least 100 families will reside at the Shelter and develop Family Service Plans based on their 
housing, educational and financial goals; 2) 50% of families will save money toward rent or a security 
deposit upon leaving the Shelter; 3) 75% of families will achieve their Family Service Plan goals; 4) 
100% of families will create a written budget to understand credit and money management as a part of 
their goal plan; 5) 40% of families will move into affordable housing upon leaving.  
Each year, a work plan is developed with measurable goals and performance targets that guide program 
activities. In order to measure effectiveness of services and outcomes, the work plan is evaluated twice 
yearly by the Program Development & Evaluation Committee of CALV’s Board of Directors and is then 
approved by CALV’s full Board. In addition, Sixth Street Shelter utilizes the Homeless Management 
Information System (HMIS) and ClientTrack to track and analyze participant data. The Shelter also 
tracks qualitative data to document the positive long-term impact of the Shelter in the community. Staff 
conduct follow-up evaluations with former residents that can be located at 3-, 6-, and 12-month intervals 
to ascertain their continued progress. 
Briefly describe any formal or informal collaborative ventures your organization has established (or will 
establish) with other entities serving similar purposes that may be relevant to this grant request. 
   
The Sixth Street Shelter partners with many government entities and non-profit agencies to ensure that 
residents receive the services and gain the skills they will need as part of a comprehensive plan to help 
them to become self-sufficient. The Shelter has a nearly 30-year partnership with the Lehigh County 
Office of Children and Youth Services, and many other local organizations contribute to programming 
for our residents. For instance, Essa Bank leads financial literacy classes, CareerLink assists with job 
searches and resume writing, and Highmark Wholecare facilitates “Wellness Friday” sessions for both 
Shelter residents and community members. The Shelter is also a member of the Lehigh Valley Regional 
Homelessness Advisory Board. The LVRHAB is a consortium of representatives from City and County 
governments, social service agencies, veterans support groups, faith-based and other community 
organizations, homeless advocates, homeless or formerly homeless people, representatives from 
legislators, and other interested parties with the mission to prevent and end homelessness in the Lehigh 
Valley. 
If this grant relates to an ongoing project or program, how long has the project or program been 
operating?

Since 1984, the Sixth Street Shelter, a program of Community Action Lehigh Valley, has been offering 
services to help prepare families for the challenges of a difficult job and rental market that too often 
result in homelessness. It is the only family shelter in the region that admits families of any 
configuration (with at least one child under 18) and houses them in their own private apartments. The 
Sixth Street Shelter has proven to be a vital resource in the community, and while it has been operating 
for almost 40 years, the program has adapted to the changing needs of the community. For instance, 
there is currently a severe shortage of available, affordable housing units locally, and landlords have 
been very selective in whom they rent to. Our caseworkers report that they struggle to find housing for 
their clients despite having rental assistance and supportive services available. This means that residents 
are experiencing longer stays in the Shelter before moving into permanent housing. We recently added a 
Housing Innovation Specialist to our staff to work with our residents and local landlords in order to 
identify affordable housing options and facilitate faster placements for families.

additional funding for bed bug resistant furniture, and that request is currently pending. The Sylvia

Demographics Comments: 
   
Last fiscal year, 71 families consisting of 316 people (including 198 children, 18 of whom were five and 
under) found refuge at Sixth Street Shelter. Nearly 60% of those families were headed by women. Many 
of the heads of households that the Shelter serves are unemployed or underemployed, or recipients of 
SSI and SSDI whose low incomes cannot support even what the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban 
Development (HUD) has determined as the Fair Market Rent (FMR) for an apartment in the Lehigh 
Valley. Others had come to the end of their unemployment benefits or welfare benefits and were poorly 
equipped educationally and vocationally to find jobs in a changing labor market. Some faced additional 
challenges, such as a disability (physical and/or mental) or experiencing domestic violence. The ongoing 
impacts of the COVID-19 pandemic continue to exacerbate the challenges our families face, as there is a 
disproportionate impact on low-income members of our community.
