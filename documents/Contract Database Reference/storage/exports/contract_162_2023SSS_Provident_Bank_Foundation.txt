Sixth Street Shelter Case Manager Expansion 
Program Budget Narrative 
 
Summary: 
A new Case Manager started at the Sixth Street Shelter in January 2023, and they bring a wealth 
of experience related to adult education and workforce development. We plan to expand their role 
so that they can implement programming that would support residents in completing their high 
school diploma and connect them with job opportunities in the community through local 
partnerships. This will complement our recent hire of a Housing Innovation Specialist who is 
working to expand our capacity to secure affordable housing for residents. This new initiative will 
enhance services to residents that will help them to secure and maintain housing through improved 
educational and employment outcomes. 
 
The $15,000 of funding per year for two years would support an increase in the Case Manager’s 
salary to reflect the additional responsibilities and provide funding for training opportunities to 
maximize their effectiveness in this role. We would also allocate a portion of the funding each year 
to a “Reimbursement Fund” for residents taking part in the program. These funds would reimburse 
the costs related to completing their individual HiSET tests. 
 
Year 1: 
Case Manager Salary = $8,320 
Professional Development / Training = $4,180 
Reimbursement Fund = $2,500 
 
A total of $8,320 will be used to support a salary increase for the Case Manager leading this 
initiative to reflect their additional responsibilities in developing and implementing the program. 
In order to support their growth and success in this role, $4,180 will be allocated to professional 
development and training opportunities. This includes opportunities (still to be identified) that will 
help them to effectively implement and administer the program, as well as prepare them to build 
out the program to increase educational and workforce development opportunities for residents. A 
total of $2,500 will be allocated to the “Reimbursement Fund” in the first year. These funds will

be used to directly reimburse residents for costs associated with completing their HiSET tests. We 
want to ensure that testing costs do not impose a financial barrier for the residents as they pursue 
their goal of achieving their high school diploma. 
 
Year 2: 
Case Manager Salary: $8,320 
Professional Development / Training = $1,680 
Reimbursement Fund = $5,000 
 
A total of $8,320 will be used to support the salary for the Case Manager leading this initiative in 
the second year as well. Funding for their continued professional development and training will 
total $1,680. This includes opportunities to prepare them to effectively train other staff members 
and volunteers to assist with tutoring and test preparation for residents. Since we aim to double the 
number of residents enrolled in the second year of the program, a total of $5,000 will be allocated 
to the “Reimbursement Fund” to assist residents in paying for their HiSET tests.

Program Dosage
Families live at the Sixth Street Shelter for 60-90 days. This enables families to begin to stabilize their income and address the problems that led to their homelessness. During their residency, the family meets at least weekly with their Case Manager to work on their Family
Service Plan, which sets household, budgeting, educational, and vocational goals, and to look for suitable housing. Following their move into permanent housing, staff maintain contact with them to offer additional assistance and to determine how well they are continuing to
move toward self-sufficiency. Staff check in at 3-, 6-, and 12-month intervals.
The current GED program available does not serve our residents well, as the courses are difficult for them to juggle. We are establishing a new program that can be more easily incorporated into a busy lifestyle, and this effort is being led by a Case Manager with extensive
experience in adult education and workforce development. This new educational initiative will be open to residents seeking to complete their high school diploma, and those eligible will be advised by their Case Manager of the opportunity. Residents who enroll will receive
regular assistance with study and test preparation using adult education curriculum and materials developed by Kentucky Educational Television (KET), which allow for a flexible online learning schedule. They will then complete the relevant High School Equivalency Diploma
(HiSET) tests to earn their high school diploma. They will be expected to complete the requirements within 2-6 months, and if a resident moves out of the Shelter before completion, they will continue to receive support from a Case Manager to assist them with completing the
program.
Representation
Last fiscal year, 34% of Shelter residents identified as Black/African American, 38% as Hispanic/Latino, and 9% as Multi-Racial; and 86% of families were headed by a female. The majority of our families are in the Low- and Extremely Low-Income categories, and we are well
equipped to help them move forward. The Sixth Street Shelter works to engage staff that reflect the community we serve and have lived expertise with homelessness. In addition, staff regularly participate in Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion and cultural humility trainings.
Program Outreach
The Sixth Street Shelter accepts referrals for families to enter the program. The Shelter participates in Connect to Home: The Coordinated Entry System of the Eastern PA Continuum of Care, which coordinates access, assessment, prioritization, and referrals for housing and
supports for people experiencing and at imminent risk of homelessness in the community. This HUD-mandated system is part of a community-wide effort to end homelessness and housing insecurity. In addition, for almost 30 years, the Shelter has partnered with the Lehigh
County Office of Children & Youth Services to accept referrals through the Family Preservation Program, which is designed to help families remain together safely during challenging times.
The Sixth Street Shelter has been in operation since 1984 and is a well-known resource in the community. We keep the community informed about the program and its services via our website, newsletter, and social media pages (Facebook and Instagram). We also send out a
bi-monthly Sixth Street Shelter e-blast that reaches over 9,000 contacts, including donors and local press.

Outcome Statement
The Sixth Street Shelter has set the following performance targets for our current fiscal year: 1) At least 100 families will reside at the Shelter and develop Family Service Plans based on their housing, educational and financial goals; 2) 50% of families will save money toward
rent or a security deposit upon leaving the Shelter; 3) 75% of families will achieve their Family Service Plan goals; 4) 100% of families will create a written budget to understand credit and money management as a part of their goal plan; 5) 40% of families will move into
affordable housing upon leaving.
Specific to this educational initiative, during the 2-year grant period we aim to: 1) launch a HiSET training and testing program; 2) enroll at least 15 individuals in need of a high school diploma; 3) administer at least 60 official HiSET tests across the four subject areas offered; 4)
achieve 75% completion of all tests; 5) graduate at least 12 program participants within 6 months of their enrollment. The goal of this program is to assist residents in earning their high school equivalency diploma, and thereby improve their ability to secure a job and increase
their income. The expanded educational and vocational program at the Sixth Street Shelter will provide residents with tools and credentials that will help them to remain stably housed when they move out of the Shelter and into permanent housing.
Evaluation
Each year, a Work Plan is developed with measurable goals and performance targets that guide program activities. In order to measure effectiveness of services and outcomes, the work plan is evaluated twice yearly by the Program Development & Evaluation Committee of
CALV’s Board of Directors and is then approved by CALV’s full Board. In addition, Sixth Street Shelter utilizes the Homeless Management Information System (HMIS) and ClientTrack to track and analyze participant data. The Shelter also tracks qualitative data to document the
positive long-term impact of the Shelter in the community. Staff conduct follow-up evaluations with former residents that can be located at 3-, 6-, and 12-month intervals to ascertain their continued progress.
To measure our progress towards the specific outcomes related to this educational initiative, we will track enrollments in the program, tests administered and their results, enrollees achieving their diploma, and length of time to completion.
Contingency Planning
Working with families experiencing the crisis of homelessness guarantees unforeseen obstacles and necessitates having contingency plans and contingency plans for those contingency plans. Shelter staff must be agile, flexible, and resourceful to navigate “the system” to
assist families in helping themselves reach self-sufficiency. Over the years, the Shelter has developed “best practices” and built networks to deal with the obstacles facing families in crisis. Shelter staff work as a team with program participants to navigate as many barriers to
self-sufficiency as possible.
Specific to this educational initiative, we may have to increase or decrease enrollment based on the current need at the Shelter, and we may have to extend enrollments beyond 6 months as needed. We must also monitor for any financial and/or technological barriers that
may arise and work to remove those barriers in order to promote participant success.

While the Sixth Street Shelter is located within the Allentown School District, families residing at the Shelter can have their children attend whatever school they may have been attending prior to entering the Shelter. This helps to prevent educational disruptions. Children
experiencing homelessness also experience more school mobility and have lower academic performance. By allowing the children to continue in their current classrooms, we aim to ameliorate these potential negative impacts.

1a. Describe how your organization defines low-to-moderate income.
As a program of a Community Action Agency, participants must have an income below 150% of the federal poverty rate. Some funders require Area Median Income (AMI). At CALV, we base our definition on current AMI levels as defined by HUD, and residents of the Sixth Street
Shelter must report their income during the intake process. Those earning 0-30% of AMI are categorized as “Extremely Low-Income,” 31-60% of AMI as “Low-Income,” and 61-80% AMI as “Moderate-Income.”
1b. Describe the economic status of the population or community that will be impacted by this program.
Last fiscal year, 12% of residents qualified as “Extremely Low-Income,” 74% as “Low-Income,” and 14% as “Moderate-Income.” In prior years, the majority of residents were “Extremely Low-Income,” with the rest “Low-Income.” This shift in economic status illustrates the reach of
the affordable housing crisis, particularly in the Lehigh Valley. As housing costs have skyrocketed in recent years, families with low to moderate incomes are experiencing homelessness at greater rates than in prior years.
