Significant changes
In 2023, Second Harvest Food Bank continued to build capacity to meet the growing demand for food
resources, while focusing more fully on meeting the nutritional needs of the community.
To meet the demand for additional food resources, we purchased and leased two new trucks that doubled
our load capacity. These investments have allowed us to more efficiently provide high volumes of food to
pantries while significantly reducing the need to make multiple deliveries to the same location on a given
day. We also increased our cold storage capacity by piloting different solutions, such as leasing
refrigerated trailers when large orders of cold/frozen product are delivered to the food bank. Having
resources like additional reefer trailers has enabled us to source more fresh, perishable produce and
proteins. 
We are also collaborating on a project with our neighboring Feeding America partner food banks to ensure
we are able to solicit as many donations as possible. Our state association, Feeding Pennsylvania, will
assist with solicitation and donor stewardship as we work to build capacity in our warehouse and on our
sourcing team. The result of this collaboration is maintaining a high-level of donated product at a time when
other food banks across the nation have reported a significant decline in product donation. 
Another exciting opportunity that launched in 2023 was the Local Food Purchasing Agreement, a grant
program through USDA that allows us to pair local farms with food pantries and pay a fair wholesale price
for local agricultural products. We have connected with farms in each of the six counties we serve to provide
high-quality, nutrient-dense local foods to people in need while supporting the local agriculture economy.
Funding for that program continues through July 2025. 
Lastly, we formally adopted a Food and Nutrition Policy at the food bank in 2023, which sets ambitious
goals to ensure the food we are providing is healthful and beneficial to our neighbors. The policy is based
on the Healthy Eating Research (HER) guidelines and follows a “stop light” model for ranking foods as
Choose Often, Choose Sometimes, and Choose Rarely. We are currently implementing phase 3 of the
policy, which allows us to have a better understanding of our inventory and how it supports community
health. 
As we move purposefully into 2024, we are looking forward to completing a new strategic plan, fully
implement digital statistic reporting that will allow us to examine deeper customer-level data, and refining
program objectives for school pantries and SNAP outreach. We will also pilot home delivery of senior
boxes throughout the service area (expanding on our 2023 deliveries in Lehigh and Northampton counties). 1/31/2024 7:34 PM CST	© 2024 e-CImpact page 4 of 35

Our program addresses the Priority Need: Basic Needs – Food. Second Harvest will address the need by
providing fresh food for distribution in Monroe County.
Access to nutritious food for under-resourced populations is an immediate need. Nationally, the emergen-cy
food network is serving historic volumes of people. Current inflation, coupled with the close of federal-ly-
funded COVID-related supplemental programs, is causing many families to visit food pantries for the first
time. Often, the only fresh, nutritious foods a family facing food insecurity has access to come from the
emergency food network. 
A pre-pandemic study published in the Journal of Nutrition Education and Behavior, titled, “The Affordabil-ity
of MyPlate: An Analysis of SNAP Benefits and the Actual Cost of Eating According to the Dietary Guide-
lines”, found that following the USDA’s MyPlate Dietary Guidelines would cost a family of four between
$1,000-$1,200 a month ($12,000-$14,400 annually) depending on the age of the family members. For a
comparison, the average middle-income family in the United States spends roughly $6,224 on food each
year with the average lower-income family spending even less at roughly $3,862 per year. Eating healthy
food consistently is simply out of reach for many families. 
Historically, grocery prices typically increase by about 2% each year, on average. According to the Bureau
of Labor Statistics (BLS), the cost of groceries has increased by 23.5% from February 2020 to May 2023.
For comparison, the average hourly wage for all workers in the private sector has risen only 17% during the
same timeframe. Wages are being outpaced by increased cost of living, and many must turn to the
charitable food network for help. 
When money is tight, often the first things cut from the family budget are fresh, nutritious foods. Second
Harvest provides food to 23 emergency food sites within Monroe County. Through partnership with our
member agencies, we work to ensure that all people have access to the foods needed to keep themselves
and their families healthy. If a family can visit our food pantries and bring home groceries at no-cost, it frees
up money to be spent elsewhere, eliminating touch choices such as buying groceries versus paying rent or
utilities.

Second Harvest uses numerous data sources to identify and document needs. As a member of the

What this data indicates in Monroe County is as follows. Feeding America’s Map the Meal Gap indicated
that in 2021, 8.6% of the Monroe County population (14,420 people), including 13.3% of children (4,410),
were food insecure. The U.S. Census American Community Survey (2022) indicates that the individual
poverty rate for Monroe County is 13.1% and 17.2% for children. This is higher than the Pennsylvania

unemployment rate in the County is 4.7% (as compared to 3.4% for all of Pennsylvania). 
Lehigh Valley Health Network’s 2022 Community Health Needs Assessment Health Profile in Monroe
County reported that 33% of Monroe County adults are considered obese (which is higher than the national
average of 29.5%). LVHN’s Health Needs Assessment also indicates that 86% of Monroe County residents
enrolled in Medicare have a diabetes diagnosis. The high incidence of obesity and diabetes may be due, in
part, to lack of access to fresh and healthy foods. The U.S. Department of Agriculture further reports that
there are two low-income, low food access areas, as well as several limited supermarket areas in more
rural parts of Monroe County.

residents comprised 246 of the responses. The survey was administered with the goal of gaining a better
understanding of the people we serve and their relationship with their local pantry, and to identify ways to
strengthen our network.

fruits in the past week.” A parallel question was asked regarding fresh vegetable consumption. 47% of
Monroe County survey participants reported consuming fresh vegetables only 1-3 times per week, and 13%
reported they did not eat any fresh vegetables in the past week.

Approach and Results
Describe the proposed program and how it will achieve the goal and outcome(s) that you
describe in the Performance Measurement Form.
If awarded, funds from Pocono Mountains United Way will support the sourcing and distribution of fresh,
nutritious foods to our network of emergency food providers in Monroe County.
At Second Harvest, we apply a nutrition-focused approach to all aspects of our operation – sourcing,
producing, distributing, and educating. Our approach entails prioritizing the sourcing of fresh, nutritious
foods; innovating distribution strategies to help more people access food, such as through our pop-up and
school pantry programs; leveraging our resources at The Seed Farm to grow food for pantries and to
establish community gardens in urban areas; and educating participants about the importance of healthy
eating and providing them tools to make healthier choices.
Last year, we distributed a total of 3.85 million pounds of food to agencies in Monroe County (the equivalent
of 3.2 million meals), including 2.29 million pounds of perishable protein, fruits and vegetables, and dairy
items. Feeding America developed the F2E framework to align with recommendations from USDA's
MyPlate and Dietary Guidelines for Americans. The framework promotes the consumption of fruits,
vegetables, lean protein, whole grains, and low-fat dairy products.
To increase our distribution of fresh and nutritious foods, and as part of our larger nutrition strategy, we
developed and implemented a Food and Nutrition Policy based on Healthy Eating Research (HER)
guidelines (which were created in partnership between the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation, Duke
University, and the charitable food network). Our Nutrition Policy prioritizes and deprioritizes food by

coincide with Feeding America’s Foods to Encourage. Our Nutrition Policy informs all aspects of our food
sourcing and distribution strategies. 
Because research shows that nutrition education is a critical component in changing food purchasing and
eating behaviors in people with lower-incomes, we implement our Healthy Pantry Initiative (HPI) in tandem
with our Nutrition Policy. HPI is a program of Feeding Pennsylvania that works to encourage pantry
customers to choose healthier food items. Second Harvest’s Nutrition Educator / Healthy Pantry
Coordinator works intimately with pantry directors and volunteers to help them create pantry spaces that are

with pantry participants to ensure that the foods available to take home are prepared and consumed in
healthy ways. Our Nutrition Educator coordinates taste-testing events, cooking demonstrations, and
provides educational materials and recipes cards. Currently, our cohort of HPI sites includes two sites within
Monroe County. 
It is our mission to provide access to food to the increasing number of individuals and families facing food
insecurity, with a distinct focus on fresh and nutritious options. Funding from Pocono Mountains United Way

AGENCY STATUS 
Significant Impact 
Describe significant changes  
In 2023, Second Harvest Food Bank continued to build capacity to meet the growing demand for food 
resources, while focusing more fully on meeting the nutritional needs of the community.  
To meet the demand for additional food resources, we purchased and leased two new trucks that 
doubled our load capacity. These investments have allowed us to more efficiently provide high volumes 
of food to pantries while significantly reducing the need to make multiple deliveries to the same location 
on a given day.  We also increased our cold storage capacity by piloting different solutions, such as 
leasing refrigerated trailers when large orders of cold/frozen product are delivered to the food bank. 
Having resources like additional reefer trailers has enabled us to source more fresh, perishable produce 
and proteins.  
We are also collaborating on a project with our neighboring Feeding America partner food banks to 
ensure we are able to solicit as many donations as possible. Our state association, Feeding Pennsylvania, 
will assist with solicitation and donor stewardship as we work to build capacity in our warehouse and on 
our sourcing team. The result of this collaboration is maintaining a high-level of donated product at a 
time when other food banks across the nation have reported a significant decline in product donation.  
Another exciting opportunity that launched in 2023 was the Local Food Purchasing Agreement, a grant

Statement of Need 
Priority Need: Basic Need (including food) 
Describe the specific Priority Need that the proposed program is planning to address. Be sure to 
describe how this program prioritizes the ALICE population in Monroe County. 
Our program addresses the Priority Need: Basic Needs – Food. Second Harvest will address the need by 
providing fresh food for distribution in Monroe County. 
 
Access to nutritious food for under-resourced populations is an immediate need. Nationally, the emer-
gency food network is serving historic volumes of people. Current inflation, coupled with the close of 
federally-funded COVID-related supplemental programs, is causing many families to visit food pantries 
for the first time. Often, the only fresh, nutritious foods a family facing food insecurity has access to 
come from the emergency food network.   
 
A pre-pandemic study published in the Journal of Nutrition Education and Behavior, titled, “The Afforda-
bility of MyPlate: An Analysis of SNAP Benefits and the Actual Cost of Eating According to the Dietary 
Guidelines”, found that following the USDA’s MyPlate Dietary Guidelines would cost a family of four be-
tween $1,000-$1,200 a month ($12,000-$14,400 annually) depending on the age of the family members. 
For a comparison, the average middle-income family in the United States spends roughly $6,224 on food 
each year with the average lower-income family spending even less at roughly $3,862 per year. Eating 
healthy food consistently is simply out of reach for many families.  
 
Historically, grocery prices typically increase by about 2% each year, on average. According to the 
Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS), the cost of groceries has increased by 23.5% from February 2020 to May 
2023. For comparison, the average hourly wage for all workers in the private sector has risen only 17% 
during the same timeframe. Wages are being outpaced by increased cost of living, and many must turn 
to the charitable food network for help.  
When money is tight, often the first things cut from the family budget are fresh, nutritious foods. Sec-
ond Harvest provides food to 23 emergency food sites within Monroe County. Through partnership with

7 
 
our member agencies, we work to ensure that all people have access to the foods needed to keep them-
selves and their families healthy. If a family can visit our food pantries and bring home groceries at no-
cost, it frees up money to be spent elsewhere, eliminating touch choices such as buying groceries versus 
paying rent or utilities.  
 
Describe the data and relevant data sources used to identify and document the specific need to be 
addressed.  
Second Harvest uses numerous data sources to identify and document needs. As a member of the 
Feeding America network, we have access to a variety of data, including the Map the Meal Gap, the 
Meals per Person in Need (MPIN), and the data site, Tableau. In addition, we use data from local Health 
Needs Assessments, U.S. Census’ American Community Survey, the USDA, and the Food Research & 
Action Center to help us determine the needs in our community. 
What this data indicates in Monroe County is as follows. Feeding America’s Map the Meal Gap indicated 
that in 2021, 8.6% of the Monroe County population (14,420 people), including 13.3% of children 
(4,410), were food insecure. The U.S. Census American Community Survey (2022) indicates that the 
individual poverty rate for Monroe County is 13.1% and 17.2% for children. This is higher than the 
Pennsylvania poverty rate of 11.8%. According to the Pocono Mountains Economic Development 
Corporation, the current unemployment rate in the County is 4.7% (as compared to 3.4% for all of 
Pennsylvania).   
Lehigh Valley Health Network’s 2022 Community Health Needs Assessment Health Profile in Monroe 
County reported that 33% of Monroe County adults are considered obese (which is higher than the 
national average of 29.5%). LVHN’s Health Needs Assessment also indicates that 86% of Monroe County 
residents enrolled in Medicare have a diabetes diagnosis. The high incidence of obesity and diabetes 
may be due, in part, to lack of access to fresh and healthy foods. The U.S. Department of Agriculture 
further reports that there are two low-income, low food access areas, as well as several limited 
supermarket areas in more rural parts of Monroe County. 
Additionally, during the months of August through November 2022, Second Harvest administered our 
“Neighbor Experience Survey” on-site at food pantries across the Second Harvest network. Monroe 
County residents comprised 246 of the responses. The survey was administered with the goal of gaining 
a better understanding of the people we serve and their relationship with their local pantry, and to 
identify ways to strengthen our network.  
Results show that Monroe County pantry participants are not consuming adequate amounts of fresh 
fruits or vegetables. When asked, “During the past week, how many times did you eat fresh fruits?”, 
41% of Monroe County participants responded with, “1-3 times per week”, and 13% responded with, “I 
did not eat any fresh fruits in the past week.” A parallel question was asked regarding fresh vegetable 
consumption. 47% of Monroe County survey participants reported consuming fresh vegetables only 1-3 
times per week, and 13% reported they did not eat any fresh vegetables in the past week.  
It is also important to note that 63% of respondents in Monroe County reported having one or more 
dietary restrictions, with the majority saying they need foods that help them manage diabetes, such as 
fresh fruits and vegetables. 
These data indicate a need for access to fresh, nutritious foods in Monroe County.

Approach and Results 
Describe the proposed program and how it will achieve the goal and outcome(s) that you describe in the 
Performance Measurement Form. 
If awarded, funds from Pocono Mountains United Way will support the sourcing and distribution of 
fresh, nutritious foods to our network of emergency food providers in Monroe County. 
At Second Harvest, we apply a nutrition-focused approach to all aspects of our operation – sourcing, 
producing, distributing, and educating. Our approach entails prioritizing the sourcing of fresh, nutritious 
foods; innovating distribution strategies to help more people access food, such as through our pop-up 
and school pantry programs; leveraging our resources at The Seed Farm to grow food for pantries and to 
establish community gardens in urban areas; and educating participants about the importance of 
healthy eating and providing them tools to make healthier choices. 
Last year, we distributed a total of 3.85 million pounds of food to agencies in Monroe County (the 
equivalent of 3.2 million meals), including 2.29 million pounds of perishable protein, fruits and 
vegetables, and dairy items. Feeding America developed the F2E framework to align with 
recommendations from USDA's MyPlate and Dietary Guidelines for Americans. The framework 
promotes the consumption of fruits, vegetables, lean protein, whole grains, and low-fat dairy products.

9 
 
To increase our distribution of fresh and nutritious foods, and as part of our larger nutrition strategy, we 
developed and implemented a Food and Nutrition Policy based on Healthy Eating Research (HER) 
guidelines (which were created in partnership between the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation, Duke 
University, and the charitable food network). Our Nutrition Policy prioritizes and deprioritizes food by 
categorizing them as “choose often”, “choose sometimes”, and “choose rarely”. “Choose often” foods 
coincide with Feeding America’s Foods to Encourage. Our Nutrition Policy informs all aspects of our food 
sourcing and distribution strategies.  
Because research shows that nutrition education is a critical component in changing food purchasing 
and eating behaviors in people with lower-incomes, we implement our Healthy Pantry Initiative (HPI) in 
tandem with our Nutrition Policy. HPI is a program of Feeding Pennsylvania that works to encourage 
pantry customers to choose healthier food items. Second Harvest’s Nutrition Educator / Healthy Pantry 
Coordinator works intimately with pantry directors and volunteers to help them create pantry spaces 
that are client-centered and promote the consumption of healthy foods. Our Nutrition Educator also 
works closely with pantry participants to ensure that the foods available to take home are prepared and 
consumed in healthy ways. Our Nutrition Educator coordinates taste-testing events, cooking 
demonstrations, and provides educational materials and recipes cards. Currently, our cohort of HPI sites 
includes two sites within Monroe County.  
It is our mission to provide access to food to the increasing number of individuals and families facing 
food insecurity, with a distinct focus on fresh and nutritious options. Funding from Pocono Mountains 
United Way will make it possible for Second Harvest to distribute fresh foods to our network of 23 
pantries in Monroe County. Collectively, these pantries serve more than 17,540 people each month.  
 
List the specific activities to be implemented under the proposed program and how they relate to the 
stated outcome(s) and output(s) for the program. 
Second Harvest will source and distribute fresh, nutritious foods to our network of emergency food 
providers within Monroe County.  
Upon receiving food, our nutrition and inventory control staff rank each product as “choose often”, 
“choose sometimes”, and “choose rarely”. This information is entered into Primarius, a web-based 
inventory management system designed specifically for food banks, The ranking system gives our pantry 
coordinators greater detail and insight into the nutritional content of the foods they order for their 
participants.  
Second Harvest member agencies use Primarius to place their orders online. Second Harvest warehouse 
crew members pick the order and schedule for delivery. Second Harvest drivers deliver the food directly 
to the food pantry. By timing our delivery to align with pantry open hours, we implement a “Just in 
Time” (JIT) delivery method, when possible. JIT delivery eliminates storage constraints at the pantry-
level, which leads to the pantry accepting more fresh, refrigerated, and frozen products, resulting in 
more families gaining access to these nutritious foods.  
Our food pantry coordinators work with their teams of volunteers to serve participants. Pantries are re-
sponsible for submitting monthly reports, stating the number of people served. Collectively, our net-
work of pantries in Monroe County serves 17,540 people each month.
