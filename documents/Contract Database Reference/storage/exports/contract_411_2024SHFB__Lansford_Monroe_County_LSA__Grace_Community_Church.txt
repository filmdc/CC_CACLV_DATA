Monroe County Local Share Account 2023 
Panther Valley Community Center
Exhibit 1: Project Description
 Proposed Project Including Activities and Expected Results 
This project would renovate and rehabilitate the English Congregational Church of Lansford (Carbon County, PA) with the goal of providing a food pantry, meal center, and wraparound support services for the Panther Valley area. Grace Community Church has purchased the building and the site has been assessed for needed repairs. The project would relocate the current Panther Valley Food Pantry from the basement of Grace Community Church, establish a meal center, recreational space open to the public, and office space for outpatient services, job support, county assistance services and elected officials. Funding through the Monroe County Local Share Account will support Phase 1 of the renovations to make necessary repairs and updates to the building’s infrastructure and ensure compliance with local building codes.
The Panther Valley Food Pantry is a member agency of Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast Pennsylvania. Second Harvest, a member of Feeding America – the nation’s largest charitable food network, serves Lehigh, Northampton, Carbon, Monroe, Pike, and Wayne counties. Last year, Second Harvest distributed 14.7 million pounds of food to its network of 200+ nonprofit agencies, (food pantries, schools, meal centers, shelters, childcare agencies, addiction treatment programs), a 44% increase from the previous year. Included in this total is 6.7 million pounds of fresh produce, perishable protein, and fresh dairy products (46% of total distribution). Annually, Second Harvest rescues more than 3 million pounds of food from ending up in a landfill. 
On average, the Second Harvest network serves more than 87,000 people each month - up 52% from last year. The pantries within the Second Harvest network are operated almost solely through the work of community volunteers. Second Harvest’s food distribution is a model of neighbors helping neighbors. 
As a member of Second Harvest, the Panther Valley Food Pantry receives food and resources for technical support. The Panther Valley Food Pantry is in excellent standing – the pantry submits timely monthly reports and is compliant in all food safety and client non-discrimination audits.  
Currently, the Panther Valley Food Pantry, a critical community resource, is located in a church basement making accessibility challenging. Even so, the pantry has seen an influx of new customers. Over the last 12 months, the pantry has served, on average, 174 individuals per month – a 67% increase from the previous 12-month period. The largest increases were seen among seniors needing food assistance (+37%).
In addition to accessibility challenges for clients, the current set-up poses difficulty for accepting food deliveries and bringing them into the building. It is an arduous process that is taking a toll on pantry volunteers. Additionally, the pantry is cramped and does not allow adequate space for refrigerated or frozen storage. Currently, the pantry must turn away donations of fresh or frozen foods (often healthier options, and the foods that pantry clients are most in need of) due to lack of storage capacity. 
Moving the Panther Valley Food Pantry into a newly renovated and much expanded space that includes a meal center, recreational space open to the public, and office space for outpatient services, job support, county assistance services and elected officials, will have a tremendous impact in the community. No pantry in the County currently provides all of these services in one location. 
To accomplish this outcome, Grace Community Church has plans to renovate the building. The building has been assessed and the general scope of work includes:
Dismantling of church tower
Upgrades to electrical and water systems
Installation of new heating system (replace oil steam with HVAC units and insulate)
Bring building up to ADA standards
General renovation of building to include space for: offices, food pantry, meal center, athletic center, and multi-purpose community room
Monroe County LSA funds will support Phase 1 of the project, which includes all repairs to the exterior of the building and all repairs, upgrades, and renovations to the first floor of the building. The first floor will house the food pantry, meeting rooms, and office spaces.  A quote from Berks Customs LLC is included as an attachment to this application and details an itemized description of the work to be performed, with associated costs.

Priority Investment in Regional Plan 
This project has been identified as a priority investment in the Second Harvest Food Bank of the Lehigh Valley and Northeast Pennsylvania’s Carbon County Food Insecurity Report, which outlines the food security needs of the community. A copy of the report is included as an attachment to this application. 
This draft report is the first step of the Carbon County Community Hunger Mapping Project, an initiative of Second Harvest Food Bank, which will assist in improving access to charitable food, a better understanding of the causes of food insecurity in Carbon County, and ultimately, working to end hunger across the county.
The project and report aim to provide actionable, evidence-based recommendations to reduce food insecurity in Carbon County, with the goal of enabling Carbon County stakeholders, policymakers, and neighbors to make informed decisions on the direction and priorities of the charitable food system.
Anticipated Community Impact
Carbon County Food Insecurity
One in nine Carbon County residents faces food insecurity. According to Feeding America’s most recent Map the Meal Gap study (2021), Carbon County has an overall food insecurity rate of 11%. This means that more than 7,000 individuals do not have certain access to adequate food throughout the year. 
Child Nutrition
14.% children in Carbon County face food insecurity – a rate higher than the state average of 12.2%
An estimated 1,820 children face food insecurity, with 74% of children in households earning less than 185% of the federal poverty level facing food insecurity and 26% of children in households earning more than 185% of the federal poverty level facing food insecurity.
Senior Nutrition
Data from the State of Senior Hunger 2021 report estimates that in Pennsylvania, 4.7% of seniors are food insecure with 1.7% facing extremely high levels of food insecurity. While the data set does not drill down to the county level, U.S. Census data show 1,321 adults older than age 65 live below the poverty level in Carbon County.
Racial and Ethnic Disparities
More than 90% of the Carbon County population is non-Hispanic white, but residents who are black and/or Hispanic face much higher levels of food insecurity across the county. 
Food insecurity among black and Hispanic residents is 19%, compared with 9% of white residents.
SNAP
Map the Meal Gap 2021 estimates that 29% of food insecure Carbon County residents are above the income threshold to qualify for SNAP benefits (200% of the federal poverty level), while 71% are below the threshold and may qualify for supplemental nutrition benefits and programs.
Lansford Borough (Census Tract 204)
Lansford Borough is designated as a low-income/low-access census tract based on USDA’s most severe determination. With more than 1/3 of the population living more than 1 mile from the nearest grocery stores and 1/3 of the population living at or below the federal poverty threshold.
Poverty rate is 22.9%
2,451 people in the borough live more than .5 miles from the nearest supermarket; 1,257 people live more than 1 mile from the nearest grocery store, including 546 low-income residents.
1,945 residents are low-income, including 890 children and 723 seniors.
According to the latest Map the Meal Gap figures (2021), 903 food insecure people live in this joint census tract.
Lansford Borough is a former coal mining town that is working toward economic growth but has limited resources currently to serve community members in need. Of the 4,110 borough residents, 23.9% are living below the federal poverty level, including nearly 35% of children and more than 19% of residents older than age 60. About 10% of the population are U.S. military veterans; of those veterans about 30% live below the poverty level and nearly 60% have a disability.
Many in the Panther Valley area who need help have no transportation, making it extremely difficult to access county resources that are concentrated in Lehighton and Jim Thorpe – especially if their needs are ongoing. Currently, the Panther Valley Food Pantry, a critical community resource, is located in a church basement making accessibility challenging. Even so, the pantry has seen an influx of new customers. Over the last 12 months, the pantry has served, on average, 174 individuals per month – a 67% increase from the previous 12-month period. The largest increases were seen among seniors needing food assistance (+37%).
There is also concern that there is no succession plan for Summit Hill Food Pantry. It is likely that this pantry will be longer service the community once current leadership steps down. Summit Hill neighbors will need a new pantry that will be able to absorb the increased numbers.
Second Harvest Food Bank is working on a project with the Panther Valley Food Pantry that would renovate the English Congregational Church of Lansford in Carbon County with the goal of providing a food pantry, meal center and wraparound support services for the Panther Valley area. No pantry in the County currently provides all of these services in one location. Centralizing multiple services and community spaces at a walkable site, built by those who worked in the coal mines many years ago, will allow borough residents to access resources in a dignified way that supports their community pride and history. Panther Valley Food Pantry will also be open additional hours for food distribution at the new location. Second Harvest is currently securing funding for this project through an appropriations earmark via Rep. Susan Wild’s office, LSA grants, and private contributions.
The pantry will serve Lansford, Summit Hill, and Nesquehoning. The pantry has already taken over all Second Harvest senior box distribution throughout the region and the new, expanded location will allow the pantry to bring Second Harvest’s MilitaryShare program to the borough.
The renovation of the English Congregational Church of Lansford to provide a food pantry, meal center, and wraparound support will have a tremendous impact for the residents of Lansford Borough and the greater Carbon County community.
